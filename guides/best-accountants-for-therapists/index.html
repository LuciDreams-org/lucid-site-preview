What a therapy group needs from an accountant

Five areas where groups go wrong, and what to ask any firm.

Clinician pay and W-2 versus 1099

A split follows what you collect. A flat rate or salary pays the clinician even when a claim is slow or denied, so you carry the gap. Ask whether the firm can model each against your payer mix.

A 1099 label does not decide classification. If you assign clients, set fees, require your systems and schedule, and supply the office, the clinician often looks like an employee, and state rules can be stricter than federal rules. Lucid reviews each clinician's setup against the IRS and state factors and shows the cost of moving from 1099 to W-2. It is a bookkeeping and tax review, not legal advice, so involve an employment attorney for a dispute.

Insurance and private pay mix

Revenue on the books can look better than cash in the bank. Ask the firm to separate insurance from private pay, reconcile deposits and show receivables aging. Lucid does that from your billing data and shows payer mix, so you can see which panels are worth keeping. Your biller still files and follows up on claims.

Credentialing lag and cash gaps

A new clinician often cannot bill a payer until credentialing finishes, while you pay wages from day one. Ask whether the firm builds that gap into a cash forecast before you make the offer. Lucid does. It does not run credentialing.

Supervisees and associates

Pre licensed associates are usually paid less than licensed peers, and supervision fees need consistent treatment in the books. Ask how the firm records them and shows what a supervisee earns the practice.

Telehealth and several states

If clinicians or clients sit in more than one state, you may owe state returns and payroll registrations in places you do not expect. Ask who tracks it. Lucid tracks revenue by state and handles multi state filings. Wellness Fi lists multi state returns for telehealth practices, at $150 for each extra state.

Questions owners ask

What are the alternatives to Heard for a group practice with employees?

Lucid is the strongest alternative for a group with staff, several locations or several owners, because you see your price from $130 a month, get a CPA on every account and 24/7 support, and every entity type is covered. Wellness Fi, Traktion Accounting and GreenOak are therapy focused alternatives. Bench, Pilot and Bookkeeper360 are general services.

Does Heard work for a group practice?

Yes, for single owner groups. Heard lists a group plan at $349 a month, or $299 billed annually. Its pricing page says multi owner practices and C corporations are not eligible, so confirm your structure with Heard. Lucid covers several owners and every entity type.

How do I know how much profit each therapist in my group practice makes?

Your books need to track collected revenue and pay by clinician, not in one lump. Lucid sets that up and shows what each clinician brings in, what they cost with payroll tax, and what is left.

Can I pay the therapists in my group practice as 1099 contractors?

Sometimes, but only when the clinician truly runs their own practice and could continue without you. If you assign clients, set fees and require your systems and schedule, that usually points to an employee. Lucid reviews each clinician and shows what a switch to W-2 would cost.

Why is my therapy group practice busy but short on cash?

Common causes are slow insurance payment, pay set above what payer rates support, and new hires who cannot bill until credentialing finishes. Lucid reconciles deposits by payer, tracks receivables aging and builds hiring gaps into a cash forecast.

How much does an accountant for a therapy group cost?

Lucid starts from $130 a month, month to month with no setup fee, and its pricing page calculator shows your price in seconds. Do I need a CPA for my group practice?

Many owners want one because pay, entity and state tax choices affect income. Every Lucid account has a dedicated CPA, so you have one from day one.

Can Lucid run payroll or file insurance claims for my group?

No. Lucid does not run payroll, billing or claims. It reconciles your payroll and deposits into the books and reports on them, with a dedicated CPA and 24/7 support. Pair it with a payroll provider and your biller.
