Short answer

For ophthalmology groups the usual shortlist is ModMed EMA, Nextech and Compulink, all of which handle surgery and an ASC. For optometry practices with a busy optical, RevolutionEHR, Eyefinity Encompass, Crystal PM and Compulink are the common picks, and RevolutionEHR and Eyefinity publish prices. Mixed groups with both sides often choose Compulink, or pair a surgical EHR with an optical system. None of them keeps your books, so plan the money side of exam, surgical and optical revenue separately.

What an eye care group needs from an EHR

An eye care practice runs up to three businesses. The exam lanes bill medical insurance and vision plans. The optical sells frames, lenses and contacts at retail. Ophthalmology groups add surgery, often through their own ASC. The EHR has to keep those apart and still let a patient move from exam to optical in one visit.

  1. Eye care charting: drawings, subspecialty templates, glaucoma and cataract workflows.
  2. Imaging: OCT, fundus photos and visual fields pulled from devices into the chart.
  3. Vision and medical billing: vision plan claims and medical claims on the same patient, with the right fee schedule for each.
  4. Optical POS: frames and lens sales, lab orders and product stock as a software feature.
  5. Surgery and ASC: surgical scheduling, operative notes and a link to the surgery center.
  6. Reports by location, provider and line of business, with exports your accountant can use.

Keep that list in front of you during demos. For a wider look across specialties, see our guide to practice management software.

ModMed EMA

ModMed builds EMA, a specialty EHR, and says its ophthalmology software is built by ophthalmologists. Its ophthalmology suite lists touch-based charting, cloud image management, medical billing and collections, and patient tools for reminders, open slots and out-of-pocket estimates. ModMed sells ophthalmology ASC software so a group can run the surgery center and practice as one.

For the optical, ModMed's page says its software interfaces with partner systems for optical point of sale and frame inventory, which work with EMA and its practice management system. ModMed's own inventory tool covers medical products dispensed in the visit, such as drops and vitamins. Pricing is not published.

Best for: ophthalmology groups with surgery and an ASC. Watch for: the optical runs through a partner system, so price both pieces and test the hand-off.

Nextech

Nextech sells an ophthalmology EHR and the Practice+ management system. Its EHR page lists subspecialty charting, layered drawings, integrated image management and Cora Scribe for documentation. Integrated ASC charting shares patient, scheduling and clinical data between the practice and the surgery center.

For the optical, Nextech has an integration with Ocuco's optical software that links exam scheduling to eyewear selection and purchase. Practice+ adds multi-location scheduling, claims and payment posting, and core reports, and Nextech offers RCM services. Pricing is by quote.

Best for: ophthalmology groups that want ASC charting tied to the clinic. Watch for: the optical runs through Ocuco, so confirm what is built in and what is a partner product.

Compulink

Compulink sells Eyecare Advantage for optometry and an ophthalmology edition, with EHR, practice management, optical POS, inventory and ASC software from one vendor. Its optical tools list frame and contact lens inventory, automatic updates of optical plans and the lens catalog, spectacle and contact lens job costing, and cost of goods and sales reports. Its billing supports unlimited plans and multiple fee schedules, which matters when the same patient has a vision plan and medical insurance. Pricing is by quote.

Best for: groups that run ophthalmology, optometry and a busy optical together. Watch for: a broad system takes setup time, so plan training for the optical staff as well as the clinic.

RevolutionEHR

RevolutionEHR is a cloud EHR and practice management system for optometry. Its pricing page lists Core at $455, Advanced from $660 and Premium from $830, plus a separate plan for exam or optical-only locations from $319. The page does not state the billing period, and it adds a one-time setup fee and a database conversion fee, with no long-term contracts. Its optical shop software covers frames, lenses, contact orders, lab orders and point of sale, and updates stock after each sale. Add-ons cover claims processing, payments and patient messaging.

Best for: optometry practices and optical shops that want a published price. Watch for: ask for the full monthly and setup cost for your locations and providers in writing.

Eyefinity Encompass

Eyefinity, part of VSP Vision, sells Encompass, an optometry EHR and practice management platform. Its pricing page lists Essential from $340 a month and Enhanced from $758 a month, for one location and one provider in year one of a one-year contract. Those prices require EncompassPay and exclude onboarding fees. Encompass includes optical order and inventory management, multi-location tools and VSP integration for real-time eligibility and authorizations. In 2025 NextGen and Eyefinity announced an alignment that links NextGen's EHR with Eyefinity's optical and retail tools.

Best for: optometry practices with heavy VSP volume. Watch for: the advertised price is tied to a contract term and EncompassPay, so model year two.

Crystal PM

Crystal PM is optometry practice management and EHR software, sold in Essentials, Plus and Pro plans on the cloud or in office. Every plan includes optical inventory, payments, claims processing and device links. Plus adds automated EOB importing and enhanced reports, and Pro adds a direct VSP integration and online scheduling. The base price is by quote, while the page lists fees for each additional provider.

Best for: exam and optical optometry practices that want plan tiers. Watch for: base prices are not published, so compare a full quote.

MDoffice

MDoffice, now from Sightview, is an EHR and practice management system for ophthalmology and optometry. Sightview lists integrated diagnostic imaging and surgical planning, ASC operations, vision and medical billing tools, and optical sales reporting. Its March 2026 cost disclosure says there is a base license and subscription fee per provider, with other fees possible for e-prescribing, the patient portal and imaging device links. Dollar amounts are not published.

Best for: ophthalmology practices that want surgery, imaging and an ASC in one system. Watch for: device link fees, so list every imaging device you need connected.

Other names on eye care shortlists include Epic Kaleidoscope for health system eye clinics, NextGen, IntelleChartPRO and EyeMD. See the vendor's site for current pricing.

How to choose

  1. Mostly surgical ophthalmology with an ASC: ModMed EMA, Nextech or MDoffice.
  2. Ophthalmology, optometry and a large optical under one roof: Compulink, or a surgical EHR paired with an optical system.
  3. Optometry with a busy optical and a published price: RevolutionEHR or Eyefinity Encompass.
  4. Optometry with heavy VSP volume: Eyefinity Encompass or Crystal PM Pro.
  5. You are tied to a hospital system: Epic Kaleidoscope.
  6. Weighing a surgery center stake: read our guide on ASC investment versus an office surgery suite.

Ask every vendor the same four questions. What is the total monthly cost for our providers, locations and modules? How do exam, surgical and optical revenue show up in reports? Can we export sales, cost of goods, payments and refunds by location to a spreadsheet? How do card payouts and vision plan payments reach our bank?

ModMed and Nextech also lead in other surgical specialties. See Best EHR for Orthopedic Practices? and Best EMR for Plastic Surgery Practices? For recall texting and review requests, see Best Patient Review and Messaging Software?

Your EHR and your books

An EHR runs the clinic. It does not run the finances. It shows charges and optical sales posted. Your bank shows vision plan and medical deposits on different clocks, and card payouts net of fees. Frame and lens costs, lab bills, payroll and rent never show up as margin in the EHR.

So the EHR cannot tell you whether the optical really pays after cost of goods, or how exam, surgical and optical margins compare by location. That takes books with each line kept apart, reconciled to the bank every month. Buyers ask for the same split, so see who is buying ophthalmology and optometry practices if a sale is on your mind.

Questions, answered

Frequently asked questions

What is the best EHR for an eye care practice?

It depends on your mix. Ophthalmology groups usually shortlist ModMed EMA, Nextech and Compulink. Optometry practices with an optical usually look at RevolutionEHR, Eyefinity Encompass, Crystal PM and Compulink.

Is Nextech or ModMed better for ophthalmology?

Both are built for ophthalmology and both offer ASC software. Nextech highlights integrated ASC charting and layered drawings, while ModMed highlights touch-based charting and cloud image management. Both handle the optical through partner integrations, so test that part in the demo.

Which eye care EHR has optical POS built in?

Compulink, RevolutionEHR, Eyefinity Encompass and Crystal PM list optical POS or optical inventory as part of their systems. ModMed and Nextech connect to partner optical software. Treat frame and lens stock as a software feature to test, not a reason to skip a demo.

How do these systems handle vision plans and medical insurance?

Most eye care systems bill both on the same patient. Compulink supports unlimited plans and fee schedules, Eyefinity and Crystal PM Pro list VSP integration, and MDoffice lists vision and medical billing tools. Ask how each one reports vision and medical revenue apart.

Which eye care EHR handles imaging?

ModMed lists cloud image management, Nextech lists integrated image management, and MDoffice lists integrated diagnostic imaging. Crystal PM includes device links in every plan. Bring a list of your OCT and other devices to every demo.

How much does an optometry EHR cost?

RevolutionEHR lists Core at $455, Advanced from $660 and Premium from $830, plus setup fees. Eyefinity Encompass lists Essential from $340 a month and Enhanced from $758 a month on a one-year contract. ModMed, Nextech, Compulink, Crystal PM base plans and MDoffice quote by practice.

Can the EHR show optical margin?

Some systems report optical sales and cost of goods, and Compulink lists job costing. True margin also needs lab bills, optical staff pay and rent, which live in your books, not the EHR.

Does Lucid replace our EHR or optical POS?

No. Lucid is not an EHR or a POS, and does not file claims, run billing, run payroll or track inventory. Lucid keeps the books, files taxes and provides CFO services beside whichever EHR you choose.
