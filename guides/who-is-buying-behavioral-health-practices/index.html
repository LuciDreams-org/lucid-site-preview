Who is buying behavioral health practices?

Short answer

Most buyers of outpatient psychiatry and therapy practices are private equity backed platforms, led by Beacon Behavioral Partners, ARC Health, Mindpath Health, Sagent Behavioral Health and PAX Health. Public and strategic buyers include LifeStance Health, which restarted small tuck in deals in 2026, and Optum's Refresh Mental Health, while Centerstone is the largest nonprofit acquirer. Published ranges put most practice deals at about 4 to 8 times EBITDA, with small counseling centers priced on SDE and payer mix moving the number. Offers mix cash at closing with rollover equity, earnouts and seller notes, so compare the full structure, not only the headline price.

The market in numbers

Behavioral health is still mostly independent. A July 2023 study in JAMA Psychiatry, cited by DealSeam, found private equity owned 6.2% of US outpatient and residential mental health facilities, though the share passed 20% in Colorado, Texas and North Carolina.

Deal volume rose sharply, then cooled, then picked up again:

  1. LevinPro HC counted 104 behavioral health deals announced in 2025, up 42% from 2024, with counseling and psychiatric care making up 52 of them (per DealSeam, updated October 6, 2026).
  2. Mertz Taggart counted 28 traditional deals closed in Q3 2026, up from 23 in Q2 2026. Mental health led with 24 closed deals, its highest total since Q1 2025.
  3. Half of Q3 2026 traditional deals were add ons by platforms backed by private equity or venture investors, per the same Mertz Taggart report (November 2026).

Mertz Taggart's managing partner said in that report that outpatient psychiatry with good payer contracts remains one of the most sought after assets his firm sees.

The buyers, one by one

Beacon Behavioral Partners. Behavioral Health Business (August 12, 2026) describes Beacon as a partner to psychiatrist owned practices: it acquires the practice, provides back end support and gives clinicians an ownership stake. Mertz Taggart named it the most active buyer of Q1 2026, and in Q3 2026 it added Pure Psychiatry in Michigan and Ohio, AR Psychiatric and Counseling Center in Georgia, and Prime Psychiatry and Invicta Healthcare in Texas. Latticework invested in 2020, and Behavioral Health Business reported that a sale process was being prepared, so ownership may change.

LifeStance Health. The largest public outpatient mental health platform. Its CEO said on the Q1 2026 call that "tuck in acquisitions are our preferred way of entering new MSAs" and that, after three years, it is back to M&A (Behavioral Health Business, May 7, 2026). It closed two tuck ins by May and a small one in Arizona in Q2. It has said larger practices have not made financial sense to buy so far, so it fits owners in markets where LifeStance is not yet present.

Refresh Mental Health (Optum). Optum, part of UnitedHealth Group, acquired Refresh from Kelso and Company in early 2022, per CT Acquisitions' tracker. Refresh's own site describes about 3,000 clinicians in nearly 300 locations. A payer owned strategic buyer weighs network fill and access, not only a resale price.

ARC Health. Backed by Thurston Group, ARC reported 1,300 plus clinicians in 91 locations across 20 states in November 2025. DealSeam notes ARC describes its practices as equity owning partners, and The Waverly Group kept its brand and clinical leadership when it joined in July 2026.

Mindpath Health. Deal databases record Centerbridge Partners and Leonard Green and Partners as backers since December 2020. CT Acquisitions lists it with LifeStance and Refresh among the main outpatient mental health platforms.

Thriveworks. DealSeam lists Regal Healthcare Capital Partners as its largest equity owner. Its site describes more than 2,600 employed, licensed clinicians. It grows mostly by opening its own clinics; Synchronous Health (October 2024) was its first acquisition.

Sagent Behavioral Health. Formerly Nystrom and Associates, backed by Nautic Partners. DealSeam lists 84 locations across Minnesota, Iowa, North Dakota, Missouri and Wisconsin with about 1,600 clinicians, after adding Ellie Mental Health's 23 Minnesota clinics in August 2025.

Centerstone. A nonprofit that completed its merger with Brightli in November 2025, creating what both called the largest nonprofit behavioral health provider in the US. Capstone Partners notes each had completed 20 or more acquisitions. Nonprofit buyers often suit Medicaid and community based practices.

Other active buyers. PAX Health (HCAP Partners and Hamilton Lane) focuses on neuropsychology and workers' compensation. Radial (General Catalyst) buys interventional psychiatry businesses offering TMS, Spravato and ketamine. KKR backed Geode Health agreed to take over Baylor Scott and White's outpatient mental health services in Texas (Mertz Taggart, Q3 2026). Trackers also list Orchard Mental Health Group (The Graham Group), Sero Mental Health (Lindsay Goldberg) and Hightop Health (JLL Partners and SV Health Investors). Universal Health Services closed its purchase of Talkspace in August 2026.

A note on Talkiatry. Talkiatry, an in network psychiatry provider, raised a $210 million Series D in February 2026 and added therapy nationwide in October 2026. It does not appear as a practice buyer in these sources, but it competes for the same psychiatrists and therapists, which matters because clinician retention is a key diligence item.

How these deals are structured

Many outpatient psychiatry deals use an MSO model. State corporate practice of medicine rules, which CT Acquisitions lists as a diligence item, often mean the platform's management company owns the business side while licensed clinicians own the clinical entity. Therapy only groups can face different rules by state, so ask a healthcare attorney.

CT Acquisitions' behavioral health sell side guide (last reviewed July 2026) gives illustrative structures by size, and stresses that practice level multiples are not consistently disclosed:

  1. Under $1 million of adjusted EBITDA. Low single digit multiples, often an asset sale, with seller notes common.
  2. $1 million to $3 million. Mid single digits from platform tuck ins, with rollover equity often 10% to 20%.
  3. $3 million to $10 million. High single to low double digits, with earnouts tied to provider retention.

Other published ranges, as compiled by DealSeam (October 6, 2026):

  1. Vallexa Advisors (June 2026): 4 to 8 times EBITDA for small practices and add ons, and 10 to 14 times for scaled, accredited platforms.
  2. Sofer Advisors (2026): 4 to 6 times for single sites under $500,000 of EBITDA, 6 to 8 times for groups with $500,000 to $2 million.
  3. Peak Business Valuation: counseling centers at 1.94 to 3.14 times SDE.

Sofer adds that earnouts are common and can add 1 to 3 turns, tied to results after the sale. Treat all of these as ranges, not quotes. See how a medical practice is valued.

What behavioral health buyers look for

Across the sources, the same items come up:

  1. Payer mix. Sofer Advisors says a practice with 60% or more of revenue from Medicaid will be valued at the low end, and a mainly commercial practice can trade at 1 to 3 times more EBITDA than an otherwise identical Medicaid practice. See insurance vs private pay for a group practice.
  2. Clinician retention. CT Acquisitions calls retention of psychiatrists, psychiatric nurse practitioners and therapists after closing the most stress tested assumption in diligence.
  3. Service mix. Beacon and LifeStance both point to interventional psychiatry such as TMS and Spravato.
  4. Market depth over map dots. At a Behavioral Health Business event (November 2, 2026), buyers said they now want deeper penetration in fewer markets, quality data and control of patient acquisition cost.
  5. Clean adjusted EBITDA. CT Acquisitions lists owner pay, rent normalization and one time items as typical add backs, plus concentration above about 20% of revenue in one payer or referral source as a flag.
  6. Credentialing speed and AR. Days from a new hire's start to the first billable session, denial rates and days in AR all show up in the review.

Most of that list comes down to the books: accrual statements that split insurance and private pay, show revenue and margin per clinician and location, and tie collections to the bank. Your clinician pay model also gets reviewed, since closing paperwork includes provider employment or contractor transition agreements. How to compare offers

Two offers with the same headline price can be worth very different amounts. Line them up on the same page:

  1. Cash at closing, after fees, debt payoff and taxes.
  2. Rollover equity: how much, at what value, what rights it carries and when it can be sold.
  3. Seller notes and earnouts: the amounts, the conditions, and who controls the numbers that decide them.
  4. Your pay after the sale: the compensation reset, productivity formula and benefits.
  5. Clinician terms: what your therapists and prescribers are offered, since their retention can drive your earnout.
  6. Non compete, term of employment and what happens if you leave early.
  7. Clinical autonomy, brand and who decides on growth.

Model each offer over five years with your CPA, and have a healthcare attorney read the paperwork. An advisor who knows behavioral health buyers can put several of them in front of you at once.

Questions, answered

Frequently asked questions

Who is buying behavioral health practices right now?

Mostly private equity backed platforms such as Beacon Behavioral Partners, ARC Health, Mindpath Health, Sagent and PAX Health. LifeStance Health restarted small tuck in acquisitions in 2026, Optum owns Refresh Mental Health, and Centerstone is the largest nonprofit acquirer.

What multiple do behavioral health practices sell for?

Published ranges put small practices and add ons at about 4 to 8 times EBITDA (Vallexa Advisors, June 2026) and counseling centers at about 1.94 to 3.14 times SDE (Peak Business Valuation). Scaled platforms reach higher. A formal valuation gives the number for your practice.

Does payer mix change what buyers pay?

Yes. Sofer Advisors says a practice with 60% or more Medicaid revenue lands at the low end, and a mainly commercial practice can trade at 1 to 3 times more EBITDA than a similar Medicaid practice.

Do buyers prefer psychiatry or therapy only practices?

The most active buyers focus on psychiatry, especially medication management with TMS and Spravato. Therapy groups still sell, often to LifeStance, nonprofit buyers or regional platforms, and often at lower multiples.

Will I keep my practice name and some ownership?

Often, yes. Beacon gives clinicians an ownership stake, and ARC Health describes its practices as equity owning partners. Get any brand or equity promise in writing.

How do I prepare my behavioral health practice for a buyer?

Start two to three years out with accrual monthly books, revenue split by payer and clinician, a documented add back file and market owner pay. Then hire an advisor and attorney before you take offers.
