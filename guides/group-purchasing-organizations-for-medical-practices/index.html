Short answer

For most independent practices, yes, if the group covers what you already buy and costs little or nothing to join. Medical groups such as MediGroup, PAA, MPPG and Afaxys publish free membership, PBG focuses on vaccines and rebates, dental groups include Sevaredent, Dental Buying Power and Dental Purchasing Partners, and eye care owners compare IDOC, PECAA and Vision Source. Judge a group by your own invoices: net savings after fees, rebates paid, and any rules on which brands you must buy. Lucid is not a GPO, but it shows whether the savings reach your margin by tracking supply cost as a percent of collections and margin by service line.

How GPOs make money

Most free GPOs are paid by vendors. The OIG's GPO safe harbor protects vendor fees paid to a GPO when the GPO has a written agreement with members and discloses the fees. In one OIG advisory opinion, a GPO's member agreements set vendor administrative fees at 3 percent or less of the purchase price; another described fees from 0.25 to 3 percent. Some groups, mostly dental and eye care, also charge a membership fee. Method, a dental purchasing platform, notes that fees can be one time, monthly or yearly, and that some groups add administrative fees on contract use. Ask every group how it is paid and whether any rebate comes back to you.

Medical GPOs and vaccine buying groups

The American Academy of Pediatrics separates two models. Vaccine buying groups contract with one or two major vaccine makers, which lowers cost and may add rebates, but limits buying outside the contract. Hospital style GPOs are not tied to certain makers, but the AAP says their pricing is often less favorable for vaccines.

MediGroup

MediGroup says it has served practices, surgery centers and other non acute facilities since 1999. It works with your existing distributor, so you can keep your current supplier and use only the contracts that fit. It also offers capital equipment pricing, non clinical services and an employee perks program. Its enrollment page reads "Join MediGroup at No Cost."

Best for: practices that want supply savings without switching distributors. Limits: rebate terms are not published on the home page.

PAA

PAA is a nonprofit buying group founded in 1992. It says it represents over 100,000 providers, membership is free, and participation in each agreement is voluntary. Agreements cover vaccines from GSK, Sanofi, Pfizer, Moderna and CSL Seqirus, plus IUDs, malpractice insurance, clearinghouse services and employee perks. PAA says its Vaccine Rebate Program has paid members $24 million, on top of manufacturer pricing. Members order from the vendor directly.

Best for: primary care and pediatric practices with high vaccine spend. See the Lucid primary care page. Limits: pricing and terms are visible only after you log in.

MPPG

MPPG (Medical Practice Purchasing Group) calls itself a buying group with over 6,000 members. It covers vaccines, medical and office supplies, essential services and liability coverage, and says it is "100% free to join." You link your practice to vaccine contracts on its participation form.

Best for: practices that want vaccine contracts plus everyday supplies. Limits: its site blocked our direct check, so details come from search results.

PBG

PBG says it has more than 40 years in vaccines, over $300 million in yearly purchases and over $65 million in rebates paid. It is a benefit corporation that says it returns most of its revenue to members. Categories now include medical supplies, equipment and furniture, office supplies, HR, merchant services and medical waste.

Best for: vaccine buyers who want rebate checks. Limits: pricing is shown only after you create an account.

Afaxys GPO

Afaxys GPO serves community and public health providers and qualified private practice clinics. Its application page says "No member fees. No minimum purchase volumes." The wider Afaxys group supplies contraceptives to that market.

Best for: women's health, family planning and community clinics. Limits: a narrower catalog than a general medical GPO.

Dental buying groups

Sevaredent

Sevaredent is a dental GPO for mid to large DSOs and group practices with several locations. It says joining is free, through a no cost participation agreement with no fees or subscriptions, and it reviews your vendor usage to find savings.

Best for: dental groups with several offices. See the Lucid dental page. Limits: built for groups, not single offices.

Dental Buying Power

Dental Buying Power says thousands of independent dental practices pool purchasing. It lists a 1 percent rebate on qualifying merchandise, 2 percent on Henry Schein brand items, implant discounts, equipment trade up offers and service discounts.

Best for: single location dental owners who buy through Henry Schein. Limits: a membership fee is not shown on the home page.

Dental Purchasing Partners

Dental Purchasing Partners publishes two plans: Basic at $97, month to month, and Pro at $1,044, which it says saves 10 percent versus monthly payments. You keep your current vendors and get pre negotiated discounts.

Best for: owners who want a published price. Limits: the savings must beat the fee, so run the numbers on your own invoices.

Optometry buying groups

  1. IDOC. Its FAQ lists three plans from $150 to $450 a month. IDOC says Essential members earn an average of 27 times their membership cost across its services, a vendor claim to check against your own invoices. It also offers labs, analytics and business advisors.
  2. PECAA. PECAA Max is $279 a month with no contract and includes vendor discounts, rebates, advisors and HR support. Its cold start option has no cost for one year and reduced second year fees.
  3. Vision Source. Vision Source is a franchise: members own their practices and pay a revenue based monthly royalty. Its 2026 disclosure document says it charges no initial franchise fee, and third party summaries list the royalty at 2.5 percent of gross receipts.

Questions to ask any GPO

The AAP's list is a good base.

  1. Which makers and vendors are in the program, and can we buy outside it during a shortage?
  2. What are the compliance rules, and can we be removed for missing them?
  3. Is there a cost to join, and how long is the commitment?
  4. Are there rebates, how are they paid, and how often?
  5. How do the prices compare with what we pay now, at our order size?
  6. Do all members get the same terms?
  7. How is the GPO paid by vendors?

How savings show up in the books

Savings are real only if you can see them in your numbers.

  1. Lower unit prices. These show up as a lower supply expense line, with no extra entry. Compare the same months year over year, since patient volume moves the total.
  2. Rebates. Under US GAAP, consideration from a vendor generally reduces the cost of what you bought (ASC 705-20). CMS's Provider Reimbursement Manual likewise treats rebates as a reduction of the cost they relate to, not as income. In practice, many small practices record rebate checks against the supply or vaccine expense account, not as other income. Rebates often arrive quarterly or yearly, so expect bumps. Ask your CPA how to handle timing on cash or accrual books.
  3. Membership fees. A GPO fee is a business expense, often booked to dues and subscriptions. Count it against the savings.
  4. Return on the fee. Method's example shows a $1,000 yearly fee that saves $650 on gloves and needles is a net loss of $350. Do the same math with your own invoices.
  5. Supply cost as a percent of collections. This is the number to watch. If it falls after you join and stays down, the GPO is working. Compare it with the sources in our benchmarking guide and see what overhead percentage is normal.
  6. Margin by service line. Vaccines, injectables and optical goods are lines where product cost decides profit. A cheaper vial matters more than total spend.

How to choose by practice type

  1. Primary care and pediatrics: start with a vaccine buying group (PAA, MPPG or PBG) and check which makers each one covers. You may join one per maker.
  2. Specialty and procedure practices: a supply GPO such as MediGroup, which works through your current distributor.
  3. Dermatology and med spa: ask about injectables and product lines, then track margin by service line. See the dermatology page and med spa page.
  4. Dental: Sevaredent for groups; Dental Buying Power or Dental Purchasing Partners for single offices.
  5. Eye care: compare IDOC and PECAA on fee and services; Vision Source if you want a branded network.
  6. Several locations: standardize purchasing across sites first, then compare supply cost by location. See the medical group page.
Questions, answered

Frequently asked questions

Are medical GPOs free to join?

Many are. MediGroup, PAA, MPPG and Afaxys GPO state free membership on their own pages, because vendors pay them administrative fees. Dental and eye care groups more often charge a monthly or yearly fee.

What is the difference between a GPO and a vaccine buying group?

The AAP describes vaccine buying groups as contracts with one or two vaccine makers that may pay rebates. Broader GPOs cover many vendors and categories. Many practices use one of each.

Can I join more than one GPO?

Often yes. PAA says participation in each agreement is voluntary, and MediGroup says you choose the contracts that fit. Vaccine groups usually work with competing makers, so some practices join one per maker. Read each agreement for exclusivity terms.

How much can a GPO save my practice?

It depends on what you buy. Vendor claims, such as IDOC's average savings figure, are not a promise. Price your top 20 items through the group and compare with your last three months of invoices.

How do I record GPO rebates in my books?

Rebates generally reduce the cost of the goods they relate to, so many practices record them against supply or vaccine expense, not as income. Timing differs on cash and accrual books. Ask your CPA to set one consistent method.

Is a GPO worth it for a small practice?

Usually, when it is free and you can keep your distributor. Paid groups are worth it only if the savings beat the fee on your own invoices. Check after three to six months.
