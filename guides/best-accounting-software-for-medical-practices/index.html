Short answer

Most small medical practices run on QuickBooks Online, with Xero as the main alternative. Practices with several locations or several legal entities often outgrow both and look at Sage Intacct or NetSuite. Software alone does not keep books right, because someone has to set up the chart of accounts, reconcile every month and review the result. Lucid is the CPA-led service option: it does that work for medical and private practices, with a dedicated CPA on every account and pricing published online.

What to look for in medical practice accounting

  1. Locations and entities. Many groups run a management company plus one or more practice entities, with money moving between them.
  2. Class and location tracking. Profit by location, provider group or service line without hundreds of extra accounts.
  3. Payroll. W-2 staff and 1099 contractors kept separate and posted to the right location.
  4. Inventory. Injectables, optical goods and supplies need stock tracking for accurate cost of goods.
  5. Deferred revenue. Memberships, prepaid packages and treatment plans create income not yet earned.
  6. Payer-level revenue. Insurance practices need revenue by payer, not just a daily deposit total.
  7. Integrations. Practice management systems and card processors should feed the books without retyping.

QuickBooks Online

Price: $38 Simple Start, $85 Essentials, $140 Plus, $340 Advanced a month (Intuit pricing page). Intuit raised Essentials, Plus and Advanced prices at renewals from August 1, 2026.

Strengths:

  • The most common tool, so most bookkeepers and CPAs know it.
  • Intuit's usage limits page lists 40 combined classes and locations on Plus and unlimited on Advanced. Simple Start and Essentials do not include them.
  • Plus includes inventory tracking, and Advanced includes everything in Plus.

Limits:

  • Plus caps the chart of accounts at 250 accounts and 5 billable users. Advanced allows unlimited accounts and 25 users.
  • One QuickBooks Online company does not consolidate separate companies. Intuit Enterprise Suite is the Intuit product that runs consolidated reports and intercompany eliminations.
  • It does not know what a payer is. Payer-level revenue needs classes, a careful chart of accounts or a reporting add-on.

Best for: solo practices up to mid-size practices at one or a few locations.

QuickBooks Desktop

Price: Intuit's Enterprise page shows starting prices for its Gold, Platinum and Diamond tiers without stating the per-user basis, says pricing depends on the number of users, and directs buyers to sales.

Intuit no longer sells new Pro Plus, Premier Plus, Mac Plus or Enhanced Payroll subscriptions to new US customers as of October 1, 2024, and existing subscribers can renew (Intuit help center). Desktop 2023 lost services after May 31, 2026.

Strengths: Enterprise has deeper inventory and reporting for long-time users.

Limits: a local install, so remote access takes extra setup. Each company file stands alone.

Best for: an established practice already on Desktop with a reason to stay. New practices should start on Online.

Xero

Price: $27 Early, $59 Growing, $97 Established a month, no per-user fees. Early allows 20 invoices a month. Payroll through Gusto is $36 a month plus $6 per employee or contractor (Xero US pricing page, which also lists 1099 management as included).

Strengths: flat pricing, clean interface, unlimited users.

Limits: a Xero organization can have only two active tracking categories (Kenomont guide). Each organization is its own ledger, and according to a Xero consolidation vendor, Xero's standard reports do not combine organizations.

Best for: a small practice whose bookkeeper already prefers Xero.

Sage Intacct

Price: not published. Sage asks buyers to request a quote.

Strengths: Sage markets Intacct for healthcare and lists EMRConnect, a tool to connect an electronic medical record (EMR) or practice management system to Sage Intacct. Sage's site also describes managing several legal entities and consolidation.

Limits: pricing is not published, so cost is unknown until you request a quote.

Best for: groups and management companies (MSOs) with several legal entities and a finance lead.

NetSuite

Price: not published. Oracle sells by quote.

Strengths: NetSuite OneWorld manages multiple subsidiaries and legal entities in one account (Oracle documentation).

Limits: pricing is not published, so cost is unknown until you request a quote.

Best for: larger groups with several subsidiaries.

FreshBooks

Price: $23 Lite, $43 Plus, $70 Premium a month (FreshBooks 2026 price change FAQ).

Limits: FreshBooks' own pricing material does not describe class or location tracking, inventory or payer reporting. Check each against your needs.

Wave

Price: Starter $0, Pro $19 a month. Payroll is an add-on, $40 a month plus $6 per employee or contractor in the US (Wave payroll page). Starter requires manual bank entry. Pro can import transactions through Plaid (Wave pricing page).

Limits: Wave's pricing page lists no class tracking, multiple locations or inventory.

Zoho Books

Price: free plan for businesses under $50K annual revenue. Standard is $20 a month, or $15 billed annually (Zoho pricing page).

Limits: we did not verify location tracking or US practice management integrations.

Bench: is it still in business?

Yes. Bench's history, sourced:

  1. Per Bench's closure notice, Bench stopped service on December 27, 2024. The notice said "we have shut down Bench services" and the platform was no longer accessible (bench.co/closure). TechCrunch reported it the same day.
  2. Employer.com took over. Bench's transition FAQ says Bench Accounting, Inc. no longer exists as a separate entity and that Employer.com was acquiring certain assets from it. Bench's FAQ says service resumed in January 2025 and Bench is "fully operational as an Employer.com company and is actively onboarding new and returning customers." TechCrunch (January 3, 2025) and Banking Dive covered the revival.
  3. On August 11, 2025, Employer.com announced it had "officially rebranded Bench, integrating several recent acquisitions into one cohesive brand: Mainstreet" (Employer.com press release), so owners may see "Bench by Mainstreet". bench.co still operates under the Bench name as of November 26, 2026.
  4. Bench is a bookkeeping service with a dedicated bookkeeper, not software you run yourself. It also offers a QuickBooks Online option.

Any report that Bench is closed reflects the December 2024 shutdown and is out of date as of November 26, 2026. Bench's pages we reviewed contain no medical practice information.

Dental and medical add-ons that are real

These are connectors from practice systems to the books, not full accounting systems.

  • Open Dental has a QuickBooks Online integration. Its manual describes it as one-way, sending deposit information from Open Dental to QuickBooks Online.
  • Cerbo EHR has a QuickBooks Online integration through FxMedSupport.
  • Sage Intacct EMRConnect, above.

Software is a tool. The real gap is setup and review.

The same software can produce clean books or a mess. What decides it:

  • Who sets up the chart of accounts. One built for a retail shop does not show collections by location, provider group or payer.
  • Who reconciles. Payer, card processor and patient deposits rarely match a bank line one to one.
  • Who reviews. A review catches miscoded payroll, contractor versus employee mistakes, and drift between entities.
  • Who answers tax questions. Owners with several entities need bookkeeping and tax to agree.

Software makes the work faster. It does not decide how revenue and costs are classified. That is why the choice is often software plus a person.

How to choose by practice type

  1. Solo practice. QuickBooks Online Simple Start or Essentials, or Xero Early, with a bookkeeper who reviews monthly. Wave only if you are cash-pay and simple.
  2. Practice with 5 to 15 staff. QuickBooks Online Plus, for classes and locations, payroll and a clean chart of accounts. A monthly review pays off most here.
  3. Multiple locations. QuickBooks Online Plus or Advanced with locations and classes. Plus caps classes and locations at 40 combined, so count them.
  4. MSO or several practice entities. Evaluate Sage Intacct or Intuit Enterprise Suite, run by a finance lead or a CPA-led service. Consider NetSuite only at significant scale.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best accounting software for a medical practice?

For most practices, QuickBooks Online Plus, for its class and location tracking, inventory and broad integrations. Larger groups often move to Sage Intacct. Who sets it up and reviews it matters more than the brand.

Is QuickBooks good for a medical practice?

Yes, if it is set up for how the practice earns money. Plus and Advanced add classes and locations. A default chart of accounts will not show payer or location results.

When should a practice move beyond QuickBooks?

Common signs are several legal entities, money moving between them, a month-end close that takes weeks, or a need for consolidated reports. Intuit Enterprise Suite or Sage Intacct are usual next steps.

Is Bench Accounting still in business?

Yes. Per Bench's closure notice it stopped service on December 27, 2024. Service resumed in January 2025 under Employer.com, per Bench's own pages and TechCrunch. Bench is a bookkeeping service, not software.

Is there accounting software made for private practices?

Not a full one that we verified. There are connectors from systems like Open Dental to QuickBooks, and Sage markets a healthcare version of Intacct. Most practices run general software with a practice-specific setup.

Should I use software or a CPA-led service?

If you have staff, several locations or several entities, a service that sets up, reconciles and reviews saves owner time. With a trusted in-house bookkeeper and a simple practice, software with a monthly review may be enough.

What does a CPA-led service cost compared with software?

Software runs from free to $340 a month for the plans listed above. Lucid starts from $130 a month, priced by service. The two do different jobs: software is the tool, and the service does the work.
