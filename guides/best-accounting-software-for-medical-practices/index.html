What to look for in medical practice accounting

  1. Locations and entities. Many groups run a management company plus one or more practice entities, with money moving between them.
  2. Class and location tracking. Profit by location, provider group or service line without hundreds of extra accounts.
  3. Payroll. W-2 staff and 1099 contractors kept separate and posted to the right location.
  4. Deferred revenue. Memberships, prepaid packages and treatment plans create income not yet earned.
  5. Payer-level revenue. Insurance practices need revenue by payer, not just a daily deposit total.
  6. Integrations. Practice management systems and card processors should feed the books without retyping.

QuickBooks Online

Price: $38 Simple Start, $85 Essentials, $140 Plus, $340 Advanced a month. Intuit raised Essentials, Plus and Advanced prices at renewals from August 1, 2026.

Strengths:

  • The most common tool, so most bookkeepers and CPAs know it.
  • Intuit's usage limits page lists 40 combined classes and locations on Plus and unlimited on Advanced. Simple Start and Essentials do not include them.
  • Advanced includes everything in Plus.

Limits:

  • Plus caps the chart of accounts at 250 accounts and 5 billable users. Advanced allows unlimited accounts and 25 users.
  • One QuickBooks Online company does not consolidate separate companies. Intuit Enterprise Suite is the Intuit product that runs consolidated reports and intercompany eliminations.
  • It does not know what a payer is. Payer-level revenue needs classes, a careful chart of accounts or a reporting add-on.

Best for: solo practices up to mid-size practices at one or a few locations.

QuickBooks Desktop

Price: Intuit's Enterprise page shows starting prices for its Gold, Platinum and Diamond tiers without stating the per-user basis, says pricing depends on the number of users, and directs buyers to sales.

Intuit no longer sells new Pro Plus, Premier Plus, Mac Plus or Enhanced Payroll subscriptions to new US customers as of October 1, 2024, and existing subscribers can renew. Desktop 2023 lost services after May 31, 2026.

Strengths: Enterprise has deeper reporting for long-time users.

Limits: a local install, so remote access takes extra setup. Each company file stands alone.

Best for: an established practice already on Desktop with a reason to stay. New practices should start on Online.

Xero

Price: $27 Early, $59 Growing, $97 Established a month, no per-user fees. Early allows 20 invoices a month. Payroll through Gusto is $36 a month plus $6 per employee or contractor.

Strengths: flat pricing, clean interface, unlimited users.

Limits: a Xero organization can have only two active tracking categories. Each organization is its own ledger, with its own reports.

Best for: a small practice whose bookkeeper already prefers Xero.

  • Cerbo EHR has a QuickBooks Online integration through FxMedSupport.
  • Sage Intacct EMRConnect, above.

Software is a tool. The real gap is setup and review.

The same software can produce clean books or a mess. What decides it:

  • Who sets up the chart of accounts. One built for a retail shop does not show collections by location, provider group or payer.
  • Who reconciles. Payer, card processor and patient deposits rarely match a bank line one to one.
  • Who reviews. A review catches miscoded payroll, contractor versus employee mistakes, and drift between entities.
  • Who answers tax questions. Owners with several entities need bookkeeping and tax to agree.

Software makes the work faster. It does not decide how revenue and costs are classified. That is why the choice is often software plus a person.

How to choose by practice type

  1. Solo practice. QuickBooks Online Simple Start or Essentials, or Xero Early, with a bookkeeper who reviews monthly. Wave only if you are cash-pay and simple.
  2. Practice with 5 to 15 staff. QuickBooks Online Plus, for classes and locations, payroll and a clean chart of accounts. A monthly review pays off most here.
  3. Multiple locations. QuickBooks Online Plus or Advanced with locations and classes. Plus caps classes and locations at 40 combined, so count them.
  4. MSO or several practice entities. Evaluate Sage Intacct or Intuit Enterprise Suite, run by a finance lead or a CPA-led service. Consider NetSuite only at significant scale.
  5. Staff, several locations or several owners, and you would rather not run the books yourself: Lucid, with a dedicated CPA doing the setup, reconciliation and review.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best accounting software for a medical practice?

For most practices that run their own books, QuickBooks Online Plus, for its class and location tracking and broad integrations. Larger groups often move to Sage Intacct. If you would rather not run the books yourself, Lucid does the setup, reconciliation and review with a dedicated CPA.

Is QuickBooks good for a medical practice?

Yes, if it is set up for how the practice earns money. Plus and Advanced add classes and locations. A default chart of accounts will not show payer or location results.

When should a practice move beyond QuickBooks?

Common signs are several legal entities, money moving between them, a month-end close that takes weeks, or a need for consolidated reports. Intuit Enterprise Suite or Sage Intacct are usual next steps, and a CPA-led service such as Lucid is another.

Is there accounting software made for private practices?

Not a full one that we verified. There are connectors from systems like Open Dental to QuickBooks, and Sage markets a healthcare version of Intacct. Most practices run general software with a practice-specific setup.

Should I use software or a CPA-led service?

If you have staff, several locations or several entities, choose a CPA-led service such as Lucid, which sets up, reconciles and reviews for you. With a trusted in-house bookkeeper and a simple practice, software with a monthly review may be enough.
