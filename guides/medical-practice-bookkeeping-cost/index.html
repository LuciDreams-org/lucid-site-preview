What drives the cost

Medical books carry more moving parts than a shop of the same revenue. Seven things move the price.

  1. Practice size. More staff, providers and transactions means more to categorize and reconcile.
  2. Number of locations. Each usually needs its own results.
  3. Number of entities. A practice company plus a management company, or several practices under one owner, means more sets of books and intercompany entries to reconcile.
  4. Payers. Insurance deposits, patient balances, write offs and cash pay sales each need their own handling. A mix of many payers takes more time than one cash pay stream.
  5. Payroll. It adds entries, tax deposits and quarterly filings to check, even when another vendor runs it.
  6. Catch up work. If past months were never reconciled, cleanup is its own project, usually priced apart from the monthly fee. At Lucid, catch up work is scoped first and given a fixed price.
  7. Tax scope. A single return costs less than tax planning, quarterly estimates, several state filings and several entities.

  3. Payroll. Some vendors run payroll as an add on. Lucid does not process payroll, so keep your payroll provider.

Lucid offers CFO services with budget against actuals and scenario planning from the same team.

Hidden costs of cheap bookkeeping

  1. Rework. Someone pays to fix miscategorized transactions.
  2. Late filings. For a late individual or C corporation return, the IRS charges 5 percent of the unpaid tax for each month or partial month, up to 25 percent. Late partnership and S corporation returns carry a separate per owner, per month penalty (the IRS failure to file page lists the current amount). Books that are not ready can delay a return.
  3. No view by location or provider. If the books mix all sites together, you cannot see which one loses money.

How to compare quotes

Ask every provider these questions, in writing.

  1. What does the monthly fee include and exclude?
  2. Is tax filing included, and who prepares and signs the return?
  3. How is catch up work priced, and is it fixed or hourly?
  4. Is the price per entity, per location or per service?
  5. Does the price change as expenses grow, and where does it change?
  6. Is there a setup fee, a minimum term or a cancellation fee?
  7. Can I see a sample report by location, provider and payer?
  8. How do you record insurance deposits, write offs, packages and memberships?
  9. Is payroll included, or do I keep my payroll provider?
  10. Can I see your pricing without a sales call?

Lucid answers number 10 with a calculator, number 6 with no setup fee and month to month terms, and number 3 with catch up work scoped first at a fixed price.
