Short answer

Most private practices need two things: a phone system for the day and coverage for calls the front desk cannot take. For the phone system, Weave, Spruce Health, RingCentral, Nextiva, Dialpad and 8x8 are the usual shortlist, and each says it will sign a business associate agreement (BAA) on the right terms. For overflow and after hours calls, Ruby, AnswerConnect, PatientCalls, Specialty Answering Service and MAP Communications are the common answering services. Ask every vendor for its BAA in writing before patient details touch the line, and compare the total monthly cost against what a missed call costs you.

Phone system, answering service or AI receptionist?

These solve different problems, and many practices use two of them.

  1. A VoIP phone system replaces your desk phones and lines. It gives you call routing, hold queues, voicemail, texting and call reports. Your own staff still answer.
  2. An answering service puts live people on calls your staff miss: overflow at peak hours, lunch, and nights and weekends. They take messages, book where allowed and page the on call provider.
  3. An AI receptionist answers with software. RingCentral sells an AI Receptionist add-on, and Nextiva sells an AI Front Office package from $500 a month. These are newer and best tested on simple calls first, such as hours, directions and booking requests.

Start with your call data. Missed calls in clinic hours point to staffing and routing. Missed calls after hours point to an answering service.

What to check first

  1. The BAA. A vendor that stores voicemails, recordings or texts with patient details is handling protected health information, so you need a signed BAA. Ask which plan and which features it covers.
  2. Features that sit outside the BAA. Some vendors exclude certain add-ons or turn features off in HIPAA mode. AnswerConnect, for example, says call recording cannot be used with its HIPAA setting on.
  3. Practice system links. Weave and some others connect to practice management systems for caller details. Confirm support for your system by name.
  4. Total cost. Per user fees, minutes, overage rates, setup fees, numbers and texting fees add up. Get a written quote for your call volume.

Weave

Weave combines phones, texting, reviews and payments, built for healthcare offices such as dental, optometry and medical practices. Its pricing page says plans start from $199 a month, with enterprise pricing by quote. Weave publishes a Business Associate Addendum that supplements its terms of service.

Best for: dental, optometry and small medical offices that want phones, texting and payments in one system. See our patient payment software guide for its payments side. Limits: if you only need lines, a plain VoIP plan can cost less.

Spruce Health

Spruce is built for medical communication: phone, text, secure messaging, video and fax. Its pricing page lists a Basic plan at $24 per user a month and a Communicator plan at $49 per user a month, which adds custom call flows. Spruce says all trials and paid plans include a BAA, which you accept when you create your organization. It also lists a one time $19.50 fee to enable outbound business texting.

Best for: primary care, behavioral health and small groups that want patient texting and fax with the BAA built in. Limits: priced per user, so cost grows with headcount. Check the billing terms on its page.

RingCentral

RingCentral is a large business VoIP provider. Its plans page lists a Core plan at $20 per user a month paid yearly, or $30 paid monthly, and an Advanced plan at $25 or $35, as shown for small teams. RingCentral says it offers to sign its BAA with customers subject to HIPAA who will use its services with patient information. You request it through your RingCentral representative, and it does not accept a customer's own BAA form.

Best for: groups with several locations that want one phone system across sites. Limits: a general business system. Confirm which services the BAA covers before you add features.

Nextiva

Nextiva sells business phone plans and AI packages. Its company information page lists Go at $10, Business at $25 and CX at $75 per user a month on yearly billing, with higher monthly rates. It says regulated customers sign a BAA separately where required. Its pricing page also lists AI Front Office from $500 a month.

Best for: practices that want a low per user price or want to test an AI front office. Limits: plan names and bundles have changed recently. Get the plan and BAA terms in one written quote.

Dialpad

Dialpad Connect is its business phone product. Its pricing page lists Standard at $15 and Pro at $25 per user a month, with Enterprise by quote. Dialpad's help center says company admins on paid accounts can sign a HIPAA BAA in the admin portal.

Best for: small and mid size practices that want self serve setup and a BAA they can sign without a sales call. Limits: it is a general business tool. Healthcare specific links to practice systems are limited compared with Weave.

8x8

8x8 sells business phones and contact center software. Its US site asks buyers to request a quote. 8x8 publishes HIPAA usage guides for customers operating under a BAA with 8x8, which tell the customer how to set up products to stay within the agreement.

Best for: larger groups with a central call center. Limits: no public price, so compare only on a written quote.

Ruby

Ruby provides live virtual receptionists. Its pricing page lists 50 minutes for $250 a month, 100 minutes for $395, 200 minutes for $720 and 500 minutes for $1,725. Ruby says HIPAA service is opt in, it does not charge extra for it, and its team provides a BAA when you sign up.

Best for: small practices that want a polished live voice for overflow and new patient calls. Limits: per minute cost is higher than some medical answering services.

AnswerConnect

AnswerConnect offers 24/7 live answering. Its plans page lists Entry at $350 a month for 200 minutes plus a $49.99 setup fee and $2.50 per extra minute, and Growth at $395 for 300 minutes. Its healthcare page says you can turn on HIPAA compliance in the online portal, but call recording is not available with that setting on.

Best for: practices that want round the clock live answering with simple setup. Limits: ask for its BAA in writing, since the HIPAA setting alone is not the agreement.

PatientCalls

PatientCalls is a medical answering service and call center. Its pricing page explains the factors behind price rather than listing plans, and lists a one time $75 setup fee. It says a service agreement and a business associate agreement must be signed to start medical answering services.

Best for: practices that want a healthcare focused service for after hours and on call paging. Limits: price is by quote.

Specialty Answering Service

Specialty Answering Service publishes simple pricing. Its pricing page lists Economy at $44 a month plus $1.54 a minute, 100 minutes for $159 a month and 220 minutes for $269 a month, with a 14 day free trial. Its help center says it will provide its business associate agreement to accounts that need HIPAA compliance.

Best for: low volume after hours lines where a per minute plan costs less than a bundle. Limits: per minute plans can climb fast in a busy month.

MAP Communications

MAP Communications is an answering service with month to month plans. Its pricing page lists Pay As You Go at $49 a month plus $1.37 a minute, Business at $179 for 125 minutes and Enterprise at $339 for 250 minutes, and says every plan is HIPAA compliant.

Best for: practices that want a low entry price and predictable bundles. Limits: its pricing page does not mention a BAA, so ask for one before you start.

How to choose

  1. Solo or small office with a busy front desk: a medical focused phone system such as Spruce or Weave, plus a per minute answering plan for after hours.
  2. Dental or optometry office that wants texting, reviews and payments too: Weave.
  3. Several locations: RingCentral, Nextiva or 8x8, with central routing and reports by site.
  4. After hours and on call paging: PatientCalls, Specialty Answering Service or MAP Communications.
  5. New patient calls that must never go to voicemail: Ruby or AnswerConnect for overflow during clinic hours.
  6. Want to test AI: start with simple calls on an AI receptionist add-on, and keep a live fallback.

Ask every vendor: will you sign a BAA, and what does it cover? What is my total monthly cost, with setup, overage and texting fees? Can I leave month to month?

What missed calls cost

A missed new patient call is usually a lost visit. To size the problem, pull one month of call reports and count missed and abandoned calls by hour. Multiply missed new patient calls by your booking rate and your average first visit revenue. Then compare that to the monthly cost of better coverage. In many offices a few saved visits a month pay for an answering service.

Then compare it to adding front desk hours, with overage counted.

Questions, answered

Frequently asked questions

What is the best phone system for a small medical practice?

There is no single best choice. Spruce and Weave are built for healthcare and publish prices. RingCentral, Nextiva and Dialpad cost less per user but are general business systems. Choose by your call volume, texting needs and the BAA terms.

Which medical answering services sign a BAA?

Ruby, PatientCalls and Specialty Answering Service say on their own pages that they provide a BAA. AnswerConnect and MAP Communications describe HIPAA settings or HIPAA compliant plans, so ask them for the agreement in writing. Always get the signed BAA before calls with patient details start.

How much does a medical answering service cost?

Published plans on this list start at $44 a month plus per minute fees at Specialty Answering Service and $49 at MAP Communications. Bundles of 200 minutes run from about $350 at AnswerConnect to $720 at Ruby, as checked November 26, 2026. Setup and overage fees add to that.

Is a VoIP phone system HIPAA compliant?

A phone system is not compliant on its own. The vendor must sign a BAA, and your practice must set it up and use it within the agreement. Ask which features are covered, such as voicemail, recordings and texting.

Should we use an AI receptionist?

It can handle simple calls such as hours, directions and booking requests. Test it on a small share of calls first and keep a live fallback for clinical or urgent calls. Confirm the AI feature is covered by the vendor's BAA.

Do we need both a phone system and an answering service?

Many practices do. The phone system runs your day. The answering service covers overflow, lunch, nights and weekends, and on call paging.
