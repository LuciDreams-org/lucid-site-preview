Short answer

By dollars lent to healthcare practices in SBA fiscal 2026, the leading SBA 7(a) lenders were Live Oak Bank, Fifth Third (home of Provide), Huntington and Newtek Bank, followed by United Midwest Savings Bank, U.S. Bank, Wells Fargo, Northeast Bank and Primis Bank (home of Panacea Financial). Live Oak and Fifth Third lean toward large loans, while Newtek and Northeast Bank made many smaller ones, and an SBA 7(a) loan suits buying a practice, a partner buyout or working capital, and a 504 loan suits buying a building. SBA program basics

All figures from SBA.gov, checked November 26, 2026.

ProgramMax loanTypical use
7(a) standard$5 millionBuying a practice, buyouts, working capital, equipment, real estate
7(a) small$350,000Smaller term loans
SBA Express$500,000Faster loans and lines, 50% guarantee
504$5.5 millionBuildings and long-life equipment

Key 7(a) rules, per SBA:

  1. Terms are 10 years or less unless the loan finances real estate or equipment with a longer useful life. Real estate can go to 25 years.
  2. Rates are negotiated but capped. For variable loans the cap is the base rate plus 3.0% above $350,000, up to plus 6.5% for loans of $50,000 or less.
  3. SBA guarantees 85% of loans up to $150,000 and 75% above that (50% for SBA Express).
  4. For loans of 15 years or longer, prepaying 25% or more in the first three years costs 5%, then 3%, then 1% of the prepaid amount.
  5. An upfront guaranty fee applies, and lenders may pass it to the borrower. Live Oak describes it as 0% to 3.75% of the guaranteed portion, often financed into the loan.

504 loans are made through Certified Development Companies (CDCs) with a bank. They carry fixed rates, 10, 20 or 25 year terms, and cannot be used for working capital. Live Oak describes the usual split as about 50% bank, 40% CDC and a 10% down payment (15% for startups or special use buildings).

The lenders

Live Oak Bank

The biggest SBA lender to healthcare practices by dollars in fiscal 2026. Live Oak says it has financed over $1.5 billion in practice loans and has healthcare lenders who only work with medical professionals. Its SBA page lists 7(a) loans up to $5 million with up to 90% bank financing, fully amortized with no balloons, and Live Oak Express SBA loans up to $350,000. On November 26, 2026 it showed a 7(a) rate range of about 6.75% to 9.75%. It is an SBA Preferred Lender, so it can approve without sending the file to SBA first.

Best for: acquisitions, partner buyouts, dental and larger loans.

Fifth Third Bank (Provide)

Second by dollars, with the largest average loan in the top group, about $1.8 million. Fifth Third finances practices through Provide, its healthcare lending brand. Provide's pages blocked direct reading from our server, so terms: see the lender's site.

Best for: larger acquisition loans with a digital application.

Huntington

Third by dollars and third by count. Huntington has a Healthcare Practice Finance group with pages for medical, optometry, dental and veterinary practices. Its pages blocked direct reading, so terms: see the lender's site.

Best for: mid-size loans in Huntington's markets with a local banker.

Newtek Bank

The most healthcare SBA loans by count in fiscal 2026, with an average near $250,000. Newtek Bank is part of NewtekOne. Its lending page lists term loans from $5,000 to $15 million with 5 to 25 year terms for acquisitions, refinancing, working capital and owner-occupied real estate.

Best for: smaller practices, working capital and loans under $500,000.

United Midwest Savings Bank

United Midwest has a medical practice SBA page. It lists down payments as low as 10%, financing up to 100% in some cases, 10 to 25 year terms depending on use, and approval in days with closing in under 30 days. It is an SBA Preferred Lender.

Best for: owners who want a fast SBA close from a specialist lender.

U.S. Bank

U.S. Bank is both an SBA lender and a conventional practice lender. Its medical loans page lists up to 100% financing, terms up to 15 years (25 for real estate) and up to six months of interest only, and says some practice loans can qualify for SBA backing.

Best for: comparing an SBA and a conventional quote from the same bank.

Wells Fargo

Wells Fargo lends to healthcare owners through Wells Fargo Practice Finance, which serves dental, optometry, veterinary and medical practices. Its average healthcare SBA loan was close to $1 million. Program terms: see the lender's site.

Best for: dental and optometry owners who bank with Wells Fargo.

Northeast Bank

Fourth by count, with small loans averaging about $175,000. Northeast Bank lists SBA loans up to $5 million, up to 90% loan to value and terms up to 25 years, plus SBA 7(a) working capital loans up to $350,000 offered with Newity as its lender service provider.

Best for: small working capital loans with an online application.

Primis Bank (Panacea Financial)

Few loans but large ones, average about $1.7 million. Panacea Financial is a division of Primis Bank that lends to practices to start, buy or grow, and it is the practice finance provider of ADA Member Advantage. Its SBA terms are not published. See the lender's site.

Best for: dental owners and larger acquisitions.

Bank of America

Bank of America calls itself one of the top SBA Preferred Lenders. Its SBA page lists 7(a) loans of $350,000 to $5 million, with terms up to 10 years for working capital, acquisition or partner buyout and leasehold improvements, 15 for equipment and 25 for real estate. It lists 504 loans of $700,000 to $40 million with up to 90% of project cost. It also runs Practice Solutions for practice loans.

Best for: owners who want SBA and conventional practice options under one bank.

First-Citizens Bank also made the top 10 by dollars. We did not review its SBA pages for this guide. See the lender's site.

SBA 504 for a medical office building

In fiscal 2026, SBA data shows 506 healthcare 504 loans totaling about $417 million, with a median of $543,000. The busiest CDCs were regional, led by Florida Business Development Corporation and The Mortgage Capital Development Corporation. The most active bank partners on those loans were First-Citizens, JPMorgan Chase and Harvest Commercial Capital. Use SBA's CDC finder to find one in your state.

How to choose

  1. Buying a practice or a partner's share. Get quotes from Live Oak, Fifth Third (Provide) and one regional SBA lender. Compare the 10 year payment, the guaranty fee and the prepayment penalty.
  2. Smaller loans under $350,000. Newtek, Northeast Bank and Live Oak Express all target this size.
  3. Buying your building. Compare a 504 loan through a CDC with a 7(a) real estate loan. A 504 needs about 10% down but cannot cover working capital.
  4. Several locations. Ask whether one lender can finance each location and how it treats intercompany debt. See our medical group page.
  5. Dental, behavioral health and therapy. Dental owners can add Panacea and Wells Fargo. Behavioral health and therapy practices made hundreds of SBA loans in fiscal 2026, so any top lender has seen your file type. See our dental, behavioral health and physical therapy pages.

Questions, answered

Frequently asked questions

Which SBA lender is best for a medical practice?

By fiscal 2026 SBA data, Live Oak Bank lent the most dollars to healthcare practices and Newtek Bank made the most loans. The best lender for you depends on loan size: large acquisitions sit with Live Oak, Fifth Third and Primis, while small loans sit with Newtek and Northeast Bank.

What is the maximum SBA 7(a) loan?

$5 million, per SBA. SBA Express loans max out at $500,000 and 504 loans at $5.5 million.

What interest rate will I pay on an SBA loan?

Rates are negotiated with the lender but capped by SBA. The median starting rate on fiscal 2026 healthcare 7(a) loans was 9.5%, per SBA data. Your rate depends on loan size, term, credit and the prime rate on the day you lock.

How long is an SBA loan for a practice?

Usually up to 10 years for an acquisition or working capital, and up to 25 years for real estate. The median healthcare 7(a) term in fiscal 2026 was 120 months.

SBA 7(a) or 504 for a building?

A 504 gives a fixed rate on the SBA portion and about 10% down, but only for fixed assets. A 7(a) can mix real estate with working capital. Many owners buying a building and expanding at the same time compare both.

Is there a penalty for paying off an SBA loan early?

On 7(a) loans of 15 years or longer, yes, in the first three years if you prepay 25% or more: 5%, then 3%, then 1%. Shorter loans have no SBA prepayment fee.

Do I need a personal guarantee?

SBA lenders generally require personal guarantees from major owners. Ask each lender for its exact rule before you apply.
