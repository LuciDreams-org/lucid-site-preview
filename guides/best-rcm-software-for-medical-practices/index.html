Short answer

The right RCM tool depends on who works your claims. Tebra, AdvancedMD and athenahealth bundle billing with practice management, CollaborateMD and EZClaim suit an in-house biller, and Waystar, Availity and Office Ally add clearinghouse and claim tools on top of your current system. Athelas and AdvancedMD RCM Services have their staff work the claims for you. None of these is an accounting service, and Lucid is the accounting layer that sits beside whichever one you pick, though it does not run billing or chase claims.

Tebra (formerly Kareo)

Tebra sells an EHR, billing, payments and patient engagement platform. Its pricing page says monthly pricing runs from $49 to $799 per provider depending on the solution or bundle, your provider type and claim volume. A Billing Starter option starts at $99 to $199 per provider per month for practices under 100 claims a month. It says it uses subscription pricing, not a percentage of collections. Its pricing policy says practices that exceed 100 claims per provider per month for three months move to standard pricing.

Best for: independent practices that want one system for charting and billing.

Limits: the final price depends on bundle, setup and volume.

AdvancedMD

AdvancedMD offers practice management, EHR and billing software, plus full service RCM. Its RCM page lists a typical range of 4 to 8 percent of collections, with exceptions possible. Its software page lists per provider ranges by specialty, for example mental health $130 to $399 and physical medicine $399. The same site shows med spa at $278 in one place and $279 in another.

Best for: practices that may start with software and move to a managed service later.

Limits: ranges are wide, so ask what the percentage covers.

athenahealth

athenahealth states that its pricing is based on a percentage of collections. PracticeAll describes its billing as network updated claim edits, payment posting and claim follow up by athenahealth staff. PracticeAll also lists cost, phone support wait times and a learning curve on claims work among common complaints.

Best for: practices that want the EHR and billing in one cloud system.

Limits: the rate is by quote, and the fit is strongest if you also use its EHR. Its pricing page returned an access error on our checks, so the model comes from a vendor search snippet.

CollaborateMD

CollaborateMD, part of EverHealth, offers cloud practice management and billing with a built in clearinghouse and ERA auto posting. Its pricing page names Core, Optimize and Pro plans, each marked "Get Pricing". Optimize adds claim scrubbing and claim follow up. Pro is described for multiple provider practices.

Best for: practices and billing companies that run billing in house and keep their own EHR.

Limits: it is billing first, and no plan shows a price.

Waystar

Waystar is revenue cycle software, not a billing service. Its packages page lists claim editing and scrubbing, claim status tracking, denial workflows and an appeals tool, and says packages are priced on request. Its client types include practices, specialty care, billing services and health systems.

Best for: practices with a billing team that want stronger claim edits and denial tools on top of their current system.

Limits: pricing is not published.

Availity

Availity runs a clearinghouse and payer portal. Providers can submit to sponsoring payers on Essentials for free. An Essentials Plus monthly subscription lets providers check eligibility and submit claims for many non sponsoring payers. Essentials Pro is its revenue cycle product and is sold by request.

Best for: practices that want free payer portal access first, or a clearinghouse layer.

Limits: paid tier prices are not published, and it is a connection layer, not a full billing system.

Office Ally

Office Ally pairs a clearinghouse with low cost practice software. Its Practice Mate has no subscription fee. Claims to par payers carry no fee. Claims to non par payers carry a monthly fee for each tax ID and rendering provider, per Champ AI's summary of Office Ally's pages.

Best for: small, cost conscious practices.

Limits: your payer mix drives the fees.

EZClaim

EZClaim is billing software for in house billers and billing companies. Monthly billing is $199 for the first user and $149 for each additional user. Annual billing is $189 and $144. It is billed month to month, and its pricing page notes that additional clearinghouse fees may apply.

Best for: a practice with a biller who wants a published price.

Limits: it is billing only, so you need separate EHR and scheduling tools.

Athelas

Athelas, part of Commure, offers a managed billing service. PracticeAll lists eligibility, claim scrubbing, submission, AI drafted appeals and patient collections, and says it integrates with EHRs such as athenahealth, eClinicalWorks, Tebra and WebPT.

Best for: practices that want to hand billing off and keep their EHR.

Limits: pricing is not published. Because the vendor works the claims, ask what visibility you get into them.

What outsourced billing costs

Most billing companies charge a percentage of collections, commonly 4 to 10 percent, with 5 to 8 percent typical for small and mid size practices. Med Precision Billing reports a Tebra 2026 survey of 190 billing company respondents in which 63 percent charge 7.99 percent or less. Ask what the percentage covers, since credentialing, patient statements and appeals are sometimes extra.

How to choose

  1. You have a biller and an EHR you like: look at CollaborateMD or EZClaim, or add a clearinghouse layer such as Waystar, Availity or Office Ally.
  2. You want one system for charting and billing: compare Tebra, AdvancedMD and athenahealth.
  3. You have no biller: compare athenahealth, AdvancedMD RCM Services, Athelas and outsourced billing companies.
  4. You have low volume and a tight budget: price Availity Essentials and Office Ally first.
  5. You have several locations: ask each vendor for a sample report by location and by payer.

Ask four questions of any vendor. What is the total cost at my collections? Who works denials, and how fast? Can I export payer level data? How does it hand data to my accountant?

RCM tools and your books

An RCM tool and your general ledger answer different questions.

  1. Cash versus billed. RCM reports show charges billed and payments posted. Your books show cash received in the bank. A deposit can bundle many claims, so the two will not match line by line until someone reconciles them.
  2. Payer level revenue. Your billing system knows which payer paid what. A basic ledger often records one lump of patient revenue. Without payer level detail, a slow payer or a falling reimbursement rate stays hidden.
  3. Receivables. On accrual books, open claims appear as accounts receivable and are reviewed by age. Cash basis books do not record them, so the balance sheet does not show what payers owe you. Many practices want an accrual or management view for this reason.
  4. Deferred revenue. On accrual books, prepaid packages and memberships are deferred revenue, recognized as services are delivered. Recording the full payment up front overstates profit, which matters most for med spas.
  5. Fees and adjustments. Contractual write downs, clearinghouse fees, percentage based billing fees and patient refunds each need a clear home in the ledger.

This is bookkeeping and accounting work, and RCM vendors are not set up for it. Lucid does not submit claims, scrub claims, work denials or chase AR. It keeps your books, files your taxes and provides CFO services, with a dedicated CPA. Your RCM tool or billing service works the claims, and Lucid reconciles payer deposits and reports by location.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best RCM software for a small medical practice?

There is no single best choice. With an in house biller, billing software such as CollaborateMD or EZClaim, or a clearinghouse tool, may fit. Without a biller, a bundled system or managed service is often the better buy.

Is RCM software priced per provider or by percentage of collections?

Both exist. Tebra and EZClaim use subscriptions. athenahealth uses a percentage of collections. AdvancedMD lists both, with software by provider and RCM services at 4 to 8 percent of collections.

Do I need a clearinghouse if I already have billing software?

Most billing systems include one or connect to one. A separate tool such as Waystar, Availity or Office Ally can add payer reach or stronger claim edits. Ask your vendor what is included first.

Does RCM software replace bookkeeping?

No. RCM tracks claims and payments. Your bookkeeping records cash, expenses, payroll and taxes. You need both, and they need to be reconciled.

Why do my billing reports not match my books?

Billing reports show charges and posted payments. Books show deposits and expenses. Timing, bundled deposits, adjustments and fees create the gap. A monthly reconciliation closes it.

Is outsourced billing cheaper than software?

It depends on collections. A percentage grows with revenue, while a subscription stays flat. Compare total cost at your own collections, including extras.
