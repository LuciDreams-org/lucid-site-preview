Short answer

For a small, single-state practice, Gusto, OnPay and QuickBooks Workforce publish simple prices and cover the basics. Groups with several locations, states or entities usually do better with Rippling, Paylocity, Paycor, ADP or Netchex, which add stronger multi-entity and HR tools. Justworks and Paychex suit practices that want a PEO to take on HR and benefits. Lucid does not run payroll, but it books payroll by location and provider, models provider pay and tracks payroll cost against collections, whichever platform you choose.

Every platform here runs W-2 payroll and files payroll taxes. Most also pay 1099 contractors. Productivity formulas, such as a share of collections, are usually worked out in a compensation model first and then entered as a bonus.

Gusto

Gusto lists three payroll plans on its pricing page. Simple is $49 a month plus $6 per person and runs payroll in one state. Plus is $80 plus $12 and covers several states. Premium is $180 plus $22 and adds HR guidance and a named advisor. Its help center says one admin can manage several Gusto companies with one login.

Best for: practices with up to a few dozen staff that want simple setup. Limits: Simple is one state only, so a second state means Plus.

OnPay

OnPay lists $49 a month plus $6 per worker for its payroll plan. The pricing page says every plan includes paying workers in multiple states, W-2s and 1099s, and federal, state and local tax filing. HR is an add-on at $15 plus $2 per worker.

Best for: small practices that want multi-state payroll without moving up a tier. Limits: multi-entity support was not stated on the page we read.

QuickBooks Workforce

QuickBooks Workforce is the current name for QuickBooks Payroll. Workforce Payroll is $50 plus $7 per employee with next-day direct deposit. Premium is $88 plus $13 and adds same-day deposit, time off tools and HR resources from Mineral. Elite is $134 plus $17 and adds expert setup, tax penalty protection and a personal HR advisor. A 70 percent off sale for three months was running when we checked.

Best for: small practices that want payroll and its HR add-ons from one vendor. Limits: time off management starts at Premium.

Paycor

Paycor's small business page lists Basic at $99 a month plus $5 per employee, Essential at $149 plus $7, and Core at $199 plus $8. Setup fees of $59 to $199 are shown by plan. Essential adds onboarding, an HR support center and a time off manager.

Best for: growing groups that want payroll and HR from one vendor with a posted price. Limits: the page shows some figures twice with different per-employee amounts, so confirm your quote.

Rippling

Rippling says pricing starts as low as $8 per month per user. Third party price checks report a base fee of $35 to $40 on top, and say payroll, benefits, time tracking and the PEO are priced separately.

Best for: groups adding locations, entities or states that also want device and IT tools. Limits: the starting price is not the payroll price, so get a full quote.

Justworks

Justworks is one of few PEOs with a posted price. Third party checks of its pricing page in October 2026 report Payroll at $50 a month plus $8 per employee, PEO Basic at $79 per employee and PEO Plus at $124. Under a PEO, Justworks shares employer duties and can offer group health plans.

Best for: practices that want a PEO's benefits and HR support at a known price. Limits: its pricing page blocked our direct check.

Paychex Flex

Paychex sells Flex Essentials online at $39 a month plus $5 per employee, per a third party check on November 22, 2026. Its package page says plans start at $65 a month plus $8 per employee, with the full packages quoted. Paychex also runs a PEO.

Best for: practices that want a large provider with local reps and PEO options. Limits: most packages are quoted.

ADP (RUN and Workforce Now)

ADP's RUN packages for 1 to 49 employees are Essential, Enhanced, Complete Payroll and HR+, and HR Pro. Its page shows multi-jurisdiction payroll in all four and "Get Pricing" instead of prices. Workforce Now serves larger employers, and a payroll analyst describes ADP organizing multi-entity payroll by company code.

Best for: practices that want a large provider at any size. Limits: quote only, and some forms carry extra fees.

Paylocity

Paylocity sells payroll, HR and finance tools by quote. Third party reviews checked in October and November 2026 found no published price.

Best for: mid-size groups that want a full HR suite with recruiting and staff engagement tools. Limits: quote only.

Netchex

Netchex markets directly to healthcare employers. Its 2026 guide says it runs RVU-based bonuses and productivity pay alongside staff payroll in one run, and manages several locations and EINs from one account. It says pricing is not published.

Best for: groups that pay providers on productivity and want one system for staff and providers. Limits: quote only, and these are the vendor's own claims.

How to choose

  1. One location, one state, under 25 staff: Gusto Simple, OnPay or QuickBooks Workforce.
  2. Two locations or staff in two states: OnPay, Gusto Plus, Paycor or Rippling.
  3. Several entities, such as a PC and an MSO: ask Rippling, ADP, Paylocity and Netchex to demo payroll for each EIN with a combined report.
  4. Providers on productivity pay: shortlist Netchex, then compare how others enter bonuses.
  5. You want benefits and HR handled: price Justworks, Paychex and other PEOs.

Ask every vendor five questions. What is my total monthly cost at my headcount? How do you handle several EINs? Can I tag pay by location and provider? How are bonuses withheld? What does the general ledger export look like?

Payroll rules practices trip on

  1. Bonus withholding. The IRS lets employers withhold federal tax on bonuses at a flat 22 percent, or 37 percent above $1 million in the year, per Publication 15 (2026).
  2. Worker classification. Paying a provider or front desk worker as a 1099 contractor when the practice controls the work can trigger back taxes and penalties. The IRS looks at behavioral control, financial control and the relationship.
  3. Owner pay. S corporation owners must take reasonable W-2 salary through payroll. See the S corp salary guide.
  4. State setup. Each new state needs its own withholding and unemployment accounts before the first pay run there.
Questions, answered

Frequently asked questions

What payroll software is best for a small medical practice?

For one location and one state, Gusto, OnPay and QuickBooks Workforce publish clear prices and cover the basics. Compare the total cost at your headcount, not just the base fee. Add HR tools only if you need them.

Is Gusto or ADP better for a medical practice?

Gusto publishes prices and suits smaller practices that want fast setup. ADP quotes by package and suits practices that want a large provider and more service. Both cover multi-state payroll on the right plan.

Which payroll software handles several entities?

Ask each vendor to demo it. Gusto lets one admin manage several companies with one login, ADP uses company codes, and Netchex says it manages several EINs from one account. Rippling and Paylocity are worth a demo for groups too.

How do I pay provider productivity bonuses?

Most practices calculate the bonus in a compensation model, then run it through payroll as supplemental pay. Netchex says it runs RVU-based bonuses in the same pay run. Withholding rules for bonuses apply either way.

Should my practice use a PEO?

A PEO can help if you want group benefits and HR support without building them yourself. Justworks posts PEO prices of $79 and $124 per employee a month. Compare the total against payroll software plus a benefits broker.

Can I pay providers as 1099 contractors?

Only if they truly work independently. If the practice sets their schedule, tools and methods, the IRS may treat them as employees. Have classification reviewed before you sign.
