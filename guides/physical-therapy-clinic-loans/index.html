Short answer

Most physical therapy clinic loans are SBA 7(a) loans: 1,393 went to PT clinics in SBA fiscal years 2023 to 2026, at a median of $150,000 over 10 years, with Huntington, Newtek Bank, TD Bank and M&T Bank the most active lenders. Larger startups, acquisitions and second clinics usually go to banks with healthcare teams, such as Live Oak Bank, Bank of America and U.S. Bank. Equipment and short cash gaps fit equipment lenders and credit lines from Crestmont Capital or Bluevine. Lucid is not a lender, but it builds the forecast, the debt plan and the financial package a PT lender asks for.

What PT clinic loans actually look like

Business Lending Bureau, an independent lending research site, analyzed SBA's own 7(a) loan data (released September 2026) for physical therapy clinics, NAICS code 621340. Its page, updated November 25, 2026, reports:

  1. 1,393 SBA 7(a) loans to PT clinics in fiscal years 2023 to 2026.
  2. A median loan of $150,000, with the middle half between $50,000 and $337,300.
  3. A median term of 10 years and a median starting rate of 10.24%.
  4. 35% of loans went to startups or businesses under two years old.
  5. The top three lenders made only 20% of the loans, so the market is spread out and shopping around pays.

Startup loans for a new PT clinic

A new clinic is underwritten on you and your plan, not on history. SBA's start-up rules, covered in full in our guide to medical practice startup loans, set two hard lines:

  1. Equity. At least 10% of total project costs, in cash you can document.
  2. Projections. The lender must see debt service coverage of at least 1.15 within two years of funding.

Some bank programs fund up to 100% of a startup, and U.S. Bank lists up to six months of interest-only payments, which helps while credentialing and first claims catch up.

How much do you need? There is no single figure, but one published reference helps. FYZICAL, a PT franchise, lists a total initial investment of $175,392 to $582,036 for a new clinic in its April 30, 2026 disclosure document. Its breakdown includes equipment of $11,000 to $76,000, leasehold improvements of $65,000 to $180,000 and operating expenses of $75,000 to $150,000. An independent clinic skips the franchise fee but faces the same buildout, equipment and working capital buckets.

Working capital is the bucket owners cut first. Insurance pays after credentialing and after claims clear, so your first months of payroll come from the loan or your savings.

Acquisition loans for buying a PT practice

Buying a clinic is usually an SBA 7(a) loan or a conventional bank loan, often with a seller note. The rules that matter most, explained in how do I finance buying a medical practice:

  1. A first acquisition needs at least 10% equity, and that floor cannot be reduced.
  2. A seller note counts toward equity only on full standby, with no payments for the life of the SBA loan.
  3. Seller earnouts are not allowed inside an SBA change of ownership.
  4. The lender needs debt service coverage of at least 1.25 times on the last year, or the average of the last two.
  5. In an initial acquisition, the seller cannot stay on as an employee, though the practice can contract the seller as a consultant for up to 24 months.

Rule 5 matters in PT, where the selling owner often treats patients and holds referral relationships. Plan the handover before you sign. If you are on the other side of the deal, see who is buying physical therapy practices.

Expansion loans for a second clinic or new equipment

Growth borrowing comes in three shapes.

  1. A term loan for a new location. SBA 7(a) or a bank term loan covers buildout, equipment and the first months of payroll at the new site.
  2. Equipment financing. Treatment tables, rehab equipment and imaging can be financed against the equipment itself. Compare options in best medical equipment financing.
  3. A line of credit for timing. Use it for slow insurance payments, not for a buildout. See the best line of credit for medical practices.

Some clinics also borrow against insurance receivables. That costs more, so read medical receivables financing first.

The lenders

Huntington

Huntington runs a Healthcare Practice Finance group and made more SBA loans to PT clinics than any other lender in fiscal 2023 to 2026, per the SBA data above. Its median PT loan was $100,000, and 59% of its PT loans went to startups. Terms: see the lender's site.

Best for: first clinics and owners in Huntington's markets.

Newtek Bank

Newtek Bank, part of NewtekOne, was second by count for PT clinics. Its lending page lists term loans from $5,000 to $15 million, with 5 to 25 year terms, for acquisitions, refinancing, working capital and owner-occupied real estate.

Best for: loans under $500,000 and working capital.

Live Oak Bank

Live Oak says it has financed over $1.5 billion in practice loans and runs healthcare lenders who only work with medical professionals. It lists SBA 7(a) loans up to $5 million, Live Oak Express SBA loans up to $350,000, and on November 26, 2026 showed a 7(a) rate range of about 6.75% to 9.75%.

Best for: acquisitions, partner buyouts and larger clinics.

Bank of America

Bank of America lists SBA 7(a) loans of $350,000 to $5 million, with terms up to 10 years for working capital, acquisitions and leasehold improvements, 15 for equipment and 25 for real estate. Its median PT loan in the SBA data was $300,000, the largest among the most active lenders. Its Practice Solutions team names dental, veterinary, medical and optometry owners, so ask whether a PT clinic qualifies for that program or goes through SBA.

Best for: larger loans and owners who want one bank for loans and accounts.

U.S. Bank

U.S. Bank's medical practice loan page lists up to 100% financing, terms up to 15 years, 25 for commercial real estate, up to six months of interest-only payments, and both secured and unsecured loans. Confirm that your clinic fits its practice program.

Best for: a long term and an interest-only start while visits ramp.

Firstrust Bank

Firstrust publishes a physical therapy practice page with a relationship manager, checking and savings accounts, loans and lines of credit, equipment financing and commercial mortgages. It does not publish terms.

Best for: owners who want a local banker who already works with PT clinics.

Crestmont Capital

Crestmont's physical therapy page lists equipment financing from $5,000 to $2 million over 12 to 60 months, working capital loans from $10,000 to $500,000 over 3 to 24 months, lines of credit from $10,000 to $250,000, and SBA 7(a) loans up to $5 million. It asks for 3 to 6 months of bank statements to start and says terms depend on your application.

Best for: equipment and fast working capital, compared against a bank quote.

Bluevine

Bluevine is a financial technology company, not a bank. Its line of credit, issued by Celtic Bank, goes up to $250,000 and asks for at least $10,000 in monthly revenue, a 625 or higher personal FICO score and 12 or more months in business.

Best for: a small line for payroll timing, once the clinic has a year of history.

What PT lenders test

Every lender checks credit, equity and coverage. For PT clinics, underwriters also look at the numbers that drive repayment.

  1. Visits per therapist per day and per week, by clinic.
  2. Net collections per visit, by payer.
  3. Payer mix, including Medicare, commercial plans, workers comp and cash pay.
  4. Days from visit to payment, and denial rates.
  5. Therapist and PTA staffing against visit volume.

Medicare rules shape the forecast. In 2026 the KX threshold for PT and speech therapy combined is $2,480, and services furnished by PT assistants are paid at 85% of the rate. Cash pay lines change the mix, as explained in cash based vs insurance physical therapy. Billing itself sits with your biller; see the best physical therapy billing companies.

The full document checklist is in what lenders look for in a practice loan.

How to choose

  1. First clinic, under $150,000. Start with Huntington, Newtek or your local bank, and compare one SBA quote with one conventional quote.
  2. First clinic, larger buildout. Add Live Oak, U.S. Bank and Bank of America, and ask how long interest-only lasts.
  3. Buying a practice. Get quotes from Live Oak, Bank of America and one regional SBA lender. Compare the 10 year payment, the guaranty fee and the seller note terms.
  4. Second or third clinic. Ask your current lender first, since it already knows your numbers, and price a separate equipment loan.
  5. Payroll timing gaps. A bank line of credit or Bluevine, not a long term loan.

Ask at least three lenders for a written term sheet. Compare rate, fees, term, interest-only months, collateral, personal guarantee and any requirement to move your accounts. For lenders across all specialties, see the best medical practice loan lenders.

Questions, answered

Frequently asked questions

Can I get an SBA loan to start a physical therapy clinic?

Yes. About 35% of SBA 7(a) loans to PT clinics in fiscal 2023 to 2026 went to startups or businesses under two years old. Expect to put in at least 10% equity and show projections that reach 1.15 debt service coverage within two years.

How much does it cost to open a PT clinic?

It depends on space, equipment and market. FYZICAL's 2026 disclosure lists $175,392 to $582,036 for a franchised clinic, including $75,000 to $150,000 of operating expenses. Build your own budget with buildout, equipment and several months of payroll.

Which bank makes the most loans to PT clinics?

In SBA 7(a) data for fiscal 2023 to 2026, Huntington made the most, 132 loans, followed by Newtek Bank, TD Bank and M&T Bank. Live Oak and Bank of America made fewer but larger loans.

What interest rate will I pay on a PT clinic loan?

The median starting rate on SBA 7(a) loans to PT clinics in fiscal 2023 to 2026 was 10.24%. Live Oak showed an SBA 7(a) range of about 6.75% to 9.75% on November 26, 2026. Your rate depends on the loan size, your credit and the lender.

How do I finance buying an existing PT practice?

Most buyers use an SBA 7(a) loan or a bank practice loan, sometimes with a seller note. SBA requires at least 10% equity and bans earnouts, and the seller cannot stay on as an employee in a first acquisition. See our acquisition guide for the full rules.

Should I use a line of credit or a term loan?

Use a term loan for a buildout, equipment or an acquisition. Use a line of credit for timing gaps, such as slow insurance payments. Mixing them up leaves you paying for a long project out of a short line.

Does a speech therapy clinic qualify for the same loans?

Generally yes. SBA groups physical, occupational and speech therapy offices together, and the same lenders and rules apply. Lenders will still test your visit volume, payer mix and collections.
