Short answer

Most physical therapy practices sell to a short list of private equity backed platforms and public companies. The most active include U.S. Physical Therapy, Upstream Rehabilitation, Athletico, Confluent Health, Ivy Rehab, PT Solutions, ATI Physical Therapy and Physical Rehabilitation Network (PRN), with regional platforms such as CORA, JAG-ONE and Therapy Partners Group behind them. CT Acquisitions tracks 18 active PT platforms, and Livingstone Partners puts add-on deals at an average of 7.8 times EBITDA for 2021 to 2026. Offers mix cash at closing with retained equity, earnouts and an employment term, so compare the full structure, not only the headline price.

The market in numbers

Physical therapy is one of the most consolidated outpatient specialties. CT Acquisitions, citing an October 2025 study in JAAOS, reports 2,591 private equity affiliated PT clinics in the US by 2024, with private equity deals rising from 4 in 2010 to 175 in 2023. The same study found the top 10 platforms run 59.3% of those clinics.

Livingstone Partners counts about 500 announced outpatient PT transactions since 2005, and most were add-ons to an existing platform. PE or Not lists 29 PT clinic groups it has researched, 24 of them private equity backed, with data as of October 31, 2026.

The buyers, one by one

U.S. Physical Therapy. The only public pure play PT buyer. CT Acquisitions reports 780 clinics in 44 states as of February 2026. USPH's site says it buys a percentage of a practice and the clinic keeps its brand. Its FY2024 Form 10-K, as summarized by CT Acquisitions, describes buying 65% to 75% of a practice in most deals, with the selling therapists keeping the rest. USPH also bought a 50% stake in a 50 clinic management services organization in late 2024.

Upstream Rehabilitation. Backed by Revelstoke Capital Partners with co-investors. Upstream has brought several regional brands together, including BenchMark Physical Therapy, Results Physiotherapy and Drayer Physical Therapy Institute. Upstream's release on the Results deal described 1,000 plus clinics in 28 states, and CT Acquisitions' tracker now estimates about 1,200.

Athletico Physical Therapy. Owned by funds of BDT Capital Partners since December 2016, per CT Acquisitions. CT Acquisitions lists about 900 locations in 24 states and DC. In June 2025 Athletico sold its onsite business, Pivot Onsite Innovations, to Concentra for $55 million, per Concentra's release.

Confluent Health. Partners Group took a significant stake in May 2019. Confluent bought MOTION PT Group in February 2023 and added Commonwealth Hand and Physical Therapy of Kentucky on October 14, 2025, when it reported 820 plus sites of care. Its site describes a wider partner network of over 1,650 sites.

Ivy Rehab. Backed by Waud Capital Partners since 2016, with a continuation fund closed October 4, 2022. Ivy runs a pediatric line, Ivy Rehab for Kids, and added Treehouse Pediatric Therapy in New Jersey on September 8, 2025. CT Acquisitions counts 560 plus clinics. Ivy's site has a "Sell your practice" page.

PT Solutions. General Atlantic took a majority stake in a deal reported in January 2022, with TowerBrook and Ascension as minority investors, per CT Acquisitions. PT Solutions reported 500 plus points of service in 25 states in January 2025, and acquired OrthoCarolina's physical therapy business on February 3, 2025.

ATI Physical Therapy. ATI was taken private on August 1, 2025 by a holder group led by Knighthead Capital Management and Marathon Asset Management, per ATI's release. Its site lists 850 plus clinics.

Physical Rehabilitation Network (PRN). Gryphon Investors acquired PRN on December 17, 2020. PRN added Spine and Sport Physical Therapy's 51 California clinics on March 3, 2025, and its site now lists more than 270 clinics in 16 states, with "Sell Your Practice" and "Partner with Us" pages.

Other active platforms. CT Acquisitions' tracker also lists CORA Physical Therapy (H.I.G. Capital, 2021), JAG-ONE Physical Therapy (Pamlico Capital), Professional Physical Therapy (Thomas H. Lee Partners), Therapy Partners Group (Shore Capital Partners), Alliance Physical Therapy Partners (BPOC, 2021), Phoenix Rehabilitation (Audax, 2018) and the FYZICAL franchise network (New Harbor Capital, 2017). Select Medical, a public company, runs about 1,900 outpatient rehab clinics. Smaller clinics also sell to a local competitor or to an associate PT.

How these deals are structured

CT Acquisitions' sell side guide (last reviewed November 2026) groups PT deals into four structures:

  1. Partnership sale. The buyer takes a majority, often 65% to 75% in USPH deals, and you keep the rest. You sign an employment agreement for up to five years and a non compete for up to two years after it ends. The buyer purchases your remaining stake later at a set multiple of earnings.
  2. Full buyout or majority recap by a private equity platform. Cash at closing, often with an earnout. SRS Acquiom found 22% of 2024 private target deals outside life sciences had an earnout, with a median potential of 31% of the closing payment.
  3. Franchise or network sale, such as joining the FYZICAL network.
  4. Internal sale to an associate PT, often funded with an SBA 7(a) loan of up to $5 million.

Multiples rise with size. Livingstone Partners' tracker (May 19, 2026) shows PT add-ons at 7.8 times EBITDA for 2021 to 2026, platforms under $200 million of enterprise value at 12.3 times, and larger platforms near 13.6 times. CT Acquisitions' tracker puts a single clinic at about 3.0 to 4.5 times EBITDA and 2 to 5 clinics at about 4.5 to 7.5 times. Treat these as ranges, not quotes. See how a medical practice is valued.

What PT buyers look for

CT Acquisitions lists four measures buyers hold your clinic against, using USPH's own figures as the benchmark:

  1. Visits per clinic per day. USPH averaged 32.2 in 2025.
  2. Net revenue per visit. USPH averaged $105.76 in 2025.
  3. Payer mix. Medicare and Medicaid were 40.6% of USPH visits in 2024. CT Acquisitions' tracker says commercial heavy clinics can lift the multiple by 1.0 to 1.5 times.
  4. Clinician retention. Signed agreements with senior PTs before you go to market, or the buyer may move price into an earnout.

Buyers also rebuild owner pay to a market PT salary and want 24 to 36 months of visits, revenue per visit, payer mix and denials by clinic and month. They test Medicare billing, including the KX threshold ($2,480 for PT and speech therapy combined in 2026) and the 85% rate on services furnished by PT assistants. Cash pay, sports and hand therapy lines are valued separately, so keep them apart. See cash based vs insurance physical therapy.

Most of that list comes down to the books: accrual statements that tie to the bank, a profit and loss for every clinic, and revenue split by payer. See how to prepare a medical practice for sale and what a quality of earnings report is.

How to compare offers

A partnership offer and a full buyout can carry the same headline value and very different outcomes. Line them up on the same page:

  1. Cash at closing, after fees, debt payoff and taxes.
  2. Retained or rolled equity: how much, how it is valued, and the exact formula and earnings period for the later buyback.
  3. Earnouts: the metric, the cap, the term, and who controls the numbers that decide them.
  4. Your pay after the sale: salary, productivity formula and benefits.
  5. Employment term, non compete and what happens if you leave early.
  6. Clinical autonomy, staffing and who decides on new clinics.
  7. Working capital and escrow terms that adjust the price at closing.

Ask every bidder for these terms in writing at the letter of intent. Model each offer over five years with your CPA, and have a healthcare attorney read the paperwork. An M&A advisor who knows PT buyers can bring two or more of them to the table.

Questions, answered

Frequently asked questions

Who is buying physical therapy practices right now?

Mostly private equity backed platforms and public companies. The most active include U.S. Physical Therapy, Upstream Rehabilitation, Athletico, Confluent Health, Ivy Rehab, PT Solutions, ATI Physical Therapy and PRN. CT Acquisitions tracks 18 active PT platforms in 2026.

What multiple do physical therapy practices sell for?

It depends on size and payer mix. Livingstone Partners reports PT add-ons averaged 7.8 times EBITDA for 2021 to 2026, while single clinics often price lower, around 3.0 to 4.5 times per CT Acquisitions. A formal valuation gives the number for your practice.

Can I keep working in my practice after I sell?

Usually, yes. In a USPH style partnership you sign an employment agreement for up to five years and keep part of the equity. Private equity buyouts often add an employment term and an earnout tied to staying.

Should I sell all of my practice or keep a stake?

A full sale gives a cleaner exit and more cash now. A retained stake suits owners who will keep treating, because the buyer purchases the rest later at a set multiple. Compare both over five years, after tax.

What makes one PT clinic worth more than another?

Visits per clinic per day, net revenue per visit, a commercial heavy payer mix and clinicians who stay. Clean Medicare billing and books that show profit by clinic also help.

How do I prepare my physical therapy practice for a buyer?

Start two to three years out with accrual monthly books, a profit and loss for every clinic, revenue by payer and an add back file with owner pay set to market. Then hire an advisor and attorney before you take offers.
