Short answer

Heading into 2027, most dermatology buyers are private equity backed groups and MSOs. The largest include Forefront Dermatology, the combined U.S. Dermatology Partners and DermCare Management, QualDerm Partners, Epiphany Dermatology, Advanced Dermatology and Cosmetic Surgery, Schweiger Dermatology Group and PhyNet Dermatology, with dozens of smaller regional platforms behind them. Trackers such as PE or Not list 31 private equity backed dermatology groups as of October 31, 2026. Since 2023, offers for practices joining a platform have mixed cash at closing (often 55% to 70% of value, per CT Acquisitions) with rollover equity, seller notes and earnouts, so compare the full structure, not only the headline price.

The market in numbers

Dermatology was one of the first specialties private equity rolled up. DealSeam, citing JAMA Dermatology, reports that from 2012 to 2018, 17 private equity backed groups acquired 184 dermatology practices, and annual deals rose from 5 to 59. DealSeam estimates that roughly 10% to 15% of US dermatology practices are private equity owned today, so most are still independent.

Consolidation continues among the platforms themselves. On August 13, 2026, DermCare Management and U.S. Dermatology Partners announced they had completed a combination spanning 12 states.

The buyers, one by one

Forefront Dermatology. Partners Group, with Forefront's existing practitioner owners, acquired Forefront from OMERS Private Equity on February 10, 2022, and OMERS kept a minority stake. The release described over 200 clinics in 22 states, more than 400 providers and an in house dermatopathology lab. DealSeam lists Swann Dermatology Partners (December 2025) and Wichita Dermatology and Aesthetics (November 2025) among recent additions.

U.S. Dermatology Partners and DermCare Management. The two completed a combination on August 13, 2026, per DermCare's announcement, which said the group spans 12 states and serves more than three million patients a year. DermCare's founder became executive chair and U.S. Dermatology Partners' chief executive leads the combined company. U.S. Dermatology Partners' own site lists over 120 locations in nine states, and DermCare said it partners with more than 270 providers in Florida, Texas, Virginia, North Carolina and California.

QualDerm Partners. BayPine announced it would acquire Pinnacle Dermatology on October 29, 2021, when Pinnacle had 87 locations in 11 states. Deal databases record Pinnacle joining QualDerm Partners in November 2022 under BayPine ownership. CT Acquisitions lists QualDerm among the leading dermatology platforms in 2026.

Epiphany Dermatology. Trackers list Leonard Green and Partners as backer since 2022. Epiphany's site describes a collaborative network of dermatology providers, nurse practitioners and aestheticians, and has a page for practices interested in partnering. DealSeam lists Norris Dermatology and Laser Northwest (June 2025) as an addition.

Advanced Dermatology and Cosmetic Surgery (ADCS). ADCS describes itself as a network that combines medical and cosmetic dermatology, founded in 1989, with over 150 locations in 14 states. Trackers list Harvest Partners, with Audax Private Equity, as backers since 2016. Its site has "Selling Your Practice?" and "Steps to Partnering With Us" pages.

Schweiger Dermatology Group. Founded in 2010, Schweiger reports 170 plus locations and over two million patients served, with locations in nine states from New York and New Jersey to California. DealSeam lists LLR Partners, SV Health Investors and LNK Partners as investors, and additions including United Skin Specialists (January 2024), California Skin Institute (June 2025) and Catskill Dermatology (September 2026). Its site offers a "Sell Your Practice" page.

PhyNet Dermatology. Based in Nashville, PhyNet is backed by Century Equity Partners and Ontario Teachers' Pension Plan, with a 2017 investment year per Century Equity's portfolio page. PE or Not lists 118 known locations.

Other active platforms. Trackers also list Anne Arundel Dermatology (Ridgemont Equity Partners, 2020), AQUA Dermatology (Gryphon Investors and GTCR), Platinum Dermatology Partners (Sun Capital and Sterling Partners, 2022), Golden State Dermatology (Sorenson Capital, 2018), DOCS Dermatology (SkyKnight Capital, 2022), Frontier Dermatology (A&M Capital, 2020) and Integrated Dermatology (Bregal Sagemount, 2023). Becker's ASC reported Integrated Dermatology's second acquisition of 2026 in August. Cosmetic chains such as LaserAway, SkinSpirit and Ideal Image buy or open aesthetic clinics, which matters if your practice is mostly cosmetic.

Buyers that are not private equity. Health systems, larger independent groups and other owners also buy practices or bring in partners. PE or Not lists several large groups owned by their own clinicians. See private equity vs MSO vs staying independent.

How these deals are structured

Most private equity dermatology deals use an MSO model. DealSeam describes it this way: the platform's management services organization owns the non clinical business, while licensed clinicians own the clinical practice. You sell the business side and usually sign an employment or services agreement.

CT Acquisitions' Dermatology M&A Multiples Report (July 2026, through Q2 2026), which draws on Provident Healthcare Partners and other sources, describes these common terms:

  1. Cash at closing. For practices joining a platform, cash at closing has run about 55% to 70% of value since 2023, down from about 70% to 80% during the 2020 to 2022 peak.
  2. Rollover equity. Often 15% to 30% of value, reinvested in the platform. It can rise or fall when the platform is sold again.
  3. Seller notes. About 5% to 20% of value, paid over time.
  4. Earnouts. The report calls provider retention earnouts the most common structure at this size, usually over two to five years. Cosmetic revenue earnouts are standard when cosmetic work is above 30% of revenue.

The same report shows multiples rising with size, from about 3.5 to 5.5 times SDE for practices under $500,000 of SDE, to about 7 to 10 times adjusted EBITDA at $1 million to $3 million, and 9 to 12 times at $3 million to $10 million. Treat these as ranges, not quotes. See how a medical practice is valued.

What dermatology buyers look for

CT Acquisitions' sell side guide (updated June 6, 2026) lists what buyers pay more for:

  1. A mix of general, Mohs surgery, cosmetic and pathology services.
  2. Commercial in network contracts, not only Medicare.
  3. Provider productivity and a modern EHR.
  4. Pathology revenue captured in house or through a compliant partner.
  5. Several providers and a succession plan, so value does not rest on one owner.
  6. Audited or reviewed financials and a real management team.

Buyers discount heavy dependence on the owner, one service line, a Medicare heavy payer mix, legacy systems and unaudited financials. Most of that list comes down to the books: clean, accrual statements that split medical, surgical, cosmetic and retail revenue and show profit by provider and location. How to compare offers

Two offers with the same headline price can be worth very different amounts. Line them up on the same page:

  1. Cash at closing, after fees, debt payoff and taxes.
  2. Rollover equity: how much, at what value, what rights it carries and when it can be sold.
  3. Seller notes and earnouts: the amounts, the conditions, and who controls the numbers that decide them.
  4. Your pay after the sale: the compensation reset, productivity formula and benefits.
  5. Non compete, term of employment and what happens if you leave early.
  6. Governance: clinical autonomy, staffing and who decides on growth.
  7. Working capital and escrow terms that adjust the price at closing.

Model each offer over five years with your CPA, and have a healthcare attorney read the paperwork. An advisor who knows dermatology buyers helps create competition. See how to choose an M&A advisor and should I sell my practice to private equity.

Questions, answered

Frequently asked questions

Who is buying dermatology practices right now?

Mostly private equity backed platforms and MSOs. The largest include Forefront Dermatology, U.S. Dermatology Partners with DermCare Management, QualDerm Partners, Epiphany Dermatology, ADCS, Schweiger Dermatology Group and PhyNet Dermatology. PE or Not lists 31 private equity backed dermatology groups as of October 31, 2026.

What multiple do dermatology practices sell for?

It depends on size and mix. CT Acquisitions' July 2026 report shows about 7 to 10 times adjusted EBITDA for practices with $1 million to $3 million of EBITDA, higher for larger groups and lower for small practices priced on SDE. A formal valuation gives the number for your practice.

How much of the price is paid in cash?

For practices joining a platform, CT Acquisitions reports cash at closing of about 55% to 70% of value since 2023. The rest is usually rollover equity, seller notes and earnouts.

What is rollover equity?

It is part of your price reinvested as ownership in the buying platform. It can grow or shrink when the platform is sold again, so ask how it is valued and when you can sell it.

Do buyers value cosmetic revenue differently?

Yes. Buyers look at medical, surgical and cosmetic revenue separately, and the CT Acquisitions report notes cosmetic revenue earnouts are standard when cosmetic work is above 30% of revenue. Books that split these lines make the buyer's review faster.

How do I prepare my dermatology practice for a buyer?

Start two to three years out with accrual monthly books, a documented add back file, market owner pay and revenue split by service line, provider and location. Then hire an advisor and attorney before you take offers.
