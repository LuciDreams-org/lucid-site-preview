Should I sell my therapy group practice to private equity?

Home / Guides / Should I sell my therapy group practice to private equity?

Short answer

Sell to a private equity backed platform if your group runs without you on the schedule, most clinicians are W-2 with written agreements, and you want a large payment now in exchange for less control. If the practice still depends on your own caseload or a few contractors, a regional therapy group, a sale to your own clinicians or two more years of growth will usually leave you with more. Published ranges put established therapy groups at about 3 to 5 times adjusted EBITDA, with larger groups that have a management layer reaching higher. In a therapy group the price is really a bet on whether your clinicians stay, so expect part of it to depend on retention after closing.

What buyers pay for in a therapy group

Buyers do not pay for your license or your waitlist. They pay for earnings that will still be there after you step back. Behavioral Health Business Broker says the strongest prices go to practices with stable clinicians, spread out referrals, clean billing and little dependence on the founder.

Its published ranges, by size:

  1. Solo practice, about $150,000 to $500,000 in revenue: 0.5 to 1 times gross revenue.
  2. Small group, 2 to 5 clinicians: 2.5 to 4 times EBITDA.
  3. Mid size group, 6 to 15 clinicians: 3.5 to 5 times EBITDA.

CT Acquisitions (May 2026) puts multi site, multi clinician groups with a management layer and $1 million to $5 million of EBITDA at about 6 to 8 times. DealFlow OS (updated October 2026) puts therapy and behavioral health groups at about 3 to 5.5 times. These are published ranges, not quotes. See how a medical practice is valued.

What moves you up or down inside a range:

  1. Owner dependence. If a large share of collections is your own caseload, that revenue can walk out with you. CT Acquisitions lists founder dependence among the things that kill deals in diligence.
  2. Clinician structure. W-2 clinicians with written agreements and low turnover raise value. Loose 1099 arrangements and high turnover lower it.
  3. Payer mix. Commercial in network and private pay revenue is valued above heavy Medicaid exposure. See insurance vs private pay for a group practice.
  4. Billing and credentialing. High denials, aged receivables or credentialing gaps show up in the review.
  5. Management depth. A clinical director and an operations lead who run the week without you.

For psychology groups, testing and assessment revenue is often priced on its own terms. Report it apart from therapy revenue so a buyer can see what each line earns.

Clinician retention: the real price driver

In a therapy group, your clinicians are the product. CT Acquisitions calls the binding constraint in behavioral health the supply of clinicians, and says buyers confirm key clinician retention through stay bonuses or non competes before closing.

Expect the buyer to ask for:

  1. A roster with start dates, caseloads, pay model and agreement status for every clinician.
  2. Turnover over the last two or three years.
  3. Your hiring funnel: how long from offer to first billable session.
  4. Revenue and margin by clinician, so the buyer can see who carries the practice.

If you run a heavy 1099 model, CT Acquisitions suggests you understand the risk and consider moving core clinicians to W-2 before a sale. That change affects payroll cost, taxes and margin, so model it a year ahead, not during diligence.

When and how you tell clinicians is a judgment call. Many owners wait until terms are settled. Talk to your attorney about what you can share and when.

Earnouts, rollover and the money you get later

Most offers combine cash at closing with rollover equity and sometimes an earnout. Behavioral Health Properties (October 2026) notes that private equity and management services buyers are more likely to use rollover equity, earnouts, standard employment packages and restrictive covenants.

For a therapy group, the later money is often tied to people. DealFlow OS describes earnouts tied to clinician retention and revenue targets over 12 to 24 months. CT Acquisitions adds that owners who still carry a caseload are often asked to stay longer, with part of the price tied to an earnout.

Before you sign a letter of intent, ask:

  1. What counts as retention: a head count, a revenue level, or named clinicians?
  2. Who controls hiring, pay and scheduling during the earnout period?
  3. What happens to the earnout if the buyer changes the pay model?
  4. How much of the total depends on you staying, and for how long?

Compare every offer on after tax cash at closing, a realistic view of the rollover, and the share that depends on targets. The full rollover and earnout detail is in should I sell my practice to private equity.

The alternatives, one by one

PE backed platform. The largest checks go to groups that are already platforms or close to it. The trade is less control, a new pay model and a non compete. Mental health platforms such as LifeStance, Thriveworks and Mindpath Health appear in the trackers cited in our buyers guide, and some, like Refresh Mental Health, invite owners to apply on their own sites. Buyer activity changes often, so check before you plan around any one name.

Regional therapy group. Olympic M&A (September 2026) lists regional therapy groups as a buyer type that may value local density, specialties or team capacity. These deals can keep your culture closer to how it is today.

Nonprofit or health system. A fit for groups built on Medicaid or community contracts, where a mission driven buyer may value access more than margin.

Sale to your clinicians. BH Practice Solutions (April 2026) describes an internal sale to a clinical director or partner, or a phased buy in where equity moves to key clinicians over time. Internal buyers often have limited capital, so deals use seller notes or bank loans. DealFlow OS notes that SBA 7(a) loans up to $5 million are used to buy established group practices. See practice partner buy in loans.

Merger with a peer group. BH Practice Solutions says a merger can add scale and make the combined group more attractive to a later buyer. You trade sole control for shared control.

Keep growing and sell later. If you are years from exit, the steps that raise a future price also raise profit now: move revenue off your own caseload, firm up clinician agreements, and build a management layer. The private equity vs MSO vs independent practice guide walks through staying independent.

How to choose

  1. You are within five years of retiring and the group runs without you. Run a process with several platform and regional buyers. An advisor helps; see how to choose a healthcare M and A advisor.
  2. You still see a full caseload. Expect a long earnout or a low multiple. Spend 12 to 24 months moving clients to associates first.
  3. You have a strong clinical director who wants to own. Price an internal sale next to any outside offer.
  4. Your group is mostly Medicaid. Add nonprofit and community buyers to your list.
  5. Your psychology group does testing. Show testing revenue and margin on its own line.
  6. Your group has several locations or entities. Get one consolidated set of books first. See Lucid for medical groups.

Whatever you choose, have a healthcare attorney read the paperwork. See how to choose a healthcare attorney.

Questions, answered

Frequently asked questions

What is my therapy group practice worth?

Published ranges put established groups at about 3 to 5 times adjusted EBITDA, small groups of 2 to 5 clinicians at about 2.5 to 4 times, and solo practices at about 0.5 to 1 times gross revenue. Larger groups with a management layer reach higher. A formal valuation gives your number.

Do private equity buyers want therapy only groups?

Some do, but many of the most active buyers focus on psychiatry and medication management. Therapy groups still sell to platforms, regional groups and nonprofits. Size, clinician stability and payer mix decide who shows up.

Will my clinicians stay after I sell?

That is the question every buyer will test. Buyers often use stay bonuses and non competes, and tie part of your price to retention. W-2 clinicians with written agreements and low turnover make the answer easier.

Should I move 1099 clinicians to W-2 before selling?

CT Acquisitions suggests owners with a heavy 1099 model understand the risk and consider moving core clinicians to W-2 before a sale. It raises payroll cost and changes margin, so model it a year ahead with your CPA.

How long does it take to sell a therapy group?

CT Acquisitions describes about 90 to 180 days from first conversation to closing for an off market sale to a platform. Getting the books and agreements ready should start one to three years earlier.

Can I sell my practice to one of my clinicians?

Yes. Internal sales and phased buy ins are common where a clinical director wants to own. Buyers often pay over time through a seller note or finance with an SBA loan.
