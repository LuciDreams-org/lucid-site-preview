Short answer

For most practice owners the best value is a short, practice-specific course: the MGMA Principles of Practice Management Certificate (free for MGMA members), the AMA business of medicine programs, or your specialty society's course from AAOS, AAOE, ADA, APTA or AmSpa. If you want a credential, the AAPC CPPM course or MGMA board certification goes deeper. If you want general business finance, a university certificate such as eCornell Healthcare Management or HBS Online Core Business Essentials costs a few thousand dollars, and an executive MBA costs tens of thousands. Lucid is not a course, but it gives you the monthly P&L and a CPA to explain it, which is what most of these courses teach you to read.

Association courses built for practices

MGMA Principles of Practice Management Certificate

MGMA is the main association for medical practice administrators. Its Principles of Practice Management Certificate is free for members and $625 for non-members. It is online and self-paced, about 12.5 hours, with one year of access. Part 2 covers accounts payable and payroll management, and Part 3 covers financial reporting and budgeting.

MGMA sells other ACMPE certificates too. The Operations Management Certificate lists at $450 for members and $840 for non-members. For a full credential, the ACMPE board certification exams (the path to the CMPE) list at $167 each.

Best for: owners and administrators who want a practical base in operations and finance, cheaply.

AMA Independent Practice Accelerator

The AMA Independent Practice Accelerator is a series of 11 self-paced courses on AMA Ed Hub. It offers up to 8.75 AMA PRA Category 1 Credits. Topics are grouped into strategic and financial foundation (strategic planning, financing, payment models and payer contracting, financial literacy), operational and legal requirements, and skills for running a healthy practice.

On November 26, 2026 the AMA page said the courses are coming "this winter" and did not list a price. Subscribe on the page for launch news.

Best for: owners starting or growing an independent practice who want CME while they learn.

AMA Business of Medicine program

This is a curated set of AMA Ed Hub activities worth 11.5 AMA PRA Category 1 Credits. It has seven introductory bundles, including health care business and financial management, billing and the revenue cycle, human resources and health IT. The AMA page does not state a price. See the AMA site.

Best for: a broad, low commitment overview with CME credit.

AAPC CPPM practice management course

AAPC is best known for coding credentials. Its CPPM course is the deeper option here. Members pay $1,195 for the self-paced course or $1,495 for Self-Paced Max, which adds a reference guide, a practice exam and two exam attempts. Topics include the chart of accounts, financial reports, revenue cycle management, HR, compliance and HIPAA. The exam is 135 questions over 4 hours.

Best for: an owner or office manager who wants a recognized credential and does not mind exam prep.

Specialty society courses

Specialty courses speak your practice's language: implants and ASC splits in orthopedics, production and collections in dental, memberships and injectables in a med spa.

Orthopedics: AAOS and AAOE

The AAOS Practice Management Certificate has seven online modules worth up to 15 AMA PRA Category 1 Credits. Topics include healthcare economics, revenue cycle, financial literacy and practice performance, and transactions. It is included in the AAOS Access Plus subscription, listed at $455 for members and $569 for non-members.

AAOE, the orthopedic administrators' group, sells Foundations of Orthopedic Practice Management for $99 (members) or $149. It is eight short on-demand modules, including finance basics and revenue cycle, with a certificate and no academic credit. See our orthopedics page.

Dental: ADA

The ADA sells short practice management courses on its learning site. For example, 10 Top Management Tools for a Successful Practice is 1.5 CE credits, listed at $229 for members and $349 for non-members. See our dental page.

Physical therapy: APTA

APTA's learning center lists Private Practice Operations 101, a recorded course on KPIs, reporting and budgeting, at $500 for members and $695 for non-members. APTA notes it was recorded in 2015, so check how current it is. See our physical therapy page.

Med spa: AmSpa Boot Camp

The AmSpa Medical Spa Boot Camp is a one-day, in person program for owners and aspiring owners. The next listed date is Anaheim, February 10 to 11, 2027 (reception the first evening, training the second day). Tickets are $895 for members and $995 for non-members. The agenda includes legal and compliance, P&L analysis, pricing and compensation models. See our med spa page.

University certificates

eCornell Healthcare Management

Cornell's online Healthcare Management certificate is $4,600, or 7 monthly payments of $708. It is 7 short courses over about 4 months at 3 to 5 hours a week. Coursework covers financial statements and ratios, projections, ROI, cost accounting and budgeting. The sequence next starts December 30, 2026.

Best for: owners who want university-level finance without leaving the practice.

HBS Online Core Business Essentials

Harvard Business School Online's Core Business Essentials is $2,799. It runs 17 weeks at 5 to 8 hours a week and covers accounting, finance, statistics and data analysis. It is general business, not healthcare specific.

Best for: owners who want to read financial statements like a banker, in any industry.

Coaching and peer programs

Coaching programs trade structured content for live access and peers.

  1. Group Practice Exchange. For therapy group practice owners. $99 a month or $445 every 6 months, 6 month minimum. Includes monthly live Q and A, quarterly sessions with HR, finance, compliance and operations advisors, and 100+ templates. See our behavioral health page.
  2. EliteNP Practice Growth Accelerator. For nurse practitioner owners with an operating practice. $3,999 for 12 weeks: six modules, 12 weekly live calls and 6 CE hours. Covers pricing, retention, hiring and a business metrics dashboard. It has no dedicated finance or tax module.

For hands-on help instead of training, see how to choose a medical practice consultant.

The expensive alternative: an executive MBA

An executive MBA is the full option. The CU Denver Executive MBA in Health Administration lists $67,500 for 45 credit hours over 16 months, hybrid with residencies, one cohort a year starting in August (next August 2027). That buys depth and a network, but most owners need a working P&L more than a degree.

How to choose

  1. New owner, small team. Start with the free or low cost options: the MGMA certificate (if you are a member) and your specialty society course.
  2. Growing to several locations. Add the AAPC CPPM or MGMA board certification for your administrator, and a finance-heavy certificate such as eCornell for yourself. See our medical group page.
  3. Orthopedics. AAOS for the owner, AAOE for the administrator.
  4. Med spa. AmSpa Boot Camp first, because the legal structure matters as much as the numbers.
  5. You want numbers, not theory. A course teaches you to read a P&L. You still need a real one every month. Read the monthly numbers a practice owner should review and compare yourself with benchmarking sources.

Questions, answered

Frequently asked questions

Do I need an MBA to run a medical practice?

No. Most owners get what they need from a short practice management course plus a good monthly financial report. An MBA helps if you plan to build a large group or move into health system leadership.

What is the cheapest practice management course?

The MGMA Principles of Practice Management Certificate is free for MGMA members. AAOE's orthopedic foundations course is $99 for members. Prices as listed November 26, 2026.

Which course earns CME credit?

The AMA programs and the AAOS certificate offer AMA PRA Category 1 Credits. ADA courses offer dental CE, and the EliteNP program offers 6 CE hours. MGMA courses earn ACMPE credits.

Is the AAPC CPPM worth it?

For an office manager or administrator who wants a recognized credential, it is a solid choice. It covers the chart of accounts, financial reports, revenue cycle and compliance. An owner who only wants to read the books may get more from a shorter course.

What finance topics should a course cover?

Look for reading a P&L and balance sheet, cash flow, budgeting, payer contracting and KPIs such as collections and overhead. Courses that cover your chart of accounts help most, because that is how your books are organized. See our chart of accounts guide.

Can course fees be deducted?

Education that maintains or improves skills in your current business is often deductible, but rules depend on your situation. Ask your CPA before you count on it.

Are coaching programs worth the money?

They work best when you will use the live calls and templates every month. Compare the yearly cost with a part-time CFO, who works on your actual numbers. See what a part-time CFO costs.
