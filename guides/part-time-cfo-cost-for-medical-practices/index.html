Short answer

Published prices for a part time (fractional) CFO serving healthcare practices cluster between about $3,000 and $10,000 a month, and the fractional model costs far less than a full time hire. A full time CFO costs far more once salary and benefits are counted. Most private practices need a CFO level view when they add a second location, a third provider or a partner, when collections pass a few million dollars, or when an outside buyer shows interest. Lucid offers CFO services on a published price list, shown on the pricing page calculator, so you can see the number before you talk to anyone.

What a part time CFO is, and what it is not

A part time CFO, also called a fractional or outsourced CFO, is a senior finance person who works for your practice a set number of hours or a set scope each month. The job is to turn your numbers into decisions: what to charge, whether to hire, whether a new location will pay for itself, how much cash to keep, and what to do before a bank or buyer asks questions.

It helps to separate three roles that owners often mix up.

  1. A bookkeeper records transactions, reconciles bank and card accounts and closes the books each month.
  2. A tax accountant or CPA prepares returns, plans for tax and advises on entity structure.
  3. A CFO uses the finished books to forecast, budget, model decisions and report to partners, lenders or buyers.

A CFO cannot do the job on messy books. If the monthly numbers are late or wrong, the first weeks of any CFO engagement go to cleanup. That is why many practices start with solid bookkeeping and add CFO work once the numbers can be trusted. See how much medical practice bookkeeping costs for that first layer.

Three points stand out.

  1. The healthcare firms and the wider market sources overlap. Both healthcare firms report $3,000 to $10,000 a month for a fractional CFO, and the general market sources run somewhat wider.
  2. Cost follows scope and complexity. Expect a simpler scope, such as quarterly planning, to cost less than a group with several locations, an entity structure to manage and a lender or buyer in the picture. The sources do not publish a price by practice size, so ask each firm to quote your scope.
  3. The fee is not the whole price. Several firms add a one time onboarding or setup charge, and some leave bookkeeping out, so you may pay for books and CFO work separately.

Compare that with the alternative. Northstar puts a full time CFO at $18,000 to $32,000 a month with benefits, and Sorso cites the Bureau of Labor Statistics median for chief executives in health care and social assistance at $197,090 a year as of May 2025, before benefits. For most practices below very large scale, a full time hire is hard to justify.

What you should get for the money

Price only makes sense next to deliverables. A useful part time CFO engagement for a medical practice covers these items every month or quarter.

  1. A financial model that reflects how your practice earns money, by payer group, provider, service line or location.
  2. A budget and a monthly budget versus actuals review, so a miss shows up in weeks, not at year end.
  3. A cash forecast, usually 13 weeks or 90 days out, that shows when payer payments and payroll will collide.
  4. Scenario planning for decisions such as adding a provider, opening a location, buying equipment or absorbing a payer rate cut.
  5. A short list of KPIs for your specialty, such as collections as a percent of billed charges, overhead as a percent of collections, and profit by location.
  6. Reporting a lender, partner or buyer can read without a call to explain it.

Sorso says bookkeeping and tax filing are not included in its CFO service. Northstar says its fractional CFO builds a payer mix analysis that shows revenue per visit and margin contribution by payer. When you compare quotes, ask each firm to list its monthly deliverables in writing and say who builds and updates the model.

When a medical practice should hire one

There is no legal threshold and no single number that makes a CFO necessary. Two healthcare firms publish similar triggers.

  1. Revenue size. Sorso says the need for strategic finance work tends to arrive around $3 million in annual revenue. Northstar points to collections of roughly $3 million to $5 million or more. Below that, strong bookkeeping and a CPA review usually cover the need.
  2. A second or third location, or a second or third provider. Consolidated numbers hide which site or provider makes money.
  3. Collections falling while patient volume is steady or rising. This often points to payer mix, write offs or slow payments, which a CFO can analyze.
  4. A planned expansion, equipment purchase or loan. Lenders ask for projections and a clear package of financials.
  5. Partner buyouts, succession plans or an outside offer. Buyers and partners look at the numbers closely. See how a medical practice is valued.
  6. You have an accountant but no one tells you what the numbers mean.

Signs you are not ready: the books are months behind, bank accounts are not reconciled, or revenue is booked only when the deposit hits. In that case fix the foundation first. Those same books also feed the cash versus accrual choice, which shapes what a CFO can forecast.

  3. Do you also handle the books, or do I need a separate bookkeeper?

Frequently asked questions

How much does a part time CFO cost for a medical practice?

Healthcare finance firms publish about $3,000 to $10,000 a month for a fractional CFO. Wider market surveys that include other industries run from about $3,000 to $15,000 a month. These are asking prices from firms that sell the service. Lucid publishes its own price list and calculator on its pricing page.

When does a medical practice need a part time CFO?

Common triggers are collections of roughly $3 million or more, a second location or provider, falling collections with steady volume, a loan or expansion plan, and partner buyout or sale talks. If the books are behind, fix that first. A CFO works from accurate monthly numbers.

Is a fractional CFO worth it for a private practice?

It can be when the CFO work changes a decision you would otherwise make blind, such as a hire, a new location or a payer change. Judge the fee against what the practice does with the reports. Ask for written deliverables so you can see what you receive each month.

What is the difference between a bookkeeper, a CPA and a CFO?

A bookkeeper keeps the monthly records, a CPA handles tax and compliance, and a CFO uses both to forecast and plan. Some firms bundle all three. Lucid offers bookkeeping, tax and CFO services with a dedicated CPA on every account.

Does a CFO replace my accountant?

No. A CFO looks forward at planning and cash, while your tax accountant prepares returns and advises on tax. They should share the same set of books. Having one team handle both avoids conflicting numbers.
