This guide explains how to find and vet a lawyer. It is not legal advice, and nothing here replaces advice from an attorney licensed in your state.

Short answer

Hire a healthcare attorney, not a general business lawyer, whenever the work touches who can own the practice, how money moves between entities, payer or referral rules, or a sale. Your main choices are national healthcare firms such as Hall Render, Polsinelli and Bass, Berry and Sims, state healthcare boutiques, niche firms that publish flat fees or memberships, and compliance consultants for program work that is not legal advice. Vet each one on matters like yours, who does the work, state licensing and a written fee estimate. When a practice needs a healthcare attorney

Brewster Law Firm notes that "healthcare attorney" is a broad label, and the right lawyer for one matter can be wrong for another. Match the lawyer to the job.

  1. Formation. Choosing the entity, the state, and who can hold ownership. Many states limit who can own a medical practice, so the form you pick matters from day one.
  2. MSO and corporate practice structures. In states with a corporate practice of medicine rule, a common setup is a professional corporation owned by a licensed clinician plus a management services organization (MSO) under a management services agreement. Prospyr's 2026 review of med spa firms describes the attorney's job as drafting those documents, setting a fair market value management fee and checking fee splitting, anti kickback and scope of practice rules for the state. See our med spa MSO vs medical director service guide and private equity vs MSO vs independent practice.
  3. Payer contracts. Reviewing commercial payer agreements, rate amendments and termination terms. Medical Economics quotes one internist who looked for a lawyer with experience with Medicare or the specific insurer for audits and payer disputes.
  4. Employment and noncompete. The AAFP lists compensation models, termination clauses, noncompetes, malpractice tail, call expectations and moonlighting as items a health care contract lawyer should spot. Noncompete law is moving fast: the FTC says its 2024 noncompete rule is not in effect and not enforceable, while several states passed new healthcare limits in 2025 and 2026, so state law controls.
  5. Partner buy ins and sales. Buy sell terms, purchase agreements and closing paperwork. Pair the attorney with a deal advisor; see how to choose a healthcare M&A advisor and how to prepare a medical practice for sale.
  6. Compliance. HIPAA, the Anti Kickback Statute, the Stark Law and billing rules. Brewster suggests asking whether the lawyer has handled a government investigation, a payer audit or a self disclosure.

Joining a group as a partner? Read How do I finance a practice partner buy-in? for the money side of the deal.

National healthcare firms

Large firms with big healthcare groups handle regulatory work, transactions and investigations across many states.

  1. Hall Render says it has focused only on healthcare law for more than 50 years. Curatrix's 2026 ranking credits it with more than 150 attorneys working only on healthcare matters.
  2. Polsinelli describes itself as one of the largest health care practices in the country, and Chambers USA ranks its healthcare department.
  3. Bass, Berry and Sims says its healthcare group has more than 325 attorneys, and it reports that the American Health Law Association (AHLA) ranked it the second largest healthcare law firm in 2026.

Best for: groups with several states, an investor or MSO deal, or a government inquiry. Limits: none of the three publishes fees. Ask for a written estimate and the rates of everyone who will bill time.

State and regional healthcare boutiques

Smaller firms that work mostly on healthcare in one state or region. Curatrix lists several, such as Pierce and Mandell in Massachusetts, which it says has served medical practices since 1995 and works with the Massachusetts Medical Society on noncompete education. Lana Ros of Pashman Stein Walder Hayden told Medical Economics that the attorney should know health law in the practice's state and also employment and business law.

Best for: formation, PC and MSO setup, payer and employment paperwork in your state. Limits: ask how they handle a second state if you plan to expand.

Med spa and aesthetics focused firms

Med spa ownership rules are strict in many states, and a few firms work mostly in this market.

  1. ByrdAdatto lists compliance, business structuring, management services agreements and practice sales. Per Prospyr, the American Med Spa Association (AmSpa) names it as its legal services provider, and AmSpa members get a short free consult. It offers "Access+" billing as an alternative to hourly work and does not publish prices.
  2. Lengea Law publishes annual memberships with prepaid hours: $4,788 a year (shown as $399 a month), $5,988 a year ($499 a month) with $6,000 in prepaid hours, and $11,988 a year ($999 a month) with up to $12,000 in prepaid hours for owners with 3 or more locations. It lists a 12 month minimum and a $499 one time initiation fee.

Best for: med spa, IV and wellness owners setting up or restructuring. Limits: ask which attorney licensed in your state signs off on the structure.

Flat fee contract review firms

For one employment agreement or noncompete, some firms publish a flat price. MC Law lists an employee side review and redline of a provider employment agreement at an $1,800 flat fee. Mirza Health Law lists $1,250 for the first contract and $900 for each additional one reviewed at the same time. Kelso Health Law lists a resident and fellow review at $595.

Best for: an owner hiring a provider, or a provider reviewing an offer. Limits: the price covers one document. The AAFP suggests confirming how many rounds of edits the flat fee covers and whether contacting the other side is included.

Directories and referrals

  1. AHLA, the largest nonpartisan educational group for health law, publishes a searchable AHLA Fellows directory.
  2. Board certification. The Texas Board of Legal Specialization and The Florida Bar both certify lawyers in health law and let you search certified lawyers. The Florida Bar's list showed 113 health law certified lawyers when checked.
  3. State and county medical societies and the health law section of your state or city bar, which the AAFP suggests as a starting point.
  4. Referrals from your CPA, banker or a peer practice owner who has done a similar deal.

Compliance consultants

Compliance consultants build and run programs; they do not give legal advice or represent you. Kohler Healthcare Consulting lists risk assessments, compliance and privacy program development, OIG and OCR inquiry support and interim compliance officer staffing. Compliancy Group sells compliance software with onboarding support and does not publish prices. Many practices use a consultant for the program and an attorney for legal questions and any government matter.

Fee models as published

Brewster Law Firm describes four structures.

ModelHow it worksCommon use
HourlyYou pay for time workedCompliance, contracts, licensing
Flat feeSet price for a defined scopeContract review, entity formation
RetainerDeposit drawn down against hourly workOngoing or larger matters
ContingencyPercentage of any recoveryMalpractice and injury claims

Memberships and subscriptions, like Lengea's, are a fifth model: a yearly fee for prepaid hours and templates. Ask whether expenses such as filing fees are billed at cost or with a markup, and get the estimate in the engagement letter.

Questions to ask before you hire

  1. What matters like mine have you handled in the last year? Brewster suggests asking for the last three healthcare matters in one sentence each.
  2. What share of your work is healthcare, and for practices like mine?
  3. Are you licensed in every state where we operate or plan to?
  4. Who will do the work, and at what rates?
  5. What fee model fits this matter, and what is the written estimate?
  6. What would push the fee above the estimate?
  7. How fast do you turn drafts, and how will we communicate?
  8. Who else should be at the table, such as our CPA, a valuation firm or an M&A advisor?
  9. Do you have any conflicts with the other side?

How to choose by practice type

  1. Solo or small single location practice: a state boutique or a flat fee firm for formation and contracts, plus a compliance consultant if you need a program.
  2. Med spa or aesthetics: a firm that works in your state's ownership rules every week. See the med spa page.
  3. Several locations or several entities: a firm that handles PC and MSO structures and multi state work. See the medical group page.
  4. Selling, buying in or taking investment: a transaction team, often at a national or large regional firm, working with your M&A advisor and CPA.
  5. Audit, subpoena or self disclosure: a firm with investigation experience. Do not start with a consultant.
Questions, answered

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a healthcare attorney or a general business lawyer?

Use a healthcare attorney for anything touching ownership rules, MSOs, payer contracts, referral laws or a sale. Medical Economics quotes a Dickinson Wright lawyer saying a healthcare focused lawyer can spot issues other attorneys miss. A general business lawyer can still handle a lease or a vendor contract.

How much does a healthcare attorney cost?

It depends on the matter and the firm. Published examples include $1,250 to $1,800 for one employment contract review and $4,788 to $11,988 a year for Lengea Law's memberships. Large firms usually bill hourly and do not publish rates, so ask for a written estimate.

What questions should I ask a healthcare attorney?

Ask about matters like yours, who does the work, state licensing, the fee model and a written estimate. Ask what would push the fee above that estimate. The AAFP and Brewster Law Firm both publish longer question lists.

What is the difference between a healthcare attorney and a compliance consultant?

A consultant builds and runs compliance programs, training and risk assessments. An attorney gives legal advice and can represent you. Practices often use both.

How do I check if a lawyer specializes in health law?

Look for board certification in health law where your state offers it, such as Texas or Florida. Check AHLA's Fellows directory, the lawyer's published articles and the matters they name.

When should I bring in an attorney for an MSO or PC setup?

Before you sign leases, hire or take investor money, because ownership rules shape every later document. Have your CPA model the management fee and the tax effect at the same time.
