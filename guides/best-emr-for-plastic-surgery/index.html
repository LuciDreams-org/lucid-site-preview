Short answer

For most plastic surgery practices the shortlist is Nextech, ModMed and PatientNow, with Symplast close behind for teams that want a mobile first system. All four handle cosmetic quotes, before and after photos and surgery scheduling, and all four price by quote. AestheticsPro and Aesthetic Record suit the med spa side, and AestheticsPro publishes its prices. None of them keeps your books, so plan for deposits, financing payouts and service line profit separately.

What a plastic surgery practice needs from an EMR

A plastic surgery practice runs on a long sales cycle. A patient comes in for a consult, gets a quote, pays a deposit, books an operating room date, pays the balance before surgery and returns for post-op visits. Many practices also run a med spa line with injectables, lasers, packages and memberships. A good system covers all of it without a second tool.

  1. Quotes: surgical and non-surgical quotes built from the consult, with self pay and insurance kept apart.
  2. Deposits and prepayments: card on file, deposits at booking, balances due before surgery, and financing such as CareCredit.
  3. Photos: consistent before and after images stored in the chart, with consent attached.
  4. Surgery scheduling: surgeons, rooms, anesthesia and devices on one calendar, with pre-op and post-op visits.
  5. Med spa add-ons: packages, memberships, retail and stock as software features.
  6. Reports by surgeon, service and location, with exports your accountant can use.

Nextech

Nextech sells an EHR and practice management system built for plastic surgery, and describes it as covering charting, quoting and packaging for spa and elective work. Its plastic surgery EHR covers consults, surgical planning and post-op follow-up, with patient photography built in. TouchMD, its visual consultation product, adds interactive consults and AI-powered photography.

Nextech Practice Plus brings scheduling, billing and reporting into one platform. It lists multi-provider and multi-location scheduling that coordinates providers, rooms, devices and resources. Payments are embedded, with cards, financing and pay by bank. Nextech also lists multi-tier memberships, treatment packages, inventory tracking as a software feature, commission tracking and exportable reports. Pricing is by demo.

Best for: established surgical practices with a med spa line and a strong photo and consult workflow. Watch for: features sit in several products (EHR, Practice Plus, TouchMD), so confirm what your quote includes.

ModMed (EMA)

ModMed's plastic surgery suite runs on EMA, an iPad based EMR that ModMed says was designed with practicing plastic surgeons. EMA captures up to 30 photos into the patient's chart with custom tags, and supports drawing on photos and diagrams. It offers digital surgical forms and procedure consents, and keeps surgical and med spa documentation in the same patient chart.

ModMed's quoting tool creates custom quotes in the chart with the option to select self pay or insurance. ModMed says its billing separates self pay cosmetic quotes from claims for medically necessary surgery such as breast reconstruction. It lists inventory tracking for implants and injectables, patient self scheduling and ModMed Pay, which posts payments to its practice management system. ModMed notes that Pay account setup, terminals and processing fees are not included. Pricing is not published.

Best for: hybrid practices that do both cosmetic and insurance-billed reconstructive surgery. Watch for: no public price, and payment processing costs sit outside the software fee.

PatientNow

PatientNow is built for plastic surgery, med spa and aesthetic practices. Its plastic surgery page says it connects consults, scheduling, photos, payments, charting and follow-up in one platform. It keeps consults, procedures, provider time, rooms and follow-ups on one schedule, and manages deposits, cash pay procedures, cards on file and checkout. RxPhoto, part of PatientNow, captures consistent before and after photos tied to the record with consent attached.

PatientNow says it also handles insurance workflows for surgical practices. Its pricing page shows three plans, each by quote, priced on the plan, the number of locations and add-ons.

Best for: cash pay surgical practices with a busy med spa, where deposits and checkout matter most. Watch for: insurance-heavy reconstructive practices should test claims workflows closely in the demo.

Symplast

Symplast says it was founded by surgeons, and it is built for plastic surgery and med spas, with a mobile first design. It combines patient charts, a Scheduling Suite, intake, billing, messaging and analytics, plus HIPAA-compliant before and after photos and video. Symplast says it won 2026 Best in KLAS for ambulatory plastic surgery solutions.

Symplast Payments covers cards, CareCredit, recurring billing, patient financing and online booking deposits inside the same system. Pricing is by demo.

Best for: surgeons who want to run the practice from a phone or tablet, and solo to mid size groups. Watch for: check reporting depth by surgeon and location if you run several sites.

AestheticsPro

AestheticsPro is an all-in-one system for med spas and aesthetic practices, with charting, AP Photo, online booking with deposits, payments, memberships and marketing. It publishes three plans: $160 a month with 2 users ($85 a month per extra user, up to 3), $285 a month with unlimited users, and $350 a month. The upper plans add multi-location support, and reports on revenue, providers, appointments and memberships can be filtered by location.

Best for: the med spa side of a plastic surgery practice, or a surgeon-owned med spa run as its own business. Watch for: it is built for aesthetics first, so surgery scheduling and surgical charting need a close look.

Aesthetic Record

Aesthetic Record combines booking, charting, photo management, inventory as a software feature, point of sale and ecommerce for injectors and med spas. Its pricing is per user, with a $399 startup and onboarding fee, and AR Pay handles deposits, packages, memberships and patient financing.

Best for: injector-led med spa work next to a surgical practice. Watch for: per user pricing grows with headcount, and it is not a surgical EMR.

Other names on plastic surgery shortlists include Prospyr, which puts operating room time on the same calendar as consults and injectables, plus Pabau, Zenoti and athenaOne. See the vendor's site for current pricing.

How to choose

  1. Mostly surgery, with a med spa line: Nextech, PatientNow or ModMed.
  2. A large share of reconstructive or insurance-billed surgery: ModMed or Nextech, and test claims in the demo.
  3. You want to run the practice from a phone: Symplast.
  4. The med spa is its own business or location: AestheticsPro or Aesthetic Record, or the med spa tools inside your surgical system.
  5. Several surgeons or locations: ask each vendor to show reports by surgeon, service line and location in a live demo.

Ask every vendor the same five questions. What is the total monthly cost for our surgeons, staff, locations and add-ons? How are deposits, refunds and retained deposits recorded and reported? How do financing payouts from CareCredit or other lenders show up? Can we export quotes, deposits, payments and package sales to a spreadsheet? How do card payouts reach our bank, and are processing fees shown separately?

Your EMR and your books

An EMR runs the patient journey. It does not run the finances. A quote is not revenue. A deposit is not revenue either: until the procedure happens, that money is generally owed to the patient. Financing lenders pay out less than the sale after their fees. Card payouts arrive net of processing fees, and package money arrives before treatments are delivered.

Your EMR shows quotes, deposits and payments posted. It does not show surgeon pay, anesthesia, implants, room costs or rent. So the EMR cannot tell you what each surgeon, procedure type or the med spa really earns after costs, or how much of the cash in the bank already belongs to patients. That takes books kept by service line and surgeon, with a deposits account reconciled every month.

Questions, answered

Frequently asked questions

What is the best EMR for a plastic surgery practice?

There is no single best choice. Nextech, ModMed and PatientNow are the most common shortlist, with Symplast for mobile first teams. Book demos with your own consult, quote, deposit and surgery workflow.

Which EMR works for a plastic surgery practice that also runs a med spa?

Nextech, PatientNow and Symplast all cover surgery and med spa work in one system, and ModMed keeps surgical and med spa notes in one chart. If the med spa runs as its own business, AestheticsPro or Aesthetic Record are also options.

Is Nextech or ModMed better for plastic surgery?

Both are built for the specialty. Nextech stands out for consult, photo and membership tools, while ModMed stands out for its iPad charting and for keeping self pay quotes apart from insurance claims. Test both with a reconstructive case and a cosmetic case.

Which plastic surgery EMR handles quotes and deposits?

ModMed and Nextech list quoting tools. PatientNow lists deposits, cards on file and cash pay checkout, and Symplast lists online booking deposits and CareCredit. Ask how each one reports deposits held, applied and refunded.

How much does a plastic surgery EMR cost?

Nextech, ModMed, PatientNow and Symplast price by quote. AestheticsPro lists $160, $285 and $350 a month as of November 26, 2026, and Aesthetic Record prices per user with a $399 startup fee. Add payment processing, which is usually charged separately.

Are surgery deposits revenue?

Generally not until the procedure is done. A deposit is money held for the patient, so it belongs in a liability account until surgery, and then moves to revenue. Your CPA applies the accounting rules for retained deposits and refunds.

Do plastic surgery EMRs track inventory?

Several do as a software feature, including Nextech, ModMed and Aesthetic Record for implants, injectables and products. Your books still need product and implant purchases recorded as costs.
