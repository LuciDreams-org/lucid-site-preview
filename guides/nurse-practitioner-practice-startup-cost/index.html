How much does it cost to start an NP practice?

Short answer

A lean telehealth or shared-space NP practice can open for about $3,000 to $11,000 in one-time costs, plus $5,000 to $15,000 of runway, according to 2026 guides from Kinstead and Prax Health. An office-based clinic with its own lease and buildout costs far more: buildout alone can run $30,000 to $150,000. The line most owners underestimate is working capital, because insurance pays nothing until credentialing is done and claims clear, often month four or five. In states that require it, a collaborating clinician agreement adds a monthly fee, commonly $450 to $750.

The one-time costs, line by line

Entity and legal

Most NP owners form a PLLC or professional corporation, depending on state rules. Kinstead lists state filing fees of $100 to $1,000 and optional legal help up to $1,000. Prax Health adds $750 to $2,000 for governing documents. The entity choice also sets your tax path, including whether an S corporation election makes sense later. See the S corp salary guide.

Licenses and registrations

Your NPI and EIN are free. A DEA registration for practitioners is $888 and covers three years. Some states also require a separate state controlled substance registration. If you practice in more than one state, each one needs its own license and fees.

Insurance

Kinstead puts malpractice at $500 to $1,500 a year for solo telehealth psychiatric work and $1,500 to $3,000 for in-person primary care. Add general liability and cyber coverage, which Kinstead lists at $500 to $1,500 a year.

Space, buildout and equipment

This is where budgets split. Telehealth needs no space. A sublease or shared room can be under $1,000 a month, with a deposit and first month due at signing. A clinic with its own lease is different: Crestmont Capital puts deposits, tenant improvements and buildout at $30,000 to $150,000, and describes one owner's $185,000 estimate for a 1,500 square foot clinic with two exam rooms and six months of reserves.

Basic exam equipment runs $1,000 to $2,000 per Kinstead. Procedures, labs or aesthetic devices add more. For financing devices, see the equipment financing guide.

Technology and marketing

An EHR with e-prescribing and telehealth runs about $100 to $400 a month. Controlled substance e-prescribing can cost extra. A website, branding and local listings run roughly $500 to $2,000 up front, then a few hundred dollars a month in ads.

Collaboration agreement costs

Whether you need one depends on your state. NP Hiring, citing the AANP State Practice Environment map, counts 27 states plus Washington, D.C. with full practice authority, 12 with reduced practice and 11 with restricted practice in 2026. In reduced and restricted states, NPs generally need a collaborative agreement, supervision or delegation with a collaborating clinician. Some full practice states also require a transition period of supervised hours before you practice on your own.

Prax Health puts typical collaborating fees at $450 to $750 a month, lower in some states and higher in others. Treat this as a fixed monthly cost, like rent, and put it in your break-even math. Fees can be flat or tied to revenue. Revenue-based fee structures can raise state fee-splitting questions, so have a healthcare attorney review the paperwork before you sign.

The credentialing gap

You can see patients before insurers pay you, but you cannot collect from them until you are in network. Prax Health lists commercial payer credentialing at 90 to 150 days per plan and Medicare at 60 to 90 days, with Medicaid varying by state. It puts first in-network payments around month four or five and steady collections around months six to eight. NP Hiring gives 90 to 180 days.

After that, collections still lag visits by 30 to 60 days. So the months before steady cash are paid from your reserve. Start applications as early as your state allows, ideally before you sign a lease. For how credentialing works and what services charge, see which credentialing services are worth paying for.

Working capital: the line that decides survival

Kinstead and Prax Health both suggest $5,000 to $15,000 of runway for a lean practice, and Prax adds a separate cushion of four to six months of personal living costs. An office-based clinic with rent and staff needs more, because its fixed costs are higher every month it waits on insurers.

A simple way to size it:

  1. List fixed monthly overhead: rent, collaboration fee, EHR, insurance, staff, phone, supplies and marketing.
  2. Estimate collections each month from a slow ramp, starting near zero until credentialing clears.
  3. Add up the monthly shortfalls until collections pass overhead.
  4. Add two extra months for a slower credentialing result.

Kinstead puts fixed overhead before staff at $200 to $750 a month for solo telehealth and $850 to $2,500 for in-person primary care. Staff often arrives around month six, once volume is steady.

Startup programs and going alone

Several companies sell help to NP owners. Kinstead publishes startup cost and overhead guides and helps NPs launch practices. Duet runs a live six-week Practice Launch course and offers billing, credentialing, coding and payer contracting support for NP-owned practices. Prax Health publishes business plan and practice authority guides. Elite NP sells courses for cash-pay side practices. For what each includes and costs, see the vendor's site.

Going alone means hiring your own collaborating clinician where required, a credentialing service, a biller and an accountant. A program can save time; going alone can cost less. Compare the program fee against the cost of each piece you would otherwise buy.

How to pay for it

Lean telehealth practices are often self-funded. Office-based clinics usually need a startup loan, an SBA 7(a) loan or equipment financing. Lenders want a business plan, monthly projections and your credentialing timeline. All of that is covered in how do I get a loan to start a medical practice.

Questions, answered

Frequently asked questions

How much does it cost to start an NP practice?

A lean telehealth or shared-space practice can open for about $3,000 to $11,000, plus $5,000 to $15,000 of runway, per 2026 guides. An office-based clinic with its own buildout can cost $30,000 to $150,000 or more for space alone. Working capital is the line to protect.

How much working capital does a new NP practice need?

Enough to cover fixed overhead until insurance collections pass it, plus two months for delays. For a lean practice, guides suggest $5,000 to $15,000. A clinic with rent and staff often needs several times that.

How long until insurance pays a new NP practice?

Commercial credentialing commonly takes 90 to 150 days per plan, and Medicare 60 to 90 days. First in-network payments often arrive around month four or five. Start applications before you open.

How much does a collaboration agreement cost?

Where a state requires one, Prax Health puts typical fees at $450 to $750 a month. Full practice states need none once any transition period ends. Have an attorney review fee structures tied to revenue.

Can I start an NP practice with no money?

Telehealth with cash-pay patients keeps costs very low, but you still need insurance, licenses and runway. Most lenders want some personal cash in the deal. Plan for living costs too.

Should I use a startup program like Kinstead or Duet?

A program can speed up setup, credentialing and billing. Compare its fee with the cost of buying each service yourself. Ask what happens to your payer contracts and patient records if you leave.

What monthly overhead should I expect?

Kinstead puts fixed overhead before staff at $200 to $750 a month for solo telehealth and $850 to $2,500 for in-person primary care. Staff, billing fees and collaboration fees come on top.
