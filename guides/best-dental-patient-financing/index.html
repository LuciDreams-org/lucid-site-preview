Best patient financing for dental practices?

Short answer

For most dental offices the strongest pair is Sunbit or Cherry for fast, soft check installment plans, plus CareCredit for patients who already carry the card. Add Proceed Finance or Happen Bank (formerly LendingClub Patient Solutions) for implants and full arch cases, and Alphaeon Credit as a second card. An in-house payment plan, or a membership plan through a tool such as Clerri (formerly Kleer), costs no lender fee but leaves you carrying the risk and the receivable. Whatever you choose, the lender's fee and any patient balance need their own lines in your books.

Sunbit

Sunbit leads with dental. Its dental page claims 87 percent approvals and approvals from a 500 credit score, with no hard credit check. It lists one practice fee "as low as 1.9%" that does not change with the patient's plan, and says pricing improves with your usage. Sunbit says you are paid in full the moment the patient checks out.

Best for: general and family dentistry that wants one predictable fee and broad approvals. Watch: the same page lists no interest plans of "up to 24 months" and "up to 72 months". Confirm what your patients will see in your state.

CareCredit

CareCredit is a health credit card from Synchrony, and dental is one of its largest categories. Promos of 6, 12, 18 or 24 months on $200 or more are deferred interest: if the balance is not paid in full in time, interest is charged from the purchase date. Purchase APR for new accounts is 32.99 percent. Longer plans of 24 to 48 months start at $1,000 and 60 months at $2,500, with reduced APRs of 17.90 to 20.90 percent. CareCredit Pay Later is a separate installment loan from $500.

For the practice: merchant fees are "as low as 1.9%" and depend on the promo used. You are paid within two business days, and CareCredit calls the program non recourse.

Best for: offices with many existing cardholders and repeat patients. Watch: longer no interest promos usually cost the practice more.

Cherry

Cherry offers installment plans with a soft credit check and an application of about 35 to 60 seconds. Plans run up to $65,000 over 1 to 60 months, with 0 percent APR options. Cherry claims approvals of "~90%" on one page and "80%+" on another. It has no setup or monthly fee, charges a fee per transaction set by your pricing settings, and says fees start as low as 1.7 percent. It deposits the amount less its fee by ACH and says it takes the credit risk. You handle refunds when treatment is not delivered.

Best for: offices that want a quick text link application chairside or at checkout. Watch: the fee depends on which plans you switch on.

Proceed Finance

Proceed Finance lists loans from $1,500 to $75,000, with terms up to 12 years, and says it is a subsidiary of Optum Bank. Long terms make big cases affordable month to month.

Best for: implants, full arch and large cosmetic cases. Watch: fees are not published. Ask how the fee changes with term length.

Alphaeon Credit

Alphaeon Credit is a card issued by Comenity Capital Bank, with Alphaeon Access as an installment loan for patients the card declines. It partners with dental groups, including periodontal and dental study club members. One partner page publishes practice fees for charges under $250: 3.5 percent under $500,000 a year in financing, 3.3 percent from $500,000 to $1 million, and 3.15 percent above $1 million.

Best for: a second card next to CareCredit. Watch: the published table is for one member group. Ask for your own schedule.

Happen Bank, Affirm and Wisetack

  1. Happen Bank Financing Solutions is the former LendingClub Patient Solutions. LendingClub became Happen Bank in June 2026. It describes installment loans from the bank and credit lines through Comenity Capital Bank.
  2. Affirm announced a partnership with Coast Dental in February 2025 for biweekly and monthly plans. Its healthcare fee is not published.
  3. Wisetack is built mostly for home services but lists dental. Its site shows terms of 3 to 120 months.

Membership plans and in-house payment plans

A membership plan is not financing. Patients pay a monthly or yearly fee for preventive care and discounts. Clerri, formerly Kleer, says it is the largest dental membership platform. Its site notes that membership plans are not insurance.

An in-house payment plan means the practice itself waits for the money. Most practice software can schedule installments and autopay. There is no lender fee, but you carry three costs:

  1. Default risk. If the patient stops paying, the loss is yours.
  2. Staff time. Someone has to chase late payments.
  3. Rules. Federal Truth in Lending rules can apply when a plan charges interest or runs more than four installments. Ask your attorney before you charge interest.

Many offices keep in-house plans short, ask for a deposit first, and send larger balances to a lender.

How dental financing shows up in your books

Each option leaves a different mark on your books.

  1. Lender payouts. On a $4,000 crown and bridge case at a 5 percent fee, the lender deposits $3,800. Book $4,000 of revenue, $200 as a patient financing fee expense, and match the $3,800 to the lender's statement. Booking only the $3,800 hides the fee and understates production.
  2. Prepaid treatment. Money collected before the work is done, such as a deposit on an implant case, is a liability until treatment is delivered. Revenue then follows your CPA's accounting rules.
  3. In-house plans. The unpaid balance is patient accounts receivable. Track it by age, and write off what will not be collected.
  4. Membership fees. Yearly fees paid upfront are earned over the year, not all in the month received.
  5. Refunds. A refund through a lender reverses the revenue. Check whether the lender returns its fee.

With these lines in place you can see the effective rate for each lender. Take total fees for the month and divide by financed production. That number tells you which provider really costs least.

How to choose

  1. General or family dentistry: Sunbit or Cherry first, CareCredit second.
  2. Implants and full arch: Proceed Finance or Happen Bank, with CareCredit's 60 month plan as a backup.
  3. Cosmetic dentistry: Cherry, CareCredit and Alphaeon. PatientFi also lists cosmetic dental.
  4. Many uninsured patients: a membership plan for preventive care, plus a lender for larger work. See our guide on dropping dental PPOs.
  5. Several offices: pick the same providers in every office so fees compare cleanly. Check that your practice management software supports them.

Ask every provider: What is my fee on each plan? When is money deposited? Who carries the risk if a patient stops paying? How do refunds work? Can I export each transaction with its fee?

Questions, answered

Frequently asked questions

Which dental financing approves the most patients?

Sunbit claims 87 percent approvals and Cherry claims about 90 percent, but these are vendor numbers and each measures differently. Offering two providers usually lifts your total approvals. Track your own approval rate per provider for three months.

Is Sunbit or CareCredit better for a dental practice?

Sunbit publishes one fee as low as 1.9 percent across plans and says you are paid at checkout. CareCredit's fee also starts as low as 1.9 percent but varies by promo, and many patients already have the card. Many offices offer both.

Should my dental office offer in-house payment plans?

They work for small balances and patients you know. You avoid lender fees but carry the default risk and the collection work. Keep terms short, take a deposit, and check Truth in Lending rules before charging interest.

What financing works for implants and full arch cases?

Long term lenders such as Proceed Finance, which lists loans up to $75,000 and terms up to 12 years, are built for these cases. CareCredit's 60 month plan and Cherry plans up to $65,000 can also fit.

How do I record dental financing fees in my books?

Record revenue at the full treatment price and the lender's fee as its own expense. Match the net deposit to the lender's statement. Prepaid treatment stays a liability until the work is done.

Is a dental membership plan the same as financing?

No. A membership plan is a recurring fee for preventive care and discounts. Financing pays you upfront for a specific treatment while the patient repays the lender.
