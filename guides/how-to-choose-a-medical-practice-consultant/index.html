How do I choose a practice management consultant?

Short answer

Start by naming the problem and checking your numbers, because many "we need a consultant" moments are really finance visibility problems: margin by provider, overhead creep or cash you cannot see. If the problem is operations, staffing, coding or compliance, hire the consultant type that fits it, such as MGMA Consulting for benchmark based operations reviews, Acuvance Coker for larger group strategy, KZA for surgical specialty coding and operations, or Kohler Healthcare Consulting for compliance. Physical therapy owners often start with a business coach such as NULA, Joe Sharp, PhysioGrowth or Sturdy McKee. Whoever you hire, insist on a written scope, a fixed or capped fee, references from practices like yours, and results you can measure in your profit and loss statement.

First question: consultant or better numbers?

A growing practice with slipping margins usually has one of three problems.

  1. You cannot see where the money goes. No profit by provider, location or service line, and books that close late.
  2. You can see it, but operations leak it. Scheduling gaps, staffing levels, coding errors or denied claims.
  3. You are facing a one time decision. A new location, a partner, a buyer or a management services offer.

Problem 1 is a bookkeeping and CFO problem, and fixing it first makes any consultant cheaper, because they start from real numbers. Problems 2 and 3 are where consultants earn their fee. Read the monthly numbers every owner should review and check your overhead percentage before you sign anything.

Real firm examples

These are examples by type, not a ranking. None of them publish engagement prices on the pages we reviewed, except PhysioGrowth's community fee.

MGMA Consulting

MGMA Consulting is the consulting arm of the Medical Group Management Association, based in Englewood, Colorado. It offers an operational assessment that it compares to a tune up, and says its independent consultants average more than 30 years of healthcare leadership experience and use MGMA DataDive benchmarks. Its financial management consulting covers charge capture, billing and coding, collections and financial planning.

Best for: practices that want their numbers set against peer benchmarks.

Acuvance Coker

Acuvance Coker is the current name of Coker Group, now part of the Acuvance healthcare advisory platform. Its medical practice services include a practice performance assessment (financial performance, provider productivity, staffing, workflows, revenue cycle and benchmarks), strategy for new locations, providers and ancillaries, and an implementation focus. It says it has worked with healthcare organizations for more than 35 years.

Best for: larger independent or affiliated groups with strategy and growth questions.

KZA (Karen Zupko and Associates)

KZA was founded in 1985 by Karen Zupko and works with surgical specialties, including orthopaedics, otolaryngology and dermatology. Its services are coding and audit education, billing compliance and practice operations, with workshops and national conferences.

Best for: surgical specialty practices with coding, compliance and operations needs.

Kohler Healthcare Consulting

Kohler Healthcare Consulting is an advisory firm whose services include compliance and privacy programs, coding and documentation review, revenue integrity and revenue cycle assessment, and interim and fractional management.

Best for: practices that need a compliance program or an independent coding review.

Physical therapy business coaches

Physical therapy owners often hire a coach rather than a consulting firm. Four examples, as described on their own sites:

  1. NULA Training Systems, led by Michael Nula, offers one to one business coaching, on site workshops, site visit assessments and online courses for physical therapy practices.
  2. Joe Sharp built Sharp Physiotherapy into three cash pay clinics with a team of 15 and now coaches clinic owners on numbers, patients, margins and team.
  3. PhysioGrowth is led by two physical therapists, Brian Wolfe and Owen Campbell. It publishes a $99 a month online community, with coaching calls and courses, as seen November 26, 2026.
  4. Sturdy McKee built San Francisco Sport and Spine Physical Therapy to six locations and more than 40 staff, sold it, and now coaches practice owners one to one.

Questions to ask before you hire

  1. What exact problem will you solve, and how will we measure it in the profit and loss statement?
  2. Who will do the work, by name, and what are their credentials?
  3. Can I speak with three practices in my specialty and size that you worked with?
  4. Is the fee fixed or capped, and is payment tied to milestones?
  5. What do I get in writing: an assessment, a plan, or hands on implementation?
  6. Do you earn referral fees from any vendor, billing company, software or buyer you might recommend?
  7. How long is the engagement, and can I end it early?
  8. What data do you need, and how will you protect patient and financial data?
  9. Who owns the work product when we are done?
  10. What will my team need to do, and how many hours a week?

Compare Practice Tools sums it up: never hire without a written scope, a fixed or capped fee, three verified references and a walk away clause.

Warning signs

  1. A fee tied to "savings" the consultant identifies, with no clear baseline.
  2. A recommendation that happens to be the consultant's own product or a partner's.
  3. No named person doing the work, or a different team after the sale.
  4. No case study with real numbers from a practice like yours.
  5. A push to sign a management services or sale offer before an independent review of value.

How to choose by situation

  1. Margins slipping, numbers unclear: start with a part time CFO and clean monthly books, then decide if you still need a consultant.
  2. Scheduling, staffing or patient access problems: an operations consultant or an association assessment such as MGMA Consulting.
  3. Coding, documentation or compliance risk: a coding and compliance consultant such as KZA or Kohler Healthcare Consulting.
  4. A new location or major growth plan: a specialty firm such as Acuvance Coker, with your CFO modelling the cash.
  5. Physical therapy owner who is the bottleneck: a business coach.
  6. A buyer, partner or management services offer: a sell side advisor, a healthcare attorney and clean books. See how to prepare a medical practice for sale, quality of earnings reports and practice acquisition loans.
Questions, answered

Frequently asked questions

Should I hire a practice consultant if my margins are slipping?

First find out why. If you cannot see profit by provider, location or service line, fix the books and reporting first, which a part time CFO can do. If the numbers point to an operations problem, hire a consultant for that specific problem with a written scope.

How much does a medical practice consultant cost?

There is no public rate card. Third party estimates from Compare Practice Tools put independent consultants at about $150 to $500 an hour and defined projects at about $5,000 to $50,000, with fractional operations leaders at $6,000 to $18,000 a month. Get the fee shape in writing before comparing proposals.

What questions should I ask a practice consultant?

Ask what problem they will solve and how you will measure it, who does the work, for three references from practices like yours, whether the fee is fixed or capped, and whether they earn referral fees from vendors they recommend. Ask for a walk away clause.

Is a fractional CFO better than a consultant?

For a margin or cash problem, often yes, because a CFO keeps working on the numbers every month instead of delivering one report. For operations, coding or compliance problems, a consultant with that specialty is the better fit. Many practices use both, with the CFO measuring the consultant's results.

What is the difference between a consultant and a management services organization?

A consultant advises and leaves. A management services organization runs operations for an ongoing fee, often a percentage of revenue, under a long term agreement. Have an attorney and your CPA review any management services offer.

Do physical therapy practices need a consultant or a coach?

Many physical therapy owners hire a business coach, such as NULA, Joe Sharp, PhysioGrowth or Sturdy McKee, because the main constraint is often the owner's time and systems. Ask any coach for results from practices your size.

How do I measure whether a consultant worked?

Agree on a baseline before the work starts, such as collections per visit, overhead percentage, provider productivity or days in AR. Then track it monthly in your financial reports. If you cannot measure it, you cannot judge the fee.
