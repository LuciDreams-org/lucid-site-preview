Short answer

The program that pays off is the one tied to the brands your patients already ask for, because every manufacturer program rewards volume with that maker. Allergan runs Alle for patients and Allergan Partner Privileges for practices, Galderma runs ASPIRE, Evolus runs Evolus Rewards, Merz runs Xperience+ and Revance runs EDGE for practices and REVA for patients. Practice side savings come as tier pricing, rebates or reward credits, while patient points are mostly funded by the maker. Judge each one on your net cost per unit or syringe after every discount and credit, and book those savings as a lower product cost, not as extra income.

How these programs work, in plain terms

Every program has two halves.

  1. The practice half. You buy product. The more you buy from one maker, the higher your tier, and the lower your net price or the bigger your rebate or credit.
  2. The patient half. Patients join an app, earn points or instant savings on treatments, and come back to a practice that accepts the program.

The practice half drives your product cost. The patient half drives retention, and in some programs the practice gives the discount at checkout. That difference matters for your margin and for your books.

The five programs

1. Allergan Aesthetics: Allergan Partner Privileges and Alle

Allergan splits the program in two. Allergan Partner Privileges (APP) is what Allergan calls its customer portfolio loyalty program for practices. Alle is the patient app.

On August 11, 2026, Allergan announced an APP refresh. Per the AbbVie press release, it streamlines brand tiers, adds a simpler pricing discount structure, counts more Allergan products toward each tier and shows net pricing at the time of ordering. New portfolio levels reward purchase volume across the portfolio. Allergan also added a BOTOX Cosmetic Growth Benefit: a quarterly rebate on eligible BOTOX Cosmetic purchases for practices with qualifying quarterly growth.

On the patient side, Alle members earn points on Allergan brands and other eligible in-office treatments. Alle publishes eligible products, point amounts and earning caps on its points and earning page, last updated January 6, 2026. Under the consumer terms, points have no cash value and cannot be transferred.

Best for: practices with heavy BOTOX Cosmetic and JUVEDERM volume, since Allergan's portfolio is the broadest of the five.

Limit: Allergan does not publish tier thresholds or discount percentages. You see your own position in the portal.

2. Galderma: ASPIRE

ASPIRE Galderma Rewards covers Dysport, the Restylane family, Sculptra and partner brands. The patient FAQ says 100 ASPIRE points equal a $10 reward, Dysport treatments earn 200 points per visit and Restylane earns 200 points per syringe, up to 600 points per product per visit. Treatments must fall within recommended frequency guidelines to earn points.

ASPIRE Galderma Practice Rewards is the practice program. Galderma describes it as business-building tools for the practice and says terms apply on enrollment.

Best for: practices that lean on Dysport and Restylane, or offer Sculptra.

Limit: practice tier rules are not published on the public site. Ask your rep for them in writing.

3. Evolus: Evolus Rewards

Evolus publishes the most about its mechanics. Per its Provider Rewards page:

  1. Patients get at least $40 off Jeuveau, once every 90 days, and $40 off Evolysse every 6 months.
  2. The practice earns 1 reward credit for every $40 patient check in, toward a reward vial or reward syringe on a later Evolus order.
  3. To opt in, the practice must buy at least 10 vials, or 10 syringes for Evolysse, within a quarter.

Read the order of events. Patients save at checkout, and the practice earns its reward later in product, not cash. Evolus's practice terms say rewards have no independent monetary value and are earned only once the patient benefit is properly redeemed.

Best for: practices that want a simple, predictable toxin program and can track check ins well.

Limit: the reward depends on scheduling and check in discipline. A missed check in is a missed credit.

4. Merz Aesthetics: Xperience+

Merz says Xperience+ combines pricing, discounts and patient rewards in one program. Per the Xeomin professional page, every dollar spent counts toward your tier, higher tiers bring bigger immediate discounts, and rebate savings are applied every 6 months. Merz's terms of sale (September 15, 2026) say purchases in each six month period, starting January 1 and July 1, set the practice's level.

Best for: practices that use Xeomin and want discounts and rebates in one portal.

Limit: Merz treats level thresholds, discounts and rebate credits as confidential, so compare your own quote.

5. Revance: EDGE and REVA

Revance publishes its practice credit rates. The EDGE page lists five levels by qualifying purchases, from Partner at 0.50% back in redeemable credits up to Elite at $120,000 and up with 1.50%. Revance says higher levels bring preferred pricing. Several EDGE benefits, such as marketing support and events, were marked coming soon.

REVA is the patient points program for Daxxify and the RHA Collection.

Best for: practices that use Daxxify or RHA and want published, simple credit rates.

Limit: the credit percentages are small next to tier pricing, so the price per unit at your level matters more.

Other programs

Other makers, such as Hugel for Letybo, run their own offers. Ask any rep for the same three things: price per unit at your volume, how rebates or credits are paid, and who funds the patient discount.

Which one pays off: the math that decides it

Points programs look alike to patients. For you, three numbers decide it:

  1. Net cost per unit or syringe after tier price, rebates and credits.
  2. Who pays the patient discount: the maker or you at checkout.
  3. How much volume you would move to reach the next tier.

Illustrative example, not a quote

Every number below is made up to show the math. None is a real price, tier or rebate from any maker.

A med spa buys $150,000 of toxin and filler a year. It can split purchases between two makers or move most of it to one.

ItemSplit two waysMostly one maker
Yearly product purchases$150,000$150,000
Tier discount4%8%

Now add the patient side. If moving brands makes some loyal patients leave, $7,500 can vanish fast. In this made-up case, losing 12 patients who spend $700 a year costs more than the saving. Run the numbers before you move volume.

Three takeaways:

  1. Concentration usually wins on price, but only if patients follow the brand switch.
  2. A program where you fund the patient discount needs a bigger product saving to beat one where the maker funds it.
  3. Credits paid in product only help if you will use that product before it expires.

How rebates and rewards show in the books

General information, review with your CPA. Treatment depends on each program's terms.

  1. Tier discounts. These lower what you pay, so product cost is simply lower. Nothing extra to book.
  2. Rebates and credits from the maker. These generally reduce the cost of the product you bought, not other income. A rebate earned on this year's purchases belongs to this year's product cost, even when it lands later.
  3. Free reward product. Reward vials or syringes received at no charge lower your average cost per unit for that product.
  4. Patient discounts you give at checkout. When you take $40 off the bill, your treatment revenue is the discounted price. The reward you earn later goes to product cost.
  5. Patient rewards the maker funds. If the maker reimburses you for a patient's points, the treatment is sold at full price: part paid by the patient, part due from the maker. Track what is owed until the deposit lands.
  6. Your own in-house points or packages. Points you promise patients, prepaid packages and memberships are usually not earned until the treatment happens. See membership plans for dental and med spa practices.

Why it matters: if rebates sit in other income, your injectable margin looks worse than it is, and your rebate income looks like a separate business. If maker reimbursements never get matched to deposits, you lose money quietly.

Unit level counts of toxin and filler live in your EHR or point of sale system. See best med spa software for options. The bookkeeping side is covered in best bookkeeping for med spas.

How to choose by practice type

  1. New med spa. Pick one toxin and one filler line your injectors know well, reach the first useful tier, and keep the patient app simple.
  2. Established med spa with one main brand. Ask whether moving more volume to that maker lifts you a tier. Run the example math above on your own numbers.
  3. Dermatology practice adding cosmetic work. Keep cosmetic product cost on its own line from day one. See Lucid for dermatology practices.
  4. Plastic surgery practice. Injectables often support surgical patients. Track product margin separately from surgical revenue. See Lucid for plastic surgery practices.
  5. Several locations. Check whether purchases from every site count toward one tier, and report net product cost by location.

Questions, answered

Frequently asked questions

Is Alle or Aspire better for my practice?

It depends on which brands your patients ask for. Alle covers Allergan brands such as BOTOX Cosmetic and JUVEDERM, and ASPIRE covers Galderma brands such as Dysport and Restylane. Many practices accept both. Compare your net cost per unit after tier pricing and rebates.

Do I have to join these programs to offer the products?

Usually not, but patients often expect them, and the practice tiers lower your product cost. Joining the patient side keeps loyal patients from going to a practice that accepts their points.

Who pays for the patient discount?

It depends on the program. In Evolus Rewards the practice gives $40 off at checkout and earns credits toward product later. In programs where the maker reimburses the practice for redeemed points, the maker funds it. Ask your rep and get it in writing.

Should I stay with one toxin brand to reach a higher tier?

Often it lowers your cost per unit, but only run the switch if patients will follow. Losing a few loyal patients can cost more than the tier saving.

Are manufacturer rebates income?

Generally no. Rebates and credits on product you bought usually reduce product cost rather than count as other income. Confirm the treatment with your CPA.

Do loyalty points expire?

Each program sets its own rules for patient points and practice credits. Check current terms on each maker's site and plan to use product credits before they lapse.

How do I know which program actually pays off?

Calculate the net cost per unit or syringe for each brand at your real volume, subtract any patient discount you fund, and compare margin per treatment. Recheck every time a tier or program changes.
