Short answer

For most private practices the shortlist is Phreesia, NexHealth, Luma Health, Solutionreach and Klara for booking, forms and reminders, Zocdoc when you want new patients from a marketplace, IntakeQ for low cost forms, and Doxy.me, Zoom or Updox for video visits. Phreesia and Luma Health suit larger groups that want intake, insurance checks and waitlists tied to the EHR, while NexHealth and Solutionreach fit smaller offices that want online booking and texting fast. IntakeQ, Doxy.me, Solutionreach and Zocdoc show public pricing, and most others quote. Lucid is not this software, but it is the accounting layer that shows what no-shows cost you and whether the tool pays for itself.

What to look for first

Five jobs decide most of the result.

  1. Online booking that writes to your real schedule. If the tool cannot read and write your practice management or EHR calendar, staff end up double entering.
  2. Digital intake. Forms, consents, insurance cards and photo ID collected before the visit, landing in the chart.
  3. Insurance eligibility checks. Coverage confirmed before the patient arrives, so the front desk is not on the phone with payers.
  4. Waitlist and cancellation fill. When a slot opens, the tool texts patients who want an earlier time.
  5. Telehealth. A video link that works in a browser, with a business associate agreement (BAA) signed.

Before you buy, check what your current system already includes. Many practice management systems ship booking, forms and video. See our practice management software guide first, then add a separate tool only for the gaps.

Phreesia

Phreesia sells patient intake built around check in. Its intake page describes mobile, in office and virtual visit registration, and says insured patients have eligibility and benefits checked. Its eligibility page describes automated verification to cut claim denials. Its appointment page lists self scheduling online or through a voice agent, 24/7 booking and scheduling rules by provider, location and visit type. Payments are part of the platform too.

Best for: groups that want intake, eligibility and scheduling in one flow. Limits: pricing is not published, and the value is highest when you use most of the platform.

NexHealth

NexHealth connects to the practice's EHR or practice management system through its Synchronizer. Its pricing page lists online booking, one click recalls, a waitlist, a scheduling widget and digital forms, and asks you to request a quote. Its forms page says new patients who book online can get intake forms sent automatically. Its help center says plans with campaigns include 5,000 texts per location each month.

Best for: dental and medical offices that want real time booking synced to the schedule. Limits: pricing is by quote, and fit depends on whether it integrates with your system.

Luma Health

Luma Health covers the patient from search to aftercare. Its pages list text and web scheduling that follows EHR availability and your rules, referral notifications, reminders, pre visit prep and a Smart Waitlist that offers open slots to waiting patients. Its service description lists cancellation management and rescheduling of canceled visits.

Best for: multi location groups with many referrals, cancellations and waitlists. Limits: pricing is not published. It is built for scale, so a small office may need less.

Solutionreach

Solutionreach focuses on the front office. Its platform page says it works with more than 400 practice management and EHR systems and covers scheduling, reminders, insurance verification, payments and recalls. Its pricing page shows packages and says pricing starts at $199.

Best for: small and mid size dental, vision and medical offices that want reminders and recalls without a big rollout. Limits: the full cost depends on the package and add ons, so get a written quote.

Klara (ModMed)

Klara is now part of ModMed, which bought it in 2022. Its pages list scheduling, digital intake that collects forms and insurance details before the visit, customizable appointment reminders and patient text messaging.

Best for: specialty practices, and especially practices already on ModMed. Limits: pricing is not published.

Zocdoc

Zocdoc is a marketplace first. Its help center says there are no upfront costs or subscription fees, and you pay a one time booking fee only when Zocdoc helps a new patient find one of your providers. The fee varies by specialty and location. It also offers free tools you can use with your existing patients.

Best for: practices that want more new patients, especially new locations or new providers. Limits: it is a patient acquisition cost, not just software. Track what each Zocdoc patient costs and pays, the same way you would track ads.

IntakeQ

IntakeQ publishes its prices. Its pricing page lists Forms Only at $54.90 a month and Practice Management at $84.90 a month, which adds appointment management and includes telehealth. Low volume plans start at $29.90 a month for forms. Extra practitioners cost more.

Best for: small practices, therapy groups and cash pay clinics that want forms and booking at a known price. Limits: built for smaller practices. Check the integration with your EHR before you switch.

Doxy.me

Doxy.me is a browser based video visit tool with a virtual waiting room. Its FAQ says the Free plan has no subscription charge and no trial deadline. Its own comparison page lists Premium at $35 per user a month, or $29 per user a month billed yearly.

Best for: practices that want simple video visits with no new system. Limits: it is video first. Booking and intake live elsewhere.

Zoom

Zoom offers a business associate agreement for healthcare customers, described in its support center. Many practices already know the app, which shortens training.

Best for: practices that already use Zoom for meetings and want one video tool. Limits: confirm you are on a plan covered by the BAA, and that settings such as recording and waiting rooms are set for patient visits.

Updox

Updox bundles patient texting, video chat and HIPAA compliant eFax. Its pricing page lets you build a plan and asks for a demo.

Best for: practices that want texting, video and fax under one login. Limits: pricing is not published.

How to choose

  1. Solo location, small staff: start with what your practice management system already does, then add Solutionreach, NexHealth or IntakeQ for the gaps.
  2. Multi location group: shortlist Phreesia and Luma Health, and ask both for references at your size.
  3. You need new patients: test Zocdoc on one location for a few months and compare its cost per paid patient with your ads.
  4. Behavioral health and therapy groups: IntakeQ and Doxy.me are low cost. See also our behavioral health EHR guide.
  5. Specialty practices on ModMed: Klara is the natural first look.

Ask every vendor the same five questions. Does it read and write my live schedule? Do forms land in the chart or as a PDF? Does it check eligibility before the visit? How does the waitlist fill a canceled slot? Will you sign a BAA?

What a no-show costs

A no-show is lost revenue plus staff time you still pay for. A simple way to price it is below.

  1. Find your average collected revenue per visit, by visit type.
  2. Count no-shows and late cancellations per month.
  3. Multiply. That is the monthly revenue gap.
  4. Subtract what you collect in no-show or late cancellation fees, if you charge them.

An example with round numbers: 60 no-shows a month at $150 collected per visit is a $9,000 monthly gap. If the waitlist refills a third of those slots, that is about $3,000 a month back. Compare that with what the software costs. If you charge cancellation fees, keep them as their own revenue line in the books, so you can see what they bring in and how often you waive them.

Questions, answered

Frequently asked questions

What is the best patient scheduling software for a small practice?

There is no single best choice. Start with what your practice management system includes. For gaps, Solutionreach and NexHealth add booking, reminders and recalls, and IntakeQ publishes low cost plans for forms and booking.

What is the difference between scheduling and intake software?

Scheduling software lets patients book and get reminders. Intake software collects forms, consents, insurance cards and ID before the visit. Phreesia, Klara, NexHealth and IntakeQ cover both.

Does intake software check insurance?

Some do. Phreesia and Solutionreach describe insurance eligibility verification, and Klara collects insurance details before the visit. Ask whether checks run automatically before every visit.

How do I fill last minute cancellations?

Use a waitlist that texts patients when a slot opens. Luma Health and NexHealth both list a waitlist. Measure how many canceled slots get refilled each month.

Is Zocdoc worth it?

It depends on what a new patient is worth to you. Zocdoc has no subscription and charges a booking fee for each new patient it brings, so track cost per paid patient like any other marketing channel. Our medical marketing agency guide shows how to measure it.

What is the cheapest HIPAA safe telehealth option?

Doxy.me has a free plan, and its Premium plan is listed at $35 per user a month. Whatever you pick, get a signed BAA before patient information moves through the tool.

Does scheduling software take patient payments?

Several do, including Phreesia and Solutionreach. For a full comparison of payment tools, see our patient payment software guide.
