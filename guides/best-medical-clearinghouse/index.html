Short answer

For a small or mid-size practice, Claim.MD, Office Ally and ClaimRev are the clearest on price: Claim.MD lists an unlimited plan at $120 a month, ClaimRev lists $45 per provider a month, and Office Ally's Service Center starts free with transaction fees. Availity Essentials lets you submit to its sponsoring payers for free and is worth having either way. Larger groups and hospitals usually choose Waystar, TriZetto Provider Solutions, Optum or Inovalon for deeper payer connections and denial tools, priced by quote. Pick on three things: whether your payers are connected, whether ERAs flow back into your system, and how eligibility checks are billed.

What a clearinghouse does

A clearinghouse sits between your billing software and the payers. It does four jobs:

  1. Claims. It checks each claim for errors, converts it to the standard format (837) and sends it to the right payer.
  2. ERAs. It returns electronic remittance advice (835 files), the payer's line by line explanation of what was paid, adjusted or denied.
  3. Eligibility. It checks a patient's coverage and benefits (270 and 271 transactions) before the visit.
  4. Claim status. It tells you where a claim sits with the payer (276 and 277).

Most practice management systems and EHRs come with a default clearinghouse. You can often switch or add one. If you are choosing the full billing platform, read our guide to the best RCM software for medical practices. This guide covers only the clearinghouse layer.

Clearinghouses with published prices

Claim.MD

Claim.MD publishes three plans:

  1. Basic: $30 a month, then $0.30 per claim, ERA or eligibility check.
  2. Small Volume: $60 a month with 100 claims, ERAs and eligibility checks, then $0.50 each.
  3. Unlimited: $120 a month with unlimited claims and ERAs and 1,000 eligibility checks a month, then $0.02 or $0.10 per extra check depending on the payer.

There is no setup fee and no per provider fee on any plan. A full payer list is on its site.

Best for: busy practices that want one predictable monthly bill, since the unlimited plan does not grow with providers.

Office Ally

Office Ally's Service Center is a web portal for claims, eligibility, claim status, attachments and ERAs. Its pricing page lists Service Center as "Start for Free", with transaction fees that may apply. Eligibility checks are $10 a month for the first 100, then $0.10 each, and claim attachments are $0.55 each. Office Ally says its EDI clearinghouse connects to all payers and that over 80,000 organizations use it. Its data sheet splits payers into par and non-par, and fees apply to some payers, so check the payer list for yours.

Best for: small practices that want a low cost portal, as long as their main payers are on the par list.

ClaimRev

ClaimRev charges per active rendering provider each month: $45 for 1 to 5 providers, $40 for 6 to 10 and $35 for 11 or more. Each plan includes 1,000 transactions per provider a month (claims plus ERAs), with no setup fee. Eligibility checks are an add-on priced by volume. ClaimRev lists 800+ payer connections and free ERA enrollment for payers you submit claims to. Users of the Healthie EHR have a separate plan at $50 per provider for 1 to 5 providers.

Best for: small groups that want a flat per provider price with ERAs included.

Availity

Availity Essentials is a multi-payer portal sponsored by health plans. Availity says providers can submit to payers on Essentials for free. Essentials covers eligibility and benefits, claim submission, claim status and remittance viewing, and Availity cites 3 million credentialed providers. Availity Essentials Plus is a monthly subscription that adds eligibility and claims for payers that do not sponsor Essentials; the price is not published.

Many plans use Availity as their main provider portal, so most practices register even if they use another clearinghouse for claims.

Best for: every practice as a second door to its largest payers, and small practices whose payers all sponsor Essentials.

Enterprise clearinghouses (priced by quote)

Waystar

Waystar is a full claims and payments platform with clearinghouse functions, eligibility, claim status, denial management and remit matching. It sells packages from Starter to Premium. Its site cites 5,000+ commercial, Medicaid and Medicare connections. Its payer list showed 5,096 active payers for professional claims, 3,345 for remits and 1,171 for eligibility as of March 18, 2026. Pricing is not published.

Best for: groups and health systems that want claim editing, denials and remits in one platform.

TriZetto Provider Solutions (Cognizant)

TriZetto Provider Solutions lists 11,000+ payer connections and partnerships with 650+ practice management vendors. It covers claims, eligibility, denials and patient payments. Its product sheet lists eligibility connections to more than 1,100 payers. Pricing is not published.

Best for: practices whose PM system already partners with TriZetto.

Optum (Change Healthcare)

Optum runs the former Change Healthcare medical network. It offers claims, eligibility, claim status, attachments and ERAs, including through APIs that many software vendors build on. Optum describes a broad national payer network. Pricing is not published.

Best for: groups and software vendors that want API access to a national network.

Inovalon

Inovalon's provider cloud covers claims management, all-payer eligibility and coverage discovery. It cites real-time eligibility across 2,300+ payers. A payer ID lookup by transaction type is on its site. Pricing is not published.

Best for: groups and facilities that want strong eligibility and coverage discovery tools.

Experian Health

Experian Health offers ClaimSource claims management, a claim scrubber, enhanced claim status and eligibility verification with coverage discovery. Payer lists are on its site. Pricing is not published.

Best for: hospitals and larger groups that also use Experian for patient access.

How to choose

  1. Solo or small practice, mostly commercial payers. Price Claim.MD, Office Ally and ClaimRev against your monthly claim volume. Register for Availity Essentials too.
  2. Growing group with several providers. Per provider pricing (ClaimRev) gets more expensive as you hire; a flat plan (Claim.MD Unlimited) does not. Run both against next year's headcount.
  3. Several locations or a hospital outpatient setting. Ask Waystar, TriZetto, Optum and Inovalon for quotes and a payer by payer match against your top 20 payers. See our medical group page.
  4. You outsource billing. Your billing company usually chooses the clearinghouse. Ask which one, and ask for ERA access in your name. See best medical billing companies and what a billing company costs.
  5. Keep a backup. A second clearinghouse connection, even a low cost one, means claims and ERAs keep moving if your main one has an outage.

Questions to ask before you sign:

  1. Are my top payers connected for claims, ERAs and eligibility, or only some?
  2. Which payers carry extra fees?
  3. How long does ERA enrollment take for each payer?
  4. Does the clearinghouse work with my practice management system?
  5. Can I export ERAs and reports if I leave?

Questions, answered

Frequently asked questions

How much does a medical clearinghouse cost?

Published prices range from free to a few hundred dollars a month for a small practice. Claim.MD lists $30, $60 and $120 monthly plans, ClaimRev lists $45 per provider a month for small groups, and Office Ally's Service Center starts free with transaction fees. Enterprise clearinghouses quote by volume. Prices as listed November 26, 2026.

Is Availity free for providers?

Availity says providers can submit to payers on Availity Essentials for free. Those are payers that sponsor the portal. Essentials Plus, which adds other payers, is a paid monthly subscription.

What is an ERA and why does it matter?

An ERA (electronic remittance advice, or 835) is the payer's electronic explanation of each payment: what was billed, allowed, paid, adjusted and denied. It lets your billing system post payments automatically and lets your bookkeeper match each bank deposit to the claims it paid.

Can I use more than one clearinghouse?

Yes. Many practices use one for claims and keep Availity or a second clearinghouse as a backup. Each payer sends ERAs to only one destination at a time, so plan ERA enrollment carefully.

How long does it take to switch clearinghouses?

Claims can often move within days, but ERA and EFT enrollment is done payer by payer and can take weeks. Run the old and new connections side by side until every payer's ERAs arrive at the new one.

Do I need a clearinghouse if I use a billing company?

You still use one, but the billing company usually chooses and pays for it. Make sure you can see the ERAs and claim reports yourself.

Is a clearinghouse the same as RCM software?

No. A clearinghouse moves claims, ERAs and eligibility checks between you and payers. RCM software runs the whole billing workflow, often with a clearinghouse built in. See the best RCM software for medical practices.
