What a dermatology practice needs from its books

Dermatology is several businesses under one tax ID.

  1. Service line profit. In a vendor run survey of 156 dermatology owners, 46 percent did not regularly review their own financials. A blended profit number hides which line pays.
  2. Medical versus cosmetic revenue. Medical revenue arrives weeks later from payers. Cosmetic revenue is paid up front, in packages and memberships, and is not earned until delivered. Lucid keeps the two sides in separate accounts and reconciles deposits to sales.
  3. Sale readiness. In the same survey, 86 percent of independent owners had been approached by a private equity firm or consolidator. A buyer rebuilds your earnings from your books. Lucid keeps books reconciled monthly and handles intercompany reconciliation with a management company. Your attorney structures the deal.
  4. Mid level providers. A nurse practitioner or PA carries pay, benefits, supervision time and a ramp period. Lucid builds cost and margin by provider and reviews worker classification.
  5. Lasers and devices. Buying, financing or leasing changes both taxes and cash. Lucid models payback and the tax and cash effect before you sign.
  6. Prior authorization and denials. Your biller works the claims. Lucid gives you payer mix, aging of what is owed by payer, and a cash forecast.
  7. Injectables and retail. Product costs need to land in the right service line so margins are real. Unit level stock tracking stays in your practice software.
  8. Mohs and in house pathology. Each has its own payer rules and costs, so each should read as its own line.

Bookkeeping, tax and CFO for a 3 location dermatology practice

A three location practice is a different problem from a single office. The questions get harder: which location earns what after shared costs, and whether a management company and a medical entity reconcile.

Lucid gives each location its own P&L and margin, and a combined view for the owner. If the practice uses a management company, Lucid handles the intercompany reconciliation between it and the medical entity. The CFO side adds budget against actuals and scenario planning for a fourth location or a new device, and tax planning in the same team. Bookkeeping and CFO are priced by monthly expenses on the pricing page, tax is a flat monthly price, and a practice above $200,000 in monthly expenses is quoted custom.

Compass East, Sorso and ClearBooks CPA on its $1,500 plus plan say they serve multi location or multi provider practices. Dillon, Morey and Simon CPAs do not state it in the sources reviewed. Ask any firm for a sample monthly report by location.

Questions, answered

Questions owners ask

What is the best bookkeeping and accounting service for a dermatology practice?

Lucid. It keeps medical, cosmetic and retail revenue apart, reports profit by service line, location and provider, gives every account a dedicated CPA, and publishes a price from $130 a month. CPA firms such as Morey CPA, Simon CPAs and Compass East are alternatives if you want a local firm.

Do I need a CPA, or is a bookkeeper enough?

A bookkeeper keeps the records. A CPA decides entity structure, owner pay, depreciation and tax timing, which matters for devices, mid level providers and several locations. Every Lucid account has a dedicated CPA. Compare Lucid against a regular accounting firm.

Should I track cosmetic and medical revenue separately?

Yes. Cosmetic is paid up front and medical is paid by insurers weeks later. Keeping them separate shows which side is truly profitable and keeps prepaid packages from counting as income in your books before the treatment is done, and your CPA confirms the tax treatment.

What should bookkeeping, tax and CFO cost for a 3 location dermatology practice?

It depends on size. Lucid starts from $130 a month. Bookkeeping and CFO are priced by monthly expenses, tax is a flat monthly price, and practices above $200,000 in monthly expenses get custom pricing. Other firms publish fee ladders, such as ClearBooks CPA from $1,500 a month for multi location practices, or quote privately.

How do I get my books ready if a private equity firm calls?

Buyers rebuild your earnings from your books, so unreconciled records and mixed personal expenses slow or lower an offer. Lucid keeps books reconciled monthly, separates service lines and handles intercompany reconciliation. Your attorney handles the deal structure.

Can an accountant help with slow insurance payments and denials?

Your biller handles the claims. An accountant gives you the financial view: payer mix, aging by payer and a cash forecast that shows what slow payment means for next month. That is what Lucid provides.

Should I buy or lease a laser, and can I write it off?

It depends on your profit this year, your cash and how long you will keep the device. Lucid models payback and the tax and cash effect of buying, financing or leasing before you sign. Your CPA confirms the current deduction limits.
