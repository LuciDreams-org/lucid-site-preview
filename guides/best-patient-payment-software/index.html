Short answer

For most private practices the shortlist is Rectangle Health, Phreesia, Weave, InstaMed, Clearwave, PatientPay and Inbox Health, with Cedar for larger groups and Stripe or Square as plain processors. Rectangle Health and Weave suit small offices that want text to pay and card on file fast, Phreesia and Clearwave tie payments to intake and eligibility, and PatientPay, Inbox Health and InstaMed focus on statements and balances after the visit. Only Rectangle Health, Weave, Stripe and Square publish a starting price, so ask every vendor for the full rate on your own card volume. Lucid is not payment software, but it is the accounting layer that reconciles these payouts and reports what patients still owe.

What to look for first

Four features decide most of the result.

  1. Text to pay. A payment link sent by text from your office number. Most vendors on this list offer it.
  2. Card on file. The patient saves a card, with consent, and you charge the balance after the claim settles. This cuts statement runs.
  3. Payment plans. Automatic charges on a schedule, set up by staff or by the patient.
  4. Posting back to your practice management system. If payments do not post automatically, staff key them in by hand and errors follow.

Then ask about fees: the card rate, any monthly platform fee, terminal costs and whether the vendor supports surcharging.

Rectangle Health

Rectangle Health sells Bridge Payments as part of its Practice Management Bridge platform. Its pages describe text to pay, bulk text to pay for sending many balance requests at once, digital wallets and automatic payment posting, and call the software HIPAA and PCI compliant. Its pricing page lists Bridge Payments plans from $40 a month and card rates from 1.99 percent. Its FAQ says it serves more than 37,000 healthcare providers, including dental and medical practices.

Best for: small offices that want a published starting price and text to pay. Limits: the rate you pay depends on plan and card mix. Get a written quote at your volume.

Phreesia

Phreesia builds payments into patient intake. Its revenue cycle page lists credit, debit, FSA and HSA cards, Apple Pay, Google Pay, payment plans, card on file and online payments. It says it can charge the card on file once insurance adjudicates, sends text or email payment prompts, and offers itemized statements. It also says its practice management integrations automate end of day reconciliation.

Best for: practices that want check in, eligibility and payments in one tool. Limits: pricing is not published, and the value is highest if you also use its intake.

Weave

Weave combines phones, texting, reviews and payments. Its payments page lists text to pay, online bill pay, payment plans, a wireless terminal, tap to pay, card on file, ACH, Affirm and Sunbit financing, plus surcharging and bulk collections texts for patients with balances. It describes flat rate pricing with no upfront or recurring fees for payments. Its pricing page says plans start from $199 a month.

Best for: dental, optometry and small offices that also want a phone and texting system. Limits: the payment rate itself is not on the public page. Some bulk collection features name specific dental systems, so confirm support for yours.

InstaMed

InstaMed is a wholly owned subsidiary of JPMorgan Chase Bank. Its provider billing page lists digital first statements, text to pay, QR codes, a Pay Over Time option for payment plans, a digital wallet that saves payment methods to automate payments, and Apple Pay and Google Pay. It says it is HIPAA compliant and a PCI Level One service provider, and that it integrates with EHR and practice management systems.

Best for: practices that want a large, bank backed processor with statements included. Limits: pricing is not published.

Clearwave

Clearwave sells patient engagement software, with a Payments Agent that collects copays at pre registration or at the visit. Its pages list past due balances, verified estimates, card on file with patient consent, EMV chip, tap to pay, Apple Pay, Google Pay, text to pay and compliant surcharging. It also offers scheduling, eligibility and kiosks.

Best for: specialty practices that want to collect before or at the visit, tied to eligibility. Limits: pricing is not published, and it is a full engagement platform, not just a payment tool.

PatientPay

PatientPay focuses on what happens after the visit. Its site describes branded statements by text, email and mail, custom payment plans, real time balances, autopay and a digital wallet that follows the patient across participating providers. It says payments post back to your system automatically.

Best for: groups that want to move from paper statements to digital billing. Limits: pricing is not published. Results figures on its site are vendor claims.

Inbox Health

Inbox Health runs patient billing and support for practices and billing companies. Its site says billing messages go out by email, text, paper mail or voice, with follow up that adapts to patient behavior. Patients can pay by card, digital wallet, ACH, phone or check, and payment plans need no staff work. It also offers HIPAA compliant live chat and AI assisted patient support, including 24/7 responses for common questions.

Best for: practices that want to hand off patient billing questions as well as payments. Limits: pricing is not published.

Cedar

Cedar sells Cedar Pay, a patient payment platform used by health systems, hospitals and large provider groups. Its pages list auto pay and promise to pay plans, AI bill summaries, discounts offered by likelihood to pay, and checkout by card, ACH, Apple Pay and Stripe Link.

Best for: large groups with high statement volume. Limits: built for scale, so a small practice may be more than it needs. Pricing is not published.

Stripe and Square

General processors work for cash pay clinics such as med spas or concierge practices. Stripe's US pricing page lists 2.9 percent plus 30 cents for online cards. Square's pricing page lists 2.6 percent plus 15 cents for in person payments and 3.3 percent plus 30 cents online.

Best for: cash pay clinics with simple needs. Limits: neither is built around insurance balances, statements or posting to your practice system. Check that a BAA is available before patient health information touches the tool.

How to choose

  1. Small office, mostly copays and small balances: start with Rectangle Health or Weave and compare written quotes.
  2. You want payments at check in: look at Phreesia or Clearwave.
  3. Paper statements are your biggest cost: compare PatientPay, InstaMed and Inbox Health.
  4. Large group with many locations: add Cedar and InstaMed to the list.
  5. Cash pay clinic: a general processor such as Stripe or Square can be enough, if it fits your privacy needs.

Ask every vendor the same five questions. What is my all in card rate at my volume? Is there a monthly fee? Does it post to my practice management system? Can I keep a card on file and run plans? What does the payout report show, gross or net of fees?

Patient payments and your books

Payment software moves money. Your books have to show what that money means.

  1. Gross versus net. Most processors deposit the payment minus fees. If the books record only the deposit, revenue is understated and fees are hidden.
  2. Batched deposits. One deposit can hold dozens of payments from several days. Without a monthly reconciliation, your patient payments report and your bank never match.
  3. Timing. Card payments taken on Friday may land on Tuesday. Month end cutoffs need care.
  4. Patient AR. Balances still owed by patients belong on an aging report, by age bucket, so you see what is slipping past 60 and 90 days.
  5. Refunds, chargebacks and surcharges. Each needs a clear home in the ledger.
Questions, answered

Frequently asked questions

What is the best patient payment software for a small practice?

There is no single best choice. Rectangle Health and Weave publish starting prices and offer text to pay. Phreesia and Clearwave fit if you want payments tied to intake. Compare written quotes at your own card volume.

What is text to pay?

It is a payment link sent to the patient by text. The patient pays on a phone without logging in to a portal. Most tools on this list offer it.

Should my practice keep a card on file?

Many practices do, with written patient consent. Phreesia and Clearwave describe charging the card once the claim settles. It cuts statement runs and follow up calls.

Can I pass credit card fees on to patients?

Some vendors, such as Weave and Clearwave, offer surcharging tools. Card network and state rules apply, so check them with your processor before you start.

What do patient payment tools cost?

Rectangle Health lists plans from $40 a month and card rates from 1.99 percent. Weave lists platform plans from $199 a month. Most others price by quote, so ask for the total cost, not only the card rate.

Why do my card deposits not match my payment reports?

Deposits are net of fees and often batch several days of payments. Refunds and chargebacks also come out of payouts. A monthly reconciliation closes the gap.

Does payment software replace billing?

No. Payment tools collect what patients owe. Claims to insurers still need billing software or a billing company. See our RCM software guide.
