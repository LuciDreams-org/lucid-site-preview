Who is buying med spas, and for what multiple?

Short answer

Most med spa buyers are private equity backed aesthetics platforms such as Advanced MedAesthetic Partners, Alpha Aesthetics Partners, Empower Aesthetics, Princeton Medspa Partners, MedSpa Partners and Aesthetic Partners, alongside large chains like SkinSpirit and LaserAway, plastic surgery platforms and individual buyers. Published 2025 ranges from the American Med Spa Association and Xite run from about 3 to 6 times EBITDA for med spas under $4 million of revenue to 7 to 12 times for regional platforms above $20 million. A small single site is usually priced on SDE, at about 2 to 4 times per CT Acquisitions (June 2026). Most deals pay 60% to 80% cash at closing with the rest in rollover equity, so compare the full structure, not only the multiple.

The market in numbers

Med spa buying is active but early. Olympic M&A's Q3 2026 report (September 2026), citing HealthFMV and Scope Research, counts more than 50 announced aesthetics deals in each of 2023 and 2024, and 42 in 2025. Private owner to owner sales are mostly unreported, so real volume is higher.

The same report says more than 90% of US med spas are still independently owned, and that six of the ten most recent deals it tracked were by sponsor backed platforms. Florida, Texas and California remain the busiest states, with platforms now expanding into secondary metros.

Strategic money is arriving too. L'Oreal holds a 36.13% stake in SkinSpirit, per CEW's November 18, 2026 news roundup, and Beauty Independent (November 16, 2026) reported its stakes in med spa operators in the US and China.

The buyers, one by one

Advanced MedAesthetic Partners (AMP). Leon Capital Group lists AMP as an operating subsidiary. Prospyr (October 2, 2026) reports AMP's site shows 20 plus brands, 77 locations and 21 states, with partners including EVER/BODY. AMP describes three tiers, from vendor access with no equity transfer to growth capital and a full joint venture, and says each brand keeps its name and clinical autonomy.

Alpha Aesthetics Partners. Backed by Thurston Group, Alpha reported 36 locations across 12 states in Olympic M&A's September 2026 report, with recent partnerships in Colorado, California and Massachusetts. Per Prospyr, Alpha pays owners upfront cash and makes them owners in Alpha, while its central team covers finance, HR, IT and marketing.

Empower Aesthetics. Shore Capital backed, described by Olympic M&A as a national platform built through practitioner led add ons, including a Waco, Texas practice listed among its recent deals.

Princeton Medspa Partners. Backed by Princeton Equity Group. Prospyr reports a $120 million growth financing in 2024 and acquisitions in Kansas, New York, North Dakota and Alabama, with centralized marketing, purchasing, recruiting, HR and accounting.

MedSpa Partners. An acquirer of aesthetics practices across the US and Canada. Prospyr notes its published process: NDA, basic financials, normalized EBITDA, a clinic visit, a letter of intent, diligence including a third party quality of earnings review, then closing. It names EBITDA, growth rate and operational efficiency as the main price drivers.

Aesthetic Partners. Miami based, backed by Norwest Venture Partners per Olympic M&A. Per Prospyr, it works with founder led plastic surgery, dermatology and med spa brands, lets founders roll equity, and usually asks partnering surgeons and providers to commit to at least two years.

SkinSpirit. KKR took a minority stake in October 2022, and L'Oreal now holds a stake as well. SkinSpirit said it opened seven new clinics in 2026 (November 12, 2026 release). It grows mostly by opening clinics, which makes it a reference point for valuations more than a frequent buyer.

LaserAway. Backed by Ares Management and Seidler Equity Partners, with 219 locations per Reuters (June 4, 2026). CT Acquisitions lists it among active buyers, alongside Ideal Image (L Catterton, with TPG investing in 2021).

Other active platforms. Olympic M&A also lists Olympus Cosmetic Group (VSS, now in nine states), Inspire Aesthetics (Hidden Harbor), AYA Medical Spa (Eagle Merchant Partners), Vos Collection (Persistence Capital Partners), MD Esthetics (New Harbor Capital), Elase Med Spas (Imperial Capital), Skin and Aesthetic Centers (Abacus Investments) and Formula Wellness (Trive Capital). Prospyr adds Spa Medicca, which reports 60 to 90 days from letter of intent to closing, and Aviva Aesthetics, a model where owners keep their practice equity and become shareholders in the platform.

Dermatology and plastic surgery buyers. Several of the platforms above, including AMP, Olympus and Aesthetic Partners, combine surgery, cosmetic dermatology and med spa services. For a medical director led med spa, a dermatology platform can be a natural buyer.

Individual and search fund buyers. For small single sites, the buyer is often a nurse practitioner, injector or first time owner using an SBA loan, or a search fund or family office. CT Acquisitions' 2026 multiples report cites Main Street Index data showing small owner run spas listed for sale asking about 2.92 times SDE, an asking price rather than a closing price.

How these deals are structured

Most platform deals use an MSO model. In many states the platform's management company owns the business side while a licensed medical entity owns the clinical side, with a medical director and supervision agreements. See med spa MSO vs medical director service.

Published multiples by size, with sources and dates:

ProfilePublished range
Single site under $500K SDE2x to 4x SDE (CT, Jun 2026)

Above $20M revenue7x to 12x EBITDA (AmSpa and Xite)

Olympic M&A adds that multi provider practices with recurring revenue and $1 million to $3 million of EBITDA have been benchmarked at 7 to 9 times. The AmSpa and Xite bands, published May 15, 2026 and cited by both CT Acquisitions and Olympic M&A, are set by revenue, so two spas with the same revenue but different margins sit in the same band at very different values.

On payment terms, AmSpa and Xite report 2025 deals ran from about 60% cash at closing and 40% rollover equity to 80% cash and 20% rollover, with earnouts or holdbacks on top where provider transition matters. CT Acquisitions (June 2026) adds that when the owner is the main injector and 60% or more of revenue runs through them, sellers should expect a 12 to 24 month earnout. Treat all of these as ranges, not quotes. See how a medical practice is valued.

What med spa buyers look for

Olympic M&A's report and CT Acquisitions' sell side guide list the same drivers:

  1. Recurring revenue. Neurotoxins, filler maintenance, laser series, skincare and memberships. CT Acquisitions says documented recurring revenue above 25% earns a premium.
  2. Low owner and provider concentration. Revenue spread across several productive injectors, with reasons to stay after closing.
  3. Clean adjusted EBITDA. Normalized owner pay, provider labor, marketing, device leases and add backs that hold up in diligence.
  4. Retention without discounting. Rebooking, referrals and membership penetration, not patients bought again through paid ads.
  5. Real infrastructure. Centralized finance, HR and standard procedures, not founder run clinics.
  6. A clean clinical and legal structure. Ownership, delegation, medical direction and drug sourcing that a buyer can inherit.

PitchBook (October 23, 2026) adds that buyers of cash pay healthcare look for growing same store sales, patient count and ticket size, and providers who stay.

Most of that list lives in the books: membership and package revenue recognized correctly, injectable and product costs matched to the treatments, and profit by provider, room and service. See how to prepare a medical practice for sale and what a quality of earnings report is.

How to compare offers

Olympic M&A's example shows why: two offers at 7 times on $1.5 million of EBITDA are both $10.5 million on paper, but a 60/40 split and an 80/20 split differ by $2.1 million at closing. Line offers up on the same page:

  1. Cash at closing, after fees, debt payoff and taxes.
  2. Rollover equity: how much, at what value, the platform's debt, and who controls the next sale.
  3. Earnouts and holdbacks: the targets, the time frame and who controls the numbers that decide them.
  4. Your role after the sale: title, pay, required hours, term and what happens if you leave early.
  5. Non compete radius and length.
  6. Medical director and entity ownership in your state.
  7. Your team: pay, benefits and planned staffing changes, plus which systems change in the first 90 days.

Model each offer over five years with your CPA, and have a healthcare attorney read the paperwork. CT Acquisitions says a competitive process with several buyers is worth 1 to 3 turns of EBITDA over a single bidder.

Questions, answered

Frequently asked questions

Who is buying med spas right now?

Mostly private equity backed platforms such as Advanced MedAesthetic Partners, Alpha Aesthetics Partners, Empower Aesthetics, Princeton Medspa Partners, MedSpa Partners and Aesthetic Partners. Large chains, plastic surgery platforms, strategic investors such as L'Oreal and individual buyers are also active.

What multiple do med spas sell for?

AmSpa and Xite report about 3 to 6 times EBITDA for med spas under $4 million of revenue, 5 to 8 times at $4 million to $20 million and 7 to 12 times above $20 million, for 2025 deals. Small single sites are priced on SDE, at about 2 to 4 times per CT Acquisitions.

What can a two location med spa get?

CT Acquisitions (June 2026) puts 2 to 5 site chains with $1 million to $3 million of EBITDA at 5 to 7 times EBITDA. Olympic M&A cites 7 to 9 times for multi provider practices of that size with recurring revenue. Provider depth and memberships decide where you land.

How much of the price is paid in cash?

For 2025 deals, AmSpa and Xite report 60% to 80% cash at closing, with the rest in rollover equity, sometimes with earnouts or holdbacks.

What if I am the main injector?

Expect a lower multiple and a longer earnout. CT Acquisitions suggests building an injector bench before going to market.

How do I prepare my med spa for a buyer?

Start 12 to 24 months ahead with clean monthly books, a documented add back file, membership and package revenue tracked correctly, profit by provider and service, and medical director and supervision paperwork in order.
