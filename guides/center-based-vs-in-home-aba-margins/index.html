Center-based or in-home ABA: which makes more?

Short answer

In-home ABA is cheaper to start and has almost no fixed cost, but paid drive time, mileage and late cancellations leak hours that never become a claim. A center bills more of each paid hour because nobody drives between sessions, but it adds rent, buildout and a census ramp that must be funded before it pays back. In published examples, a center only pulls ahead once it fills enough technician hours to cover its fixed cost, often somewhere around 15 to 20 or more full schedules. Payer mix decides the rest, because Medicaid, commercial plans, TRICARE and school districts pay different rates on different timelines.

A hybrid (a small center plus in-home routes) is common. It spreads fixed cost but adds the complexity of both.

Rent and buildout

A center is a real estate project before it is a clinical one. Alturascope, which surveys ABA sites, says a typical center runs 4,000 to 6,000 square feet, with larger ones at 10,000 to 12,000. It notes gross motor rooms need ceilings of about 12 feet, plus child-height restrooms, observation rooms, secure reception and often a fenced outdoor area. Those needs push up buildout cost and narrow the list of usable spaces.

Finni Health's startup model shows the scale of the gap. Its synthetic center case budgets $130,000 for deposit, setup, furniture and buildout and a funding target of $747,500 once pre-opening burn, four months of runway and a 15 percent contingency are added. Its lean home and community case needs $75,900. These are planning examples, not market prices, but the order of magnitude is the point: a center needs roughly ten times the starting cash.

Rent is also fixed. It is due whether the rooms are full at 4 pm or empty at 10 am. My ABA CFO points out that centers are often busy from 3 to 6 and quiet in the morning, because school-age clients cannot attend until mid-afternoon. An empty morning still pays rent.

To fund the buildout, owners usually combine landlord tenant improvement money, an SBA 7(a) loan and owner cash. See the best SBA lenders for medical practices and how a practice line of credit works.

Drive time and mileage

In-home work pays people to sit in traffic. Under federal wage rules (29 CFR 785.38), travel from one job site to another during the workday is part of the principal activity and counts as hours worked. So an RBT driving between two families is on the clock, and nothing bills for that time. State rules can add to this.

Mileage is a second cost. The IRS business standard mileage rate for 2026 is 72.5 cents per mile. Many agencies reimburse at or near that rate.

Finni Health's synthetic capacity test makes the leak visible. A four-technician home team with 120 paid hours a week loses 16 hours to travel and 8 to admin and training, leaving 96 planned service hours. At 82 percent completion, that is 78.7 delivered hours. The same team in a center, after 8 hours of setup and coordination, has 112 planned hours, and at 88 percent completion delivers 98.6. That is about 20 more delivered hours a week from the same payroll.

Cancellations

Cancellations hit both models, but differently. My ABA CFO gives the classic in-home case: a family cancels at 2:45, the RBT is across town, and the hour is gone. In a center, another client is often down the hall, and a scheduler can refill part of the hour.

Cancellation pay is a real cost, not just lost revenue. Acuity News, reporting a former operator's figures, shows a 35-hour RBT week at $25 an hour dropping from $875 to $750 when 5 hours cancel, a 14.3 percent pay cut for the technician. Agencies that guarantee hours to keep staff carry that cost themselves. Some states also have reporting-time pay rules, for example Massachusetts and California, that require pay when an employee reports and is sent home. A BACB exit survey cited in the same article found 34 percent of former RBTs who responded named unpredictable pay as a reason they left.

So the choice is often between eating cancellation cost in-home or reducing it with a center's denser schedule.

Staffing ratios and supervision

The BACB requires RBTs to receive ongoing supervision for at least 5 percent of the hours they spend providing behavior-analytic services each month. The BACB does not set a fixed number of RBTs per supervisor. Its handbook asks supervisors to weigh their caseload and the time supervision takes.

That rule shapes cost. In-home, a BCBA drives to each home to observe, so supervision time includes travel. In a center, one BCBA can oversee several sessions without getting in a car, which My ABA CFO lists as one of the center's main advantages. Alturascope's example of a 5,000 square foot center serving 15 to 20 children has 15 to 25 staff on site at once. Centers also need front desk and operations staff that a home-based agency may not.

Payer rules

Payers decide what a setting is worth.

  1. Setting approval. NC Medicaid's 2026 guidance says the setting (clinic, home, school, community or a mix) is chosen by the individual's goals. It also lists childcare, custodial, transportation and administrative activity as noncovered.
  2. Travel is not billable. TRICARE East accepts both home and center settings with preapproval, but travel to either location is not reimbursable.
  3. Place of service. CMS keeps a place of service code set with Home, Office and clinic values, but each payer decides which codes it accepts for ABA. Check your contracts before you open a center.
  4. Rates vary widely. ABA Digital Marketing's September 2026 review puts the national Medicaid median for CPT 97153 at $15 per 15-minute unit, with state rates from $9.90 to $30.10. Commercial rates are private contracts and are not published, so track what each payer actually pays you.
  5. Rate changes. State Medicaid programs can cut rates mid-year. Indiana, for example, reduced its maximum fees for individual ABA services in 2026.
  6. School districts. Revenant Care cites district rates of about $45 to $55 an hour in a mid-sized Southeast district and $70 to $85 in large urban districts, with 60 to 90 day net payment terms.

A center works best when its payers approve center-based care, pay a rate that covers the overhead, and pay on a timeline your reserve can carry. If most of your clients are on a low-rate Medicaid plan, the center has a thinner cushion for rent.

At 25 full schedules, an assumed $130,000 buildout pays back in a little over a year. At 10, it never does. My ABA CFO runs a similar example and finds break-even near 21 RBTs at $20,000 a month of center cost.

The lesson: a center is a volume bet. It pays when referrals, authorizations and staffing let you fill afternoon and morning slots. It loses money when rooms sit empty.

How to choose

  1. Stay in-home if you are new, cash is tight, families are spread out, or your payer mix is heavy on a low Medicaid rate.
  2. Open a center if you have a waitlist, families within a short drive, payers that approve center care, and enough cash for buildout plus several months of runway with no collections.
  3. Go hybrid if you want to test demand. A small center plus home routes lets you move clients in as they fit, but track each setting's numbers separately.
  4. Before you sign a lease, model the ramp month by month. Finni Health's funding model assumes zero collections until documented collection timing begins. That is a safe starting point.

Questions, answered

Frequently asked questions

Is center-based ABA more profitable than in-home?

It can be, once it is full. A center bills more of each paid hour because there is no drive time between sessions and cancellations can sometimes be refilled. But rent and buildout are fixed, so a half-empty center often earns less than an in-home team.

How many RBTs does an ABA center need to break even?

It depends on rent, rates and utilization. In published illustrative examples, break-even lands roughly between 15 and 21 full technician schedules. Model your own rent, payer rates and expected fill before signing a lease.

Do I have to pay RBTs for drive time?

Under federal wage rules, travel between client sites during the workday counts as hours worked. State law can add more. Drive time is not billable to most payers, so it is a cost with no claim behind it.

What does it cost to build out an ABA center?

Costs vary by market and space. Finni Health's synthetic center case budgets $130,000 for deposit, furniture and buildout and a total funding target of $747,500 including runway. Get signed quotes before you commit.

Does Medicaid pay the same for home and center ABA?

It depends on the state and plan. Many programs accept several settings when they fit the individual's goals, but each payer sets which place of service codes it accepts. Check your contracts and current fee schedule.

Are school district contracts better than insurance?

District hourly rates can be attractive, but payment terms of 60 to 90 days are common. That ties up cash. Compare the rate after your actual collection timing, not just the hourly figure.

Should I go hybrid?

Hybrid works when you have enough clients near a center to fill it and others too far away to bring in. It spreads fixed cost but you run two operating models, so keep each one's numbers separate.
