Short answer

For a private practice buying seats itself, Freed, Nabla, Heidi and Sunoh.ai are the easiest to start with, because each publishes a price or a free plan and sells to individual clinicians. Suki, Abridge, Microsoft Dragon Copilot, Ambience, DeepScribe and Commure are sold mainly on contract to groups and health systems, often with deeper EHR integration. The right pick depends on your EHR, your specialty and how many providers will use it. Lucid is not a scribe, but it helps you check whether the seats pay for themselves, by tracking software cost as a percent of collections.

What to check before you buy

  1. Note quality in your specialty. Ask for a trial with your own visits. Accuracy figures on vendor sites are vendor claims.
  2. BAA. Any scribe that hears patient visits handles protected health information. Get a signed BAA before the first recorded visit.
  3. EHR fit. A true integration writes into the chart. A browser extension or copy and paste works, but adds clicks.
  4. Data handling. Ask whether audio is stored, for how long, and whether your data trains the vendor's models.
  5. Price per seat, and who pays for seats that sit unused.

Freed

Freed sells to individual clinicians and groups. Its pricing page lists plans from $39 a month, with a Starter tier capped at 40 notes a month, a Core tier with unlimited notes and a Premier tier that adds EHR push integration, visit summaries and patient letters. Group plans add an organization wide BAA, central billing, license management and volume discounts. Freed says it is HIPAA compliant, SOC 2 Type 1 and 2 certified, and that audio is deleted once notes are complete.

Best for: small practices that want a low published price and a quick start. Limits: chart integration is a push to browser based EHRs on the higher tier, not a deep two way link.

Nabla

Nabla's help center lists an individual subscription at $119 a month per user after a 30 day free trial, with group rates by request. Its EHR page names Epic, Oracle and athenahealth among supported systems, and it publishes terms of service that include a BAA. Nabla reports 24 percent less documentation time for athenahealth users, which is a vendor claim.

Best for: practices on Epic or athenahealth that want a published monthly price. Limits: group pricing is by request.

Heidi

Heidi offers a free plan with unlimited transcription and standard templates, plus paid Clinician, Practice and Enterprise options with a 14 day trial. Its HIPAA page says Heidi is a business associate and makes a BAA available to every covered entity it works with. Paid seat prices shown in your currency are on the vendor's site.

Best for: practices that want to test a scribe at no cost before buying seats. Limits: confirm which EHRs it writes into for your setup.

Sunoh.ai

Sunoh.ai is tied to eClinicalWorks but sold widely. Its pricing page lists pricing starting at $149 per user a month for a limited time. Its home page says there is no long term commitment, implementation is included and a BAA is included. It names integrations with athenahealth, Cerner, eClinicalWorks, Epic, ModMed, Practice Fusion and Tebra, plus a browser extension for other web based EHRs. It also says it saves up to 2 hours a day on documentation, a vendor claim.

Best for: practices on eClinicalWorks or one of its named EHRs. Limits: the price is labeled limited time, so confirm what renews.

Suki

Suki lists two way EHR integrations with Epic, athenahealth, Oracle Health and MEDITECH, and says thousands of athenaOne customers use it. Its trust center lists a SOC 2 Type 2 report. Prices are not published.

Best for: groups on athenahealth or Epic that want notes written straight into the chart. Limits: pricing by quote.

Abridge

Abridge sells mainly to health systems. Its site says it is trusted by more than 300 health systems and won Best in KLAS in 2025 and 2026, and its product page describes Abridge inside Epic. It publishes BAA terms. Prices are not published.

Best for: larger groups on Epic or groups affiliated with a health system that already uses it. Limits: built for enterprise buying, so a small independent practice may not be the target.

Microsoft Dragon Copilot

Dragon Copilot is Microsoft's clinical assistant that brings together its dictation and ambient tools, including the product formerly sold as DAX Copilot. Microsoft says clinicians can use it in a web browser, a mobile app, a desktop app or embedded in supported EHRs such as Epic, and it offers a path from Dragon Medical One. Pricing is not published.

Best for: practices already using Dragon dictation that want ambient notes from the same vendor. Limits: pricing and BAA terms come through Microsoft or its partners.

Ambience

Ambience sells specialty specific ambient AI with coding support. Its site says it syncs schedules and problem lists from Epic, athenahealth and Cerner, and shows logos for other EHRs. It reports 95 percent coding compliance as verified by AAPC and a KLAS score of 97.7, both vendor claims.

Best for: groups that want documentation and coding support in one tool. Limits: aimed at larger organizations. Pricing is not published.

DeepScribe

DeepScribe focuses on specialty care, with oncology first and pages for cardiology, gastroenterology, neurology, orthopedics and urology. It adds coding support for E/M, ICD-10 and HCC. Pricing is not published.

Best for: oncology and other specialty groups. Limits: confirm EHR support and price with the vendor.

Commure Ambient

Commure offers tiered ambient products, from self service AI scribing to Ambient Assist, where human documentation specialists refine AI drafts. It says it integrates with more than 60 EHRs and links documentation to its revenue cycle tools.

Best for: practices that want a human review layer on top of AI notes. Limits: pricing is not published.

How to choose

  1. Solo clinician or small group, price first: trial Freed, Heidi and Nabla side by side.
  2. eClinicalWorks or a named Sunoh EHR: add Sunoh.ai to the trial.
  3. Group on athenahealth or Epic that wants notes in the chart: compare Suki, Nabla and Abridge.
  4. Specialty group with complex notes: look at DeepScribe and Ambience.
  5. Practice already on Dragon dictation: ask Microsoft about Dragon Copilot.

Run a two to four week trial with three or four providers, on real visits, before you buy seats for everyone.

Can a scribe pay for itself?

A scribe pays off in one of two ways. Providers see more patients per day, or they spend less time charting after hours and stay longer. The first shows up in collections. The second shows up in retention and is harder to price.

Here is a simple way to check the first. Take the monthly cost of all seats and divide by your average collections per visit. That is how many extra visits per month the practice needs to break even. For example, 10 seats at $119 a month cost $1,190. If an average visit collects $100 (an example figure, use your own), the practice needs about 12 extra visits a month across all 10 providers to cover it. Track it for three months after rollout.

Also watch software cost as a share of collections. Scribes add to EHR, billing and phone costs, and the total creeps up seat by seat.

Questions, answered

Frequently asked questions

Which AI scribe is best for a small practice?

Freed, Nabla, Heidi and Sunoh.ai sell directly to clinicians and publish a price or a free plan. Trial two or three on real visits and compare the notes. The best one is the one your providers keep using.

How much does an AI medical scribe cost per provider?

Published prices on November 26, 2026 ran from a free Heidi plan to $39 a month for Freed's entry plan, $119 a month for Nabla and from $149 a month for Sunoh.ai. Enterprise tools such as Abridge, Suki and Dragon Copilot are priced by quote.

Do AI scribes sign a BAA?

Many do. Freed offers one on group plans, Heidi and Sunoh.ai say a BAA is available or included, and Nabla and Abridge publish BAA terms. Get a signed BAA before using any scribe on patient visits.

Which AI scribe works with my EHR?

Suki lists Epic, athenahealth, Oracle Health and MEDITECH. Sunoh.ai lists seven EHRs including eClinicalWorks and Tebra. Abridge and Dragon Copilot work inside Epic. Ask each vendor to show the note landing in your own EHR during the trial.

Are AI scribe accuracy claims reliable?

Treat them as vendor claims. Figures like time saved or coding compliance come from the vendor or its customers. Your own trial is the real test.

Do patients need to consent to an AI scribe?

Many practices ask for consent at the start of the visit, and some vendors give sample wording. State recording laws vary, so check the rules in your state.

How do I know if the scribe pays for itself?

Compare seat cost to extra visits and collections, monthly. Lucid tracks software cost as a percent of collections and builds the rollout into CFO planning.
