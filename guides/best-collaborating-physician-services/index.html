Short answer

If your state requires a collaboration agreement, a matching service is the fastest way to find a collaborating partner. Collaborating Docs has the largest network and a 14 day match guarantee, Zivian Health pairs matching with compliance software for growing groups, and Medical Director Co and NP Collaborator are the two that publish a monthly price. Single Aim is a marketplace where you negotiate the fee yourself, and Moxie is built for med spa injectors. A local agreement drafted by your own attorney can cost less, but you do the finding and the compliance tracking yourself.

What a collaboration service actually does

A collaboration service finds a licensed partner who agrees to collaborate with or supervise you, then usually helps with the agreement, protocols and ongoing compliance. What each one includes varies. Ask every service what is in the monthly fee: chart review, meetings, prescribing oversight, malpractice coverage for the partner, renewals and a rematch if the partner leaves.

Collaborating Docs' buyers guide makes a useful point: a low monthly fee often means either a partner working with many clinicians or services removed from the arrangement. Compare total annual cost, not the headline price.

1. Collaborating Docs

Collaborating Docs promises a vetted collaborating partner in 14 days or less, says most clients are matched within 48 hours, and backs the timeline with a money-back guarantee. It cites a network of more than 3,500 vetted partners and says it keeps each collaboration active and compliant, including state requirements, supervision obligations and renewals. It also serves employers and multi-state groups with centralized matching and compliance monitoring.

Strengths: speed, network size and a published guarantee.

Limits: no price on its home page; you request a quote. Its own buyers guide tells you to ask what the price includes, so do.

Best for: an NP opening soon who needs a match fast, and groups adding NPs in several states.

2. Zivian Health

Zivian Health combines collaboration matching with a compliance platform. Its site lists more than 7,000 matched collaborations, more than 2,000 vetted partners, an average match time of 3.8 days and coverage in all 50 states. It drafts agreements and says its software automates ongoing compliance and chart review tracking. It also publishes a 50 state rules reference.

Strengths: software that tracks chart review and compliance, useful once you have several NPs.

Limits: no published pricing.

Best for: growing NP groups and clinics that want compliance tracked in one system.

3. Medical Director Co

Medical Director Co publishes a plan starting at $799 a month that includes the partner's fees and the collaborative agreement, with $0 legal fees, no startup fee and month to month terms with no termination fee. It says most clinics are paired within 12 to 48 hours, that it handles collaboration paperwork including treatment protocols and good faith exam documentation, and that it serves clinics and med spas in every state. It also covers med spa oversight for injectables, laser hair removal and chemical peels.

Strengths: a clear published price, month to month terms, and coverage for both primary care NPs and med spas.

Limits: the starting price may change with specialty or scope; confirm what your quote includes.

Best for: owners who want a known monthly number for the budget.

4. NP Collaborator

NP Collaborator lets you create a profile and get matched with verified partners. Its site shows sample partner listings from $449 to $650 a month and a "starting at $424 a month" offer, with no long-term contracts, cancel anytime terms and a 30 day satisfaction guarantee. It lists partner malpractice as included and says it has partners in all 50 states, filtered by state and specialty after you register.

Strengths: the lowest published starting price in this list and visible per-partner pricing.

Limits: the site does not explain how the $424 starting price relates to the listed partner prices, so confirm your actual fee.

Best for: cost-focused solo NPs and small clinics.

5. Single Aim

Single Aim is a marketplace. It shares your state, specialty and practice needs with partners in its network, and they message you directly. Fees are negotiated between you and the partner. Single Aim says there are no startup costs, asks for 50 percent of the first month's payment once you match, and handles invoicing and payments through its platform. It lists partners in all 50 states and Washington, D.C.

Strengths: you choose the partner and negotiate the fee.

Limits: no standard price, and the platform fee is not published.

Best for: owners who know what a fair fee is in their state and want control.

6. Moxie

Moxie offers medical director matching for aesthetic practices. It introduces up to three vetted candidates, says matching typically takes up to 10 days, and adds a state compliance checklist, editable SOPs, protocols and consent forms, and alerts for missing or expired documents. Matching is not offered in Florida.

Strengths: aesthetics-specific paperwork and compliance tools.

Limits: rates are described as transparent and standardized but not published, and it is built for med spas, not primary care.

Best for: NP injectors and med spas. For the wider med spa structure question, see med spa MSO vs medical director service.

7. A local agreement

You can also find a collaborating partner yourself, through a former employer, a local group or a professional network, and have a healthcare attorney draft the agreement.

Strengths: a partner who knows your local market and may refer patients; possibly a lower fee.

Limits: you handle finding a backup, tracking chart review and meetings, and renewing the agreement. If the partner retires or moves, you may have a gap.

Best for: owners with a trusted relationship. See how to choose a healthcare attorney.

State requirements, in general terms

The AANP groups states into three practice environments (page updated May 2026):

  1. Full practice. NPs practice under the state board of nursing alone, with no required collaboration agreement once any transition period is complete.
  2. Reduced practice. NPs need a career-long collaborative agreement with another clinician, or some elements of practice are limited by setting.
  3. Restricted practice. NPs need career-long supervision, delegation or team management by another clinician.

Some full practice states still require a transition period of collaborative or supervised hours before independent practice. Rules on chart review percentages, meeting frequency, prescribing, distance and how many NPs one partner may work with differ by state and change often. Check your state board of nursing, and your state medical board where it shares oversight, before you sign. A service's compliance promise does not replace your own check.

How to choose

  1. Opening in a few weeks: pick a fast matcher with a guarantee, such as Collaborating Docs or Medical Director Co.
  2. Growing past two or three NPs: look at Zivian Health or an employer plan that tracks compliance for every NP.
  3. Tight budget: compare NP Collaborator and Single Aim quotes, then compare what each includes.
  4. Med spa injector: start with Moxie or Medical Director Co.
  5. Trusted local partner: a local agreement with an attorney review.

In every case, ask about a backup partner, the rematch fee, malpractice coverage, notice periods and records access if the arrangement ends.

The money side

A collaboration fee is a fixed monthly cost, like rent. Put it in your forecast and break-even math before you open.

Illustrative only: at $799 a month and $110 collected per visit, the fee alone takes about 7 or 8 paid visits a month to cover. Add a second NP and, depending on the service, you may add a second fee.

Fee structure matters too. The services above that publish a price charge a fixed fee each month. Fees tied to a percent of your revenue can raise state fee-splitting questions and, with government payers, referral law questions. Have counsel review any revenue-based fee.

Tax treatment matters as well. The fee is generally a deductible business expense. If you pay an individual partner directly, you may owe them a Form 1099; your CPA can confirm the filing rules for the year. If you pay through a platform that invoices you, the reporting may differ.

Questions, answered

Frequently asked questions

What is a fair monthly fee for an NP collaboration agreement?

Of the services that publish a price, NP Collaborator starts at $424 a month and Medical Director Co at $799 a month, as of November 26, 2026. Others quote on request. Compare what each fee includes, not just the number.

Does my state require a collaboration agreement?

It depends on whether your state is full, reduced or restricted practice under the AANP groupings. Some full practice states also require a transition period. Check your state board of nursing before you open.

How fast can I get matched?

Medical Director Co says most clinics pair within 12 to 48 hours, Collaborating Docs guarantees a match within 14 days, Zivian lists an average of 3.8 days, and Moxie says up to 10 days. Start early anyway, because credentialing also takes time.

Can I pay my collaborating partner a percent of revenue?

Percent of revenue fees can raise fee-splitting questions under state rules. The services that publish a price charge a fixed fee each month. Have a healthcare attorney review any revenue-based arrangement.

What should the agreement include?

Scope, chart review and meeting terms, prescribing protocols, malpractice coverage, the term, renewal and notice periods, and what happens to records if it ends. Your state may require specific language.

Do I need a new collaborating partner for each NP I hire?

It depends on your state and the partner. Some states limit how many NPs one partner may work with. Ask the service how it handles groups and backups.

Is the collaboration fee tax deductible?

It is generally an ordinary business expense for the practice. Book it on its own line so you can see it in your numbers. Your CPA confirms any 1099 filing for the partner.
