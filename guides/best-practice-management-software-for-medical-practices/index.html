The other options, one by one

athenahealth (athenaOne)

athenaOne combines the EHR, practice management and billing in one cloud system. Its site says cost is a percentage of collections, and no rate is published. Limits: the fee scales with your collections, so model it against a flat per-provider fee. Accounting: we found no first-party QuickBooks connector, only third-party tools.

AdvancedMD

AdvancedMD publishes ranges by specialty. Medical specialties run $429 to $1,070 per provider per month, mental health $130 to $399, physical medicine $399, and med spa $278 (about). Revenue cycle services are priced at 4 to 8 percent of collections. Limits: add-ons are sold separately and a provider is defined as a mid-level clinician or higher, so staff counts affect cost. Accounting: AdvancedMD lists qbSolutioneers as a marketplace partner for QuickBooks,.

Tebra

Tebra describes two platform bundles, Practice Essentials (billing, EHR, telehealth) and Practice Automation (adds patient engagement). Its pricing page says cost depends on provider count, features and setup, and it shows no flat rate. It targets independent practices in primary care, family medicine, pediatrics and mental health. Limits: you need a quote. Accounting: no first-party QuickBooks connector found.

eClinicalWorks

eClinicalWorks publishes its prices. The EHR alone is $499 per provider per month, EHR with practice management is $599, and its billing service is 2.9% of practice collections. Limits: fees apply per patient statement and per messenger transaction, and airfare is billed separately. Accounting: eClinicalWorks has a financial integrations page, but we could not confirm a QuickBooks connector from it.

ModMed

ModMed builds specialty systems for more than 10 medical specialties, including dermatology, ophthalmology and orthopedics, with EHR, practice management and billing. Limits: its specialty list does not include primary care, and pricing is not published. Accounting: no first-party QuickBooks connector found.

SimplePractice

SimplePractice is built for behavioral health and wellness. Starter is $49 a month, Essential $79, and the Plus plan lists $99 for the first clinician and $74 for each additional clinician. This entry is aimed at group practices with several clinicians. Accounting: we found no direct QuickBooks integration, so plan on report exports.

Jane

Jane covers scheduling, online booking, charting, payments and insurance billing for allied health clinics. Jane's US pricing page lists Balance at $59, Practice at $79 and Thrive at $99 a month. Additional full-time clinicians are billed on top. Every plan includes unlimited admins and locations, and Packages and Memberships sit on the $99 Thrive plan. Insurance billing is an add-on. Accounting: Jane has no direct accounting integration. Plan on report exports.

Open Dental

Open Dental starts at $199 a month per location for up to three providers, dropping to $149 a month after 12 months on the U.S. fee page. Accounting: Open Dental has a first-party QuickBooks Online setup that sends deposit information and creates accounting transactions. It is one-way.

Boulevard

Boulevard handles booking, checkout, forms and charting for med spas and salons. Its pricing page shows salon plans from $159 a month, and Aesthetics Essentials is reported at about $179 a month billed annually. Limits: the QuickBooks Online sync requires the Premier plan or higher, and is reported as a $45 a month add-on per location. Boulevard says the sync suits businesses that do not pre-sell usage-based units, such as discounted injectable units in bulk.

WebPT

WebPT serves physical therapists, occupational therapists and speech-language pathologists. Its site lists plans from $79 to $249 per provider per month and says pricing can be per provider or per visit. Accounting: no first-party QuickBooks connector found. WebPT is aimed at clinical and billing work, so the books stay outside it.

How to choose a system

  1. Count clinicians and locations. Per-location pricing (Open Dental, Boulevard) and per-provider pricing (eClinicalWorks, AdvancedMD) diverge as you grow.
  2. Decide who bills. Percentage of collections models bundle the work and the fee.
  3. Ask for sample reports: deposits, revenue by provider, service and payer, and refunds.
  4. Ask about accounting hooks in writing. A first-party connector, a partner and an export are different things.

What your practice software does not do for your books

Practice systems record activity. They do not run your accounting. Five gaps appear again and again.

  1. Clean revenue by service line and provider. The system holds charges, but only your books tie revenue to the right service line, provider and location after adjustments, refunds and write-offs.
  2. Payer mix. Insurance versus private pay, and which payers are slow, shows up in the ledger only if payments are posted to the right accounts.
  3. Deposit reconciliation. A bank deposit often combines card payouts, insurer payments and patient payments from several days. A bookkeeper has to match the deposit back to the system report. When revenue is booked from deposits, payouts that land a couple of days after visits can push late month revenue into the next month.
  4. Deferred revenue for packages and memberships. Money collected for a package or membership is a liability until the visits are used, under accrual accounting. Boulevard says its QuickBooks sync does not suit pre-sold injectable units, which is why this needs a bookkeeper.
  5. Sales tax on retail. Retail products sold at a med spa or clinic can carry sales tax that differs by state and product. The system may collect it, but filing and reconciling it is accounting work.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between practice management software and an EHR?

Practice management covers scheduling, check in, billing and reports. An EHR covers charting and clinical records. Most systems here, such as athenaOne, AdvancedMD, Tebra and eClinicalWorks, combine both.

Which practice management software works best with QuickBooks?

Open Dental and Boulevard have first-party QuickBooks Online connections, and Zenoti connects through POSAccounting. Most others rely on partner tools or exports.

Do I still need a bookkeeper if my software has billing?

Yes. Billing tracks claims and patient balances. A bookkeeper reconciles deposits, records expenses and payroll entries, and closes the month. Lucid does that work with a dedicated CPA on your account.
