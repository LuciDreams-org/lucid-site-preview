How do I choose a medical marketing agency?

Updated November 26, 2026

Short answer

Pick the agency type that matches your gap (website, SEO, paid ads, reviews or all of them), then judge it on one number: what you pay in agency fees plus ad spend for each new patient who books and pays, tracked in your books by channel. Published fees run from a $299 a month website and SEO starter package (Officite) and $575 to $1,500 a month SEO plans (Delmain) to full service healthcare agencies where most clients invest at least $10,000 a month (Healthcare Success), as seen November 26, 2026. Ask for month to month or a short first term, written offboarding steps, and reports on booked patients, not impressions. Walk away from guaranteed rankings, guaranteed lead counts and long lock ins with no performance review.

Do you need an agency at all?

An agency makes sense when the work is steady and technical: content, technical SEO, ad management and weekly review of results. If you only need a website refresh or help with reviews, a website platform or review tool may be enough. If the real problem is that patients call and do not book, that is a front office problem. What each service should do

SEO

SEO means your site and your Google Business Profile show up when local patients search. It is slow: one SEO hiring guide for medical practices says to expect three to six months before results show, and Golden Proportions says SEO can take six to nine months. A good SEO agency interviews you before it writes service pages, explains how it handles health content that search engines judge strictly, and reports on calls and bookings from search, not only rankings.

Paid ads

Google Ads and Meta ads can bring leads in the first month, but they stop the day the budget stops. Ask who owns the ad accounts (it should be you), how the management fee is set, and whether ad spend is billed by the agency or paid straight from your card. Delmain, for example, publishes a management fee of $650 to $975 a month by ad spend tier, moving to 10% of spend above $9,750 a month.

Reviews and reputation

A steady pace of new reviews matters more than a large total. Ask how the agency asks for reviews, how replies stay HIPAA safe, and whether it ever writes or buys reviews (it never should).

Websites

You should own the domain, the content and the site files, or have a written right to take them with you. Ask what happens to the site if you leave. Delmain lists a custom website at $6,500 one time plus $175 a month for hosting and support when bundled. Platform sites such as Officite's starter package fold the site into a monthly fee.

Fees as published

Most agencies quote after a call. These publish numbers, as seen November 26, 2026.

  1. Officite: a Premium Plus Starter Package at $299 a month with HIPAA compliant hosting, SEO and live support, offered to new customers.
  2. Delmain: SEO at $575 (only after six months on a higher tier), $1,100 or $1,500 a month, plus $200 a month per extra location; social media at $725 a month; branding $2,500 and photos and videos $8,500 one time.
  3. Healthcare Success: says most clients invest at least $10,000 a month, with larger programs in the low six figures a year.

Third party guides give wider ranges. One SEO hiring guide for medical practices puts quality SEO at $1,500 to $5,000 a month and warns about $500 a month offers. A Dental Economics guide (August 7, 2026) says costs run from a few hundred dollars a month to several thousand for full service work with ads. Golden Proportions suggests a practice aiming for fast growth budget 7% to 9% of collections for marketing.

Contract terms to check

  1. Length: month to month, or a short first term. Delmain, for example, asks for six months on SEO or paid ads, then goes month to month with 30 days notice.
  2. Exit: written cancellation and offboarding steps, and how long they take.
  3. Ownership: your domain, site, content, ad accounts, call tracking numbers and analytics stay yours.
  4. Exclusivity: whether the agency works with a competitor in your city and specialty.
  5. Reporting: what you get each month and who reviews it with you.
  6. Ad spend: whether it passes through the agency or is billed to you directly.

How to measure cost per booked patient

This is the test that matters. Rankings, clicks and leads are steps along the way. A new patient who books, shows up and pays is the result.

  1. Total cost for the month: agency fee plus ad spend plus any tools.
  2. Booked new patients from that channel, from call tracking and your scheduling software.
  3. Cost per booked patient = total cost divided by booked new patients.
  4. Then check how many of those patients showed up and paid, and what they paid. That gives cost per paid patient, which is the number to compare across agencies.

A simple example: an agency fee of $2,000 and ad spend of $3,000 is $5,000 a month. If that brings 25 booked new patients, cost per booked patient is $200. If 20 of them show up and pay, cost per paid patient is $250. Compare that to what a new patient is worth to you over a year.

To do this, your books need marketing split by channel and vendor, not one "advertising" line. See how to set up a chart of accounts, and track it monthly with the numbers every owner should review and a practice analytics dashboard.

By specialty

Dental

Dental has the most dental only agencies and website platforms. Track new patients by source and case acceptance, and compare marketing cost to collections, not production, because PPO write offs can make production look better than the cash. See how Lucid handles dental practices.

Dermatology

Dermatology practices often market cosmetic services that patients pay for directly, next to insurance work. Ask the agency to report bookings and revenue for cosmetic services separately, since that is where paid ads usually aim.

Orthopedics

Orthopedic practices often rely on referrals as well as search. Ask how the agency tracks self referred patients versus referred ones, and judge it on booked visits for the service lines you want to grow, not total traffic.

Questions to ask

  1. How many of your current clients are practices in my specialty?
  2. Do you work with a competitor in my city and specialty?
  3. Who works on my account, by name, and is that the person I am talking to now?
  4. What will you report each month, and will it include booked patients by channel?
  5. Who owns the website, content, ad accounts and call tracking numbers?
  6. What are the cancellation and offboarding terms, in writing?
  7. How do you handle patient privacy in forms, tracking and review replies?
  8. Can I speak with two current clients like my practice?
  9. How will you help my practice show up in AI answer engines as well as Google?

Warning signs

Described generally, not about any agency named in this guide.

  1. Guaranteed rankings or a guaranteed number of leads in a set time.
  2. Reports full of impressions and clicks with no bookings.
  3. Long lock ins with no performance review or exit path.
  4. The agency, not you, owns your site, domain or ad accounts.
  5. No named person on your account, or a different team after the sale.
  6. Reluctance to give references.
  7. Offers to write or buy reviews.
  8. A generic pitch deck instead of a plan for your market.
Questions, answered

Frequently asked questions

How much does a medical marketing agency cost?

Published fees run from about $299 a month for a website and SEO starter package to $575 to $1,500 a month for SEO plans at a specialty agency, and many full service healthcare agencies start around $10,000 a month. Ad spend is usually on top. Get the total monthly cost in writing.

How do I know if my marketing agency is working?

Track cost per booked patient and cost per paid patient by channel each month, using your books and your scheduling software. Rankings and clicks are early signs, not results. Give SEO three to six months; paid ads should show leads within the first month.

Should I hire a specialty agency or a general one?

A specialty agency knows your patients, your services and privacy rules, which shortens the ramp up. A general agency can work if it can show results for practices like yours. Ask how many current clients are in your specialty.

What contract length is normal for a marketing agency?

Many agencies ask for a first term, often around six months for SEO or paid ads, then move to month to month. Avoid long lock ins with no performance review, and get cancellation and offboarding steps in writing.

Who should own my website and ad accounts?

You should. Your domain, site files, content, ad accounts, analytics and call tracking numbers should be in your name or transferable on exit. Put this in the paperwork before you start.

How much should a practice spend on marketing?

It depends on growth goals and competition. Golden Proportions, a dental agency, suggests 7% to 9% of collections for practices aiming for fast growth. Set the budget against cost per paid patient, not a percent alone.

What are red flags when hiring a healthcare SEO agency?

Guaranteed rankings or lead counts, vanity metrics instead of bookings, working with a competitor in your city, no references and long contracts with no accountability. Also be wary if the agency cannot explain how it handles health content.
