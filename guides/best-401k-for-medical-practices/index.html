Short answer

For a practice with a handful of staff, Human Interest, Gusto 401(k) (Guideline), Ubiquity, Vestwell and Employee Fiduciary publish their prices and handle most of the paperwork online. Fidelity and ADP also post starting prices for small plans, while Paychex, Principal and Ameritas quote through sales teams or advisors, and Ascensus now runs the small business plans that use Vanguard funds. Owners who want to save the most usually pair a safe harbor 401(k) with a profit sharing formula that gives owners a larger share, and some add a cash balance plan on top. The provider matters less than the plan design and the total fee at your headcount.

Human Interest

Human Interest lists three plans. Essentials is $80 a month plus $5 per eligible employee. Complete is $180 plus $7 and adds 3(16) fiduciary services, a signed and filed Form 5500, the ERISA fidelity bond and loan approvals. Concierge is $280 plus $9 and adds a named account manager and audit support. A one-time setup fee of $499 may apply. Employees pay a total of 0.06 percent of plan assets a month to Human Interest, plus fund fees.

Best for: practices that want the provider to take on filings and testing. Limits: the asset fee grows as balances grow, so compare it with flat-fee options once the plan is large.

Gusto 401(k) (Guideline)

Guideline is now part of Gusto. Its pricing page lists Starter at $49 a month plus $6 per active participant, with a top tier at $179 plus $8. Third party checks in November 2026 list Core at $119 plus $8. Its help center (August 26, 2026) says Starter has a $6,000 employee limit and no employer money, while Core adds safe harbor, matching and profit sharing.

Best for: small practices that run payroll on Gusto and want a quick setup. Limits: Starter does not allow employer contributions, so most practices with staff need Core or higher. Our direct checks of the pricing page were blocked.

Ubiquity

Ubiquity sells flat-fee plans with no asset-based fees. Saver(k) is $97 a month plus $6 per eligible employee and comes pre-designed with automatic enrollment. Custom(k) is $177 plus $6 and adds 3(16) services and design help. Reserve(k) is $257 plus $6 and adds tax credit filing help and audit support. Each carries a $495 setup fee for new customers.

Best for: practices that expect balances to grow and want the fee to stay flat. Limits: Saver(k) is pre-designed, so custom profit sharing needs a higher tier.

Vestwell

Vestwell lists Starter(k) at $49 a month, Workplace at $125 and Plus at $175, each plus $8 a month per active participant and a 0.20 percent asset-based investment fee. Starter(k) has a $6,000 employee limit and no employer money. Workplace adds safe harbor options. A setup fee may apply.

Best for: practices that want a published price and room to move up tiers. Limits: both an asset fee and a participant fee apply.

Employee Fiduciary

Employee Fiduciary posts a $500 setup fee for new plans and a $1,500 annual base fee that covers up to 30 eligible employees, plus $30 for each one after that. A custody fee of 0.08 percent of plan assets applies.

Best for: cost-focused practices comfortable with an online provider. Limits: ask for the full schedule of per-event fees.

Fidelity Advantage 401(k)

Fidelity's small business page lists a $500 one-time implementation fee and a $100 monthly fee paid by the employer. Participants pay under $10 a month in recordkeeping and a 0.125 percent advisory fee. Third party reviews say it is a pre-set safe harbor plan for 2 to 1,000 employees in which Fidelity acts as plan sponsor and fiduciary.

Best for: a first plan where the owner wants a large brand to carry most of the work. Limits: preset features leave less room for custom owner-heavy profit sharing. See Fidelity's site for current fees.

ADP

ADP's retirement page lists Starter-k Complete for 1 to 9 employees at a $100 monthly base fee, with no participant fee for up to 4 employees and $5 per participant from 5. Its other plans, including 401k Essential, are quoted.

Best for: small practices already on ADP payroll. Limits: larger plans are quote only.

iQ401k

iQ401k charges flat fiduciary fees. For plans under 100 participants with 3(38) investment service, it lists $2,500 plus $200 per participant a year, and $2,500 plus $100 per participant to set up. New plans can pay 0.70 percent of assets instead until balances grow. It also builds 401(k) plans paired with defined benefit and cash balance plans.

Best for: higher-income groups that may add a cash balance plan. Limits: the flat fee is high for a very small plan.

Paychex, Principal, Ameritas and Ascensus

Paychex sells 401(k) plans and a pooled employer plan alongside its payroll, priced by quote. Principal sells Simply Retirement by Principal for small businesses, run with Ubiquity. See Principal's site for current pricing. Ameritas offers custom 401(k) and pooled plans through financial professionals, by quote.

Vanguard no longer runs its own small business plans directly. Ascensus agreed to buy Vanguard's Individual 401(k), SEP and SIMPLE IRA business in April 2024, and in August 2026 launched Ascensus Essentials 401(k) with Vanguard funds.

Best for: practices that already work with these firms. Limits: ask for a written fee disclosure.

Plan designs in plain terms

Most practices with staff end up with one of these designs. A CPA or TPA should confirm which fits your census.

  1. Traditional 401(k). Staff and owners defer pay, and the practice may match. It must pass yearly tests comparing what highly paid people save with everyone else, and owners can get money refunded when staff save little.
  2. Safe harbor 401(k). The practice promises a set contribution, and in return most of those tests go away. Per the IRS and the Department of Labor, the common formulas are a 3 percent of pay contribution to every eligible employee, a basic match of 100 percent of the first 3 percent deferred plus 50 percent of the next 2 percent, or an enhanced match of at least 4 percent. Safe harbor money is generally fully vested right away.
  3. Safe harbor plus profit sharing. The practice adds a profit sharing contribution each year. With a cross-tested design, often called new comparability, owners can receive a larger percent of pay than staff while the plan still passes testing. This is how most owners reach the full annual limit.
  4. Adding a cash balance plan. Owners who want to put away more than the 401(k) limit add a cash balance plan on top. See our cash balance plan guide for limits, costs and staff rules.

Two rules shape the timing. The IRS says a nonelective safe harbor can be added later in the plan year under SECURE Act rules, while a match must be in place from the start. And new 401(k) plans set up after December 29, 2022 must automatically enroll staff, starting at 3 percent of pay, unless the business is under 3 years old or has 10 or fewer employees, per section 414A of the tax code. Treasury issued final rules on this on November 17, 2026.

Tax credits for a new plan

New plans at practices with 100 or fewer employees can claim credits, per the IRS.

  1. Startup cost credit: up to the lesser of $250 per eligible non-highly paid employee or $5,000, with a $500 minimum, for three years. It is 100 percent of costs for practices with 50 or fewer employees.
  2. Auto-enrollment credit: $500 a year for three years.
  3. Employer contribution credit: up to $1,000 per employee in years one and two, then stepping down through year five. Pay above $100,000 (2023 figure, indexed) does not count.

How to choose

  1. Under 10 staff, first plan: compare Human Interest Essentials, Gusto Core, Vestwell Workplace, ADP Starter-k Complete and Fidelity Advantage on total monthly cost.
  2. Owners want the full limit: shortlist a provider that supports cross-tested profit sharing, such as Human Interest, Ubiquity Custom(k), Employee Fiduciary or iQ401k, and ask for a census-based illustration.
  3. Balances already large: a flat-fee provider like Ubiquity, Employee Fiduciary or iQ401k often costs less than an asset fee.
  4. Considering a cash balance plan: pick a provider or TPA that can run both plans together.
  5. Several practices or entities: ask how the plan handles a controlled group, since related businesses usually must be tested together.

Ask every provider for total cost at your headcount, who signs the Form 5500 and who does testing.

Questions, answered

Frequently asked questions

What is the best 401(k) for a small medical practice?

There is no single best. Human Interest, Gusto 401(k), Ubiquity, Vestwell and Employee Fiduciary publish prices and suit most small practices. Choose on total cost at your headcount and whether the plan supports profit sharing.

What does a practice 401(k) cost?

Published employer prices start around $49 to $125 a month plus a per-person fee, as checked November 26, 2026. Asset fees and fund fees are on top unless the plan is flat fee. Startup credits can offset much of the first three years.

Do I need a safe harbor 401(k)?

Not always, but most practices with staff benefit. Safe harbor removes the tests that often cap what owners can save. The cost is a set contribution to eligible staff each year.

How much can a practice owner save in a 401(k)?

In 2026 the total into a 401(k) per person is $72,000, before catch-up, per the IRS. That counts deferrals and employer money together. Reaching it usually takes a profit sharing contribution.

Do new 401(k) plans have to auto-enroll staff?

Plans set up after December 29, 2022 generally must, starting at 3 percent of pay. Practices under 3 years old or with 10 or fewer employees are exempt until they pass those limits.

When should I add a cash balance plan?

When owners already max the 401(k) and profit is high and steady. Our cash balance plan guide covers limits and costs.
