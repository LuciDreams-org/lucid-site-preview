Short answer

For most practices the strongest equipment financing comes from three places: manufacturer and vendor finance arms (Henry Schein Financial Services, Canon Medical Finance, Siemens Financial Services, BrainsWay for TMS), banks with practice lending teams (Bank of America Practice Solutions, Panacea Financial, U.S. Bank), and specialty or marketplace lenders (NCMIC Finance, Crestmont Capital, Axiant Partners). Vendor programs are often fastest, banks suit owners who already bank there, and marketplaces bring several quotes at once; pick a $1 buyout lease or a loan for equipment you will keep, and a fair market value (FMV) lease for technology you expect to replace. Lucid is not a lender, but it models the payback and the tax effect before you sign.

Lease or loan: the three structures

Most offers fall into one of three structures. Ask each lender which one you are being quoted, because the monthly payment alone hides the difference.

  1. Equipment loan. You own the equipment from day one, the lender holds a lien, and you pay principal and interest. Best for equipment you will use for years, such as dental chairs, exam tables and sterilizers.
  2. $1 buyout lease. You make lease payments and own the equipment for $1 at the end. For tax and accounting it usually works like a purchase.
  3. FMV lease. The payment is lower because you do not own the equipment at the end. You can return it, upgrade it or buy it at fair market value. Best for imaging, lasers and other technology that dates quickly.

Axiant Partners, in its own medical leasing guide updated November 20, 2026, says lease payments typically run 10% to 20% below a loan on the same equipment. That saving is real each month, but you give up ownership, so compare the total paid over the term plus the end-of-lease cost.

The lenders and lessors

Henry Schein Financial Services

Henry Schein's medical finance site offers equipment financing and leasing with 100% financing, same day approvals and terms from 1 to 15 years, plus leasehold improvement funds and working capital. Its current "Route 66" promotion on equipment and technology purchases advertises no payments for 6 months, then $99 a month for 6 months, then level payments, with 5 to 7 year terms, for well-qualified customers.

Henry Schein says it is not a bank and that transactions need credit approval by third party lenders. It also discloses that it may receive a marketing fee from the vendor. For dental chairs, CBCT and scanners, see our guide to dental practice loans and lenders.

Best for: practices already buying from Henry Schein that want one point of contact for equipment and buildout.

Canon Medical Finance

Canon Medical Systems runs its own finance arm, Canon Medical Finance, with Canon Financial Services, a subsidiary of Canon USA. Its page lists capital expenditure lines of credit for qualified customers up to $25 million with no fees, financing secured by the equipment only, and financing for some non-Canon assets such as IT and leasehold improvements, subject to approval. It says credit decisions typically come within days.

Best for: imaging centers, orthopedic and multi-specialty groups buying Canon imaging.

Siemens Financial Services

Siemens Financial Services finances Siemens Healthineers equipment and other healthcare assets. Its U.S. healthcare page names practitioners, clinics and radiology practices, and lists leasing, rental and lease with purchase option, plus plans to upgrade or add equipment during the term. It does not publish amounts or terms.

Best for: practices buying Siemens imaging that want an upgrade path written into the deal.

Bank of America Practice Solutions

Bank of America Practice Solutions lists equipment financing alongside its startup, acquisition and real estate loans. It does not publish equipment terms. Its loan programs are covered in our guide to the best medical practice loan lenders.

Best for: owners who want the equipment loan, the operating accounts and any buildout loan with one bank.

Panacea Financial

Panacea Financial, a division of Primis Bank, lists new equipment loans with terms up to 10 years, along with startup and buy-in loans. It is the practice finance provider of ADA Member Advantage, which matters for dental owners.

Best for: dental and medical owners who want a long bank term on new equipment.

U.S. Bank

U.S. Bank lists equipment purchases as a use of its medical and dental practice loans, which come with terms up to 15 years and up to six months of interest-only payments. It does not publish equipment rates.

Best for: practices that want equipment and expansion financing from one large bank.

NCMIC Finance

NCMIC Finance publishes clear equipment terms: 12, 24, 36, 48 or 60 months, an option to defer the first payment up to 90 days, up to 100% financing with no down payment, no prepayment penalty, and a $200 documentation fee at funding. It says credit decisions can come in as little as 2 hours. Its pages are written mostly for chiropractors, so confirm your specialty qualifies.

Best for: smaller equipment purchases where speed and a short term matter.

BrainsWay (TMS)

BrainsWay, the maker of Deep TMS systems, says practices can choose leasing, direct purchase or financing. Its leases include technical service and device maintenance for the length of the contract. Pricing is not published. For psychiatry and behavioral health groups, a lease with service included can make the monthly cost easier to forecast.

Best for: behavioral health practices adding TMS.

Crestmont Capital

Crestmont Capital's medical equipment page publishes equipment financing from $10,000 to $2 million, a typical term of 2 to 7 years, at least 6 months in business and about $10,000 a month in revenue. It says terms depend on the lender's review, and it covers new, used and refurbished equipment.

Best for: newer practices that meet its revenue floor and want a quick range before talking to a bank.

Axiant Partners

Axiant Partners is a lender matching service, not a lender. It says it uses banking relationships to find lenders likely to approve you, charges business owners nothing, and is paid by its lending partners. It lists equipment loans and leases from $10,000 to $5 million, with decisions often in 24 to 48 hours.

Best for: owners who want several equipment offers without filling in several applications.

Section 179 and bonus depreciation

Tax rules often decide whether buying beats leasing. This section is general information, not tax advice. Have your CPA run your own numbers.

  1. Section 179. A business can elect to deduct the cost of qualifying equipment in the year it is placed in service, instead of depreciating it over several years. For tax years beginning in 2025, the IRS says the maximum is $2,500,000, reduced once total qualifying purchases pass $4,000,000, and the amounts are adjusted for inflation from 2026 (Rev. Proc. 2025-32). IRS draft 2026 instructions show $2,560,000 and $4,090,000 for 2026. The deduction is generally limited to business taxable income.
  2. Bonus depreciation. The IRS says the 2025 law made a permanent 100% bonus depreciation deduction, taken in the year the property is placed in service, for qualified property acquired after January 19, 2025.
  3. Leases. With a loan or a $1 buyout lease, you are generally treated as the owner, so these deductions may apply. With a true FMV lease, the lessor owns the equipment and your lease payments are generally deducted as a business expense when paid.

Timing matters. The equipment must be placed in service in the tax year, not just ordered. For other owner deductions, see our guide to tax deductions for medical practice owners.

How to choose

  1. Imaging (CT, MRI, X-ray, ultrasound). Start with the manufacturer: Canon Medical Finance or Siemens Financial Services. Then get a bank quote from your own bank to compare. See our orthopedics and ophthalmology pages.
  2. Lasers and aesthetic devices. Upgrade cycles are short, so price an FMV lease next to a loan. See our pages for dermatology and med spa practices.
  3. Dental chairs, CBCT and scanners. Compare Henry Schein Financial Services, Panacea and your bank. These last a long time, so a loan or $1 buyout usually costs less overall. See our dental page.
  4. TMS. Ask BrainsWay or the device maker for lease and purchase quotes, and check whether service is included. See our behavioral health page.
  5. Several devices or several locations. A capital line, such as Canon's, or one bank relationship can be simpler than a separate lease per device.
  6. You want many quotes fast. Use a matching service such as Axiant, then compare every offer on the same terms.

For every quote, compare the rate, term, total paid, end-of-term option, documentation fees, prepayment terms, service coverage and any requirement to move your bank accounts. What lenders will ask for is in our guide on what lenders look for in a practice loan. For SBA routes, see the best SBA lenders for medical practices.

Questions, answered

Frequently asked questions

Is it better to lease or finance medical equipment?

Finance or take a $1 buyout lease for equipment you will keep for many years. Use an FMV lease for technology you expect to replace, because the payment is lower and you can upgrade at the end. Compare total cost over the term, not just the monthly payment.

What is the difference between a $1 buyout lease and an FMV lease?

With a $1 buyout lease you own the equipment for $1 when payments end, so it works much like a loan. With an FMV lease you can return, upgrade or buy the equipment at its market value at the end. The FMV payment is usually lower.

Can I take Section 179 on financed equipment?

Generally yes, if you are treated as the owner, as with a loan or a $1 buyout lease, and the equipment is placed in service that year. The IRS limit for 2025 is $2,500,000 and it is adjusted for inflation from 2026. Your CPA should confirm the deduction fits your income.

Do manufacturers offer their own financing?

Many do. Canon Medical Finance and Siemens Financial Services finance imaging, BrainsWay offers leases for TMS, and Henry Schein arranges financing for equipment it sells. Vendor programs can be fast, but compare them with a bank quote.

How fast can I get approved for an equipment loan?

It depends on the lender and the amount. NCMIC says decisions can come in as little as 2 hours, Henry Schein advertises same day approvals, and Canon says days rather than weeks. Large imaging deals and bank loans take longer.

Can I finance used or refurbished equipment?

Some lenders do. NCMIC and Crestmont both say they finance used equipment. Expect the lender to ask for the vendor quote and, for used equipment, details of its condition.

How do I record an equipment loan or lease in my books?

A loan or $1 buyout lease is usually recorded as an asset with a matching loan balance, and each payment is split between principal and interest. An FMV lease is usually an expense, though some leases must also be shown on the balance sheet under lease accounting rules. Ask your accountant which applies.
