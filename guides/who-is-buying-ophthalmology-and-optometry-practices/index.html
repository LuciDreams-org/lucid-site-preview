Who is buying ophthalmology and optometry practices?

Short answer

In retina and ophthalmology, the largest buyers are now two drug distributors: Cencora, which owns about 85% of Retina Consultants of America, and McKesson, which owns about 80% of PRISM Vision Group. Private equity backed platforms such as EyeCare Partners, EyeSouth Partners, Vision Innovation Partners, Eye Health America and Unifeye Vision Partners also keep adding practices. In optometry, MyEyeDr is the largest buyer and agreed in September 2026 to buy more than 300 EyeCare Partners optometry offices, alongside AEG Vision, VSP Ventures, InFocus Eyecare and private optometrists. Buyers pay most for surgery center ownership, documented retina and surgical revenue, optical revenue reported on its own lines and a team that will stay, so compare offers on cash at closing, rollover and pay after closing.

How active is the market?

  1. Bayshore Growth Partners (March 2026) counts about 18,500 ophthalmologists in the US, with about 2,500 in sponsor-backed vision care platforms.
  2. LevinPro HC counted 24 eye care deals in 2024, down from 47 in 2023 (via DealSeam).
  3. A 2026 Jobson Research survey of owners who sold an optometry practice in the past five years found 37% sold to private equity and 37% to a private optometrist (Vision Monday, via DealSeam).
  4. Since 2025 the biggest deals went to strategic buyers, not to other funds. CT Acquisitions calls 2026 ophthalmology "a strategic buyers' market".

Buyer type 1: drug distributors

  1. Retina Consultants of America (RCA). Cencora closed its purchase of about 85% from Webster Equity Partners on January 2, 2025, at an announced price of about $4.6 billion plus up to $500 million in contingent payments. RCA had nearly 300 retina specialists in 23 states at signing. On March 23, 2026, Cencora agreed to buy the retina business of EyeSouth Partners for $1.1 billion. Bayshore lists Montana Retina Consultants joining RCA in February 2026.
  2. PRISM Vision Group. McKesson closed an 80% interest for about $850 million in April 2025, with PRISM's clinicians keeping about 20%. PRISM then acquired Spectrum Vision Partners from Blue Sea Capital. Axinn says the deal closed on November 2, 2026, adding more than 150 providers in New York, Pennsylvania, Connecticut, New Jersey and West Virginia.

Both distributors kept the treating clinicians as minority owners. CT Acquisitions points out that in these structures, the minority stake is the main route to a later payout, because a distributor is not planning a resale the way a fund does.

Buyer type 2: PE-backed ophthalmology platforms

  1. EyeCare Partners. Partners Group since December 2019. On September 28, 2026 it agreed to sell its optometry division to MyEyeDr and focus on ophthalmology and ASCs (Business Wire).
  2. EyeSouth Partners. Olympus Partners since September 2022. Bayshore lists add-ons including Sunvera Group (August 2025) and Katz Eye Center (September 2025). Its retina business is under agreement with Cencora.
  3. Vision Innovation Partners. Gryphon Investors since April 2022. 69 locations in Maryland, DC, Virginia, Pennsylvania and Delaware, including 12 surgery centers, with integrated optometry in every practice. Added Ophthalmic Associates of Alexandria (February 2026) and Frederick Eye Institute (March 2026).
  4. Eye Health America. LLR Partners. Florida, South Carolina and Georgia. Quigley Eye Specialists joined in February 2025 as its 29th partnership.
  5. Unifeye Vision Partners. Waud Capital Partners. Texas and California. Added Brooks Eye Associates in Plano in May 2025.
  6. NVISION Eye Centers. Ontario Teachers' Pension Plan majority since November 2020, focused on refractive and cataract surgery in the western US.
  7. Other platforms in Bayshore's March 2026 list include American Vision Partners (H.I.G. Capital), Midwest Vision Partners (Alpine Investors), SightMD and Ascend Vision Partners (Chicago Pacific Founders), ReFocus Eye Health (Zenyth Partners), Bridgeview Eye Partners (Sentinel Capital Partners) and Comprehensive EyeCare Partners (Gauge Capital). Ascend added two Oklahoma practices in January 2026.

Stout (September 2024) notes that, outside a few health systems, hospitals have not focused on buying ophthalmology practices, which is why most bids come from platforms.

Buyer type 3: PE-backed optometry platforms

DealSeam (October 6, 2026) counts 13 named consolidators buying US optometry practices, 10 of them PE-backed.

  1. MyEyeDr. Goldman Sachs since 2019, with Charlesbank as a minority investor. Opened its 1,000th office in June 2026. Agreed on September 28, 2026 to buy the EyeCare Partners optometry division, more than 300 offices, with closing expected in the fourth quarter of 2026. Also added Lumina Vision Partners (April 2026).
  2. AEG Vision. Riata Capital Group. About 575 offices in 35 states (Vision Monday, April 2026).
  3. InFocus Eyecare. Regal Healthcare Capital Partners. More than 30 full scope optometry practices in seven states, with add-ons through July 2026.
  4. Others named by DealSeam: Simon Eye (Brightstar Capital Partners, May 2026), Elevate Eyecare (Bison Capital), Sight360 (SBJ Capital) and US VisionMed Partners.

Buyer type 4: strategic optical companies

  1. VSP Ventures, backed by VSP Vision. Added Total Vision's 57 practices in 2025 and Borel Eye in July 2026 (DealSeam).
  2. Shopko Optical, owned by Fielmann Group. Added practices in Wisconsin, Minnesota and North Dakota in 2025.
  3. TeamVision, an MSO inside EssilorLuxottica. Added three Wisconsin groups in 2025.

Buyer type 5: private buyers

The same Jobson survey found private optometrists bought as many practices as private equity did. An associate, a local peer or a partner group is often the natural buyer for a single location optometry practice, and the process is usually simpler. The price is typically paid more in cash or seller financing and less in rollover.

What eye care buyers look for

  1. Retina and drug revenue. Medicare pays most Part B drugs at average sales price plus 6%, so buyers rebuild drug revenue from injection volume by drug and payer. Present drug and professional revenue separately (CT Acquisitions, citing CMS).
  2. Cataract and refractive mix. For 2026, the Medicare surgeon fee for cataract surgery (CPT 66984) fell 11% to $462.94, while the ASC payment rose 3.4% to $1,255.73 (ASCRS, via CT Acquisitions).
  3. Surgery center ownership. The facility fee goes to whoever owns the center, so ASC equity often moves the price.
  4. Optical and optometry. Some buyers value optometry referral flow; others are focusing on ophthalmology. Optical revenue and margin should be split from medical revenue.
  5. Payer mix. Vision plans and medical insurance pay on different rules. Buyers want revenue by payer and contract renewal dates.
  6. A durable team. Stout (2024) describes a "grade A" practice as stable or growing, with few normalizing adjustments, a mix of senior, mid career and early career clinicians, and "systems where the data for the practice is readily available to buyers".

Typical deal structures, in general terms

  1. Cash at closing. The share of price paid on the closing date, before escrow and holdbacks.
  2. Rollover equity. Part of the price taken as ownership in the buyer. Clinicians kept about 20% of PRISM Vision and about 15% of RCA.
  3. Contingent pay. RCA's price included up to $500 million tied to business targets, an earnout at platform scale.
  4. Pay after closing. AAO EyeNet reported in November 2019 that selling clinicians were typically paid about 30% of their own collections, excluding drugs and testing. Ask for the formula applied to your last 12 months.
  5. Non competes. One covenant usually sits in the purchase paperwork and one in the employment agreement. The FTC dropped its national ban in September 2025, so state law decides. Texas caps employment non competes for these clinicians at one year and five miles for agreements from September 1, 2025.

How to compare offers

  1. Cash at closing per owner, after escrow, holdbacks and fees.
  2. Rollover: which entity issues the units, their class, and what happens if you leave early.
  3. Hold period: when the owner bought the platform and when it expects a next sale. Distributor-owned platforms may not plan one.
  4. Contingent pay: the metric, the period and who keeps the books that measure it.
  5. Your pay: the formula on your last 12 months of collections, and when it resets.
  6. The ASC and real estate: priced at the same multiple, a different one, or left with you, and the lease terms.
  7. Optical and optometry: whether the buyer wants them, and at what value.
  8. Non competes: which document holds each, for how long and over what radius.

Model each offer after tax against staying independent over the same years.

How to choose by situation

  1. Retina is most of your collections: include RCA and PRISM Vision alongside PE platforms.
  2. You own an ASC that does most of your cataract cases: ask each bidder to price it separately.
  3. Optometry or optical is most of your revenue: include MyEyeDr, AEG, VSP Ventures and private optometrists, and do not assume ophthalmology platforms want it.
  4. You plan to stop practicing within five years: weight cash at closing and a shorter employment term.
  5. You plan to practice ten or more years: accept more rollover only with information rights and a clear payout order.
  6. Several locations or owners: model the deal owner by owner. See medical groups.
Questions, answered

Frequently asked questions

Who is buying ophthalmology practices in 2026?

Cencora through Retina Consultants of America and McKesson through PRISM Vision lead in retina and general ophthalmology. PE-backed platforms including EyeCare Partners, EyeSouth, Vision Innovation Partners, Eye Health America and Unifeye keep adding practices.

Who is buying optometry practices?

MyEyeDr is the largest, with AEG Vision, InFocus Eyecare, VSP Ventures, Shopko Optical and TeamVision also active. A 2026 Jobson survey found private optometrists bought as many practices as private equity.

What multiple does an eye care practice sell for?

Published brackets run from 2.0x to 4.5x SDE for small optometry practices to 10x to 12x EBITDA or more for scarce retina platforms. Surgery center ownership, retina revenue and how much depends on one surgeon move a practice between bands.

Why do drug distributors buy retina practices?

Retina practices bill injected drugs, so drug volume matters to a distributor. Both Cencora and McKesson kept clinicians as minority owners.

What is rollover equity in an eye care sale?

It is part of the price paid as ownership in the buyer instead of cash. Its value depends on the next sale price, the debt ahead of it and your exit terms.

Do I need to separate optical revenue before a sale?

Yes. Buyers value exam, medical, surgical and optical revenue differently. Report optical margin on its own lines so a buyer does not guess.
