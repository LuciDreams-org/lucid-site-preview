Short answer

For most independent orthopedic groups the shortlist is ModMed EMA, Nextech and athenaOne for Orthopedics. ModMed stands out for its cloud PACS link, its WebPT interface for therapy and its own ASC software. eClinicalWorks publishes a per provider price and covers ASC workflows, while Epic and NextGen Enterprise fit large groups and health system affiliates. None of them keeps your books, so plan the money side of imaging, PT, DME and the ASC separately.

What an orthopedic group needs from an EHR

An orthopedic practice is rarely one business. The clinic bills the professional fee. Imaging, physical therapy and durable medical equipment (DME) often run as their own lines, and many groups own part of an ambulatory surgery center (ASC). The EHR has to connect those pieces without staff typing the same data twice.

  1. Orthopedic charting: body part templates, injection and fracture care, post-op protocols.
  2. Imaging: a PACS link so X-ray and MRI images open from the chart, plus orders to outside radiology.
  3. Surgery scheduling: case booking, pre-op tasks and global period tracking.
  4. DME: tracking braces and supplies dispensed in clinic, tied to the visit and the charge.
  5. Physical therapy: orders sent to the therapy team and notes sent back, whether PT is in house or not.
  6. ASC: either an ASC module from the same vendor or a clean interface to the surgery center's system.
  7. Reports by location, provider and service line, with exports your accountant can use.

Keep that list in front of you during demos. For a wider look across specialties, see our guide to practice management software.

ModMed EMA

ModMed builds EMA, a specialty EHR, and says its orthopedic software was built by orthopedic surgeons. On imaging, ModMed lists eVue by Efferent, a cloud PACS that interfaces with EMA, handles most DICOM modalities and lets providers measure, annotate and compare images on screen. It also offers an HL7 modality worklist interface for two-way transfer of records and results with radiology centers.

For therapy, ModMed and WebPT built a two-way interface. Providers order physical therapy from EMA during the exam, therapy notes come back into the chart, and therapy charges with CPT codes flow into ModMed's practice management system. ModMed Scribe can suggest follow-up tasks such as PT orders from the visit conversation.

ModMed also sells ASC software connected to its practice EHR, and an inventory tool that tracks quantities dispensed across locations and cabinets, which is how many groups handle braces and supplies. RCM services and analytics are available. Pricing is not published.

Best for: independent groups that run imaging, in house PT and an ASC. Watch for: no published price, so get one quote covering EMA, PACS, ASC, inventory and RCM.

Nextech

Nextech sells an orthopedic EHR called SRSPro with its Practice+ management system. Its orthopedic page says providers can open PACS imaging, patient history and test results in one place, with templates set by subspecialty and provider. Practice+ lists multi-provider and multi-location scheduling with room and resource booking, inventory tracking by SKU and lot, and reports. Nextech also offers RCM services. Pricing is by quote.

Best for: groups that want imaging inside the chart and strong scheduling. Watch for: Nextech's site says less about PT and ASC links for orthopedics than ModMed's, so ask for a live demo of both.

athenaOne for Orthopedics

athenahealth launched athenaOne for Orthopedics in October 2024 as an EHR, revenue cycle, practice management and patient engagement package for orthopedic practices. Its orthopedics page speaks to high-volume clinics, complex surgical workflows and multi-site care, and the launch release highlights ambient note taking for busy clinic days. The page also says ASC capabilities for orthopedics customers are coming soon.

athenahealth says its pricing model corresponds to your collections, and no rate is published.

Best for: groups that want billing handled by the vendor. Watch for: a fee tied to collections grows with revenue, and ASC features were not yet live when this guide was written.

eClinicalWorks

eClinicalWorks is a general ambulatory EHR with an orthopedic workflow. Its pricing page lists $499 per provider a month for the EHR and $599 with practice management. Its orthopedic page says providers can document physical therapy assessments, capture patient limits and goals, and search for imaging results such as a hospital MRI. Surgery Navigator tracks pre-op and post-op tasks and forms in one place.

eClinicalWorks also says it covers ASCs, from scheduling and intake through documentation and billing, with preference cards, inventory management, procedure notes, nursing and anesthesia documentation.

Best for: groups that want a published price and an ASC workflow from one vendor. Watch for: implementation fees apply above nine providers, so ask for the full cost.

Epic

Epic is the system of most large health systems. Orthopedic centers use EpicCare with Bones, Epic's orthopedics application, which supports documenting common procedures such as joint injections, collecting patient-reported outcomes and reporting to orthopedic registries. Independent groups usually reach Epic through Community Connect, where a hospital system that runs Epic extends it to affiliated practices. Epic also launched Garden Plot, a hosted option for independent groups, which it said in 2022 was for groups with more than 40 providers.

Best for: large orthopedic groups, academic practices and health system affiliates. Watch for: less control over setup and cost when a host system runs it.

NextGen Enterprise

NextGen sells Enterprise for practices with 10 or more providers and NextGen Office for smaller ones. Its orthopedic page lists image-enabled orthopedic PACS, so the full viewing and reporting workflow happens in the EHR, plus specialty orders and surgery scheduling. NextGen also offers RCM services. Pricing is by quote.

Best for: larger groups that want PACS and surgery scheduling in one enterprise system. Watch for: Enterprise and Office are different products, so confirm which one is quoted.

Other names on orthopedic shortlists include AdvancedMD, which lists $429 to $1,070 per provider a month for medical specialties, Greenway Intergy and DrChrono. See the vendor's site for current pricing. If your PT clinic runs as a separate business, our guide to the best physical therapy EMR covers therapy systems.

How to choose

  1. Imaging, in house PT and an ASC under one roof: ModMed EMA, then compare Nextech.
  2. You want billing handled by the vendor: athenaOne, or the RCM services from ModMed, Nextech or NextGen.
  3. You want a published per provider price: eClinicalWorks or AdvancedMD.
  4. You are tied to a hospital system: Epic through Community Connect.
  5. Ten or more providers and a central PACS: NextGen Enterprise or Epic.
  6. Thinking about an ASC stake: read our guide on ASC investment versus an office surgery suite before you pick ASC software.

Ask every vendor the same four questions. What is the total monthly cost for our providers, staff and modules? How do clinic, imaging, PT, DME and ASC charges show up in reports? Can we export charges, payments and adjustments by location and provider to a spreadsheet? Does the ASC sit in the same database or a separate one?

Your EHR and your books

An EHR runs the clinic. It does not run the finances. It shows charges and payments posted. Your bank shows insurance deposits weeks later, and workers compensation cases can pay months after surgery. Implant costs, payroll, rent and the ASC's own expenses never show up in the clinic EHR.

So the EHR cannot tell you whether a knee case, an MRI or a brace really pays after costs, or what each entity and surgeon earns. That takes books kept by entity and service line, reconciled to the bank every month. Owners thinking about a sale will need the same numbers, because buyers ask for them first. Questions, answered

Frequently asked questions

What is the best EHR for an orthopedic practice?

There is no single best choice. ModMed EMA, Nextech and athenaOne for Orthopedics are the most common shortlist for independent groups. Groups that want a published price look at eClinicalWorks, and large groups or hospital affiliates often use Epic or NextGen Enterprise.

Is ModMed or Epic better for orthopedics?

They fit different groups. ModMed is built for independent specialty practices and lists a cloud PACS, a WebPT interface and ASC software. Epic, with its Bones application, fits large groups and practices tied to a health system that already runs Epic.

Which orthopedic EHR connects to PACS?

ModMed offers eVue, a cloud PACS that interfaces with EMA, plus an HL7 worklist link to radiology. Nextech and NextGen both say PACS imaging opens inside the chart. Test image loading speed with your own studies in the demo.

Which orthopedic EHR has an ASC module?

ModMed sells ASC software connected to its practice EHR, and eClinicalWorks lists ASC workflows from scheduling to billing. athenahealth lists ASC features for orthopedics as coming soon. Ask whether the ASC shares the clinic database or runs separately.

How do orthopedic EHRs handle physical therapy?

ModMed has a two-way WebPT interface that sends orders and returns notes and charges. eClinicalWorks documents PT assessments in the chart. If PT is a separate company, plan how its charges reach your reports.

How much does an orthopedic EHR cost?

eClinicalWorks lists $499 per provider a month, or $599 with practice management. AdvancedMD lists $429 to $1,070 per provider a month for medical specialties. ModMed, Nextech, NextGen and Epic quote by practice, and athenaOne ties its fee to collections.

Can the EHR show profit by service line?

EHR reports show charges and collections by service and provider. Profit by imaging, PT, DME or the ASC also needs payroll, rent, implant and supply costs, which live in your books, not the EHR.
