Short answer

For a practice with staff or several locations, Lucid is the strongest choice, because it pairs a live dashboard of P&L, cash flow, and margin by location and provider with the CPA team that keeps the numbers behind it right. Fathom, Jirav, Reach Reporting and LivePlan are capable reporting and planning tools, but they chart whatever is in your ledger, so someone still has to keep the books clean. What a practice financial dashboard should show

  1. Profit, not only revenue: a live P&L by location and by provider.
  2. Cash: what is in the bank today, what is coming, and when it gets tight.
  3. Payer mix: which payers pay, how much and how fast.
  4. Budget versus actuals, with variances called out.
  5. Forecasts: what a new hire, a second location or a slow season does to cash.
  6. Trust: numbers reconciled to the bank, so you can act on them.

Point 6 is where most tools stop. A dashboard is only as good as the books it reads.

Fathom

Fathom is reporting, forecasting and consolidation software that sits on top of accounting systems such as Xero and QuickBooks Online. Its pricing page lists Fathom Pro at $450 a month for 25 companies, with extra companies at $17 a month, and Pro includes three way cash flow forecasting, consolidations and custom KPIs. A Portfolio plan for advisors is $67 a month for 100 companies with lighter features.

Best for: accountants and advisors serving many clients, or groups with an in house bookkeeper and clean books. Limits: it reports on your ledger. It does not keep or reconcile the books, and its pricing is built around portfolios of companies.

Jirav

Jirav is FP&A software for budgets, rolling forecasts and scenario plans. Its business pricing page lists Starter at $10,000 a year and Pro at $15,000 a year, billed annually, with Enterprise by quote. Every plan includes guided implementation, accounting and payroll integrations, budget versus actual analysis and integrated balance sheet and cash flow projections.

Best for: larger groups or MSOs with a finance lead who runs formal planning. Limits: the cost is high for a single practice, and you still need someone to own the books and the model.

Reach Reporting

Reach Reporting is financial reporting and planning software aimed at accounting firms. Its pricing page says pricing is based on the number of data connections, starting at $149 a month. Its FAQ says it supports scenarios, cash flow forecasting and rolling forecasts.

Best for: accountants or controllers building custom report packages. Limits: built for financial professionals, so an owner usually needs an accountant to set it up and run it.

LivePlan

LivePlan is business planning software. Its pricing page lists Standard at $15 a month and Premium at $30 a month, billed yearly, with introductory discounts shown. Budget versus actual dashboards and the accounting connection to QuickBooks Online and Xero are on Premium.

Best for: a new or small practice writing a plan for a lender or tracking a simple budget. Limits: it is a planning tool, not a live view by provider or payer.

Power BI and do it yourself dashboards

Microsoft Power BI can build any dashboard you can design. Third party guides list Power BI Pro at $14 per user per month, and every viewer needs a license on standard plans.

Best for: larger groups with a data analyst and clean source data. Limits: someone has to build, maintain and trust the data model. Wrong books make a wrong dashboard faster.

Practice by Numbers

Practice by Numbers is dental software with analytics dashboards. Its pricing page says practices can start as low as $249 a month, with bundles named Analyze, Core, Flow, Scale and Thrive, and pricing tailored for solo practices, groups and DSOs. Its analytics track production, collections and performance by provider, team or location, and an Enterprise Dashboard covers multiple locations.

Best for: dental practices and DSOs that want operational metrics from the practice management system. Limits: production and collections are not profit. Pair it with books that carry payroll, supplies and overhead. See the Lucid dental page.

Dental Intelligence

Dental Intelligence sells analytics and reporting that sync with a dental practice management system, plus patient engagement, payments and scheduling tools. Its analytics include metrics, a morning huddle, scorecards and a patient finder. We did not find a published price on its own site.

Best for: dental teams that want daily operational metrics and patient tools in one platform. Limits: operations focused, not a P&L or cash forecast.

Jarvis Analytics

Jarvis Analytics is a dental analytics platform built for DSOs and groups. It aggregates data from one or all practices so you can compare and rank metrics, with modules for aging, financials, RCM, scorecards and morning huddles. Its pricing page prices per office by number of locations and asks for a quote.

Best for: dental groups that want one view across offices. Limits: dental only, and pricing is not published.

MD Clarity

MD Clarity is a revenue optimization platform. It focuses on payer underpayment detection, denial management, contract modeling and patient cost estimates. Pricing is not published.

Best for: practices with many payer contracts that suspect they are underpaid. Limits: it watches revenue and payers, not your full P&L or cash.

EHR and practice management reports

Most EHRs include reports. Tebra, for example, lists revenue and practice growth analytics, and SimplePractice puts advanced analytics on its Plus plan. These show visits, charges, collections and claims by provider.

Best for: daily clinic and billing activity. Limits: the EHR never sees payroll, rent, supplies or bank deposits, so it cannot show profit or cash. Lucid uses these reports alongside the books.

How to choose

  1. You want profit by location, provider and payer without hiring a finance team: Lucid.
  2. You already have a strong bookkeeper and want reporting on top: Fathom or Reach Reporting.
  3. You are a larger group or MSO with a finance lead doing formal planning: Jirav, or Lucid CFO Services if you want the team too.
  4. You are a dental practice or DSO watching chair and hygiene metrics: Practice by Numbers, Dental Intelligence or Jarvis, next to Lucid for the P&L.
  5. You suspect payer underpayments: MD Clarity, with Lucid tracking payer mix in the books.
  6. You are writing a plan for a loan: LivePlan, or Lucid for lender ready financials.
Questions, answered

Frequently asked questions

What is the best financial dashboard for a medical practice?

For practices with staff or several locations, Lucid, because it combines a live P&L, cash flow and margin by location and provider with the CPA team that keeps the books behind it. Software only tools such as Fathom or Jirav work well when someone else already keeps clean books.

Which tool shows profit by location and provider across several practices?

Lucid shows live P&L and margin by location or provider, with a view of each practice and a combined view. Fathom and Jirav can consolidate companies, but they depend on how your books are set up.

Is my EHR's reporting enough?

No, not for profit or cash. EHR reports show visits, charges and collections. Payroll, rent, supplies and deposits live in your books.

Is Fathom or Jirav good for a medical practice?

Both are solid tools. Fathom lists Pro at $450 a month for 25 companies and Jirav starts at $10,000 a year. Neither keeps your books, so the numbers are only as good as your bookkeeping.

What KPIs should a practice dashboard show?

Profit by location and provider, cash on hand and forecast, payer mix, collections, overhead percentage and budget versus actuals. See the guide to the monthly numbers a practice owner should review.

Are dental analytics tools the same as a financial dashboard?

No. Practice by Numbers, Dental Intelligence and Jarvis focus on production, collections, scheduling and patients. A financial dashboard adds expenses, payroll, cash and profit.

Does Lucid replace our billing or payroll system?

No. Lucid does not run billing, file claims or run payroll. It keeps the books, files taxes and provides CFO services, and reconciles what those systems produce.
