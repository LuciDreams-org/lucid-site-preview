How do I choose a dental practice broker?

Short answer

Choose a dental-only broker with recent sales like yours, a fee in writing and a clear answer on whom it represents. Large networks such as Henry Schein Dental Practice Transitions, ADS Transitions, DDSmatch and PARAGON cover most states, while firms such as Tusk Practice Sales, McLerran and Associates and Legacy Practice Transitions describe seller-only models. Published guidance puts dental broker commissions at roughly 6% to 12% of the sale price, usually paid at closing. Interview three or four brokers, and compare how each values, markets and protects your practice before you sign.

Henry Schein Dental Practice Transitions

Henry Schein Dental Practice Transitions (DPT) is a dental brokerage and valuation firm and part of Henry Schein. It says more than 40 transition consultants live and work in the markets they serve, valuations are available in all 50 states, and sellers reach more than 36,000 registered purchasers. DPT states that it never represents both sides of a transaction. It has worked on dental transitions since 1993 and operates under licensed real estate broker entities.

Good fit: an owner who wants a local consultant backed by a large buyer database.

ADS Transitions

ADS Transitions is a national network of independent dental brokers. Its site lists hundreds of practices for sale nationwide, offers valuations from a broker, and helps owners weigh a DSO sale against other options. The model pairs local brokers with national marketing and a shared buyer database.

Good fit: an owner who wants a local broker with national listing exposure.

DDSmatch

DDSmatch covers dental practice sales, associate placement, partnerships, transition planning and sell side DSO representation. It also runs a practice preparation program for owners who plan to sell later. DDSmatch works through regional teams, such as DDSmatch South in the Southeast.

Good fit: an owner who may want an associate or partner first and a sale later.

PARAGON

PARAGON helps dentists sell, buy and merge practices, and also handles valuations, equity associateships and partnership buy ins and buy outs. Its consultants have worked together since 1988. PARAGON describes its model as dual representation: one consultant guides both the buyer and the seller toward a deal both sides accept. It reports closing 95% of the deals it takes part in, which is the firm's own figure.

Good fit: an owner selling to a private buyer who prefers a cooperative, one-consultant process.

McLerran and Associates

McLerran and Associates runs the Dental Transitions website and says it has 35 years of dental-only experience. It describes a CPA-led valuation, a structured 45 to 60 day multi-buyer process, and seller-only representation, with separate pools of private buyers and DSOs.

Good fit: an owner who wants a competitive process that tests private and DSO buyers side by side.

Tusk Practice Sales

Tusk Practice Sales is a healthcare sell side advisor based in Charlotte, North Carolina, serving owners across the United States. It describes itself as an exclusive sell side advisor for dental practices and says its analytics team rebuilds financials from the general ledger up to find add backs.

Good fit: a larger dental practice or group that expects DSO or private equity interest.

Legacy Practice Transitions

Legacy Practice Transitions is a national firm that reports more than 30 years in business and over 3,000 completed transitions. Its Southeast office, led by a former practicing dentist, covers North Carolina, South Carolina, Georgia and Florida. It says it works only for the seller and matches buyers on fit before price, keeping private buyers and associates on the table alongside DSOs.

Good fit: an owner in the Southeast who wants a private buyer who will keep the team in place.

Carroll and Company

Carroll and Company has provided dental practice sales and appraisals since 1963, with a Northern California focus. Its services include buyer screening and practice matching, help arranging financing, and contract coordination.

Good fit: a Northern California owner looking for a long-established local firm.

Broker, DSO advisor or selling direct

  1. Dental broker. Values the practice, markets it, screens buyers and helps close. Best for selling to another dentist or a small group.
  2. Sell side advisor for a DSO sale. Runs a process with several DSOs or private equity backed groups and compares cash, equity and your employment terms. DDSmatch and Tusk both describe this work. McLerran notes that up to 40% of a DSO deal can be paid in equity rather than cash. For the wider choice, see the guide on whether to sell your dental practice to a DSO.
  3. Selling direct. Works when you already have a buyer, such as your associate. DDSmatch South notes that some brokers offer reduced fees for deal management when you bring the buyer.

For the DSO path, read Should I sell my dental practice to a DSO?. To compare advisors for a larger sale, read How do I choose an M&A advisor for my practice?

How the listing process works

  1. Valuation. The broker reviews collections, overhead, patient base, staff, equipment, payer mix and local market, as Henry Schein DPT lists. Ask whether the valuation is done in house or by an outside firm.
  2. Listing agreement. Sets the fee, the exclusive period, the tail clause and how you can end it.
  3. Confidential marketing. A blind profile first, with an NDA before buyers see financials.
  4. Offers and letter of intent. Compare price, cash at closing, any equity, your time commitment and the noncompete.
  5. Due diligence and closing. The buyer and its lender review your books, contracts and lease.

Read how to prepare a medical practice for sale, which applies to dental practices as well, before you call a broker.

Seller only or dual representation

This is the biggest difference between dental brokers. Henry Schein DPT, McLerran, Tusk and the Legacy Southeast office all say they represent the seller only. PARAGON states that it represents both the buyer and the seller in one process. Each firm explains why it uses its model. Ask every broker which model it uses, who pays its fee, and whether it accepts any payment from buyers. Get the answers in the listing agreement.

Confidentiality

Staff who hear about a sale from a rumor can unsettle a practice fast. Ask the broker to describe its steps: a blind profile, an NDA before financials, and your approval before any buyer learns your name. Henry Schein DPT and Legacy Practice Transitions both describe confidential marketing as standard. Agree in advance when and how your team and patients will be told.

Fees, in plain numbers

  1. Commission. McLerran puts most sell side dental broker commissions at roughly 6% to 12% of the final deal value (August 22, 2026). DDSmatch South cites the ADA practice transitions guide at 8% to 12% (July 16, 2025).
  2. When it is earned. Ask whether the fee is earned at letter of intent or at closing. Many sellers prefer closing.
  3. Extra fees. DDSmatch South says some brokers charge for valuations, marketing materials or advertising. Elias Partners publishes $3,500 for a formal written valuation, with a free verbal range when you list (2026 site).
  4. Negotiation. Legacy Practice Transitions notes that some fee structures are negotiable, especially on larger multi-dentist practices.

Questions to ask before you sign

  1. How many practices like mine have you sold in the last two years, and can I call two of those sellers?
  2. Do you represent only me, or the buyer as well?
  3. Do you accept any referral fee or payment from buyers or DSOs?
  4. What is your commission, when is it earned, and are there extra fees?
  5. How long is the exclusive, what does the tail clause cover, and how do I end the agreement?
  6. Who does the valuation, and how do you handle add backs a buyer may challenge?
  7. Which buyers will you reach: private dentists, associates, groups or DSOs?
  8. What is your license, and in which states? DDSmatch South notes dental brokers typically need a real estate license.

How to choose by practice type

  1. Solo general dentist retiring and selling to a private buyer. A local consultant from a national network (Henry Schein DPT, ADS, DDSmatch, PARAGON) or a regional firm such as Carroll and Company.
  2. Practice with DSO interest. A seller-only firm that runs a multi-buyer process, such as McLerran or Tusk, and compare private and DSO offers side by side.
  3. Owner who wants an associate or partner first. DDSmatch or PARAGON, both of which list associateship and partnership work.
  4. Southeast owner focused on legacy and team. Legacy Practice Transitions Southeast.
  5. Already have a buyer. A broker for deal management only, plus an attorney and clean books.
Questions, answered

Frequently asked questions

How much does a dental practice broker charge?

Published guidance puts most dental broker commissions at about 6% to 12% of the sale price, usually paid at closing. DDSmatch South cites an 8% to 12% range from the ADA practice transitions guide. Few brokers publish their own rate, so get it in writing.

What is dual representation?

It means one broker works for both the buyer and the seller in the same sale. PARAGON uses this model, while Henry Schein DPT, McLerran and Tusk say they represent the seller only. Ask which model a broker uses before you sign.

How many brokers should I interview?

DDSmatch South suggests three or four. That is enough to compare fees, valuation methods and buyer reach without slowing you down.

Do I need a broker if my associate wants to buy?

Not always, but you still need a valuation, an attorney and clean books. Some brokers offer a reduced fee for deal management when you bring the buyer.

How long does it take to sell a dental practice?

It depends on the practice, market and buyer type. McLerran describes a 45 to 60 day marketing process, and DDSmatch South tells owners to plan on working with a broker for 6 to 12 months.

How are dental practices valued?

Brokers start with collections and adjusted earnings, often adjusted EBITDA for larger practices or seller's discretionary earnings for smaller solo practices. Overhead, payer mix, equipment and lease terms move the number. See how is a medical practice valued.

How will my buyer pay for the practice?

Most private buyers borrow, so the lender also reviews your financials. See best dental practice loans to understand what your buyer's lender will look for.
