Short answer

The cheapest dental equipment financing is usually the lowest total cost, not the lowest monthly payment: compare a dealer program (Henry Schein Financial Services, Patterson Dental, Benco's Clarion Financial), a bank equipment loan (Panacea Financial, U.S. Bank, Bank of America Practice Solutions, Wells Fargo Practice Finance) and a marketplace quote (Crestmont Capital, Axiant Partners) on the same equipment. Dealer programs win on speed and promotions, banks often win on long terms and existing relationships, and manufacturer rebates, such as Dentsply Sirona's CAD/CAM cash back for 2026, can beat a low rate. CBCT, CAD/CAM, chairs and lasers age at different speeds, so match the term to the useful life. Before you sign, check how many scans, crowns or procedures a month cover the payment.

Match the financing to the equipment

Dental equipment falls into two groups. Some of it lasts a decade or more. Some of it is technology you will want to replace in five to seven years. The term should not outlast the equipment's useful life to you.

Chairs, delivery units, cabinetry and the mechanical room. These last a long time and rarely go out of date. A loan or a $1 buyout lease usually costs less overall, and a long bank term keeps the payment low. This is where Panacea's 10 year equipment term or a practice loan from U.S. Bank fits.

CBCT and panoramic imaging. Large purchase, long life, but software and sensors move on. Many owners finance over five to seven years. Ask whether the price includes installation, training, software licenses and a service plan, because those can be bundled into the financed amount or left out.

Intraoral scanners and CAD/CAM mills. These change fastest. A shorter term, or a lease you can upgrade at the end, avoids paying for a scanner you no longer use. This is also where manufacturer rebates and trade in offers are most common.

Lasers. Value depends on how often you use it. Price it against the procedures you will actually do each month, not the brochure. If use is uncertain, a shorter term limits the risk.

Dealer and distributor financing

Henry Schein Financial Services

Henry Schein's dental financing page says you can finance up to 100% of the cost of equipment and technology, with same day approvals and terms from 1 to 15 years, plus leasehold improvement and working capital funds. Its "Route 66" program on equipment and technology purchases advertises no payments for 6 months, then $99 a month for 6 months, then level payments, with 5 to 7 year terms.

Henry Schein says it is not a bank, that transactions need credit approval by third party lenders, and that it may receive a marketing fee from the vendor. Its CBCT offers page listed a 6% rebate on orders placed by December 4, 2026.

Best for: owners buying through Henry Schein who want equipment, buildout and working capital from one contact.

Patterson Dental

Patterson Dental lists equipment and technology financing, small equipment financing and office design and project financing among its financial services for dental practices. It runs seasonal financing offers on CBCT, CAD/CAM, operatory and mechanical room equipment. Current terms and offer dates: see the vendor's site.

Best for: practices already buying from Patterson that want the quote and the financing together.

Benco Dental and Clarion Financial

Benco's equipment pages point to Clarion Financial, which says it offers 100% financing for equipment, real estate, practice remodels, supplies, relocations, start ups and practice transitions. Clarion's own site describes leasing of office equipment, furniture and dental equipment. Terms and rates: see its site.

Best for: Benco customers planning a remodel or a new operatory along with the equipment.

A note on dealer programs

Dealer financing is convenient, and a promotion with deferred payments can help while a new device ramps up. Two cautions. A deferral period usually makes the later payments larger or the term longer, so compare the total paid. And the financing is tied to that dealer's equipment quote, so get the equipment price and the financing priced separately before you compare with a bank.

Manufacturer rebates and promotions

Equipment makers often sell through dealers and offer rebates rather than loans. Dentsply Sirona's "T3 2026 CAD/CAM Cash Back" promotion gives a cash back rebate on qualifying CAD/CAM equipment bought through an authorized Dentsply Sirona dealer. It applies to equipment ordered between August 15, 2026 and December 31, 2026 and installed and invoiced by January 31, 2027, and the rebate is paid after installation.

A rebate and a low rate are different kinds of savings. A rebate is cash back after installation. A promotional rate lowers each payment. Ask the dealer whether you can combine the manufacturer rebate with outside financing, such as your bank, and then compare:

  1. The dealer's promotional financing, without the rebate if they cannot be combined.
  2. A bank equipment loan at its quoted rate, with the rebate.

Whichever leaves you with the lower total cost after the rebate is the cheaper deal. Promotions end, so confirm dates in writing before you order.

Bank equipment loans

Panacea Financial

Panacea Financial, a division of Primis Bank, lists new equipment loans with terms up to 10 years, along with startup, buy-in and expansion loans. It describes itself as the exclusive practice finance provider of ADA Member Advantage, which matters for ADA members.

U.S. Bank

U.S. Bank's dental loans page lists equipment among its loan uses, with practice loan terms up to 15 years and up to six months of interest-only payments. It describes rates as competitive fixed rates without a figure.

Bank of America Practice Solutions

Bank of America's dental loans page covers equipment along with startups, acquisitions, real estate and remodels. Some loans come with banking requirements, so ask whether you must move your operating accounts.

Wells Fargo Practice Finance

Wells Fargo Practice Finance offers loans to dental owners through practice finance specialists. Amounts and terms: see the lender's site.

A bank loan makes most sense when you are buying several pieces at once, already have a relationship with the bank, or want one loan instead of several dealer contracts.

Marketplaces and lender matching

Crestmont Capital's equipment page lists financing from $10,000 to $2 million with a typical term of 2 to 7 years, for practices at least 6 months in business with about $10,000 a month in revenue. Axiant Partners is a lender matching service, not a lender, and lists equipment loans and leases from $10,000 to $5 million, paid by its lending partners rather than the borrower. Both are useful to get several offers quickly. Compare them on the same terms as a bank quote.

Will the device pay for itself?

The real question behind any CBCT or mill is whether the extra production covers the payment. A simple check, with illustrative numbers only:

  1. Monthly cost. Payment plus service plan plus supplies or materials. Say $2,400 a month.
  2. Margin per use. What the practice keeps per scan or per same day crown after materials and any lab fee saved or added. Say $120 per use.
  3. Break even. $2,400 divided by $120 is 20 uses a month to cover the device.

If your schedule already supports well above the break even, the device likely pays for itself during the term. If you are not sure you will reach it, a shorter term, a smaller unit or waiting for a promotion may be the better choice. Use your own collections, not production, because write offs and plan fees reduce what you actually receive.

Tax timing

Section 179 and bonus depreciation can let you deduct much of the cost in the year the equipment is placed in service, which is why year end promotions are popular. The equipment must be installed and in use by December 31, not just ordered, and the deduction depends on your income and current rules. Limits and lease treatment are covered in our medical equipment financing guide and our guide to tax deductions for medical practice owners. This is general information, not tax advice.

How to choose

  1. Chairs, cabinetry and the mechanical room: a long bank term from Panacea, U.S. Bank or your own bank, or a dealer loan if the price is better.
  2. CBCT: get the dealer's program and a bank quote, and ask for service, software and training to be priced separately.
  3. Scanners and CAD/CAM: check manufacturer rebates first, then pick the shortest term your cash allows.
  4. Lasers: price against realistic monthly use and keep the term short.
  5. Several devices or a full remodel: one bank loan or a dealer project financing can be simpler than several contracts.
  6. Many quotes fast: Axiant or Crestmont, then compare every offer on the same terms.

For every quote, compare the rate, term, total paid, deferral terms, end of term option, fees, prepayment terms and any banking requirement. What lenders ask for is in our guide on what lenders look for in a practice loan.

Questions, answered

Frequently asked questions

What is the cheapest way to finance a CBCT?

Compare total cost, not monthly payment. Get the dealer's financing offer and a bank equipment loan quote on the same price, then add any manufacturer rebate to the option that allows it. The one with the lower total cost after the rebate is cheaper.

Is dealer financing or a bank loan better for dental equipment?

Dealer programs are fast and often carry promotions, such as Henry Schein's deferred payment program. Banks can offer long terms, such as Panacea's equipment loans up to 10 years. If you bank with one already, get both and compare.

Can I finance 100% of dental equipment?

Several sources say yes. Henry Schein Financial Services says up to 100% of equipment and technology, and Clarion Financial says 100% financing for equipment. Approval and terms depend on your credit and practice history.

Should I lease or buy dental chairs?

Chairs and cabinetry last many years, so a loan or a $1 buyout lease usually costs less overall. Leases you can return or upgrade suit fast changing technology, such as scanners, better than chairs.

Do manufacturer rebates work with outside financing?

It depends on the promotion. Dentsply Sirona's 2026 CAD/CAM rebate requires purchase through an authorized dealer and pays after installation. Ask the dealer in writing whether the rebate stays if you finance through your own bank.

How long should I finance a scanner or mill?

No longer than you expect to use it. Many owners replace scanners and mills within five to seven years, so a shorter term avoids paying for equipment you have replaced.

How do I book a dental equipment loan?

Record the equipment as an asset and the loan as a liability, then split each payment between principal and interest. Record any rebate as a reduction of the equipment cost unless your CPA says otherwise. See best bookkeeping for dental practices.
