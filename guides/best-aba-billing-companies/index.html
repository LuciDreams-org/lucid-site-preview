Short answer

If you want to keep your current ABA software, look first at the independent ABA billing companies: The Missing Piece, Cube Therapy Billing, Operant Billing Services and Plutus Health. If you would rather have billing run by your software vendor, compare CentralReach CR BillMax, Rethink Managed Billing Services and Raven Health Concierge, and note that Raven publishes its rate. Mental Health Billing SRG publishes a low rate and lists ABA, and Alpaca Health is a different model that pays BCBAs a set rate per session. Whichever you pick, check its results each month against the deposits in your bank.

The Missing Piece

The Missing Piece says it has over 15 years of exclusive focus on ABA billing. It offers end to end revenue cycle work, from benefits checks and authorizations to appeals and collections, and says you only pay when insurance pays you. It can work in your system or move your team onto its own practice management software.

Best for: clinics that want a biller that does only ABA. Limits: rates are not published, so ask for a written scope and notice period.

Cube Therapy Billing

Cube Therapy Billing verifies coverage and ABA benefits before sessions, submits authorization requests, tracks unit counts and alerts you before approvals expire. It reviews each claim for ABA codes and payer modifiers, and manages BCBA and RBT enrollment with commercial payers and Medicaid.

Best for: growing clinics with many denials or a backlog of unpaid claims. Limits: pricing is not published. Ask how many ABA clinics it bills for.

Operant Billing Services

Operant Billing Services lists ABA billing with A/R management, charge entry, claims management, and credentialing and enrollment.

Best for: clinics that want a dedicated contact and to keep their software. Limits: pricing and supported software are not stated, so see the vendor's site and ask on a call.

Plutus Health

Plutus Health runs a behavioral health billing team that names ABA as a focus. Its ABA billing services cover authorization management, RBT and BCBA service tracking, parent training billing and telehealth rules. It also bills surgical and emergency specialties.

Best for: agencies adding clinics quickly that want a larger billing team. Limits: pricing is not published, and ABA is one of several specialties.

CentralReach CR BillMax

CentralReach is a large ABA software platform, and CR BillMax is its managed billing service. CentralReach says you can offload the entire billing process to its team of billing experts, who follow up on payments, A/R and denials.

Best for: clinics already on CentralReach that want billing run by the same company. Limits: pricing is not published. Ask whether you can use it without CentralReach software. See our ABA practice management software guide for the platform itself.

Rethink Managed Billing Services

RethinkBH sells both billing software (BillAI) and Managed Billing Services. Its site says both run on the same technology, and the difference is who operates it: your billers or Rethink's ABA billing team. It also says it manages payer enrollment during onboarding.

Best for: clinics on RethinkBH that want to hand billing off. Limits: pricing is not published, and the service runs on Rethink's platform.

Raven Health Concierge

Raven Health sells ABA software and managed billing. Concierge is 5% of monthly claims paid. Concierge Pro is 7% and adds one dedicated ABA expert for billing, credentialing, authorizations and private pay. Both run on an annual contract with a $1,000 monthly minimum, which applies only when the percentage comes to less.

Best for: clinics that want a published rate and a single contact. Limits: the annual term and minimum. Raven's own example says a clinic billing $40,000 a month pays $2,000 on Concierge.

Mental Health Billing SRG

Mental Health Billing SRG bills for therapists, psychiatrists, psychologists, ABA providers and group practices. It lists 3.99% of what it collects, with credentialing, denials, patient statements and reporting included, and says ABA authorizations are obtained and tracked before units run out.

Best for: agencies that bill ABA alongside therapy or testing. Limits: it is not ABA only, so ask for ABA references.

Alpaca Health

Alpaca Health is not a billing company in the usual sense. It offers a practice in a box for BCBAs. It bills insurance, credentials you under contracts it already holds, and pays a set rate per session every two weeks, absorbing delays and denials.

Best for: BCBAs opening a practice who want no billing risk. Limits: it works only in certain states and only on its own platform, and its agreement runs one year with 90 days' notice. Like any network model, you give up rate control.

What makes ABA billing different

These are the jobs to hand a billing company, and the questions to ask about each one.

  1. Authorizations. Most ABA hours need payer approval, and each approval caps units for a set period. Ask who requests renewals, who watches units left, and how much notice you get before an authorization runs out.
  2. Unit billing. ABA codes such as 97153 bill in 15 minute units, and rules on rounding, overlapping sessions and who can render each code vary by payer. Ask how the biller checks units against session notes before claims go out.
  3. Modifiers and rendering providers. Payers differ on modifiers for RBT, BCaBA and BCBA work. Ask for the biller's payer rule list for your state.
  4. Medicaid. State Medicaid plans and managed care plans have their own rules and slower payment. Ask how many claims the biller sends to your state's plans today.
  5. Credentialing. Each new BCBA and RBT must be enrolled before claims pay. Ask whether credentialing is in the rate.

What outsourced ABA billing costs

Published ABA rates on vendors' own sites sit between 3.99% and 7% of collections, and most companies quote by volume. At $80,000 a month collected, 5% is $4,000 a month and 7% is $5,600. An in house biller costs salary, benefits, software seats and cover for time off. For billing pricing models in general, see what a medical billing company costs.

Software vendor service or independent biller?

A billing service from your software vendor, such as CR BillMax, Rethink or Raven, runs inside the platform your team already uses. Setup is simpler, and the billers see the same session notes and authorizations your staff see. The trade off is that billing and software come from one company, so leaving one can mean leaving both.

An independent ABA biller, such as The Missing Piece, Cube Therapy Billing or Operant Billing Services, works across platforms. You keep the freedom to change software later. Ask for read only access to their claim reports so you are not relying on summaries.

How to choose

  1. One clinic, BCBA owner, new to insurance: compare Alpaca Health and Raven Health Concierge, then get a quote from an independent biller.
  2. On CentralReach or RethinkBH already: price the vendor's own service, then one independent ABA biller that works in your platform.
  3. Several clinics or states: talk to Plutus Health, The Missing Piece and Cube Therapy Billing, and ask for references at your size.
  4. Large denial backlog: ask Cube Therapy Billing and Operant Billing Services how they work aged claims.
  5. ABA beside therapy or testing: add Mental Health Billing SRG to the list.

Ask every company four questions. What is the percentage charged on, and what is the minimum? Who owns authorizations? How fast are denials worked? What reports will I get by payer and by location?

Questions, answered

Frequently asked questions

Should my ABA clinic outsource billing?

Often, yes, when you have no trained biller, denials are rising or authorizations keep lapsing. Outsourcing turns staff cost into a percent of collections. Compare the fee at your own collections with the full cost of a biller.

What percentage do ABA billing companies charge?

Published rates run from 3.99% to 7% of collections, and Raven Health adds a $1,000 monthly minimum. Most others quote by volume and payer mix.

Who handles authorizations when I outsource ABA billing?

It depends on the plan. Raven Health Concierge Pro, The Missing Piece, Cube Therapy Billing, Plutus Health and Mental Health Billing SRG list authorization work. Get it in writing, since authorizations are a top cause of unpaid units.

Do I have to change software to outsource billing?

Not always. Independent billers such as The Missing Piece and Cube Therapy Billing say they work in other platforms. CentralReach, Rethink and Raven services run on their own software.

How long does it take to switch ABA billing companies?

Plan for a few weeks of setup and payer enrollment. Claims already sent keep paying after you switch, so agree in writing who works them.

How do I know my ABA biller is collecting everything?

Match each payer deposit to its remittance, review A/R aging by payer and track your net collection rate each month. Your bookkeeper or accountant can do this from the bank side.

Does Lucid do ABA billing or authorizations?

No. Lucid does not file or chase insurance claims, run billing, track authorizations or run payroll. Your billing company and ABA platform do that. Lucid keeps your books, taxes and CFO planning.
