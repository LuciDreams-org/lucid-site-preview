Short answer

For a practice with staff and several providers, start with athenahealth or CareCloud if you want billing bundled with the EHR, and with Medical Billers and Coders, Practolytics or NCG Medical if you want a dedicated billing team on your current system. Tebra can match you with a billing partner if you already use Tebra, and MedBillingExperts suits practices that want dedicated offshore billing staff. R1, AGS Health and Ensemble are built for large groups, MSOs and health systems. Whichever you pick, check its work every month against the cash that actually lands in your bank.

athenahealth (athenaOne)

athenahealth sells athenaOne as EHR, practice management and billing in one cloud system. Its cost and value page says the price is based on a percentage of collections, and the rate comes by quote. Third party guides such as Pabau estimate a base EHR license near $140 per provider a month plus 4 to 7 percent of collections, but athenahealth does not publish those figures.

Best for: practices that want one vendor for charting and claims. Limits: the rate is not published, and the fit is strongest if you also use its EHR.

CareCloud

CareCloud offers managed billing services tailored to over 70 specialties, per its billing services page. Its annual report describes its standard end to end fee as a percentage of each client's healthcare revenue, with a monthly minimum fee and a one time setup fee. Business.com notes it does not publish standard pricing.

Best for: practices that want software and outsourced billing from the same company. Limits: no public rate. Ask for the minimum and the setup fee in writing.

Medical Billers and Coders (MBC)

MBC says it has more than 25 years in medical billing and offers end to end revenue cycle work, coding, credentialing and scribing. Its pricing page says it does not use a generic rate card and quotes by specialty. It offers a free 90 day AR analysis to new prospects.

Best for: multispecialty groups and practices that want specialty coders working inside their current EHR. Limits: no public rate. Software Finder estimates 3 to 9 percent of collections from industry benchmarks, not from MBC.

Practolytics

Practolytics says it is trusted by over 1,400 practices across 31 states. It is one of the few billing companies that publishes a rate: its behavioral health brochure lists four plans from 3.49 to 4.99 percent of net collections, a $750 monthly minimum, and setup fees waived on 12 month agreements. Its site also lists dermatology, orthopedic, oncology and urgent care billing.

Best for: behavioral health groups, including psychiatry, TMS and Spravato programs. Limits: the published tiers are for behavioral health. Other specialties are quoted.

NCG Medical

NCG Medical, based in Florida, says it has provided medical billing and revenue cycle work since 1979. Its service pages cover family medicine, dermatology, gastroenterology, radiation oncology and surgery centers, and its pages describe certified coders, MBAs and CPAs on its team.

Best for: practices that want a US based partner that works like part of the office. Limits: pricing is not published.

Tebra managed billing

Tebra does not do the billing itself. Its managed billing page says a Tebra rep reviews your goals and matches you with a vetted billing partner that handles claims, denials and reimbursements, while you keep your EHR and see partner performance in Tebra. It says most practices are connected in 3 to 4 weeks.

Best for: practices already on Tebra, especially primary care, pediatrics, mental health and nurse practitioner practices. Limits: the fee is set by the partner, so compare partners the same way you would compare any billing company.

MedBillingExperts (OutsourceRCM)

MedBillingExperts, now branded OutsourceRCM, is part of the Flatworld Solutions group. Its pricing page lists a dedicated staff model at $1,300 to $1,800 per full time person a month, plus percentage and per transaction options without published rates. It says the prices are tentative.

Best for: practices or billing teams that want added staff hours and will manage the work. Limits: you need someone in house to direct and review the team.

R1 RCM

R1 serves health systems, hospitals and provider groups. Its pages describe revenue cycle management for groups across specialties and EHRs.

Best for: large groups that want enterprise scale and technology. Limits: built for size. A small group may not be a priority account.

AGS Health

AGS Health lists provider groups, MSOs, ancillary providers and specialty practices among its clients, alongside nearly half of the 20 most prominent US hospitals. It offers coding, billing, denials and patient financial services.

Best for: MSOs and larger groups that want coding and AR capacity. Limits: enterprise focus, and no public pricing.

Ensemble Health Partners

Ensemble runs end to end revenue cycle work for health systems, including hospitals and their affiliated provider groups, per its investor Berkshire Partners.

Best for: a practice owned by or tied to a health system. Limits: not aimed at independent practices.

How to choose

  1. One to five providers on a common EHR: compare athenahealth, CareCloud and Tebra partners, and one independent company such as NCG Medical.
  2. Five to twenty providers, several specialties: get quotes from MBC, Practolytics, NCG Medical and CareCloud, using the same claim data for each.
  3. Behavioral health group: Practolytics publishes rates, which makes the first comparison easy. Dietitian or speech therapy group: see billing services for dietitians and SLPs.
  4. MSO or large group: talk to R1, AGS Health and Ensemble.
  5. You have a billing lead in house who needs more hands: price MedBillingExperts staff hours.

Ask every company the same questions:

  1. What percent, minimum and setup fee apply at my collections?
  2. What counts as a collection? Patient payments, old AR and secondary claims are sometimes included.
  3. Which work is extra? Credentialing, patient statements, appeals and old AR cleanup are common add ons.
  4. Can I get monthly reports by payer, provider and location, with days in AR and denial rate?
  5. What happens to my data and open claims if I leave?

How to check your biller from the bank side

A billing company reports what it posted. Your bank shows what actually arrived. These are not the same thing until someone ties them together.

  1. Match each payer deposit to its remittance. One insurance deposit can cover dozens of claims.
  2. Track your collection rate and days in AR every month, not only when something feels wrong.
  3. Watch the billing fee as a percent of collections. If it creeps up, ask why.
  4. Review AR aging by payer. A balance that keeps sliding into the 90 plus day bucket is a question for your biller.
Questions, answered

Frequently asked questions

Is it worth outsourcing medical billing for a group practice?

Often, yes, when the in house team is small, turnover is high, or denials are growing. Outsourcing turns staff cost into a percent of collections. Compare the full cost at your own collections before deciding.

What do medical billing companies charge?

Most charge a percentage of collections, commonly 4 to 10 percent, with 5 to 8 percent typical for small and mid size practices, per vendor guides. Some charge per claim or a fixed monthly fee. Minimums and setup fees are common.

Which billing companies publish their prices?

Few do. Practolytics publishes behavioral health tiers and MedBillingExperts publishes a staff rate. Most others quote by specialty and volume.

How long does it take to switch billing companies?

Tebra says most practices connect to a partner in 3 to 4 weeks. Plan for old claims to keep paying out for months, and agree who works them.

Do billing companies handle credentialing?

Many offer it, often as an add on. MBC and Practolytics both list credentialing. Ask whether it is in the base rate.

How do I know my billing company is collecting everything?

Reconcile payer deposits to remits each month and review AR aging by payer. Track your net collection rate and days in AR over time. Your bookkeeper or accountant can do this from the bank side.
