What do lenders look for in a practice loan?

Short answer

Lenders want proof that your practice makes enough cash to cover the new payment with room to spare. They check three years of tax returns and financial statements plus current interim statements, a debt schedule, debt service coverage, collections history, AR aging, projections, and a personal guarantee from the main owners. SBA 7(a) loans set written minimums: a debt service coverage ratio of at least 1.15 on standard loans and an unlimited guaranty from anyone owning 20 percent or more. Conventional practice lenders often look for a coverage ratio closer to 1.2 to 1.25, and the practice with clean, current books gets the faster yes.

Financial statements

Lenders start with your profit and loss statement and balance sheet. SBA rules for standard 7(a) loans ask lenders to analyze the three most recent years of financial information plus an interim statement. A conventional bank asks for much the same.

What trips practices up:

  1. Cash basis books with no receivables. A lender wants to see what payers owe you. Accrual or management view statements show it. See cash vs accrual accounting for medical practices.
  2. Books that are months behind. An interim statement that stops in June tells a lender nothing about today.
  3. Personal spending mixed into the practice. Every personal charge becomes a question in underwriting.
  4. One lump of revenue. Lenders want collections by payer or service line, and by location if you have several.

Lenders also look at EBITDA, the cash a practice earns before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization. A healthcare lender quoted in a February 2026 practice management article said lenders typically look for EBITDA margins of 10 to 20 percent for most outpatient specialties, with surgical groups often higher and primary care lower.

Tax returns

Tax returns are the lender's check on your statements. SBA lenders run a tax verification step to confirm that the business filed its returns and that the statements in the application agree with what was filed with the IRS. An applicant that has not filed required federal returns is not eligible for SBA financing.

If your P&L shows more profit than your return, expect hard questions. The usual causes are year-end adjustments that never reached the books, or books that were never closed. Fix the gap before you apply, not during underwriting.

Debt service coverage

Debt service coverage ratio (DSCR) is the number most lenders decide on. It is operating cash flow divided by all required principal and interest payments, including the new loan.

  1. Example: your practice produces $300,000 of cash flow a year after a fair owner salary. Existing and new loan payments total $220,000 a year. DSCR is 300,000 divided by 220,000, about 1.36.
  2. SBA standard 7(a) loans need a DSCR of at least 1.15 on a historical or projected basis, and 1.0 on a global basis that includes the owners and affiliates. SBA small loans use 1.10.
  3. Medical Economics, in a November 2026 article, says lenders generally want 20 to 25 percent more cash than debt payments, a ratio of roughly 1.2 to 1.25.

Lenders adjust cash flow before they divide. SBA rules list owner's draw, distributions for S corp taxes, rent, non-recurring income and unfunded capital spending as items a lender must justify. That is why owner add-backs need a clear, documented schedule.

Collections history and payer mix

Lenders care about what you collect, not what you bill. They look at trailing collections, usually by month, to see whether revenue is steady, growing or slipping. They also look at payer mix: Medicare, Medicaid, commercial insurance and self-pay. A practice that depends on one payer, or on a payer cutting rates, carries more risk.

For an acquisition, the lender also asks whether the collections will stay when the seller leaves. Buyers should show their own production next to the seller's history. See how is a medical practice valued.

AR aging

Accounts receivable aging shows how fast payers pay you. The same lender said days in AR under 40 to 45 is very strong, and that balances over 90 days should be less than 15 percent of total AR. Old AR that will never be paid weakens both your balance sheet and your collateral.

Bring an AR aging report by payer, and be ready to explain anything over 90 days. If your billing system and your books disagree on AR, reconcile them first.

Projections

Startups, acquisitions and expansions are approved on projections. SBA rules ask for detailed projections with supporting assumptions that reach a DSCR of 1.15 within two years of funding. When the most recent year does not show enough coverage on an existing business, SBA lenders must get two years of projections and the assumptions behind them.

Good projections show:

  1. Monthly revenue by provider or service, with a ramp for new providers.
  2. Payer lag, so cash arrives later than visits.
  3. Staff, rent and supply costs that match the expansion.
  4. The new loan payment and every existing payment.
  5. A downside case, such as a slower ramp or a payer rate cut.

Debt schedule

Lenders want a current schedule of every loan, equipment lease, line of credit and shareholder loan, with balances, rates, payments and maturity dates. SBA rules call out a debt schedule prepared by the applicant, including any shareholder debt. Missing a lease or a merchant cash advance and having the lender find it later damages trust fast.

Personal guarantees

Expect to sign one. Under SBA rules, any individual who owns 20 percent or more of the applicant must provide an unlimited full guaranty, and each guarantor provides a personal financial statement dated within 90 days of approval. If no one owns 20 percent, at least one owner must still guarantee. Conventional practice lenders usually ask owners to guarantee too, though terms vary by bank. Ask whether a guarantee is limited, unlimited, or reduces as the loan pays down.

SBA 7(a) loans cover working capital, equipment, real estate, refinancing and ownership changes. SBA 504 loans are for real estate and long-life equipment, with 10, 20 and 25 year terms, fees of about 3 percent of the debt, and a certified development company working with a bank. SBA loans also require that the borrower cannot get the same credit on reasonable terms elsewhere.

Conventional practice loans come from banks with healthcare teams, such as those in our guide to the best banks for medical practices. Several of these lenders say they offer up to 100 percent financing for practices. Online lenders and fintech lines, such as Bluevine's line of credit up to $250,000, are faster but smaller.

How to get lender-ready in 30 days

  1. Close the books through last month, on accrual or with an AR view.
  2. Reconcile every bank, card and loan account.
  3. Match the last three years of statements to the filed tax returns, and explain any gap.
  4. Build an add-back schedule for owner pay, one-time costs and personal items.
  5. Pull an AR aging report by payer and clean up old balances.
  6. List every debt, lease and line in one schedule.
  7. Build a two year projection with a downside case, and calculate DSCR with the new loan.
  8. Update each owner's personal financial statement.

Then apply to two or three lenders in the same week with the same package. You compare real offers instead of waiting on one bank.

Questions, answered

Frequently asked questions

What documents do I need for a practice loan?

Expect three years of business tax returns, three years of financial statements, an interim P&L and balance sheet, a debt schedule, AR aging, projections and each owner's personal financial statement. Acquisitions also need the seller's records and the purchase agreement.

What DSCR do I need for a practice loan?

SBA standard 7(a) loans need at least 1.15, and SBA small loans 1.10. Many conventional lenders look for about 1.2 to 1.25. Higher is better.

Do I have to personally guarantee a practice loan?

For SBA loans, yes, if you own 20 percent or more. Conventional lenders usually ask for one too. Ask whether it is limited or unlimited.

Is an SBA loan better than a bank loan for my practice?

An SBA loan can be larger or longer when history or collateral is thin, but the paperwork is heavier. A conventional practice loan is often faster for an established practice with strong coverage.

Can I get a loan to start a new practice with no history?

Yes, practice lenders fund startups, and some advertise up to 100 percent financing. Approval rests on your projections, your experience and your personal finances.

Do lenders care whether my books are cash or accrual?

They care that the books are complete and current. Accrual or AR-inclusive statements show what payers owe you, which helps the lender see real performance.

How long does a practice loan take?

It depends on the lender and the loan type. The biggest delay is usually missing or inconsistent financials, so a complete package up front speeds every lender.
