Which dental membership plan software is best?

Short answer

For a practice that wants the plan designed, marketed and run for it, Clerri (formerly Kleer) is the full service choice, priced by quote. For a practice that wants to run its own plan on published pricing, BoomCloud is the best known self serve option, with flat fee rivals such as Dentaloo and Practice One Plans and low entry options such as DentalHQ and illumitrac. If you already run Curve or CareStack, their built in membership tools may be enough, while Open Dental and Dentrix practices usually pair the practice software with an add-on such as Clerri or BoomCloud. Whatever you pick, a yearly fee paid up front is deferred revenue, earned over the plan year.

Why owners are asking

About 72 million US adults, or 27 percent, have no dental insurance, per CareQuest Institute. A membership plan gives those patients a monthly or yearly price for preventive care plus a discount on other work, and it gives the practice recurring revenue. It is also the usual bridge when a practice drops a PPO, as our guide to dropping dental PPOs explains.

Three ways vendors charge

  1. Flat software fee. One price per location, unlimited members. Dentaloo and Practice One Plans work this way. Cost stays fixed as the plan grows.
  2. Software fee plus a per member fee. BoomCloud charges a base fee and a per member amount that, per BoomCloud, is built into the price members pay along with card processing. illumitrac has no monthly fee but builds about $3 to $4 per member into dues.
  3. Quote for a full service program. Clerri prices per practice and includes plan design, marketing and a success manager.

The options, one by one

1. Clerri (formerly Kleer)

Kleer and Membersy merged in 2024 and took the Clerri name in 2025. Clerri says it runs membership plans for more than 25,000 dentists, from single offices to 7 of the 10 largest DSOs. It does not publish a price list: pricing is quoted per practice, setup is free and practices are month to month, with a dedicated success manager, training and patient marketing. It lists direct integrations with 13 practice management systems, including a native two-way Dentrix integration and an integration inside Open Dental.

Best for: practices and groups that want the plan built and run for them. Limits: no published price, so compare quotes.

2. BoomCloud

BoomCloud publishes its pricing: Basic is $197 a month per location plus $4.50 per member a month, and Pro is $397 a month per location plus $3.50 per member a month. BoomCloud says the per member amounts and standard card processing are built into the membership price members pay, not added to the practice's software bill. Pro adds payment plans, a dedicated success rep and practice software connections. There is a 30 day free trial and no setup fee, and multi-location groups get custom pricing.

Best for: practices that want to run their own plan on known pricing. Limits: you design the plan and handle state rules with your own advisers.

3. DentalHQ

DentalHQ's enrollment page lists DHQ Pro at $109 a month, with unlimited plans, member payment processing, single and multi-location dashboards and reminders, and a month free on annual billing. See the vendor's site for current terms.

Best for: practices that want a low monthly fee and group dashboards. Limits: check processing and any setup fee before you sign.

4. illumitrac

illumitrac uses a one time setup fee with no monthly software fee. Its FAQ says patients pay about $3 to $4 per member a month inside their dues, which covers processing, maintenance and support, and the practice keeps its full membership fee.

Best for: small practices testing a plan with little fixed cost. Limits: the per member amount grows with the plan.

5. Dentaloo

Dentaloo's Standard plan is $150 a month per location with unlimited members and plans, no per patient fees and no transaction caps, for groups with up to four locations. Larger groups get custom pricing.

Best for: practices expecting many members. Limits: a newer name; confirm your practice software link on a demo.

6. Practice One Plans

Practice One Plans lists a starting price of $149 a month, unlimited members, no per member fees and more than 18 practice software integrations.

Best for: flat fee buyers on less common practice software. Limits: confirm what the starting tier includes.

7. Curve Memberships

Curve builds membership plans into its cloud practice software. Its platform terms state that a plan created on the platform is not a contract of insurance and urge practices to consult their legal advisers. Pricing is by quote.

Best for: practices already on Curve. Limits: only for Curve users.

8. CareStack

CareStack lists membership and payment plans in its Essentials tier, from $829 a month, alongside claims, eligibility and reporting. It makes sense when you are choosing new practice software anyway. Our dental practice management software guide compares it with the rest.

Best for: practices replacing their practice software. Limits: a large change to get one feature.

9. Open Dental and Dentrix with an add-on

Open Dental's built in Discount Plans feature applies plan discounts automatically and can be used for in-house membership plans, but its manual describes discounts, not recurring billing. Most Open Dental practices add a platform such as Clerri, which is integrated with Open Dental, or BoomCloud. Dentrix does not list a native membership feature; Clerri offers a native two-way Dentrix integration, and BoomCloud says it runs alongside Dentrix, Eaglesoft and Open Dental.

Best for: practices that want to keep their current software. Limits: two systems to reconcile.

How membership revenue is recognized

This is where most practices get the books wrong.

  1. Monthly dues are earned in the month they cover. Book them as membership revenue that month.
  2. Yearly fees paid up front are a liability on the day they arrive, called deferred revenue, and are earned across the plan year. A $420 yearly fee is about $35 of revenue a month. Booked as income on receipt, it makes the month of sign up look strong and later months look weak.
  3. The treatment discount is not an expense. Record the treatment at the member price you actually charge.
  4. Platform payouts arrive net of fees. Record gross dues as revenue and the platform and card fees as an expense, so payouts tie to the bank.
  5. Cancellations and refunds reduce the deferred balance, not current month revenue.
  6. Tax timing can differ from your books. A cash basis practice generally reports fees when received. An accrual basis practice may be able to defer part of an advance payment to the next tax year under IRS rules. Our cash vs accrual guide explains the difference; confirm with your CPA.

Track membership revenue by plan, member count, churn and renewals each month, separate from insurance collections.

Compliance in one paragraph

An in-house plan is a package of your own services for a fee, not insurance. Some states regulate discount plans and prepaid dental arrangements, and many vendors leave state rules to the practice and its attorney. Ask every vendor in writing what it has checked for your state.

How to choose

  1. You want it done for you, or you run several offices: get a Clerri quote and compare it with BoomCloud's group pricing.
  2. You want to run it yourself on known pricing: BoomCloud, Dentaloo or Practice One Plans.
  3. You want the lowest fixed cost to test the idea: illumitrac or DentalHQ.
  4. You are on Curve or choosing CareStack: start with the built in tool.
  5. You are on Open Dental or Dentrix and staying: pick an add-on with a direct integration so members and payments sync.

Questions, answered

Frequently asked questions

What is the best dental membership plan software?

Clerri for a full service program, BoomCloud for a self serve plan with published pricing, and Dentaloo or Practice One Plans for a flat fee with no per member charge. Curve and CareStack users can start with the built in tools.

Is Kleer the same as Clerri?

Yes. Kleer and Membersy merged in 2024 and became Clerri in 2025. Reviews that still say Kleer describe Clerri.

How much does dental membership software cost?

Published fees run from a one time setup fee with per member amounts in dues, to $109 to $397 a month per location. Clerri quotes per practice.

Can Open Dental or Dentrix run a membership plan on their own?

Open Dental's Discount Plans feature applies discounts but does not run recurring billing, so most practices add a platform. Dentrix practices typically use an add-on such as Clerri or BoomCloud.

How do I book a yearly membership fee?

As deferred revenue when received, then earned monthly across the plan year. Monthly dues are revenue in the month they cover.

Is a dental membership plan insurance?

No. It is a package of your own services for a fee. State rules on discount and prepaid plans vary, so check yours with counsel.

How many members do I need to cover the software?

Divide your monthly software cost by the profit per member after the cost of the included visits. At $150 a month and $20 of profit per member, eight members cover it.
