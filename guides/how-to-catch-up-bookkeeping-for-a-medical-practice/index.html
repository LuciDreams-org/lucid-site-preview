How do I catch up on bookkeeping that is months behind?

Short answer

Catching up means rebuilding each missed month in order: collect every bank, card, loan and payroll statement, reconcile each account back to the statement balance, then categorize and review the transactions. Start with the oldest unfiled return deadline, because late business returns carry penalties that grow each month. Practices usually get the best result from a cleanup project with a defined scope and a fixed price, followed by a normal monthly routine so the backlog does not return. First, find out how far behind you are

Behind means different things. Sort your situation into one of four groups, because each needs a different plan.

  1. A few months of unreconciled transactions, with the last tax return already filed.
  2. A full year or more with no reconciliation, and a return that was filed from estimates or from the bank balance.
  3. A return that is late, or about to be late.
  4. Several entities, or several locations, where only some sites are behind.

Write down, for each group, the oldest month with no reconciliation, the oldest tax return not filed, and any payroll tax filings you are unsure about. That list is the scope of work. Any firm you talk to will ask for it.

What to gather before anyone starts

You can speed up the work a great deal by collecting documents first.

  1. Bank and credit card statements for every month in the backlog, for every account the practice used, including accounts a partner or manager used for practice expenses.
  2. Loan and line of credit statements, and equipment lease schedules.
  3. Payroll reports from your payroll provider: quarterly filings, year end forms and the pay register.
  4. Deposit detail from your billing system or clearinghouse, so insurance deposits can be matched to what was billed.
  5. Prior tax returns, the last filed set of financial statements and the current chart of accounts.
  6. Any IRS or state notices.
  7. Invoices or receipts for large purchases such as equipment or build outs.

Missing statements can usually be downloaded from the bank. Records that cannot be found are listed and handled later, as questions for the owner.

The catch up process, step by step

  1. Fix the foundation. Confirm the opening balances. The opening balance of the first month you are fixing should agree with the last month that was properly reconciled, or with the prior filed return.
  2. Reconcile each account month by month, oldest first. Each account is reconciled to the statement, one month at a time. A gap in one month breaks the next, so skipping ahead causes rework.
  3. Categorize. Assign every transaction to the right account in the chart of accounts. Group routine items by rule, and review unusual items one at a time.
  4. Handle the medical practice items. Insurance deposits usually cover many patients and many dates of service, and have adjustments. Patient payments, card processor deposits and cash pay sales are separate streams. Reconcile each to its report. Lucid does not run billing or file claims.
  5. Clean up payroll. Compare payroll reports to the books each quarter, and check that withholdings and employer taxes were deposited.
  6. Treat owner items. Owner draws, personal expenses paid by the practice and loans between owners and the practice are common sources of mess. They need a written treatment your CPA agrees with.
  7. Review and lock. A CPA or reviewer checks the finished months against statements and prior returns. The closed months are then locked so nobody edits them by accident.
  8. Produce reports. Profit and loss, balance sheet and cash flow for every month rebuilt. At this point you can see, for the first time in a while, where the practice actually stands.

Do the deadlines first

Your tax filing status decides the order of work. The IRS lists the failure to file penalty as 5 percent of the tax due for each month or part of a month a return is late, up to a maximum of 25 percent. For returns due after December 31, 2025, the IRS lists a minimum penalty of $525 (or the full tax if smaller) for individual and most corporate returns more than 60 days late.

Partnerships and S corporations are treated differently. The IRS lists a penalty of $255 per partner or shareholder for each month or part of a month a Form 1065 or Form 1120-S is late, for up to 12 months, for returns due after December 31, 2025. A practice with three owners that files an S corporation return five months late would face a penalty computed on those three owners for five months. The IRS instructions for Form 1120-S state that the penalty does not apply if the corporation shows reasonable cause.

The due date for a calendar year S corporation return is the 15th day of the third month after the tax year ends, which was March 16, 2026 for 2025 returns because March 15 fell on a Sunday. The corporation can request an automatic extension on Form 7004, filed by that date. An extension gives more time to file, not more time to pay, so estimated tax still matters.

Payroll taxes need special care. Withheld income and employment taxes are trust fund taxes. The IRS says the Trust Fund Recovery Penalty can be assessed against a person who is responsible for collecting or paying those taxes and who willfully fails to do so, and that the penalty equals the unpaid balance of the trust fund tax. If your catch up work finds payroll deposits that were missed, tell your CPA right away so the issue is handled before anything else.

Can the penalties be reduced?

Sometimes. The IRS offers relief in two common forms.

  1. Reasonable cause. If the failure had a good reason, the IRS can waive a penalty. Your CPA will gather the facts and write the request.
  2. First time abate. The IRS describes it as an administrative waiver for taxpayers with a clean compliance history, including timely filing for the prior three years and no recent penalties. The IRS says you must contact it to request this relief.

Neither is automatic, and relief does not remove the tax itself. The failure to pay penalty can keep growing until the tax is paid. Ask your CPA whether either applies to you.

What if a past return was wrong?

Catch up work often shows that an earlier return used estimates. If the books show a different number, your CPA may recommend an amended return. Amended returns can lower or raise the tax. The IRS says a claim for credit or refund generally has to be filed within 3 years from the date you filed the original return or 2 years from the date you paid the tax. Lucid's blog explains the three year and two year refund rules for medical practices.

Records matter here. The IRS says generally to keep records for 3 years from the filing of the return, 6 years if you did not report income that exceeds 25 percent of gross income shown, and employment tax records for at least 4 years. If you never filed a return, the IRS says to keep records indefinitely.

Keep it from happening again

  1. A monthly close date. Pick a day each month, such as the 10th, when last month is reconciled and locked.
  2. One owner of the process. A named person, in house or outside, who is responsible.
  3. Direct feeds. Connect bank and card accounts to your accounting system so transactions arrive daily. Lucid says its bank and card connections are read only.
  4. Billing exports. Send the deposit detail from your billing system each month so deposits can be matched.
  5. A monthly look. Ten minutes on a clear report. Lucid reports include P&L and cash flow by location or provider.
Questions, answered

Frequently asked questions

My practice's books are months behind. Where do I start?

List the oldest unreconciled month, the oldest unfiled return and any payroll issues. Gather statements for every account, then reconcile oldest month first. File or extend any late return before finishing the full cleanup.

What happens if I file my business taxes late?

The IRS lists a penalty of 5 percent of the unpaid tax per month, up to 25 percent, for most returns. Partnerships and S corporations face a per owner monthly penalty. Penalties can sometimes be reduced, so ask your CPA.

Can I catch up the books myself?

Yes, if the backlog is small and you know how to reconcile. For a year or more, several accounts or payroll gaps, a professional usually finishes faster and with fewer errors. A CPA should review the result either way.

How much does bookkeeping cleanup cost?

Prices vary by months, accounts and condition of records. Lucid scopes first and gives a fixed price, so you know the number before work starts. Ask any firm whether the quote is fixed or hourly.

Do I need to catch up before I file taxes?

Your return needs accurate numbers, so books should be reconciled for the year first, or the return is filed on best available figures and amended later. Your CPA decides which approach fits. Do not let a deadline pass without filing or extending.

What if I cannot find some statements?

Banks and card issuers can provide copies of past statements, often online. Items that cannot be documented are listed for the owner and the CPA to decide how to treat them. Do not guess at missing figures.
