Short answer

For an independent primary care group, the usual shortlist is athenaOne, eClinicalWorks, Elation and NextGen, with Epic when a local health system offers it. Pediatric groups should start with the two pediatric-only systems, Office Practicum and PCC, which are built around vaccines, well visits and state immunization registries. eClinicalWorks and Elation publish per provider prices, while the rest quote. None of them keeps your books, so plan payer deposits, per member payments and cash forecasting separately.

What a primary care or pediatric group needs from an EHR

Primary care and pediatrics run on volume, fee schedules and follow-up. A busy group sees many short visits a day, manages patients with chronic conditions between visits, and increasingly earns part of its income from quality bonuses, care management codes or per member payments. Pediatrics adds vaccines, well-child schedules and registry reporting on top.

  1. Fast charting for high volume visits, with well visit and chronic condition templates.
  2. Chronic care management: enrollment, care plans, time tracking and the billing codes that go with them.
  3. Immunizations: forecasting, registry reporting and, for pediatrics, private and VFC stock as a software feature.
  4. Value-based care reporting: quality measures, MIPS, gaps in care and support for ACO or payer contracts.
  5. Billing and payer reporting by location and provider.
  6. Exports your accountant can use: charges, payments and adjustments by payer, provider and location.

For a wider look across specialties, see our guide to practice management software. If your practice runs on memberships instead of insurance, our guide to the best direct primary care software covers that model.

athenaOne

athenaOne combines the EHR, billing and practice management, and patient engagement in one cloud system. athenahealth says athenaOne supports practices whether they are new to value-based care or managing several contracts, with care management, population health tools and a library of pay for performance and quality recognition programs. In a November 10, 2026 release, athenahealth said more than 95% of eligible athenaOne clinicians earned positive MIPS payment adjustments.

athenahealth says its pricing corresponds to your collections, and no rate is published.

Best for: groups that want billing and quality programs handled by the vendor. Watch for: a fee tied to collections grows with revenue, so model it against a per provider fee.

eClinicalWorks

eClinicalWorks is a general ambulatory EHR used widely in primary care and pediatrics. Its pricing page lists $499 per provider a month for the EHR and $599 with practice management, and RCM as a Service at 2.9% of practice collections. Its chronic care management module supports enrollment and care plans for at-risk patients, and in April 2026 it announced a CCM Specialist Service with healow, where its staff handle outreach for the practice. Its value-based care suite covers population health reporting.

Best for: groups that want a published price and built-in CCM. Watch for: fees for statements, messaging and add-ons sit outside the base price, so ask for a full quote.

Elation

Elation is a cloud EHR built for independent primary care. Its product guide lists Standard Provider at $425 per provider a month, Standard DPC Provider at $375, Standard Non-Prescribing Provider at $215 and Premium Provider at $500. Elation says it no longer offers month to month contracts and requires an annual commitment.

For value-based care, Elation lists MIPS reporting, CQM dashboards with a real time view of quality measure performance, automatic coding, clinical reminders and risk assessment tools. Its help center notes it is certified to a set of eCQMs, and other measures can be reported through an outside registry.

Best for: independent primary care groups, including those moving into value-based contracts. Watch for: the annual term, and check pediatric templates if you also see children.

NextGen

NextGen sells two systems: NextGen Office for practices under 10 providers and NextGen Enterprise for 10 or more. Both have primary care and pediatrics pages. NextGen Office supports chronic care management, MACRA and MIPS reporting and practice-wide financial analytics. NextGen Population Health aggregates EHR, HIE, patient and paid claims data to manage chronic conditions, and NextGen points primary care groups to ACO support through a partnership with Vytalize Health. Pricing is by quote.

Best for: primary care and pediatric groups that want population health data from several sources. Watch for: Office and Enterprise are different products, so confirm which one your quote covers as you grow.

Epic

Epic is the system of most large health systems, and its population health tools track chronic conditions across care settings. Independent groups usually reach Epic through Community Connect, where a hospital system that runs Epic extends it to affiliated practices. Epic also launched Garden Plot, a hosted option for independent groups, which it said in 2022 was for groups with more than 40 providers. Terms come from the host or from Epic directly.

Best for: groups tied to a hospital system or a clinically integrated network. Watch for: less control over setup and cost when a host system runs it.

Office Practicum

Office Practicum (OP) is built only for pediatrics. Its VacLogic+ tool uses each child's immunization history and AAP recommendations to forecast which vaccines are due now and next, including catch-up schedules. OP shows private and VFC stock with lot numbers, doses and expiration dates as a software feature, reports administered vaccines to state and regional registries, and offers bi-directional connections with supported state registries so history flows back into the chart. It also offers RCM services and recall reports for overdue visits and vaccines.

OP does not publish a price list. It says pricing reflects your providers, whether they work full or part time, and the solutions you choose, and is set after a demo.

Best for: pediatric groups that want deep vaccine tools and optional billing services. Watch for: modules and services drive the price, so get every item in writing.

PCC

PCC is also pediatric only and focuses on independent pediatric practices. It charges one monthly fee that it says covers software, data conversion, training, support and customization, with $0 startup costs and $0 data conversion. The rate itself is not published.

PCC includes state and local immunization registry connections in its plan and says they are bidirectional unless noted, with a few states listed as send only. Its forecaster compares each patient's history to the recommended schedule, and its reports can filter immunizations by insurance, for example to find Medicaid patients due for a second dose.

Best for: independent pediatric practices that want support and setup included in one fee. Watch for: it is built for pediatrics only, so a mixed family medicine group would need another system.

Other names on primary care shortlists include Practice Fusion (part of Tebra), Healthie and Akute Health. See the vendor's site for current pricing.

How to choose

  1. Pediatric group: start with Office Practicum and PCC, then compare eClinicalWorks or athenaOne if you also see adults.
  2. Primary care group with several offices: athenaOne, eClinicalWorks, Elation or NextGen. Ask each to show reports by location and provider in a live demo.
  3. Heavy in chronic care management: eClinicalWorks or NextGen, and ask whether the vendor can run the CCM outreach for you.
  4. In an ACO or value-based contracts: athenaOne, Elation or NextGen, and read our guide to value-based care partners.
  5. Tied to a hospital system: Epic through Community Connect.
  6. You want a published price: eClinicalWorks or Elation.

Ask every vendor the same four questions. What is the total monthly cost for our providers, staff and modules? Which state immunization registries do you connect to, and both ways? Can we export charges, payments and adjustments by payer, provider and location? How are per member and quality bonus payments recorded, if at all?

Your EHR and your books

An EHR runs the clinic. It does not run the finances. It shows visits, charges and payments posted. Your bank shows insurance deposits weeks later, often in batches that mix many claims. Per member per month (PMPM) payments and shared savings arrive as lump deposits with no visit attached. Vaccine orders leave the bank long before claims pay them back.

So the EHR cannot tell you whether fee-for-service visits or PMPM contracts really pay after staff costs, what each payer is worth, or how much cash you need for flu season orders. That takes books kept by payer, revenue type, provider and location, reconciled to the bank every month. If vaccines are a large cost for you, see our guide to vaccine buying groups vs VaxCare.

Questions, answered

Frequently asked questions

What is the best EHR for a primary care practice?

There is no single best choice. athenaOne, eClinicalWorks, Elation and NextGen are the most common shortlist for independent primary care, and Epic fits groups tied to a hospital system. Book demos with your own visit types and payer mix.

What EHR should a pediatric group with 3 offices use?

Start with Office Practicum and PCC, which are built only for pediatrics and handle vaccines and registries well. Ask both to show reports by office and provider, and compare them with eClinicalWorks or athenaOne if you also see adults.

Is Office Practicum or PCC better for pediatrics?

Both are strong pediatric systems. OP stands out for VacLogic+ forecasting and optional RCM services, while PCC stands out for one all-inclusive monthly fee with setup and support included. Ask each for a written quote for your providers.

Which EHRs connect to state immunization registries?

OP offers bi-directional connections with supported state registries, and PCC includes registry connections that are bidirectional unless noted. For athenaOne, eClinicalWorks, Elation, NextGen and Epic, ask which registry your state uses and whether the link works both ways.

Which primary care EHR is best for chronic care management?

eClinicalWorks has a CCM module and a CCM service run by its own staff, and NextGen Office supports chronic care management with a partner program. athenaOne lists care management tools as well.

How much does a primary care EHR cost?

eClinicalWorks lists $499 per provider a month, or $599 with practice management. Elation lists $425 a month for a Standard Provider and $500 for Premium, on an annual term. athenaOne ties its fee to collections, and NextGen, Epic, OP and PCC quote.

Does the EHR report fee-for-service and PMPM revenue separately?

The EHR tracks charges and claims, but PMPM and shared savings often arrive outside it as lump deposits. Your books need separate revenue lines for fee-for-service, PMPM and bonus payments to show what each earns.
