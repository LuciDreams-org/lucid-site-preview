Short answer

Most ABA therapy companies sell to private equity backed autism platforms. The largest include Action Behavior Centers, BlueSprig, Behavioral Innovations, Hopebridge, Proud Moments ABA, LEARN Behavioral, Caravel Autism Health and Autism Learning Partners, and newer platforms such as Ascendia Autism Care, ACES and InBloom Autism Services are also buying. Hendon Partners (May 2026) puts quality ABA agencies at about 5 to 8 times EBITDA, down from 8 to 10 times in 2018 to 2021. Offers mix cash at closing with rollover equity and earnouts tied to staffing or delivered hours, so compare the full structure, not only the headline multiple.

The market in numbers

A Brown University study in JAMA Pediatrics (January 7, 2026) counted 574 private equity acquired autism service sites in 42 states from 2015 to 2024. Acuity News reports that nearly 80% of those acquisitions closed from 2018 to 2022.

Deal flow has steadied since. LevinPro counted 11 autism provider deals in 2025, with private equity or PE backed buyers in 59 of 78 deals from 2020 to 2024, per Olympic M&A's Q3 2026 report. Mergium Advisors counted 22 pediatric therapy deals in the first half of 2026, about 65% by sponsor backed platforms, and about 93 PE backed pediatric therapy platforms, per Acuity News (July 29, 2026). Only three new platforms were formed in that half, against ten in 2025, and Mergium attributes that to a shortage of founder owned companies with enough scale, not to weaker appetite.

The buyers, one by one

Action Behavior Centers. Charlesbank Capital Partners bought Action Behavior Centers from NexPhase Capital in 2022, in a deal Behavioral Health Business (citing Axios Pro) valued at $840 million on more than $60 million of projected adjusted earnings. It listed 404 locations in nine states in May 2026, the largest ABA footprint by site count, per CT Acquisitions.

BlueSprig. KKR created BlueSprig as a clinic based provider and grew it through add-ons, including a 2020 combination with Florida Autism Center and the October 2023 purchase of Trumpet Behavioral Health (37 locations in seven states). Acuity News counted 155 locations in 19 states in February 2026.

Behavioral Innovations. Tenex Capital Management bought Behavioral Innovations from Shore Capital Partners on June 6, 2024, when it had 77 centers in Texas, Oklahoma and Colorado. Olympic M&A reports a $300 million deal. Acuity News counted 110 locations in February 2026.

Hopebridge. Arsenal Capital Partners acquired Hopebridge in May 2019, with Baird Capital keeping a minority stake. Founded in Indianapolis in 2005, Hopebridge grew from about 35 centers in four states to 103 locations in 12 states, per Breaking News ABA and Acuity News.

Proud Moments ABA. Nautic Partners bought Proud Moments from Audax Private Equity on February 3, 2025, when it had 70 clinics in 12 states. Olympic M&A notes a dedicated chief development officer leads its acquisitions.

LEARN Behavioral. Gryphon Investors has held a majority since March 2019. LEARN bought Little Leaves Behavioral Services, about 18 centers in Maryland, Virginia and Florida, on May 11, 2026, and Olympic M&A notes a named head of corporate development.

Caravel Autism Health and Autism Learning Partners. GTCR agreed to buy Caravel from Frazier Healthcare Partners in May 2024, and Caravel listed 77 locations in nine states in May 2026. FFL Partners has backed Autism Learning Partners since its purchase from Great Point Partners, which built the company from 2010.

Kyo, CARD and InBloom. Kyo Autism Therapy (formerly Gateway Learning Group) is backed by Norwest Venture Partners since 2019. The Center for Autism and Related Disorders (CARD) has been owned since August 2023 by Pantogran LLC, led by its founder, and counted 108 locations in February 2026. Elysium Management, a family office, bought InBloom Autism Services from Webster Equity Partners in a deal completed near the end of 2025, which Axios reported at $75 million, per Acuity News.

Other active acquirers. Olympic M&A's Q3 2026 report also lists Center for Social Dynamics (Goldman Sachs Alternatives), ACES (General Atlantic), Ascendia Autism Care (Cathay Capital, formed in 2026), Behavioral Framework (Renovus Capital Partners) and Behavior Frontiers (NexPhase Capital, May 2025). CT Acquisitions adds Centria Autism (Thomas H. Lee Partners), Acorn Health (Ontario Teachers' Pension Plan) and ABS Kids (Petra Capital Partners).

Employee ownership. An ESOP is a less common route. Mergium counts five ABA ESOPs since 2017, the latest North Arrow ABA in June 2026. Mergium notes the company must produce steady cash flow to service the debt used to buy the shares.

How these deals are structured

Olympic M&A describes lower middle market ABA deals this way: part of the price is paid in rolled equity, and a portion may sit behind an earnout tied to staffing or delivered hours. Its illustration shows two offers of 6 times on $1.5 million of EBITDA, both worth $9.0 million: one pays $7.2 million in cash at closing, the other $5.4 million, with the rest rolled into the platform.

Hendon Partners' size ladder (May 4, 2026), as published by Olympic M&A and CT Acquisitions:

  1. $300,000 to $750,000 of EBITDA, single state: about 4.0 to 5.5 times.
  2. $750,000 to $2.5 million, regional: about 5.0 to 6.5 times.
  3. $2.5 million to $7 million, multi state: about 6.0 to 7.5 times.
  4. Above $7 million with a strong clinical model: about 7.0 to 9.0 times or more.

Large platform trades such as Action Behavior Centers priced well above this ladder and are not direct comparables for a smaller company. Treat these as an advisor's ranges, not quotes. See how a medical practice is valued.

What ABA buyers look for

Olympic M&A and CT Acquisitions list the drivers buyers test:

  1. BCBA and RBT stability. Hendon cites 25% to 40% BCBA turnover as typical and under 20% as exceptional.
  2. Authorized hours that reconcile to scheduled, delivered, billed and paid hours. Fill rate is a core diligence measure.
  3. Payer mix. Commercial rates generally run 15% to 25% above Medicaid, and single state Medicaid or one managed care plan concentration gets extra scrutiny.
  4. State Medicaid rate exposure. Indiana cut its maximum fees for individual ABA services 6% on April 1, 2026, with another 4% scheduled for April 1, 2027, per IHCP bulletin BT202627.
  5. Clinical governance and documentation: medical necessity, progress measures and session notes that support every billed unit.
  6. Regional density, so centers share supervision, recruiting and scheduling.
  7. A founder who is not also the CEO, clinical director and main recruiter.
  8. Financial quality: a revenue bridge based on cash collected, normalized staffing cost and no aggressive add-backs for new centers.

Is now a good time to sell? The sources describe a selective market: buyers still compete for clinically sound, regionally dense providers, while multiples sit below the 2018 to 2021 peak. Your timing depends on your payer mix, staffing and growth plan, so get a valuation before you decide.

Most of the financial list comes down to the books: accrual statements that tie to the bank, revenue and margin by payer, location and provider, and a cash view that matches payer timing. How to compare offers

Two offers that both say 6 times can put very different amounts in your hands. Line them up on the same page:

  1. Cash at closing, after fees, debt payoff and taxes.
  2. Rollover equity: how much, at what value, what rights it carries and who controls the next sale.
  3. Earnouts: whether targets depend on hours you can staff or on the platform's recruiting, and who controls the numbers.
  4. Your role and pay after the sale, including clinical leadership duties.
  5. Non compete, employment term and clinical autonomy.
  6. Who carries the risk of any open Medicaid or managed care audit.
  7. Working capital and escrow terms that adjust the price at closing.

Model each offer over five years with your CPA, and have a healthcare attorney read the paperwork. An M&A advisor who knows autism platforms helps create competition.

Questions, answered

Frequently asked questions

Who is buying ABA therapy companies right now?

Mostly private equity backed autism platforms. The largest by locations include Action Behavior Centers, BlueSprig, Behavioral Innovations, Hopebridge, Proud Moments ABA and Caravel Autism Health, with LEARN Behavioral, ACES, Center for Social Dynamics and Ascendia Autism Care among recent acquirers.

What multiple do ABA companies sell for?

Hendon Partners publishes about 4.0 to 5.5 times EBITDA for a small single state provider, rising to 7.0 to 9.0 times or more for a large platform. A formal valuation gives the number for your company.

Is now a good time to sell my ABA company?

Buyers are active but selective. Multiples are below the 2018 to 2021 peak, and providers with stable staff, a commercial heavy payer mix and clean documentation get the best terms.

How much of the price is paid in cash?

It varies by offer. Part of the price is often rolled into the buying platform and part may depend on an earnout, so compare cash at closing first.

Does a Medicaid heavy payer mix lower my value?

Usually, yes. Commercial rates generally run above Medicaid, and buyers price in state rate cuts, hour caps and audit risk. Books that show revenue and margin by payer make that review faster.

How do I prepare my ABA company for a buyer?

Start 12 to 24 months out with accrual monthly books, margin by location and provider, a documented add back file and steady BCBA staffing. Then hire an advisor and attorney before you take offers.
