Pelora Marketing

Pelora is a health marketing agency with a dedicated med spa offer. Its med spa page lists video shot by a vetted videographer in your state, Botox, filler and laser ads written to Meta and Google rules, treatment pages built to book consults, and campaigns that bring past clients back.

Fees as published: plans start at $3,500 a month, with management in every plan ranging from $3,500 to $50,000 a month. A larger SEO and AI search program adds $2,000 to $4,000 a month, and a CRM automation build runs $1,500 to $6,500. Pelora says ad spend is billed to you by Google and Meta and is not marked up.

Results, as Pelora states them: more than $5 million in ad spend managed and a 5.0 Google rating.

Best for: med spas that want content, ads and the booking site from one team.

Aesthetix Media

Aesthetix Media says it works only with medical aesthetics practices and publishes a full price menu.

Fees as published: Launch is $1,500 a month plus a $500 one time setup, for one paid channel. Starter is $3,000 a month for Google and Meta ads. Growth is $6,500 and Scale is $9,500 a month, and Enterprise for groups with several locations starts at $12,000 a month with per location reporting. Single services start at $800 a month for email and text campaigns and $1,500 a month each for local SEO, content and social. Terms are month to month, and ad spend is billed to you directly.

Results, as Aesthetix states them: a 4 to 8 times average return on ad spend and first leads in 48 to 72 hours.

Best for: owners who want to pick a tier and know the fee before any call.

Foundgrove

Foundgrove is a search and paid media agency for service businesses, with a med spa comparison on its blog. It is not med spa only.

Fees as published: SEO and AI search visibility from $2,500 a month, paid ads from $3,500 a month and website design from $8,500 as a fixed fee. Terms are month to month and Foundgrove says you own every asset it builds. It offers a free 10 minute video review of your site before you commit.

Best for: med spas whose main gap is showing up in Google and AI answers.

Growbi

Growbi runs Meta and Google ads with original video, booking funnels and fast text follow up for med spas, and also serves dental and local service businesses.

Fees as published: the management fee is not listed. Growbi says most med spa clients start with $2,000 to $5,000 a month in ad spend, paid from ad accounts the business owns and separate from its fee. It works month to month and reports cost per booked appointment.

Results, as Growbi states them: more than 120 new appointments a month on average, a 146 percent average return on ad spend, and appointments booked at $20 to $35 each.

Best for: owners who want paid ads measured by booked appointments.

Forever Booked

Forever Booked installs a marketing system inside the med spa and runs it with you, rather than acting as an outside agency. Its page describes an 8 week setup covering a signature offer, a two step text campaign to past patients, Meta ads launched live with you, and an AI assistant that answers new leads within 60 seconds.

Fees as published: pricing is not published; it starts with a demo.

Results, as Forever Booked states them: its reactivation campaign generates $6.06 per contact on average, and its system helps med spas add $20,000 to $50,000 or more a month in revenue.

Best for: owners who want to keep control of the ad accounts and the process.

Diamond Accelerator

Diamond Accelerator describes itself as a consulting, recruiting and full service digital marketing agency for medical aesthetics and wellness, working with med spas since 2012. Services include Google and Facebook ads, SEO, Google Business Profile work, social media, web design, branding, lead follow up and scheduling, startup consulting and recruiting.

Fees as published: not published; it starts with a free strategy session. Its FAQ says it does not tie clients to long term agreements.

Best for: new or growing med spas that want marketing, hiring help and business coaching from one firm.

Incredible Marketing

Incredible Marketing is a medical and aesthetic marketing agency focused on SEO, content marketing, social media and websites.

Fees as published: not published. Its site says there are no long term agreements: you can cancel with 60 days notice. New clients go through a three month onboarding, with a team of three to seven people depending on the plan.

Best for: aesthetic practices that want organic search and content over paid ads.

The Kay Media

The Kay Media works only in aesthetics, in Canada and the US, and takes a small number of clinics by application. Its founder is a former Fortune 500 marketer, and it says offers, ads and content are built by its own team, not outsourced.

Fees as published: a one hour strategy session is $250, credited if you join within 30 days. Ongoing fees are set after the application.

Results, as The Kay Media states them: more than 25 injectors and clinics scaled, and one client clinic with four months in a row above $80,000.

Best for: solo injectors and small clinics moving into their own space.

How agencies charge

Five fee models show up across these pages.

  1. Tiered retainer. A fixed monthly fee by package, like Aesthetix Media or Pelora. Easy to budget.
  2. Retainer plus ad spend. Almost every agency here bills its fee separately from the money paid to Google and Meta. Ask whether the ad accounts are in your name.
  3. Build fees. Websites, CRM setups and video shoots are often one time charges on top of the retainer.
  4. Installed system. Forever Booked's model: the agency sets up the system and your team runs it with support.
  5. Application and consult first. The Kay Media and others set the fee after reviewing your clinic.

Before you sign, ask for the notice period, who owns the ad accounts, the website and the content, and what happens to them if you leave.

How to tell if an agency is working

Agencies report clicks, leads and cost per lead. Those numbers matter, but they stop short of the one that pays your bills.

  1. Cost per lead. Ad spend divided by form fills and calls.
  2. Cost per booked consult. Ad spend divided by consults that land on the calendar.
  3. Cost per paid patient. Total marketing cost, meaning the agency fee plus ad spend plus build fees, divided by new patients who actually paid for a treatment.

Illustrative example: you pay a $3,500 retainer and $4,000 in ad spend, and the month brings 150 leads, 60 booked consults and 30 new paying patients. Cost per lead is $50, but cost per paid patient is $250. If a first treatment earns $300 after injector pay and product, the month barely breaks even, and the real payoff depends on how many of those patients come back.

That is why marketing needs a cap tied to your P&L. Med spa margins are thin once injector pay and product are paid. Set the monthly marketing budget from your gross margin and the number of new patients your providers can actually treat, then review it every month against booked revenue.

How to choose

  1. You want to see prices first: start with Pelora, Aesthetix Media and Foundgrove.
  2. Your calendar has gaps and you have a list of past clients: ask Forever Booked and Growbi how they handle reactivation and fast follow up.
  3. You are opening or hiring: Diamond Accelerator pairs marketing with consulting and recruiting.
  4. Search and content are the gap: Incredible Marketing and Foundgrove lead with SEO.
  5. You are a solo injector growing into a clinic: The Kay Media is built for that stage.
  6. You run several locations: ask for per location reporting, as Aesthetix lists on its Enterprise tier, and make sure your books split marketing cost by location too.

Questions, answered

Frequently asked questions

How much does a med spa marketing agency cost?

Among agencies that publish fees, retainers start around $1,500 a month for one channel and run to $9,500 or more for a full program, with group plans from $12,000, as of November 26, 2026. Ad spend is extra and paid to Google and Meta. Several agencies set fees only after a call.

How much should a med spa spend on ads?

Growbi says most of its med spa clients start with $2,000 to $5,000 a month in ad spend. The right number depends on your margin per treatment and how many new patients your providers can see. Set a cap from your P&L, not from the agency's proposal.

Are agency results claims reliable?

Treat them as the agency's own figures. Case studies show what is possible for one clinic, not what you will get. Ask for references from med spas your size and measure cost per paid patient from month one.

Should the ad accounts be in my name?

Yes, ask for it. Growbi and Pelora both say ad spend is paid from accounts the business owns or billed to you directly. Owning the accounts keeps your data and history if you change agencies.

Do med spa agencies require long agreements?

Many say they do not. Aesthetix Media, Foundgrove and Growbi say month to month, Incredible Marketing asks for 60 days notice, and Diamond Accelerator says it avoids long term agreements. Read the notice terms before you sign.

What is a good cost per lead for a med spa?

It varies by treatment and market, and a low cost per lead can still mean a high cost per paid patient if few leads book or show up. Track all three numbers. The one that decides profit is cost per paid patient.

Can a med spa run marketing in house?

Yes. Forever Booked is built around that model, and many owners keep social and email in house while an agency runs ads. Count staff time as a marketing cost when you compare.
