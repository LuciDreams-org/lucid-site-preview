Short answer

For a group with several offices, start with a cloud system built for groups: Denticon, CareStack, Dentrix Ascend, Curve or Oryx. For a single office that wants a low, published fee and strong accounting exports, Open Dental is hard to beat, and tab32 Alpine is the lowest published cloud price we found. Dentrix and Eaglesoft remain the familiar server-based choices, with deep support networks and long track records. Whatever you pick, the system records production and claims, but it does not close your books, so plan for the accounting side too.

The options, one by one

1. Dentrix (Henry Schein One)

Dentrix is the long-standing installed system from Henry Schein One. The company describes Dentrix as "on premises control" next to Ascend's "cloud flexibility". Its pages list real-time dashboards and multi-location reporting.

Accounting export: Dentrix documents DXPort, which exports daily deposit and receipt totals into accounting software such as QuickBooks or Excel, or into a text file. It sends totals, not a full ledger, so someone still has to match them to the bank.

Best for: offices that already run Dentrix and want to keep their own server. Limits: you own the server, backups and IT, and pricing is not published.

2. Dentrix Ascend

Ascend is Henry Schein One's cloud system. Its pages list integrated eligibility, claims and patient billing, cloud imaging, and real-time KPI tracking with multi-location dashboards. An API exchange connects third-party tools.

Price: on October 23, 2026 Henry Schein One launched Ascend Starter at $199 a month for its first 12 months, for practices with fewer than 500 patients a year. Other Ascend plans are not published.

Best for: Dentrix users who want to move to the cloud without changing vendor. Limits: the published price is a 12-month starter offer, so ask what month 13 costs.

3. Eaglesoft (Patterson Dental)

Eaglesoft is Patterson's server-based system. Patterson itself contrasts "cloud-based Fuse and server-based Eaglesoft". Patterson lists over 55 authorized integrations, including revenue cycle and patient engagement tools, and U.S.-based support and training. It is sold through Service Club plans without public prices.

Accounting export: Eaglesoft can save day sheet totals, provider productivity totals and deposit report totals to a file for spreadsheet programs, and many reports export to CSV or Excel.

Best for: Patterson offices that want a known system and phone support. Limits: a physical server, and for groups Patterson points to Fuse.

4. Open Dental

Open Dental publishes its fees. In the United States it is $199 a month per location or office, covering up to three providers, for the first 12 months, then a reduced rate of $149 a month, month to month. It is self-hosted by default.

Accounting export: this is the strongest one we found. Open Dental has a first-party QuickBooks Online integration that sends deposit information and creates accounting transactions. It is one-way.

Best for: cost-conscious offices and groups that are fine running their own server or hosting partner. Limits: you handle hosting, and the per-location fee grows with each office.

5. Curve Dental

Curve is a cloud system with scheduling, insurance billing and verification, patient billing, membership plans, charting and reporting. It has separate pages for independent, multi-location and startup practices, and it gives startups special pricing on implementation and license fees. Pricing is by personal quote.

Accounting export: Curve's help center has an article titled "Does Curve integrate with Quickbooks?". We could not read its answer, so confirm the scope with Curve.

Best for: offices leaving a server that want an established cloud product. Limits: no public price.

6. Denticon (Planet DDS)

Planet DDS positions Denticon as cloud practice management for DSOs, with plans for practices with one or two locations and up. Its pages list revenue cycle tools for claims, payments and collections, real-time reporting by location, and an open API and marketplace. Planet DDS says Denticon is browser based and runs on Windows only.

Price: the Planet DDS pricing page lists plans with a "Talk to Sales" button, so the price is not published.

Best for: groups and DSOs that want to standardize every office on one system. Limits: Windows only, and you need a quote.

7. CareStack

CareStack publishes starting prices: Essentials from $829 a month and Intelligence from $1,299 a month, with custom plans for groups. Essentials includes real-time insurance eligibility, electronic claims with scrubbing, ERAs with automated claim posting, membership and payment plans, and a daily huddle dashboard. Intelligence adds AI notes, voice perio charting and AI x-ray analysis.

Accounting export: CareStack runs a developer portal with accounting summary resources. A third-party tool, QBIS Sync, sells a CareStack to QuickBooks connection.

Best for: growing groups that want claims automation and are comfortable with a higher monthly fee. Limits: the highest published starting price on this list.

8. Oryx

Oryx publishes full plans. Oryx Pro is $650 a month for up to two providers, and Oryx Automate is $899 a month, with $100 a month for each additional provider. New customers start paying three months after signup. A startup offer is $0 a month until the practice reaches 200 patients or 12 months, whichever comes first. Plans include eligibility and claims processing, billing and payments, and dashboards. Automate adds auto ERA posting, automated collection sequences and KPI dashboards. Oryx lists DSO and multi-location tools with cross-site patient management and enterprise analytics.

Best for: new practices and groups that want a published price. Limits: we found no stated accounting export.

9. tab32 Alpine

tab32 Alpine is cloud software from $95 a month with unlimited providers and users and a 14-day free trial. Its pages list eligibility, claims, auto-posted remits, appeals and reports on production, collection, recall and aging. When a practice grows to several locations, tab32 says Alpine runs on the same platform as Summit, its product for groups and DSOs.

Best for: small offices that want the lowest published cloud price. Limits: the group product, Summit, has no price on the pages we reviewed, and we found no stated accounting export.

Also on shortlists

Dentally is Henry Schein One's cloud system, sold mainly in the UK, Canada and other markets outside the US, so US offices usually compare Ascend instead. Planet DDS is the company behind Denticon, not a separate system. Fuse is Patterson's cloud system for groups.

How to choose

  1. Server or cloud first. If you want no server in the closet, cross off Dentrix and Eaglesoft, and treat Open Dental as self-hosted unless you add a hosting partner.
  2. Count locations three years out. For a group, ask each vendor for a demo of the location roll-up report and how one patient moves between offices. Denticon, CareStack, Oryx, Curve and Ascend all market group tools.
  3. Price the whole bill. Per-location (Open Dental), per-provider (Oryx) and flat plans (CareStack, tab32) diverge as you add providers and offices. Ask what changes after an introductory or startup rate.
  4. Test insurance work. Ask to see eligibility checks, claim scrubbing, attachments and ERA auto-posting on your own payers.
  5. Ask for the accounting export in writing. A first-party integration (Open Dental), a totals export (Dentrix DXPort, Eaglesoft) and an API are different things. Get a sample deposit report and a production versus collections report before you sign.

What dental software does not do for your books

Your practice system holds production, adjustments, claims and patient balances. Your books hold what actually reached the bank. Four gaps show up in every system above.

  1. Production versus collections. Production is dentistry done. Collections are money received. The gap is PPO write offs, unpaid claims and patient balances, and it only shows clearly when the books and the practice reports are reconciled each month.
  2. Deposits. One bank deposit can mix card payouts, insurance payments and patient checks from several days. Someone has to tie each deposit back to the deposit report.
  3. Several offices. A group needs the same chart of accounts in every office, a clear rule for shared costs, and a combined owner view.
  4. Aging. Insurance and patient AR in the system has to match what the books say is owed, or the cash forecast is wrong.
Questions, answered

Frequently asked questions

What is the best dental practice management software for a 3-location group?

Look first at cloud systems that market group tools: Denticon, CareStack, Dentrix Ascend, Curve and Oryx. Ask each for a demo of reporting by location and a combined view. Open Dental also prices per location if you are fine self-hosting.

Is Dentrix or Eaglesoft better?

Both are mature server-based systems from large dental suppliers, Henry Schein One and Patterson. The choice usually follows your supplier, your IT setup and what your team already knows. If you want the cloud, compare Dentrix Ascend and Patterson's Fuse instead.

Which dental software publishes its price?

Of the systems here, Open Dental, CareStack, Oryx, tab32 Alpine and Dentrix Ascend Starter publish prices. Dentrix, Eaglesoft, Curve and Denticon quote on request.

Should my dental office move from a server to cloud software?

Cloud removes the office server, backups and much of the IT bill, and lets you log in from anywhere. A server keeps data on site and may run faster on local imaging. Ask each vendor about data conversion and how long it takes.

Which dental software works best with QuickBooks?

Open Dental has a first-party one-way QuickBooks Online deposit integration. Dentrix exports deposit and receipt totals through DXPort, and Eaglesoft saves totals to file. CareStack connects through a third-party sync. Most others rely on reports and exports.

Does my dental software replace a bookkeeper?

No. It tracks production, claims and patient balances. A bookkeeper reconciles deposits to the bank, records payroll and expenses, and closes the month. See the dental bookkeeping guide.

How long does it take to switch dental software?

It depends on data conversion, imaging and training, and vendors quote it per practice. Plan the switch at a month end so the books have a clean cutover date.
