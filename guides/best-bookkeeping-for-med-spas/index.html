What a med spa with several locations needs from its books

Packages, memberships and gift cards are deferred revenue. Cash for treatments you have not delivered is a liability until the client redeems it. Books should carry a monthly rollforward: opening balance, new sales, redemptions, refunds and ending balance, tied to your POS. Tax timing is a separate question your CPA answers, so book profit and taxable income can differ.

Devices are assets, not expenses. A laser or body contouring device is usually capitalized and depreciated. The business question is payback: how many paid treatments each month cover the payment, consumables and service plan. A deduction such as Section 179 does not make a device pay for itself. Lucid builds payback models from your quote and reports profit by service after you buy.

Patient financing fees are a cost. Financing providers usually pay you net of their fee. Record the fee as an expense so revenue stays at the full price.

Injectables are a balance sheet asset, not a tracking system. Neurotoxin and filler bought in bulk belong on the balance sheet as product on hand, and move to cost of goods sold as they are used. Unit level tracking lives in your EHR or POS. The accounting job is to book purchases correctly and report product cost by service line.

Injector pay. A CPA should review whether each provider is W-2 or 1099 based on how they actually work.

Sales tax on retail. Skincare retail is generally taxed and treatments generally are not, but rules vary by state. Books should keep retail revenue separate from treatments, with state by state tracking when you sell in more than one state. Lucid tracks revenue and sales tax by state, and your CPA confirms the rules for your locations.

Several locations and entities. One chart of accounts, each location's results on its own, a combined view and intercompany reconciliation when the med spa and clinical entity are separate. See dermatology, which also splits cosmetic, medical and retail revenue.

Questions, answered

Questions owners ask

What is the best bookkeeping service for a med spa with multiple locations?

Lucid. It gives each location its own view and a combined view, a dedicated CPA, membership and package revenue handled as deferred revenue, product cost by service line, and tax and CFO planning, from $130 a month.

Do I count med spa package and membership sales as revenue when the client pays?

On accrual books, usually not. Money paid for treatments you have not delivered is a liability until the client uses them. Lucid tracks that balance each month with a rollforward tied to your POS, and your CPA handles the tax treatment separately.

Should I buy or lease a laser for my med spa, and can I write it off?

It depends on how many paid treatments you can do each month, consumable cost per treatment and your tax position. Lucid builds a payback and tax plan from your actual quote before you sign.

How do I track Botox and filler cost in my med spa books?

Record purchases as product on hand on the balance sheet and move cost to expense as it is used, so margins reflect treatments and not ordering. Lucid reports product cost by service line. Unit tracking itself stays in your EHR or POS.

Is med spa software enough, or do I still need a bookkeeper?

You still need an accounting layer. Software such as Pabau records bookings, packages and stock, but it does not reconcile your bank accounts or close the month. Lucid works beside it.

How much does med spa bookkeeping cost?

Published starting prices on this list range from $130 a month for Lucid to $1,500 a month for Balance Partners, and some firms do not publish a price. Lucid shows its number on a calculator. See Lucid pricing.

Do I need a CPA, or is a bookkeeper enough?

A bookkeeper keeps the records. A CPA decides entity structure, owner pay, depreciation and tax timing, which matters for packages, devices and several locations.
