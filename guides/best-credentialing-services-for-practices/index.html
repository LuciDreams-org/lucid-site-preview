Which credentialing services are worth paying for?

Short answer

Pay for credentialing when a new clinician's lost billing weeks cost more than the fee, which is true for most practices adding more than one or two providers. Small practices get the best value from per payer services with published prices, such as MedSole RCM, TLC Credentialing and Consulting or GetPaneled, on top of a clean CAQH profile you keep yourself. Growing groups and multi state organizations look at platforms such as Medallion, Assured, Verifiable or Modio Health, which quote by scope. Whatever you pick, plan on 90 to 120 days for most commercial payers and budget the cash gap while the new hire waits.

What credentialing actually includes

Credentialing and payer enrollment are two steps that people often lump together. Credentialing checks a clinician's license, training, malpractice history and sanctions. Enrollment, sometimes called contracting, gets the clinician or group added to a payer's network with an effective date and a fee schedule, so claims pay at in network rates.

A full service job usually covers:

  1. NPI checks and CAQH profile setup and attestation.
  2. Each payer application, Medicare, Medicaid and commercial.
  3. Follow up calls until an effective date is confirmed in writing.
  4. EFT and ERA setup so payments land in your bank.
  5. Upkeep: CAQH re-attestation every 120 days, recredentialing about every three years, and Medicare revalidation every five years.

Ask every vendor which of these five are in the price. Upkeep is where quotes differ the most.

The options

CAQH Provider Data Portal (the base layer)

CAQH is now called DataSpring, and CAQH ProView is now the CAQH Provider Data Portal. Your login and profile stay the same. DataSpring says the portal is provided at no cost to clinicians, provider groups and practices, and profiles are kept on a 120 day attestation cycle. Most commercial payers read from it, so a lapsed attestation can stall a file that is otherwise complete.

Strengths: free, and you own the profile. Limits: it is a data store, not a service. Someone still has to file each payer application and chase it.

MedSole RCM

MedSole publishes $99 per payer application, charged only after the payer approves it, with revalidation and recredentialing included and no monthly fee. It also offers billing at a published percentage of collections. It is one of the lowest published per payer rates we found.

Strengths: low price, pay on approval, upkeep included. Limits: a billing company first, so ask how many credentialing staff work your state's payers.

TLC Credentialing and Consulting

TLC publishes a full price list. An initial group or individual commercial contract, with credentialing for one provider, is $400. Medicaid, managed care or Tricare contracts are $500. Adding a provider to an existing group contract runs $50 to $200 per commercial payer, or $250 for Medicaid. Recredentialing is $50 to $100 per provider per payer, and half of each fee is due up front. TLC also lists Tricare certification for RBTs, which ABA groups need.

Strengths: clear prices, contract work for groups, ABA detail. Limits: half paid before work starts, so you carry the fee even if a panel is closed.

GetPaneled

GetPaneled is built for therapists. It publishes $79 per payer, a three payer bundle for $219 and a five payer bundle for $349, plus $49 for CAQH and NPI setup alone. Groups of two or more providers get 10% off, and the page offers a refund of the package price if a clinician is not paneled within six months of a complete file. It enrolls clinicians under your own practice's contracts.

Strengths: low published prices and a refund promise. Limits: focused on therapy, so not a fit for medical groups with hospital privileges.

Practolytics

Practolytics runs billing and credentialing together, with a dedicated manager. Its page says pricing is per provider, per plan or monthly, set after a review of your practice. It gives 60 to 180 days as a typical range, with many files landing around 90 to 120 days, and advises starting four to six months before a start date.

Strengths: one vendor for credentialing and claims. Limits: no published price.

Medallion

Medallion is a credentialing and enrollment platform with services on top, used by more than 300 organizations according to its pricing page. On September 15, 2026 Medallion announced it had acquired Andros, the company formerly known as CredSimple. Medallion offers direct enrollment with every payer, and a Fast Track Network that it says gets eligible enrollments billable in days, not months. Pricing is a tailored engagement, by quote.

Strengths: scale, many states, payer fast track. Limits: built for organizations, so a two clinician practice may find it more than it needs.

Assured

Assured is an NCQA certified CVO that uses software agents to fill and follow up on applications. It says it submits enrollment applications within 72 hours of complete provider information, that providers get in network up to 30% faster than industry averages, and that most organizations are live within one week. It tracks revalidation and starts renewals 60 days before expiration. Pricing is by quote.

Strengths: speed and automation. Limits: payer approval time still depends on the payer.

Verifiable

Verifiable is credentialing software for in house teams, with automated primary source checks, NCQA packets, enrollment tracking and roster reports. It runs on Salesforce. Pricing is by demo.

Strengths: control for groups with their own credentialing staff. Limits: software, so your team still does the payer work.

Modio Health

Modio sells OneView, a platform that stores credentials and sends expiry alerts, plus a credentialing service team. Modio says its service gets practices to an effective date in an average of 60 days, against what it calls an industry standard of 120.

Strengths: software and service from one vendor. Limits: the 60 day figure is a vendor average, not a promise.

Health systems that also need hospital privileging usually look at enterprise suites such as symplr. Those are outside the scope of most private practices.

How long credentialing takes

Payer typeTypical time
Medicare, clean online application95% processed within 15 days, all within 50
Medicare, paper or extra review30 to 100 days
Commercial payers90 to 120 days, up to 150
Medicaid and managed careVaries by state

Medicare times come from CMS processing standards for its contractors. Medicare lets clinicians bill up to 30 days before the effective date in some cases. Commercial payers do not offer that as a rule, so start four to six months before a start date.

What it costs, added up

Published rates for outside services, read in October and November 2026, run from about $80 to $250 per commercial payer application and up to $270 for Medicare or Medicaid. One provider joining six payers lands between about $500 and $1,500 at those rates. Three new providers with five payers each is 15 applications, or about $1,200 to $3,750.

The fee is rarely the big number. The bigger cost is the months a new clinician is on payroll before insurance pays. See the cash gap example under "Where Lucid fits".

How to choose

  1. Solo or two clinicians, one state. Keep CAQH current yourself and use a per payer service with a published price.
  2. Therapy or counseling group adding clinicians. A therapist focused service with group pricing, or compare a platform such as Headway or Alma. See credentialing service or Headway and Alma.
  3. ABA group. Pick a service that lists Medicaid, managed care and Tricare work, including RBT steps.
  4. Group adding several clinicians a quarter, or several states. Get quotes from platforms such as Medallion, Assured or Modio Health.
  5. Group with in house credentialing staff. Software such as Verifiable gives your team tracking and automation.
  6. Already outsourcing billing. Ask your biller first. Many bundle credentialing at a per payer rate.
Questions, answered

Frequently asked questions

How much does credentialing cost per payer?

Published rates we found in late 2026 run from about $80 to $250 per commercial payer, and up to $270 for Medicare or Medicaid. Some services charge only after approval. Most payers charge individual clinicians no application fee.

How long does insurance credentialing take?

Plan on 90 to 120 days for most commercial payers, and up to 150 when a panel is busy. Medicare is usually faster when the online application is clean. Start four to six months before the clinician's first day.

Can we do credentialing ourselves?

Yes. The CAQH Provider Data Portal is free, and payer applications have no fee for most clinicians. The cost is staff time, follow up calls and the risk of a stalled file.

What is the difference between credentialing and contracting?

Credentialing checks the clinician's background. Contracting, or enrollment, gets the group or clinician into the network with a fee schedule and an effective date. You need both before claims pay at in network rates.

Is credentialing software worth it for a small practice?

Usually not until you have several clinicians or several states. Below that, a per payer service plus a calendar for re-attestation dates is enough.

What happens if CAQH attestation lapses?

Payers that read your profile may stall or pause a file. Re-attest every 120 days, or have your service track it for you.

Is the credentialing fee tax deductible?

Credentialing fees are generally a business expense. For a practice that has not opened yet, they may count as startup costs with different rules. Ask your CPA how to book them.
