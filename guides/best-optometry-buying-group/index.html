Short answer

There is no single best group; the right one is the one whose vendors you already buy from and whose fee your rebates cover. IDOC ($180 a month for Essential) and PECAA ($279 a month for PECAA Max, no contract) are fee based alliances with vendor rebates, advisors and HR help. Vision Source is a franchise network with no initial franchise fee and a revenue based royalty of up to 2.5 percent in its 2026 disclosure document. PEN, VSP Premier Edge and CooperVision's practice resources fill other roles, so compare all of them on your own invoices.

How buying groups make money for you

A buying group pools purchases from many practices so vendors give better prices. You gain in three ways:

  1. Discounts off wholesale on frames, lenses, contact lenses, supplies and equipment.
  2. Rebates paid later, often quarterly, based on what you bought.
  3. Services such as consolidated billing, business advisors, HR help, staff training and benchmarking.

You pay in one of three ways: a flat fee each month (IDOC, PECAA), a share of revenue (Vision Source) or an admin fee built into purchases (PEN). The math is simple: savings and rebates must beat the cost. See our broader guide on whether a group purchasing organization is worth it for medical and dental groups.

The options

1. IDOC

IDOC describes itself as a buying group combined with an optical alliance for independent practices.

  1. Cost: IDOC's FAQ lists Essential membership at $180 a month, with no hidden fees or long term commitment. A contact lens share program is offered as an add on.
  2. Rebates and savings: vendor discounts and rebates. IDOC says Essential members average 4 times return from vendor programs alone; that is IDOC's own figure.
  3. Services: IDOC Insider monthly reports on revenue, exams, optical and contact lenses with benchmarking against more than 1,200 practices, IDOC HR Now, labs, financial services, education and a member community.

Best for owners who want rebates and a monthly benchmark report in one membership.

2. PECAA

PECAA (Professional Eye Care Associates of America) runs PECAA Max as part of VSP Vision's private practice programs.

  1. Cost: $279 a month flat, no contract, per PECAA's site. A cold start option serves new practices.
  2. Rebates: VSP Vision's August 2025 update lists discounts and rebates from more than 200 vendor partners, access to the VSP Vision Exam Rebate, and a new Marchon and Altair frame rebate from January 1, 2026. It also offered qualifying members a rebate on monthly dues from January through June 2026.
  3. Services: business advisors in finance, operations, optical, billing and coding, marketing and cold start; HR support; continuing education and staff training; an annual meeting and local events. PECAA Max members also get HEA Advantage at no extra charge, per PECAA.

Best for owners who want hands on advisors and a broad vendor list for a flat fee.

3. Vision Source

Vision Source is a franchisor. Members are franchisees who own their practices.

  1. Cost: the 2026 Franchise Disclosure Document, issued March 11, 2026, says Vision Source charges no initial franchise fee. Members pay a revenue based monthly royalty, summarized from Item 6 of that document as up to 2.5 percent of gross receipts. The document estimates $500 to $40,500 in total investment to convert an existing office, none paid to the franchisor.
  2. Network: 3,027 franchised locations at the end of 2025, per the same document as summarized by Franopolis.
  3. Services listed by Vision Source: managed care strategies, cost of goods savings through buying power, equipment pricing, practice and professional development tools, marketing, real estate services and peer networks.

Because the royalty grows with revenue, the cost rises as you grow. Best for owners who value the brand, the peer network and managed care support enough to pay a share of revenue.

4. PEN (Primary Eyecare Network)

PEN was founded in 1984 by private optometrists and is now a division of ABB Optical Group, per Vision Monday.

  1. Cost: PEN charges an admin fee and says it discloses that fee rather than folding it into discounts. The amount is not published; see the vendor's site.
  2. Services: one consolidated monthly statement for purchases through ABB Optical Group and other PEN vendors.

Best for owners who want simpler payables across many vendors.

5. VSP Premier Edge

VSP Premier Edge is VSP's program for participating private practices, not a buying group in the usual sense.

  1. Cost and eligibility: from January 1, 2026, Premier Edge moved to a single tier. Eligibility includes a minimum eyewear purchase amount and offering conveniences such as retinal imaging and free fittings and adjustments. The earlier frame rewards program ended December 31, 2025.
  2. Benefits: a Premier Edge banner in VSP's online provider directory (VSP says banner practices get 3.7 times more listing clicks on average), marketing support, training, and special offers from VSP Vision businesses and partners.

Best for practices with many VSP patients that want more VSP member traffic.

6. CooperVision practice resources

CooperVision is a contact lens maker, not a buying group. Its US practitioner site offers marketing materials, patient rebate forms, fitting calculators and an online success center, and it runs a Best Practices recognition program for innovative practices. No membership fee is listed. Patient rebates go to the patient, so they do not change your cost of goods.

Best for contact lens heavy practices that already buy CooperVision lenses. It sits alongside a buying group rather than replacing one.

  2. Add rebates, say $5,000 a year, for $17,000 of total value.
  3. Subtract the cost of the group. A flat fee group leaves most of that value. A revenue royalty needs services worth more than the gap, such as managed care help or marketing you would otherwise pay for.

How to choose

  1. Mostly private pay with a big optical: compare IDOC and PECAA on the frame and lens lines you carry.
  2. Heavy VSP mix: look at Premier Edge eligibility and PECAA Max together, since both sit under VSP Vision.
  3. Want a brand and peer network: Vision Source, if the royalty pays back in services you use.
  4. Many small vendors and messy payables: PEN's single statement may save staff time.
  5. Contact lens heavy: add CooperVision's resources, and ask your group about contact lens share programs.
  6. Cold start: PECAA and IDOC both describe programs for new practices.
  7. Planning a sale: clean, separate optical and rebate lines help in diligence. See who is buying ophthalmology and optometry practices.

Questions to ask any group

  1. Which of my current vendors are on your list, and at what discount?
  2. How and when are rebates paid, and are they paid to me or applied to invoices?
  3. What is the total cost: fee, royalty or admin fee?
  4. Is there a contract term or a minimum purchase?
  5. Can I belong to another group at the same time?
Questions, answered

Frequently asked questions

Is an optometry buying group worth the fee?

Usually, if you buy enough from its vendors. A flat $180 or $279 a month is easy to pay back on frames and contact lenses alone. A revenue royalty needs a closer look as you grow.

What is the difference between a buying group and an alliance?

A buying group mainly negotiates prices and rebates. An alliance adds services such as advisors, HR, training and benchmarking. IDOC and PECAA do both; Vision Source is a franchise network.

How much does Vision Source cost?

Its 2026 disclosure document lists no initial franchise fee and a revenue based royalty of up to 2.5 percent of gross receipts, as summarized from Item 6. Read the full document with an attorney and your CPA before you sign.

How much does IDOC cost?

IDOC's FAQ lists Essential membership at $180 a month with no long term commitment, checked November 26, 2026. Add ons such as contact lens share programs cost extra.

How do I book buying group rebates?

Under US accounting rules, rebates from a vendor generally reduce the cost of what you bought, not count as sales, per PwC's guide to ASC 705-20. Membership fees and royalties are an operating expense. Your CPA confirms the treatment for your books and tax return.

Can I join more than one group?

Some owners do, for example a buying group plus a VSP program. Ask each group whether its agreement allows it and how rebates are split.
