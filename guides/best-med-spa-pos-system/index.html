Short answer

For most med spas the best POS is the one built into your med spa software: Boulevard, Zenoti, Mangomint, Aesthetic Record (AR Pay) or PatientNow, because memberships, packages, deposits and tips then live in one system with the chart. Vagaro is a low cost all in one for smaller spas. Square, Clover and Stripe Terminal publish clear card rates but need extra work for packages and memberships. Add patient financing such as Cherry at checkout, and have Lucid reconcile every payout to your bank so fees, tips and prepaid balances land in the right place.

What a med spa POS has to handle

A med spa checkout is not a coffee shop checkout. Before you compare rates, check that the POS handles these:

  1. Memberships with recurring billing, credits that roll over and a clear cancel flow.
  2. Packages and unit banks that a provider redeems session by session.
  3. Gift cards sold online and in the spa, with a balance report.
  4. Deposits and no show fees charged to a card on file.
  5. Tips added at the terminal and reported by provider.
  6. Retail products with sales tax, kept apart from treatments.
  7. Patient financing at checkout for larger plans.
  8. Payout reports that show gross sales, fees, refunds and the net deposit.

Item 8 is the one owners skip, and it is the one your books depend on.

Boulevard

Boulevard's pricing page lists card processing starting at 2.65%. Its Offset program shares the cost with clients: the client pays a 3% fee on card transactions and Boulevard says you pay about 1%. Boulevard offers volume discounts if you bring a processing statement. It uses its own Duo terminal. Buy now, pay later through Affirm and Klarna went live at checkout in 2026.

Strengths: memberships, packages and gift cards inside a strong booking system. Limits: surcharge and fee sharing rules vary by state and card network, so check them before turning Offset on.

Zenoti

Zenoti Payments runs inside Zenoti with tips at terminals or the front desk, memberships and packages, gift cards, payroll and tipping, and card on file for deposits and no show fees. Zenoti says payouts often arrive the next business day. Rates are by quote.

Strengths: one payments setup across many locations. Limits: no published rate, so compare the quote against the flat rates on this page.

Mangomint

Mangomint publishes 2.45% plus 15 cents for in person payments, and 2.90% plus 30 cents on its other rate line, which covers card not present payments. You may use your own processor, but cards on file, deposits, self checkout and Express Booking need Mangomint's processing.

Strengths: a published rate, which many med spa platforms do not offer. Limits: you give up key features if you bring your own processor.

Aesthetic Record (AR Pay)

AR Pay lives inside Aesthetic Record. It handles deposits, packages and prepayments, memberships and subscriptions, and tap to pay on an iPhone. Bank account payments through Text2Pay are listed at 2.1%. Patient financing options include Klarna, Affirm, PatientFi, Sunbit and Cherry.

Strengths: the widest built in financing menu on this list. Limits: the card rate is not on the page we reviewed, so ask for it in writing.

PatientNow Payments

PatientNow offers card on file, text to pay, contactless and in person payments, with memberships, packages, recurring billing, deposit and fee policies, next day payouts and Cherry financing. Pricing is by quote.

Strengths: strong for a plastic surgery practice that also runs a med spa. Limits: no published rate.

Vagaro

Vagaro publishes its rates. Under $4,000 a month in card sales it charges 2.6% plus 10 cents per dip or tap with no monthly fee. Above that it charges 2.29% plus 19 cents for $10 a month. Keyed in cards are 3.5% plus 19 cents, and Tap to Pay adds 6 cents. Vagaro also offers Affirm at checkout and can split deposits across several merchant accounts.

Strengths: clear tiers and a low starting cost. Limits: the free EMV reader offer requires 12 months of processing, so read the merchant terms.

Square

Square's pricing page lists a free plan at 2.6% plus 15 cents in person, Plus at $49 a month and Premium at $149 a month with lower rates down to 2.4% plus 15 cents. Online and keyed rates are higher. Square sells gift cards, and packages need a paid Appointments, Plus or Premium plan.

Strengths: easy retail and simple hardware. Limits: Square is general purpose, so charting, consents and med spa membership rules sit elsewhere.

Clover

Clover advertises rates as low as 2.3% plus 10 cents, depending on plan and hardware, sold online or through partners. It is strong at retail and counter sales.

Strengths: good retail hardware. Limits: it is built for retail first, so check med spa features such as unit banks and memberships, and read the agreement, since terms vary by where you buy it.

Stripe Terminal

Stripe Terminal is listed at 2.7% plus 5 cents for US in person cards on standard pricing. It is a building block: your developer or software vendor builds the checkout around it.

Strengths: clear pricing and flexible APIs. Limits: no med spa features out of the box.

Cherry (financing at checkout)

Cherry is patient financing, not a POS. Patients apply at checkout and pay over time, and the practice is paid up front. Cherry advertises 6 months at 0% APR for qualifying patients on select Allergan Aesthetics treatments at no cost to the practice. It is built into Aesthetic Record and PatientNow. Merchant fees are by quote.

Strengths: helps patients say yes to larger plans. Limits: the fee comes out of your payout, so price it into the treatment.

For a wider look at lenders and payment plans, see What is the best patient financing for a medical practice?

What processing really costs

Card rates look small until you multiply them. At 2.6% plus 15 cents, $100,000 a month in card sales at an average ticket of $250 costs about $2,660 a month. Half a point lower saves about $500 a month. Ask every vendor for three numbers:

  1. The in person rate, the keyed rate and any monthly fee.
  2. Fees for refunds, chargebacks and instant payouts.
  3. How long the rate is locked and what ends the contract early.

Then compare the full cost, not the headline rate.

How to choose

  1. You already chose med spa software: use its built in payments unless the rate is far above the published rates here. Memberships and deposits work best inside the same system.
  2. One location, simple menu, retail heavy: Vagaro or Square, with charting in a separate tool.
  3. Several locations: Boulevard, Zenoti or Mangomint, with reports by location. See the medical group page for books across sites.
  4. Med spa inside a surgical practice: PatientNow or Aesthetic Record with financing built in.
  5. Custom app or online booking flow: Stripe Terminal, built by your developer.

Your structure decides which entity owns the merchant account. If you run a management company beside a medical practice, read med spa MSO vs medical director service first.

Questions, answered

Frequently asked questions

What is the best POS for a med spa with memberships?

Boulevard, Zenoti, Mangomint, AR Pay and PatientNow all run memberships with recurring billing inside the same system as booking. Pick the one that matches your software, then compare its rate with the published rates above.

Can I use Square for a med spa?

Yes, for payments and retail. Square sells gift cards and supports packages on paid plans, but it is not med spa software, so charting, consents and membership rules sit in another system.

What credit card rate should a med spa pay?

Published in person rates on this list run from about 2.29% to 2.7% plus a per transaction fee, as of November 26, 2026. Keyed in and online payments cost more. Bring a processing statement and ask for volume pricing.

Can I pass card fees to clients?

Some platforms, such as Boulevard Offset, let clients pay a fee on card payments. Surcharge rules differ by state and card network, and debit cards are treated differently, so check the rules for each location first.

How should tips be handled?

Tips belong to staff, not to the business. Collect them at the terminal, report them by provider and pay them through payroll. Your books should hold tips as a balance owed until they are paid out.

Do gift cards and packages count as sales?

Not when they are sold. They are prepaid balances until the client redeems them. Your POS should report sales, redemptions and the open balance, so your books can roll that balance forward each month. See best bookkeeping for a med spa.

Why does my POS total not match my bank deposit?

Deposits arrive net of processing fees, refunds and chargebacks, sometimes a day or two later, and gift card or financing payouts may land separately. Reconcile the POS payout report to each deposit, record fees as an expense and keep gross sales as revenue.
