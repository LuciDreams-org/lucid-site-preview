A regular accounting firm

A regular accounting firm serves many kinds of clients: local businesses, individuals, families, trusts and nonprofits. A solo CPA or a small partnership is the most common version.

What a regular firm offers:

  1. Local and in person. You can meet, drop off paperwork and call someone who knows your town.
  2. One advisor for the household. Your personal return, your practice, a rental property or an estate plan can sit in one place.
  3. Broad range. Regular firms often handle many entity types, state and local tax questions and IRS notices, and some offer audit or attest work.
  4. Pricing data. In Ignition's July 2025 survey of 219 US accounting firms that are Ignition customers (a vendor survey that is not specific to medical practices), monthly bookkeeping fees most commonly fell between $250 and $499 (29 percent of firms) and $500 to $749 (27 percent). About 13 percent charged $1,000 to $1,499, and 3 percent charged more than $2,500. For business tax returns, $1,000 to $1,499 was the most common fee (29 percent), followed by $1,500 to $1,999 (26 percent).
  5. Pricing models. Fixed fees are the norm in the same survey, and only about 4 percent of firms billed bookkeeping hourly. Intuit's 2021 rate survey, as reported by indinero, put the average CPA at $126 an hour and the average bookkeeper at $66. Those figures are dated.
  6. CFO level work. About 62 percent of the surveyed firms said they offer CFO or controller services. The most common monthly fee was over $2,500 (23 percent of firms).

Where a regular firm differs from Lucid:

  1. Medical setup varies by firm. Medical books have their own needs. PwC's health care guidance notes that most health care service revenue has some element of variable consideration, and that the transaction price is the amount the provider expects to be entitled to, not simply the amount billed. A med spa that sells prepaid packages or memberships generally tracks that cash as a liability until treatments are delivered on management or GAAP style books, per a CPA authored guide, while taxable income timing is analyzed separately. Lucid's pages describe handling these items. With any regular firm, ask how it records insurance deposits, write offs, packages and memberships.
  2. Reports by location and provider. Ask for a sample P&L by location and by provider. Lucid reports them as standard.
  3. Tax season capacity. In Rightworks' 2026 post tax season report, 41 percent of firms said tax season still feels overwhelming, and 84 percent dealt with late or unprepared clients. AICPA reports that accounting degrees fell 6.6 percent in the 2023 to 2024 academic year, a slower decline than in the two years before. Ask any firm about start dates and response times from January to April.
  4. Turnaround is not standardized. No neutral survey of month end turnaround at small firms was found. Ask for the date by which you will have your monthly reports, in writing.
  5. Software. In the 2026 CPAFMA survey of 162 mostly mid sized and larger firms, QuickBooks Online and QuickBooks Desktop were the top accounting tools, and 75 percent of firms used email as the main way to communicate with clients. Ask what a firm builds on top of the ledger for a medical practice.

Questions, answered

Frequently asked questions

Do I need an accountant who specializes in medical practices?

If your practice has several payers, locations, providers, packages or memberships, yes, a specialized setup helps. Lucid is built for that, with reporting by location, provider and payer and a dedicated CPA on every account. A very simple one location practice can manage with a regular firm.

How much does an accountant cost for a medical practice?

In a July 2025 survey of 219 US accounting firms that are Ignition customers (a vendor survey that is not specific to medical practices), 56 percent charged $250 to $749 a month for bookkeeping, and the most common business tax return fee was $1,000 to $1,499. Practice size and complexity change the number. Lucid publishes its prices and starts from $130 a month.

Can a regular CPA handle a practice with several locations?

Some can. Ask for a sample report by location and by provider before you sign an engagement letter. Lucid reports consolidated results across locations, with margin by location or provider.

Will a regular firm understand payer deposits, packages and memberships?

Ask how it records insurance deposits, write offs, patient balances, prepaid packages and memberships, and ask for an example from another practice with names removed. Lucid's specialty pages describe how it handles each.

What should I ask a regular firm before hiring?

How many medical clients it has, who prepares your returns, what software it uses, when you get monthly reports, how fast it replies, whether it accepts new clients in January to April, and what the minimum term is.

Is a CPA different from an accountant or bookkeeper?

A CPA is a licensed credential. Bookkeepers and many accountants are not CPAs. Lucid puts a dedicated CPA on every account. Ask your current firm who prepares your returns.

Can Lucid replace my local CPA?

For most practices with staff or several locations, yes. Lucid does bookkeeping, tax, tax credits and CFO planning, and handles the switch. It does not list audit or attest work.

Are they secure and HIPAA ready?

Lucid says it is aligned to SOC 2 criteria and HIPAA aligned handling, with a Business Associate Agreement available. A regular firm varies. Ask for its security documents and whether it will sign a Business Associate Agreement.
