Short answer

For most new DPC practices the shortlist is Hint for membership billing and employer plans, Atlas.md for one flat priced all in one system, and Elation for fast charting with built in membership billing. Akute is a lower cost modern EHR that adds Hint billing, Cerbo and Charm suit cash pay clinics that want a configurable or low cost EHR, and Spruce handles patient phone, text and messaging beside any of them. Compare processing fees as well as subscriptions, because on a full panel the card fees can cost more than the software. None of these do your accounting, which is where Lucid fits.

What DPC software has to do

A DPC practice runs on three jobs, and some products do all three.

  1. Membership billing. Plans by age or family, enrollment fees, recurring card and ACH charges, failed payment alerts and employer invoices. AAFP's 2026 DPC survey found 64 percent charged an enrollment fee and 55 percent had employer contracts.
  2. EHR. Charting, prescribing, labs and referrals. AAFP's 2026 survey found Atlas.md was the most used EHR among DPC respondents (26 percent), taking over from Elation, which led in 2022 and 2024.
  3. Messaging. Members expect text, phone and portal access. Some EHRs include it, others pair with Spruce.

Hint Health

Hint is the best known membership billing platform in DPC and now sells a full platform with an EMR, scheduling and messaging. Launch starts at $290 a month with one clinician and unlimited members. Pro starts at $320 with 100 members and adds Hint AI, employer management and sales tax handling. Scale starts at $770 with 500 members and adds affiliate and commission tracking. Hint also sells billing only pricing for practices that keep another EHR, and startups pay nothing until they launch. Card fees run 3.00 percent plus $0.30 on Launch, falling to 2.60 percent on Scale.

Best for: practices with employer contracts, several clinicians or several sites. Limits: member tranches on Pro and Scale raise the price as the panel grows.

Atlas.md

Atlas.md is an all in one EHR built for direct care, with membership billing, labs and messaging inside one system. Its terms list $300 a month per registered provider with unlimited patients, and card payments at 2.1 percent plus $0.30, per Tabflows' summary of its pricing page. Atlas.md's own site blocked our fetch.

Best for: solo and small DPC practices that want one tool at one flat price. Limits: less freedom to swap parts of the stack. E-prescribing and fax fees are separate, per Tabflows.

Elation Health

Elation is a primary care EHR known for fast charting. Its Membership Management feature handles recurring plans, patient self enrollment, automatic billing on a schedule, cards on file and membership reporting. Its product guide lists a Standard DPC Provider at $375 per provider a month, and a Standard Provider at $425.

Best for: practices that care most about charting speed, or hybrids that still bill some insurance. Limits: lab connections vary by region, and some practices pair it with Hint or Spruce, per Tabflows.

Akute Health

Akute is a newer EHR with a clean interface. Launch is $100 a month for one clinician and 50 patients, or the first six months. Essentials is $300 a month for one clinician and 100 patients, then $0.75 per added patient. Premium is $550 for two clinicians and 300 patients. Membership billing runs through a Hint Core add on, priced from $50 to $110 a month by plan. Tabflows notes native links to Spruce, Hint, Quest, LabCorp and Surescripts.

Best for: new practices that want a low starting price and a modern EHR. Limits: per patient pricing adds up on a large panel, and billing is a separate Hint add on.

Cerbo

Cerbo is a highly configurable EHR used by integrative, functional and cash pay clinics, with membership add ons. Pricing is per user: $281 a month for a full time prescribing provider, $174 part time, and $63 for added staff. There is a one time setup fee, with a discount for new part time solo practices. It says there are no long term contracts.

Best for: clinics that want to configure almost everything. Limits: a steeper learning curve, and per user pricing.

Charm Health

Charm is a low cost EHR with practice management and billing. Its free plan allows 50 encounters a month. The encounter plan costs $0.50 per encounter with a $25 monthly minimum. The provider plan is $200 per provider a month. Integration and add on charges are separate.

Best for: new practices watching every dollar, or practices with low visit volume. Limits: less DPC specific than Hint or Atlas.md. Check how it handles recurring memberships before you choose it.

Spruce Health

Spruce is not an EHR. It is a phone, text, fax, video and team messaging app for clinics, with a HIPAA business associate agreement. Basic is $24 per user a month and Communicator is $49, with a 14 day free trial and month to month billing.

Best for: any DPC practice whose EHR messaging is not enough. Limits: one more tool to pay for and connect.

Is DPC worth it?

It can be, if you can fund the ramp. AAFP's 2026 survey found 47 percent of DPC respondents reported lower earnings than before and 30 percent higher, while most reported better care and work life balance. The average panel was 504 patients, and 40 percent named panel growth as a major challenge. Hint's data shows a median practice with 45 members at month six reaching about 218 by year three. For the full decision, including concierge options and Medicare opt out points, plan with your CPA and a healthcare attorney.

Questions to ask on every demo

  1. What does the price become at 300, 500 and 1,000 members, and with a second clinician?
  2. What are the card and ACH rates, and is there a cap per ACH charge?
  3. Can it bill employers on one invoice and handle family and child add ons?
  4. How do I export members, plans and payments if I leave?
  5. Which labs, e-prescribing and messaging tools connect natively, and which cost extra?
  6. Is there a setup or onboarding fee, and is there a long term contract?

Chart a few real visits in each trial, and run one month of test billing before you move live members. Moving a full panel's cards later is the hard part, so choose with your third year in mind.

How to choose

  1. Solo, opening soon, want one bill: Atlas.md, or Hint Launch.
  2. Employer contracts or several clinicians: Hint Pro or Scale.
  3. Charting speed first, or a hybrid with some insurance: Elation.
  4. Lowest starting cost: Akute Launch or Charm.
  5. Integrative or functional clinic: Cerbo.
  6. Messaging gaps in any of the above: add Spruce.
Questions, answered

Frequently asked questions

Is Hint or Atlas.md better for a new DPC practice?

Atlas.md gives one flat price per provider with unlimited patients and everything in one place. Hint gives stronger membership and employer billing and lets you keep another EHR. If employers matter to you, start with Hint.

Do I need a separate EHR with Hint?

No. Hint now sells a full platform with an EMR. Practices that like their current EHR can buy billing only pricing instead.

What does DPC software cost per month?

For one clinician, published prices start around $100 to $375 a month, before processing fees. Member based tiers rise as your panel grows.

Are processing fees a big cost?

Yes. On a full panel, card fees can be larger than the subscription. Ask each vendor for card and ACH rates, and push members to ACH where you can.

Can DPC software bill employers?

Hint's Pro and Scale plans include employer management and billing. Ask other vendors how they invoice employers and handle group rosters.

Does my DPC software do my accounting?

No. It collects fees and reports on members. Your books still need bank reconciliation, membership revenue matched to the months it covers, expenses, payroll records and taxes.

What should I charge members?

Hint's 2026 report put the median retail fee at $99 a month in 2025. Since January 2026, patients can pay qualifying DPC fees from an HSA if the fee is at or under $150 a month for one person, per IRS Notice 2026-5.
