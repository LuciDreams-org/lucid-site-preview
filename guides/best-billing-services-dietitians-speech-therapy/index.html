Short answer

A nutrition group that wants insurance has two real paths: join a network such as Berry Street or Fay, which handle credentialing and billing and pay your dietitians per session, or keep claims under your own group and hire a dietitian biller such as Nutrition Billing or Camille DeSimone Medical Billing. A speech therapy group usually wants a rehab biller that works inside its current system, such as Brace Health, Clinical Billing Solutions, Ensora RCM for Fusion users, or Luxen. Only some of these publish a fee, so compare quotes on one month of your own claims. Then judge the winner on what reaches your bank, by payer and by clinician.

Network or biller: the first decision

This choice matters more than the vendor name.

With a network such as Berry Street or Fay, the platform credentials your dietitians, finds many of the patients, bills the payers and pays the dietitian. Fay says dietitians are not its employees and receive a 1099 from Fay. Your group's revenue then arrives as platform payouts, not as payer deposits.

With a biller, claims go out under your group's own contracts and tax ID. Payers pay you. The biller takes a fee. You own the payer relationships, the patient list and the data if you ever switch.

For more on the money side of taking insurance at all, see insurance vs private pay for group practices.

Berry Street

Berry Street says it handles "the entire insurance process" for dietitians: credentialing, eligibility and billing, plus an EHR, scheduling, video calls and reminders. It lists a path for group practices and says it offers guaranteed billing, so providers are paid even if claims are denied.

Its provider handbook lists solo provider rates of $35 an hour for initial appointments and $70 an hour for follow ups with Berry Street sourced insurance patients, and $75 an hour for provider sourced insurance patients. Group practice rates vary by practice.

Best for: dietitians and small groups that want patient flow and no claim work. Limits: you are paid a platform rate, not the payer rate. Ask for a group quote in writing.

Fay

Fay offers insurance credentialing and billing, marketing and referrals, and AI tools for dietitians, and says it is free to join. It describes itself as a national network of registered dietitians, and says dietitians run their own book of business rather than working as employees.

Best for: dietitians who want a steady referral source alongside their own practice. Limits: payout rates are not published on its public pages. Confirm how group practices are set up.

Nutrition Billing

Nutrition Billing is a billing service built for registered dietitians. It helps providers get in network, submits claims electronically and shows claim status in real time. It publishes its fees: 12 percent of amounts paid up to 99 claims a month, falling to 11, 10, 9 and 8 percent as volume grows. Onboarding is $100 per provider, with set fees for out of network claims and denied in network claims.

Best for: nutrition groups that want claims under their own contracts and a clear rate card. Limits: read the optional fee list, since some steps cost extra.

Camille DeSimone Medical Billing

This firm focuses on dietitian billing and says it has served medical providers since 1993. Its service list covers a free consultation on payers that cover registered dietitians, free setup, manual review of each claim before submission, claims processed within 72 hours of receipt, secondary claims, follow up on open claims and appeal letters. It also offers credentialing.

Best for: groups that want an RD specialist with a long track record. Limits: no published fee.

Soundry Health

Soundry Health pairs an EHR with built in billing. Its pricing page says automation submits claims, checks eligibility and posts payments, and its billing team helps fix rejected claims in the app. The published price is $100 a month plus $2 per claim plus 3 percent of what you get paid, with unlimited providers.

Best for: small nutrition groups that want one tool and some billing help. Limits: this is software plus support, not a team that owns every claim. Confirm how much follow up is included.

Brace Health

Brace Health sells an outsourced billing service run by a named, US based billing partner backed by AI tools. It says it works inside your existing EMR and reports an average response time under 30 minutes. Brace also publishes a speech therapy billing comparison that lists itself.

Best for: therapy groups whose biller left, or that want a single accountable contact. Limits: no published rate. Ask for SLP references.

Clinical Billing Solutions

CBS runs billing for PT, OT and speech clinics. Its FAQ says its coders apply time based unit rules, NCCI edits and therapy modifiers, including GN for speech.

Best for: speech clinics that also run PT or OT. Limits: no published pricing. Confirm support for your EMR.

Ensora RCM

Ensora offers managed billing as an add on for Fusion and TheraNest users. Its team submits claims, posts payments and follows up on denials inside the EHR. It says the service fits practices collecting over $15,000 a month from insurance.

Best for: speech groups already on Fusion. Limits: built for Ensora's own systems. Pricing is quoted.

Luxen

Luxen bills for speech therapy practices, from eligibility to AR follow up, working inside the practice's existing EHR. It states pricing of 3 to 6 percent of collections.

Best for: SLP groups that want a stated percentage before the first call. Limits: confirm what sits outside the base rate.

Medicare rules in plain terms

Both specialties have Medicare rules that a general biller can miss.

  1. Nutrition. Medicare Part B covers medical nutrition therapy for patients with diabetes or kidney disease, or a kidney transplant in the last 36 months, with a referral. Coverage is 3 hours of initial services, then 2 hours each later calendar year. Only a registered dietitian or qualified nutrition professional can provide it, and the patient pays nothing if eligible.
  2. Speech therapy. Medicare pays outpatient speech therapy under a plan of care, with the GN modifier on claims. Above a yearly threshold, shared with physical therapy and set at $2,480 for 2026, claims need the KX modifier to confirm the care is still medically necessary.

Commercial plans and Medicaid differ by state and by plan. Your biller should know your payers by name.

How to choose

  1. Solo dietitians or a small team without contracts yet: a network such as Berry Street or Fay is the fastest start.
  2. A nutrition group with its own contracts: compare Nutrition Billing, Camille DeSimone and Soundry on one month of claims.
  3. A speech group on Fusion: price Ensora RCM first, then one independent quote.
  4. A speech group on another system, or running PT and OT too: compare Brace, CBS and Luxen.
  5. Strong biller in house: keep them and outsource only old AR.

Ask every service the same questions:

  1. What percent, minimum and setup fee apply at my volume?
  2. Who owns the payer contracts and patient data if we part ways?
  3. Will I get monthly reports by clinician and payer?
  4. Who tracks Medicare MNT hours and therapy thresholds?
  5. Who works open claims after we leave?

How to know your biller is working

A biller reports what it posted. Your bank shows what arrived. Check these every month:

  1. Deposits against remits. Every payer deposit should match its remittance.
  2. Net collected per visit or per 15 minute unit, by payer.
  3. Write offs by reason, such as missing referrals or timely filing.
  4. Open claims by age, especially past 90 days.
  5. Billing fee as a percent of collections.

On a network model, check payouts against sessions delivered instead, clinician by clinician.

Questions, answered

Frequently asked questions

Should my nutrition group bill itself, hire a biller, or join Fay or Berry Street?

If you have no payer contracts and want patient flow, a network is the fastest path, but you are paid a platform rate. If you already hold contracts, a dietitian biller keeps the payer rate and your data. Billing yourself works when volume is low and someone owns follow up.

What does dietitian billing cost?

Nutrition Billing publishes 12 percent of paid amounts at low volume, falling to 8 percent at high volume, plus $100 onboarding per provider. Soundry Health publishes $100 a month, $2 per claim and 3 percent. Fay and Berry Street are free to join and earn their share through the payout.

What does speech therapy billing cost?

Luxen states 3 to 6 percent of collections. Brace Health, CBS and Ensora quote per practice. Compare at your own monthly collections, with every add on fee included.

Does Medicare cover dietitian visits?

Yes, for medical nutrition therapy for diabetes, kidney disease or a recent kidney transplant, with a referral. Coverage is 3 hours of initial services, then 2 hours each later calendar year. The dietitian must be enrolled with Medicare.

What is the KX modifier for speech therapy?

Medicare sets a yearly spending threshold for outpatient speech and physical therapy combined, $2,480 in 2026. Claims above it carry the KX modifier to confirm the care is medically necessary, backed by documentation.

How do I know my billing company is collecting everything?

Match payer deposits to remits every month, and track net collected per visit by payer, write offs by reason and claims past 90 days. Your bookkeeper or accountant can check this from the bank side.
