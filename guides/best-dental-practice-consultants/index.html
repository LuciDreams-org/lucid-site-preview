Which dental practice consultants are worth the fee?

Short answer

A dental consultant is worth the fee when you can name the problem, agree on a baseline number before the work starts, and see the change in your production, collections and overhead within a few months. Large coaching firms such as Levin Group, Fortune Management, Scheduling Institute, Jameson and Productive Dentist Academy do not publish fees, so you will get a quote after an assessment call. Smaller firms such as NOW Dental Practice Management Consulting, Today's Dental Consulting and ExecTech do publish monthly fees, from $395 to about $4,800 a month as listed on their own pages on November 26, 2026. Whichever you pick, treat every results claim as the firm's own marketing until your books confirm it.

Start with the problem, not the firm

Most dental consulting work falls into four buckets.

  1. Front office: phones, scheduling, new patient conversion and case acceptance.
  2. Clinical and hygiene: chair time, hygiene department systems, treatment planning.
  3. Leadership and team: hiring, pay, culture, the owner as bottleneck.
  4. Business and numbers: overhead, PPO mix, fees, a second office, a sale.

Buckets 1 to 3 are where coaching firms earn their fee. Bucket 4 is often a finance visibility problem first. If your books close late or production never matches your deposits, fix that before you pay anyone to raise production, because you will not be able to prove the raise. For the general version of this decision across all specialties, read how to choose a medical practice consultant. This guide covers dental firms only.

What the published fees look like

Most large firms quote after a free assessment or discovery call. Three smaller firms put numbers on their pages.

  1. NOW Dental Practice Management Consulting lists monthly meetings for a solo practice with up to eight staff at $1,025 for 1.5 hours, $1,850 for 3 hours and $2,150 for 4 hours. A second dentist or location adds 50%. Its practice analysis is listed at $1,025 and is free for new clients. It says there is no contract to sign.
  2. Today's Dental Consulting lists Full Consulting at $1,395 a month and Consulting Light at $395 a month, both built around Open Dental setup and team training, month to month with no setup fee.
  3. ExecTech lists a flat fee of about $3,400 to $4,800 a month for a custom consulting and coaching program, based on goals, practice size, meeting location and how much coaching you get.

Use these as a reference point when a larger firm quotes you. Ask for the total cost over 12 months, including events, travel and any software, so you compare like with like.

Levin Group

Levin Group was founded in 1982 by Roger P. Levin, DDS. Its consulting covers scheduling, production, case acceptance, collections, overhead control and team performance, plus referral marketing programs for specialists such as oral surgeons, orthodontists, periodontists, endodontists and pediatric practices. It offers a free practice assessment call.

Results, as the firm presents them: its site says it has been chosen by over 30,000 practices and quotes client testimonials reporting production growth of 30% to 49% within about a year. These are the firm's own client quotes, not audited figures.

Best for: established general and specialty practices that want a structured production program.

Fortune Management

Fortune Management says its leadership has been coaching since 1989, with more than 150 executive consultants working locally, in the office and at local events, from its base in Reno, Nevada. It offers a free practice analysis, training camps and programs such as Fortune 50, DDS to CEO, Hygiene Mastery and Clinical Mastery.

Results, as the firm presents them: it says it has coached more than 15,000 dentists and runs more than 400 local live events a year. It does not publish outcome figures on its home page.

Best for: owners who want a local coach in the office, not only video calls.

Scheduling Institute

Scheduling Institute focuses on new patient acquisition, retention and referrals, and case acceptance, with training for phones, scheduling, front office and team. It offers a free 5-Star Challenge assessment and a discovery call.

Results, as the firm presents them: it says it has worked with thousands of practice owners for nearly three decades and promises growth within 90 days. It does not publish figures behind that line.

Best for: practices whose main leak is the phone and the front desk.

Jameson

Jameson publishes its coaching plans by name: an Office Evaluation (data, on site assessment, recommendations), a one to one Blueprint session, Accelerate (monthly virtual coaching, e-learning, analytics dashboards, webinars and an annual planning session), Propel (one to two day on site coaching for one problem), Leadership Coaching and Hygiene Optimization. Prices are not published.

Results, as the firm presents them: better results, better work and life balance and higher patient satisfaction, without published figures.

Best for: owners who want to start small, with one evaluation or session, before committing to ongoing coaching.

Productive Dentist Academy

Productive Dentist Academy says it has been coaching since 2004. It offers personalized coaching on production, operations and practice value, plus a leadership program, a marketing agency and an online clinical training institute. Its "Pledge" program targets a $300 an hour increase in production, with terms set out in its services agreement.

Results, as the firm presents them: it says its methods have helped practices raise productivity by $300 or more per hour, and it quotes a client who says production doubled in two years. Ask for the pledge terms in writing before you sign.

Best for: owners focused on production per hour and case acceptance.

Weiss Practice Enhancement

Weiss Practice Enhancement uses your practice statistics to find why a system is or is not working, then focuses each call on fixing it. Programs include The Resilient Leader, The Strategic Leader, Leading By Your Numbers, a Team Culture Diagnostic and a Compensation Kit, plus a dental wage planner. Prices are not published.

Results, as the firm presents them: a client team member describes monthly production rising from $88,000 to a pace of $111,000 a year later.

Best for: owners whose problem is leadership, team pay and reading their own numbers.

CascadEffects

CascadEffects works with orthodontic and dental practices as an embedded operations partner. It offers a practice performance analysis, a local competitive analysis, a six month "deep work" engagement as a fractional COO, ongoing leadership coaching and virtual assistant staffing. It says scope and investment are proposed after a free first call, based on size, locations and team.

Results, as the firm presents them: its own blog cites a 17% production increase over six months at a multi location orthodontic practice.

Best for: multi location and orthodontic groups that need hands on operations leadership.

How to tell if a consultant is working

Agree on four or five numbers before day one, then review them every month in your financial reports, not only in the consultant's dashboard.

  1. Production, by provider and by office.
  2. Collections, and the gap between production and collections.
  3. Overhead as a percent of collections, with the same definition each month. See overhead percentage.
  4. New patients and case acceptance, from your practice software.
  5. Cash at the end of the month, after the consultant's own fee.

If production rises but collections and cash do not, the gain may be PPO write offs or unpaid balances. That is a books question, and it is why reconciled dental bookkeeping matters before you judge any coach. The monthly numbers every owner should review is a good checklist.

How to choose by situation

  1. Phones ring but chairs sit empty: a front office and new patient program such as Scheduling Institute.
  2. Production is flat in an established practice: a structured production program such as Levin Group or Productive Dentist Academy.
  3. You want someone in the office: a local coaching model such as Fortune Management, or Jameson's on site sessions.
  4. Team, pay and leadership are the problem: Weiss Practice Enhancement or a leadership program.
  5. Several offices and no operations leader: a fractional COO such as CascadEffects.
  6. You want a known monthly fee and short commitment: firms that publish fees, such as NOW Dental, Today's Dental Consulting or ExecTech.
  7. Margins are slipping and you cannot see why: clean books and a part time CFO first. See part time CFO cost.
  8. You are thinking about PPOs or a sale: model it first. See dropping dental PPOs and selling to a DSO.

If the gap is new patients rather than systems, compare How do I choose a medical marketing agency? If you want more steady revenue from uninsured patients, see Should my practice offer a membership plan?

Questions to ask a dental consultant

  1. Which of my numbers will you move, from what baseline, by when?
  2. What is the total cost over 12 months, including events, travel and software?
  3. How long is the commitment, and what does it take to end it early?
  4. Who coaches us, by name, and how often?
  5. Can I speak with two practices my size, with my PPO mix, that you worked with?
  6. Is any results claim on your site audited, or is it a client's own report?
  7. Do you earn referral fees from vendors, labs, software or buyers you recommend?
Questions, answered

Frequently asked questions

Is a dental consultant worth it?

It can be, when the problem is clear and measurable, such as new patient conversion, hygiene scheduling or case acceptance. Set a baseline before you sign and check production, collections and overhead in your books each month. If you cannot measure the change, you cannot judge the fee.

How much does a dental practice consultant cost?

Most large firms quote after an assessment. Firms that publish fees list $395 to $1,395 a month (Today's Dental Consulting), $1,025 to $2,150 a month for a solo practice (NOW Dental Practice Management Consulting) and about $3,400 to $4,800 a month (ExecTech), as seen November 26, 2026.

Which dental consultants publish their prices?

Of the firms in this guide, NOW Dental Practice Management Consulting, Today's Dental Consulting and ExecTech publish monthly fees. Levin Group, Fortune Management, Scheduling Institute, Jameson, Productive Dentist Academy, Weiss Practice Enhancement and CascadEffects quote after a call.

Can I trust the results consultants advertise?

Treat them as the firm's own marketing. Most are client testimonials without a stated baseline or method. Ask for references from practices like yours and agree on your own numbers to track.

How long before a dental consultant shows results?

Firms often promise early wins within about 90 days, and some engagements start at six months. Agree on a review date, such as month three and month six, and the numbers you will check then.

Should I hire a consultant or a CFO first?

If production does not match deposits, or you cannot see profit by office or provider, start with reconciled books and a part time CFO. If the numbers are clear and the problem is operations or team, a consultant is the better first hire.

Do dental consultants help with a sale or DSO offer?

Some coaching firms advise on practice value, but a sale needs a broker or advisor, an attorney and clean, reconciled books. Get an independent view of value before you sign any offer.
