Short answer

For a therapy or psychiatry group that wants its insurance billing done for it, the services that publish rates are the easiest place to start: Mental Health Billing SRG, Practolytics, ePsych Billing, Advance A Practice, Practice Solutions and TheraThink. Ensora RCM suits groups already on TheraNest or Fusion, and Billing for Therapists bundles its service with a free EHR. Compare them on what the percentage is charged on, the monthly minimum and who works denials. Then check the result every month against the deposits that land in your bank.

Mental Health Billing SRG

Mental Health Billing SRG says it bills for therapists, psychiatrists, psychologists, ABA providers and group practices. Its home page lists 3.99% of what it collects, with credentialing, denials, patient statements and reporting included, and says you pay nothing if it does not collect. It also lists testing, IOP and PHP, and medication authorizations.

Best for: groups that want the lowest published percentage with credentialing in the base rate. Limits: ask for a written list of what "included" covers and the notice period.

Practolytics

Practolytics bills across many specialties, and its behavioral health brochure lists four plans from 3.49% to 4.99% of net collections, a $750 monthly minimum, and setup fees waived on 12 month agreements. It is covered in more depth in our best medical billing companies guide.

Best for: behavioral health groups with enough volume to clear the minimum. Limits: the minimum is high for a small group, and the lower rates sit in higher tiers.

ePsych Billing

ePsych Billing works with therapists, psychologists, psychiatrists and group practices. Its site lists 4.75% of collections with a $400 monthly minimum, plus $4 per benefits verification. It says there are no setup fees, terms are month to month, and it offers a 30 day trial. It also says it posts EOBs and ERAs within 24 hours.

Best for: groups that want a mid range rate without a long agreement. Limits: benefits checks are billed per check, so count them for a busy intake month.

Advance A Practice

Advance A Practice sells billing, credentialing and practice setup with published package prices. Ongoing billing is 7% of collections for a solo provider with a $300 monthly minimum. The rate falls to 6.5% for 2 to 5 providers, 6% for 6 to 10, 5% for 11 to 25 and 4.5% for 26 to 50, each with a per provider minimum. There is no setup fee, and old claims over 90 days can be worked for 20% of what it recovers.

Best for: groups that plan to keep hiring, since the rate steps down with headcount. Limits: your monthly fee is the greater of the percentage or the combined minimum, so model a slow month.

Practice Solutions

Practice Solutions offers done-for-you billing for solo therapists through group practices, covering eligibility and benefits through the rest of the revenue cycle. It says it charges 6% of collections, with set pricing available on request. It also sells billing training and do it yourself tools.

Best for: groups that want a service now and may train an in house biller later. Limits: ask what the 6% covers in your consultation, as its site suggests.

TheraThink

TheraThink is a mental health billing service that charges 6.5% to 8% of the contracted rate or allowed amount of each claim it helps collect, depending on volume. It invites groups with two or more providers to ask about group rates. It lists no startup fee, no per claim fee and free eligibility checks. A $35 monthly clearinghouse fee per tax ID and rendering NPI applies in months with claims, waived when that fee would be under 5% of your invoice.

Best for: small groups that want a percentage they can check against each claim. Limits: the allowed amount includes the client's share, so the base can be larger than what the insurer pays you.

Ensora RCM

Ensora Health sells Ensora RCM as an add-on for its TheraNest and Fusion EHRs, and its team works inside your EHR. RCM Essentials is for practices with 1 to 5 clinicians at a set monthly rate, covering daily claim submission and payment posting. RCM Advanced is priced as a percentage of posted payments and adds A/R follow up, appeals and an assigned performance manager.

Best for: groups already on TheraNest or Fusion. Limits: both plans are by quote, and you need Ensora's EHR. See our behavioral health EHR guide for the software side.

Billing for Therapists

Billing for Therapists offers mental health insurance billing services with its own billing software at no charge. Its site lists daily claims filing, benefits checks within 2 business days, appeals and denials, EAP, Medicare, Medicaid and TriCare billing, credentialing, a month by month agreement and free account setup.

Best for: small groups that want the service and an EHR from one place. Limits: pricing is not on its site, so get it in writing.

Not billing services, but often compared

  1. Headway, Alma and Grow Therapy are networks. They credential clinicians under their own contracts and pay you, so you give up rate control. Our insurance vs private pay guide covers how they pay a group.
  2. Thrizer handles out of network billing for clients, so it suits private pay groups, not in network claims.
  3. SimplePractice now presents insurance billing tools and phone access to insurance specialists on its site, not a done-for-you billing service, so it is not listed above.

These are not like for like. Scope differs, and TheraThink charges on the allowed amount, not on collections. The real test is your net collection rate after the fee, which a cheap rate with slow denial work can lose.

What makes mental health billing different

  1. Time based codes. Psychotherapy codes such as 90834 and 90837 depend on session length, and add-on codes apply when therapy is paired with a medication visit. Ask how the service checks time and add-on rules.
  2. Supervised clinicians. Pre licensed clinicians often bill under a supervisor, and payer rules differ. Ask how the service handles rendering and supervising providers.
  3. Psychiatry. Medication visits, prior authorizations and treatments such as Spravato bring medical style billing. ePsych, Mental Health Billing SRG and Practolytics all list psychiatry work.
  4. Telehealth. Place of service codes and modifiers vary by payer. Ask for the service's telehealth rules by payer.
  5. Credentialing. Each new hire needs to be credentialed before claims pay. Ask whether credentialing is in the rate.

How to choose

  1. 2 to 5 therapists, mostly insurance: compare Mental Health Billing SRG, ePsych Billing and Advance A Practice, then ask TheraThink for its group rate.
  2. Group with prescribers: start with ePsych Billing, Mental Health Billing SRG and Practolytics, and ask each for a psychiatry reference.
  3. 10 or more clinicians: get quotes from Practolytics and Advance A Practice, where the published rates fall with volume.
  4. Already on TheraNest or Fusion: price Ensora RCM first, since it works inside your EHR.
  5. Want to bring billing in house later: look at Practice Solutions, which also trains billers.

Ask every service four questions. What is the percentage charged on? What is the minimum? Who works denials and old A/R, and how fast? What reports will I get by payer and by clinician?

Questions, answered

Frequently asked questions

What does a mental health billing service cost?

Most charge a percentage of collections. On their own sites, published rates run from 3.49% to 8%, often with a monthly minimum. For what billing companies charge in general, see what a medical billing company costs.

Is a billing service better than Headway or Alma for a group?

It depends on control. A billing service bills under your own contracts and rates. Headway and Alma use their contracts and set the rate, but take the claims work away. Model both on your own numbers before moving clinicians.

Can a billing service handle psychiatry and therapy in one group?

Yes, several do. ePsych Billing, Mental Health Billing SRG and Practolytics list psychiatry. Ask for references from groups with prescribers.

Do billing services include credentialing?

Some do. Mental Health Billing SRG includes it in its rate, and Billing for Therapists lists it as a service. Others price it separately, so ask.

How long does it take to switch billing services?

Plan for a few weeks of setup and a few months of overlap. Claims already sent keep paying out after you switch, so agree in writing who works them.

How do I know my billing service is collecting everything?

Match each insurance deposit to its remittance, review A/R aging by payer and track your net collection rate each month. Your bookkeeper or accountant can do this from the bank side.
