What a dental practice needs from its books

PPO fee schedules and write offs. Most dental practices bill a full fee and are paid a contracted PPO amount, and the difference is a write off. Practice software often reports production net of it, hiding what a plan gives away. Books should show gross production, the contractual adjustment, net collected and write off percent by plan, so you see which plans earn their chair time. Lucid reports revenue by plan from your practice software reports and your books. What you renegotiate or drop is your decision.

Overhead. Staff, supplies and lab costs can rise faster than production. Accounting sources commonly cite overhead of 55 to 65 percent of collections for a general practice, though definitions differ on collections versus production and owner pay. Lucid tracks each line the same way monthly and shows what moved.

Production versus collections. Production is dentistry done. Books record money that arrived. The gap is write offs, unpaid claims and patient balances, so reconcile them monthly. Lucid does.

Claim timing and cash. Insurance pays on a lag, so a profitable practice can be short two days before payroll. Your biller handles the claims. Lucid does not file or chase them. Lucid adds visibility: AR aging reports and a cash forecast that put your bank balance, insurance payments and patient balances in one view.

Associate and hygienist pay. Associates are commonly paid around 25 to 35 percent of production or collections, and the base matters as much as the percent. Lucid's CFO planning can model what each formula costs using your numbers, and Lucid shows margin by provider. Your CPA also reviews W-2 versus 1099 status.

Patient payment plans. Third party financing usually pays you net of a fee, so book the fee as an expense and keep revenue at full price. An in house plan creates a patient receivable that belongs on your aging report.

Several offices and groups. A strong first office can carry a weaker second one inside a blended P&L. Each office needs the same chart of accounts, a clear rule for shared costs and management fees, and a combined owner view. Lucid reports each office and the group, with intercompany reconciliation when a management company sits above the practices.

Equipment and Section 179. Loan principal drains the bank account without showing on the P&L, so a profitable practice can feel cash poor after buying imaging or a milling unit. Equipment can sometimes be deducted in the first year through Section 179 or bonus depreciation, depending on current rules and your income, so review before you sign. Lucid models payback and the effect on tax and cash, and your CPA confirms current limits.

Owner pay. For an S corporation owner, the split between salary and distributions affects payroll tax. Your CPA sets reasonable compensation, and Lucid's team handles year round tax planning, quarterly estimates and S corporation strategy.

Questions, answered

Questions owners ask

What is the best bookkeeping and tax service for a dental practice?

Lucid. It gives you a dedicated CPA, PPO write offs and production versus collections reconciled each month, results by office, and bookkeeping, tax, tax credits and CFO planning in one team, from $130 a month.

How do I know how much my PPO write offs are really costing my dental practice?

Compare each plan's contracted payment to your full fee and total it by plan. Lucid builds that report from the reports your practice software produces and your books. You decide what to renegotiate or drop.

Why is my dental practice always short on cash when production looks great?

Insurance often pays weeks after the work, and part of what you produce is never collected. Lucid puts your bank balance next to insurance and patient balances and builds a cash forecast. Your biller still handles the claims.

Can I write off a new CBCT or milling machine for my dental practice?

Often a large part of the cost can be deducted in the first year through Section 179 or bonus depreciation, depending on current rules and your income. Review it before you sign. Lucid models the effect on your tax bill and cash, and your CPA confirms the rules.

How much does dental bookkeeping cost?

Published starting prices on this list run from $130 a month for Lucid to $800 a month per location for AccruDent, and some firms quote privately. Lucid shows its number on a calculator. See Lucid pricing.

Do I need a dental specific bookkeeper, or will a general online service work?

A general service keeps simple books but will not reconcile production to collections or report PPO write offs unless you set that up. Lucid has a dental page and a CPA on every account. Compare Lucid against a regular accounting firm.

Can my practice software replace a bookkeeper?

No. Dentrix and Open Dental record production, collections and deposits, but neither closes your books nor signs a tax return. Lucid works beside your software. For claims and billing tools, see the RCM software guide.
