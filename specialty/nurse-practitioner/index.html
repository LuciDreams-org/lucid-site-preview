The real problems

Money problems we see in nurse practitioner practices

Paying a new hire before they can bill

A second nurse practitioner restarts credentialing, which can take months before the first billable payer, while salary, benefits and malpractice are already due.

How Lucid handles it: Lucid adds a ramp cost for each planned hire to your cash forecast and CFO scenarios. Credentialing and billing stay with your partners.

Paid less per visit, plus collaboration fees

Medicare pays nurse practitioners billing under their own number 85 percent of the standard rate, and some payers follow. State collaboration fees can add more cost.

How Lucid handles it: Lucid reports collections per visit by payer and by provider, books each collaboration fee on its own line, and shows margin per provider before you add the next.

From employee to owner with staff

A 1099 label does not decide worker status, and an S corp saves less than many owners expect when your clinical work sets a high salary.

How Lucid handles it: Your Lucid CPA runs your actual profit through the election first, compares pay models, sets quarterly estimates and flags classification questions for your employment attorney.

Questions, answered

Nurse practitioner practice questions owners ask

How much cash does my practice need before a new nurse practitioner starts?

Plan for salary, benefits, equipment and software for the months before the new provider's payers start paying. Lucid's cash flow forecast and CFO scenario planning show how much runway you need and when the hire should break even.

How do I cover payroll while a new nurse practitioner waits to get credentialed?

Treat the wait as a real cost of the hire: salary and benefits are paid for months before that provider's visits can be billed to some plans. Lucid adds that ramp period to your cash forecast, so you know how much cushion you need before you hire.

Why does my nurse practitioner clinic get paid less per visit for the same service?

Medicare pays nurse practitioners 85 percent of the standard rate for the same service, and some commercial plans follow. Lucid tracks revenue and collections by payer, so you see exactly what each plan pays per visit and where your margin is thinnest.

Can I pay my nurse practitioner as a 1099 contractor?

Sometimes, but the label does not decide it. If the practice sets the schedule, supplies the tools and directs how work is done, the clinician may be an employee under IRS or state tests. Lucid's classification review flags risk so you can confirm with an employment attorney.

Should my nurse practitioner practice be an S corp, and how much salary should I pay myself?

It depends on your profit. An S corp can lower self employment tax, but you must pay yourself a reasonable salary for your clinical work, and the savings are smaller than many people expect at lower profit. Lucid reviews your actual numbers and tells you whether the election pays off.
