Three money problems we see in med spas

1. Package, membership and gift card cash that looks like profit

Med spas sell prepaid laser series, injectable packages, memberships and gift cards, so cash arrives before the treatment happens. If the point of sale system books the sale as revenue the day it is paid, profit looks better than it is and the unearned balance gets spent as if it were free. Lucid sets up a prepaid and membership liability account, reconciles point of sale sales to your deposits each month with a rollforward, and reports earned revenue next to cash collected. Your CPA keeps tax timing separate.

2. Laser and device purchases that are hard to judge

One energy device can cost a six figure amount. The real question is how many paid treatments each month cover the payment, the consumables and the service plan. Before you buy, Lucid builds a payback model from your own quote, financing terms and provider pay. After you buy, Lucid reports profit by service, so you see what the device earns against its payment, and your CPA plans depreciation or the Section 179 election around the year's expected income. Lucid does not arrange financing.

3. Injectables, product cost and provider profit across locations

Neurotoxin and filler are expensive, perishable and tracked by the unit. Many med spas expense everything on purchase, so one big order spikes cost in a month and hides the margin on each treatment. Lucid reports product cost by service line. Your CPA also reviews whether injectors are paid as W-2 or 1099 workers, since that changes provider profit. Each location gets its own books on one chart of accounts, plus a combined view, so you see profit by location, provider, room and service.

Questions, answered

Med spa accounting questions owners ask

What is the best bookkeeping service for a med spa with multiple locations?

Look for a team that gives each location its own books, a combined view, membership and package revenue recognition, injectable cost by service and a dedicated CPA. Lucid gives you all of that, publishes its pricing and works month to month.

Do I count med spa package and membership sales as revenue when the client pays?

Usually not in your books. Money paid for treatments you have not delivered yet is a liability until the client uses them. Lucid tracks that balance each month so your profit reflects what you earned, and your CPA handles how it is treated for taxes separately.

Should I buy or lease a laser for my med spa, and can I write it off?

It depends on how many paid treatments you can do each month, the consumable cost per treatment and your tax situation. A purchased device may qualify for a first year deduction, but a deduction does not make a device pay for itself. Lucid builds a payback and tax plan from your quote before you sign.

How do I track Botox and filler cost in my med spa books?

Injectables are normally booked as an asset on the balance sheet and moved to cost of goods as they are used. That way one large order does not distort profit and you can see your margin on each treatment. Lucid reports product cost by service line.

How do I keep the books for my med spa management company and the medical practice?

Each entity needs its own books, with the management fee recorded the same way on both sides. Mixing the two makes reports unreliable. Lucid keeps them separate and gives you a combined view. Your healthcare attorney confirms the structure.
