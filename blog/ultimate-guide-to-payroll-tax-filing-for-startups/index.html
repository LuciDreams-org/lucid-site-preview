Payroll Tax Filing for Medical Practices: An Owner's Guide

November 3, 2026 · 10 min read

A payroll provider may calculate taxes, submit returns and initiate payments, but the practice still needs someone to verify that the work is complete. Paying provider and administrative staff is not the same as confirming that every applicable tax account, deposit and return is correct.

For a staffed private medical practice, the owner's job is to establish a reliable control process: identify the employing entity, confirm the relevant work locations, assign responsibilities, reconcile payroll to the books and review evidence of filing and payment.

This guide explains how to build that process. It is not an execution-ready tax calendar. Your CPA or qualified payroll tax adviser must confirm applicable obligations and current deadlines for each entity and jurisdiction before the calendar is used.

Separate Payroll, Tax Deposits and Tax Returns

These activities are connected, but completing one does not prove the others are finished.

Activity What it accomplishes Evidence to review
Payroll processing Calculates gross pay, deductions, withholding and net pay Approved payroll register and funding report
Tax deposit or payment Sends funds toward a tax obligation Payment confirmation, bank settlement and agency account information where available
Tax return filing Reports wages, withholding and employer tax information Copy of the return and acceptance acknowledgment or other applicable filing evidence
Year-end wage reporting Reports employee wages and withholding to employees and the relevant authorities Reconciled wage statements, submission evidence and employee delivery status

A return marked “filed” does not establish that its associated payments cleared. A withdrawal by a payroll provider does not, by itself, establish that the correct agency received the funds under the correct employer account.

Federal reporting may include Forms 941, 940 and W-2, depending on the practice's circumstances. Ask your adviser to identify the applicable forms, filing methods and deposit requirements using current IRS employment tax guidance. Do not assume a return's reporting cycle is also its deposit schedule.

Contractor information reporting belongs in a separate workflow. Whether a clinician or administrative worker should be treated as an employee is a classification question for qualified tax and legal advisers, not a decision based solely on a contract label.

Establish the Employer and Work-Location Records

Start with an employer account register rather than a list of office addresses. One entity can operate several locations, while separate entities can share an office or administrative team.

For each employing entity, record:

  • Legal name, EIN, business address and authorized contacts.
  • Payroll provider account and business funding account.
  • Applicable federal, state and local employer account identifiers.
  • Registration status, effective dates and unresolved account issues.
  • Who can access agency portals and receive official correspondence.
  • Which deposits, returns and year-end services the provider has agreed to perform.

Have the adviser confirm required registrations before payroll begins in a new jurisdiction. The SBA overview of federal and state tax identification numbers provides setup context, but it does not replace jurisdiction-specific review.

Track work locations separately from financial reporting locations

Maintain current employee residence and actual work-location information through the practice's approved payroll process. A remote administrator's work location may require a different review from that of an employee working at the practice's office. Ask the adviser to evaluate withholding, unemployment and local requirements rather than assuming the same treatment applies everywhere.

For management reporting, employees may also have department or location codes. Those codes allocate expense; they do not determine the legal employer or settle jurisdictional questions.

Illustrative example: A practice has two offices under one employing entity. Its administrator divides time between both offices. The bookkeeper may allocate that employee's expense between location P&Ls, while the payroll tax records remain tied to the employing entity and the adviser-approved work-location treatment.

For separate entities sharing staff costs, have the CPA document the employer arrangement and intercompany accounting. Moving an expense between entities in the ledger does not transfer the underlying payroll filing obligation.

Assign Responsibility Before the Next Filing Cycle

“Handled by payroll” is not a sufficient assignment. The service agreement should distinguish calculation, funding, deposits, returns, year-end reporting, amendments and notice support.

Use this responsibility matrix as a starting point, then replace the role descriptions with named people and backups.

Control Prepares or performs Reviews or verifies Approves or escalates
Employer registrations and jurisdiction assessment Manager gathers facts; adviser evaluates requirements CPA or qualified payroll tax adviser Owner approves setup and service scope
Employee and work-location changes Practice manager Payroll specialist; adviser for jurisdiction questions Designated practice approver
Payroll run and funding Payroll provider using approved inputs Manager reviews register; finance checks cash Authorized payroll approver
Deposit and filing calendar Payroll provider or adviser Qualified adviser verifies current authority Owner or finance lead approves operational use
Payroll-to-ledger reconciliation Bookkeeper CPA or finance lead Owner receives unresolved exceptions
Filing and payment evidence Payroll provider supplies evidence Finance lead verifies completeness Owner escalates missing or rejected items
Tax notices and corrections Manager logs notice; provider investigates Adviser determines response Authorized owner approves response and payment
Year-end reconciliation Provider and bookkeeper CPA or qualified adviser Designated approver authorizes release

Where staffing limits separation of duties, use a compensating review. For example, the owner can review payroll changes, funding totals and unresolved exceptions even if the manager both prepares inputs and coordinates with the provider.

Build a Filing-Control Calendar, Not a Generic Deadline List

Create a separate calendar row for each entity, jurisdiction, tax obligation and reporting period. Keep deposits separate from returns, including when the same provider handles both.

The worksheet below is illustrative. Its milestones are internal review triggers, not statutory deadlines. Every deadline field requires current verification before use.

Control item Internal planning milestone Statutory deadline field Evidence needed to close
Applicable federal tax deposit Before the provider's funding cutoff Deadline requires verification from current authority Payment confirmation, settlement and correct account/period
Applicable federal employment tax return After period reconciliation and before submission authorization Deadline requires verification from current authority Approved return copy and filing acceptance evidence
State withholding deposit or payment Before the applicable funding cutoff Deadline requires verification from current authority Payment evidence matched to state account
State withholding return After state wage and withholding reconciliation Deadline requires verification from current authority Return copy and acceptance evidence
State unemployment report and payment After jurisdiction-level wage reconciliation Deadline requires verification from current authority Report, filing evidence and payment verification
Applicable local payroll obligation After adviser confirms applicability and before authorization Deadline requires verification from current authority Local return and/or payment evidence
Year-end employee wage reporting After annual reconciliation and before release Deadline requires verification from current authority Approved statements, submission and delivery evidence

Verify and approve dates before activating reminders

For every applicable row, add the entity, agency account, reporting period, responsible person, backup, verified deadline and internal cutoff. Also record the official source URL, date checked, verifier and approval status.

Use current IRS guidance for federal obligations and the relevant official state or local authority for jurisdiction-specific requirements. A provider's calendar is useful operational input, but the qualified adviser should verify applicability and deadlines against current authority, including relevant agency notices.

The owner or designated finance lead then approves the verified calendar for operational use. Until that review is complete, keep the row marked Deadline requires verification from current authority; do not treat it as ready for execution.

Recheck affected rows when an employee changes work location, an entity is added, an agency issues a notice or the provider changes an account setting. Set internal review milestones early enough to resolve exceptions before the verified external deadline and provider funding cutoff.

Reconcile Payroll Before Approving Returns

A payroll report can be internally consistent while still being posted incorrectly to the practice's books. Reconciliation connects employee pay, tax liabilities, cash movements and reported amounts.

The IRS business recordkeeping guidance supports complete business records, separate business accounts and bank reconciliation. For payroll oversight, turn those principles into a repeatable close process.

Review each payroll run

Before authorization, compare the payroll register with approved inputs. Review new hires, terminations, provider compensation changes, administrative hours, bonuses, deductions and work-location changes. Investigate unexpected differences from the prior run.

Check total funding needs against available cash. Payer receipts and patient collections may arrive unevenly, so the practice's cash forecast should distinguish net pay, employer taxes, withheld amounts, benefits and provider fees. Do not treat unpaid payroll tax liabilities as available operating cash.

Reconcile the ledger and bank activity

At close, the bookkeeper should connect:

  • Gross wages and employer tax expense to payroll reports.
  • Employee withholding and other deductions to liability accounts.
  • Net pay, tax payments and provider fees to bank activity.
  • Payroll clearing balances to identifiable transactions still in transit.
  • Location allocations and shared administrative costs to the approved allocation method.

Reconcile by employing entity before consolidating results. A balanced consolidated total can conceal a payment or posting assigned to the wrong entity.

Tie reporting periods to returns

Before a return is authorized, compare its wages, withholding and employer tax figures with payroll reports and reconciled ledger balances for the same period. Document legitimate differences rather than forcing balances to match through unexplained entries.

Illustrative example: A provider withdraws combined payroll funding, but the bank feed categorizes the entire withdrawal as wage expense. The bookkeeper separates net pay, tax liabilities, employer tax expense and fees using the payroll reports. The reviewer then checks that subsequent tax payments clear the appropriate liabilities rather than recording the same expense twice.

Close Filings and Payments With Evidence

Use separate statuses for “prepared,” “submitted,” “accepted” and “payment verified.” A provider dashboard showing submission is not the same as confirmation that a return was accepted.

For each calendar item, review:

  1. Identity: Does the evidence show the correct entity, account and reporting period?
  2. Amount: Does it agree with the approved return or deposit calculation?
  3. Filing status: Was the submission accepted, rejected or left pending?
  4. Payment status: Did the payment settle, and is agency confirmation available where applicable?
  5. Exceptions: Are unexplained balances, rejected payments or missing acknowledgments assigned to someone?

Keep supporting evidence in the practice's controlled financial records, linked to the calendar item. This is a filing-completion workflow, not a record-retention schedule; have the CPA separately establish applicable retention requirements.

Escalate notices through one owner

Route agency correspondence to a named practice contact even when the provider receives copies. Log the entity, agency, tax period, issue, response deadline shown on the notice and assigned adviser.

Send the complete notice promptly through the approved document-sharing process. Ask the provider for relevant returns, payment confirmations and account details. The adviser should determine whether the matter calls for an account correction, amended filing, payment, agency inquiry or another response.

Do not assume the notice is resolved because it was forwarded. Close it only after the designated reviewer has evidence of resolution or a documented next action and follow-up date. Confirm with the adviser before making an additional payment that might duplicate an earlier remittance.

Reconcile Year-End Reporting Before Release

Before wage statements are finalized, reconcile annual payroll totals to period returns, corrections and the ledger. Review employee information through the approved payroll process, confirm adviser-approved treatment of relevant compensation and benefits, and identify unresolved adjustments.

Track agency submission and employee delivery separately. If a correction is needed after release, assign responsibility for the corrected reporting and any related return or ledger changes.

When changing payroll providers, confirm who handles remaining deposits, open returns, year-end statements and amendments. Obtain records from the outgoing provider before access ends, and reconcile transferred year-to-date balances before relying on the new system.

Make the Owner's Review Exception-Focused

The owner does not need to inspect every payroll calculation. A useful review shows whether registrations are complete, payroll is reconciled, calendar dates are verified, filings are accepted, payments are confirmed and notices have accountable owners.

FAQs

If a payroll provider files for the practice, what should the owner still review?

Confirm the agreed service scope, correct employer accounts, verified calendar, payroll reconciliation and evidence of accepted filings and completed payments. Review unresolved exceptions and notices rather than relying only on a dashboard's overall status.

Should every practice location have its own payroll tax calendar?

Organize the calendar primarily by employing entity and applicable jurisdiction, then include work locations where relevant. Multiple offices under one entity do not automatically mean separate employer accounts, and separate entities should not be combined simply because they share an office. Have the adviser confirm the structure.

What happens when an administrator starts working remotely from another state?

Collect the proposed work location and effective date before the change begins. Ask a qualified adviser to evaluate registration, withholding, unemployment and local obligations. Update the payroll setup and verified calendar based on that review rather than assuming the employee's existing treatment continues unchanged.

Can this worksheet be used immediately as a filing calendar?

No. It is an illustrative planning framework. The qualified adviser must verify applicable obligations and current deadlines from official authorities, and the owner or designated finance lead must approve the completed calendar before operational use.
