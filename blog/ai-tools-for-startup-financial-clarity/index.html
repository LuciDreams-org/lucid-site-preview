AI Tools for Explaining Medical Practice Financial Results

November 3, 2026 · 9 min read

A prepared financial package can still leave a practice owner with unanswered questions. Why did profit increase while cash fell? Why does one location show higher administrative payroll? What does a growing balance in an intercompany account mean?

AI-assisted explanation tools can help turn those questions into a more focused financial review. Their role is to explain terminology, compare approved statements and organize questions—not to establish that the books are correct or invent reasons for unexpected results.

For owners and managers of staffed US private medical practices, the useful starting point is an already prepared package: the profit and loss statement, balance sheet, cash flow statement where prepared, and relevant supporting schedules. Treat every generated interpretation as provisional until its figures and reasoning have been checked.

Choose Explanation Functions, Not a Broad AI Promise

Start with the task you need help completing. A tool that writes fluent commentary is not necessarily able to read a financial table accurately, distinguish entities or support its conclusions with references.

Function Useful practice-owner request Boundary to maintain
Terminology explanation “Explain why loan principal payments differ from interest expense.” Explain the concept without claiming how a specific transaction was recorded unless the records establish it.
Questions about unexpected balances “What should I ask our CPA about this growing clearing-account balance?” Produce questions and evidence requests, not an unsupported diagnosis.
Comparison of approved statements “Compare administrative payroll across these two approved monthly P&Ls.” Use matching periods, account definitions and reporting scope.
Draft management commentary “Draft a paragraph explaining the verified changes for our owner meeting.” Separate supported observations from unresolved causes.

Other financial tools address different jobs:

  • Forecasting software models future receipts, expenses and cash under stated assumptions. Explaining last month’s results does not establish next month’s outlook.
  • Bookkeeping automation helps process transactions and prepare records. An explanation tool should not silently recategorize transactions or change the ledger.
  • Executive-summary generation condenses a package. Explanation goes further by connecting a specific owner question to source evidence and identifying what remains unknown.
  • Workflow-bottleneck analysis examines process delays and handoffs. A change in payroll expense alone does not establish a staffing or scheduling bottleneck.

Select the function that addresses the immediate question. Do not buy a forecasting capability merely because a monthly statement is hard to understand.

Prepare a Small, Reliable Source Package

An AI explanation cannot repair incomplete bookkeeping through better wording. Before using a tool, ask the preparer which package is approved for owner review and whether any balances remain provisional.

The IRS guidance on business recordkeeping supports complete records, separate business accounts and reconciliation of bank records. Those practices also provide a sound foundation for financial explanations; an articulate answer is not a substitute for them.

Identify the reporting boundaries

Include a short cover note stating:

  • The reporting entity and any locations included.
  • The reporting period and comparison period.
  • The accounting basis used in the package.
  • Whether the statements are final for review or subject to adjustments.
  • Which supporting schedules are included and which are unavailable.
  • Any known changes to account classifications or shared-cost allocations.

For a multi-entity practice, do not mix an entity-level balance sheet with a combined P&L without explaining their different scopes. Likewise, a location P&L may reflect allocated costs rather than costs paid directly by that location.

Supply only the evidence needed

A payroll question may need provider and administrative payroll totals, not employee-level wage records. A collections question may need aggregate payer receipts and patient-balance summaries, not patient names or encounter details.

Use only information the practice has authorized for the selected environment. Remove unnecessary identifiers, bank account details and employee information. Review spreadsheets for hidden tabs, comments and embedded details before sharing them.

Do not place patient-identifiable information into a general-purpose AI tool or ordinary Slack conversation. The HHS minimum necessary guidance describes a standard with exceptions; it is not a blanket rule that every financial upload is permitted once names are removed.

If a proposed workflow involves electronic protected health information, obtain appropriate privacy and legal review before proceeding. HHS cloud guidance explains that a cloud provider maintaining ePHI can have business-associate responsibilities even when it cannot decrypt the information. Encryption alone does not settle the requirements.

Test Tools Against Your Actual Questions

Avoid product rankings based on generic demonstrations. Instead, test a candidate tool using an authorized, minimized package and questions whose answers your finance team can verify.

Look for whether the tool can:

  • Read columns, negative amounts, subtotals and reporting dates correctly.
  • Identify the document, page or tab and account line supporting an answer.
  • Keep entity and location results separate.
  • State when a supporting schedule is missing.
  • Distinguish an arithmetic change from an explanation of its cause.
  • Follow instructions not to infer missing facts.

Also review the vendor’s terms, data-use practices, access options and suitability through the practice’s approval process. Financial reasoning quality and permission to handle the information are separate checks; passing one does not establish the other.

A useful test question is: “What explains this balance?” If the supplied statements cannot answer it, a reliable response should identify the gap rather than fill it with a plausible story.

Use an Owner Question Log

A question log keeps the review anchored to decisions rather than an open-ended chat. Assign each unresolved question to someone who can obtain the evidence or confirm the accounting treatment.

The following is an illustrative owner question log, not a client case study.

Owner question Approved source to reference What AI may help explain Evidence or reviewer needed
Why did profit rise while cash fell? P&L, comparative balance sheet and cash flow statement if prepared Differences among earnings, receivables, debt payments and distributions CPA or finance team to verify the cash bridge and classifications
Why did provider payroll increase? P&L and aggregate payroll summary Amount and timing of the reported change Payroll lead to confirm compensation timing; CPA to assess accrual treatment
Why is one location less profitable? Comparable location P&Ls and allocation schedule Direct-cost differences versus allocated shared costs Finance team to confirm the allocation method and consistency
What does this intercompany balance mean? Entity balance sheets and intercompany schedule General purpose of intercompany accounts CPA to reconcile counterpart balances and determine any corrections
Do higher patient receivables mean collections worsened? Aggregate receivables aging and collection summaries Changes in aging categories and limits of the evidence Billing lead and finance team to investigate causes without supplying patient-identifiable detail to general-purpose AI

Do not close a question merely because an answer sounds reasonable. Close it when a source supports the conclusion, the responsible reviewer confirms it and the owner understands any remaining limitation.

Ask for Observations Before Causes

A disciplined prompt makes unsupported reasoning easier to spot:

Use only the approved financial documents supplied. Identify the entity, period and accounting basis. Answer the owner’s question in plain English. Separate verified observations, calculations, possible explanations and missing evidence. Reference the document and page or tab plus account line for each financial claim. Do not invent operational causes, recommend journal entries or infer tax treatment. If evidence is insufficient, state what the CPA or finance team must confirm.

Illustrative example: profit increased, but cash declined

Suppose an approved accrual-basis package reports net income of $90,000 while cash declines by $20,000. The comparative balance sheet shows receivables increasing by $70,000. Supporting schedules show $25,000 of loan principal payments and $15,000 of owner distributions, with no other cash-reconciling changes.

These are illustrative figures, not practice benchmarks or actual results. The simplified bridge is:

Item Effect on cash in this illustrative example
Net income $90,000
Increase in receivables −$70,000
Loan principal payments −$25,000
Owner distributions −$15,000
Net change in cash −$20,000

A useful explanation says that income and cash differ because the identified items reconcile the two measures in this example. It does not say that payer delays caused the receivables increase unless supporting evidence establishes that cause.

The next question is therefore specific: “What explains the receivables increase, and which aggregate schedules support that explanation?” Other practices may have additional reconciling items; this simplified bridge is not a template to force onto every package.

Apply a Response-Review Rubric

Use a review gate before generated commentary enters an owner meeting package. The rubric below is a practical control, not a certification standard.

Review dimension Acceptable response Return or escalate when…
Source references Financial claims point to identifiable statements, tabs or schedules A reference is missing, invented or does not support the claim
Scope and comparability Entity, location, dates and accounting basis are explicit The response mixes scopes or compares unlike periods without qualification
Arithmetic Amounts, signs, differences and percentage calculations reconcile A total fails to tie or a percentage uses the wrong denominator
Assumptions Assumptions are labeled and separated from established facts A possible cause is presented as a confirmed explanation
Missing evidence The response names the missing schedule or confirmation needed It fills gaps with confident language
Action boundary Proposed next steps request evidence or professional review It recommends unsupported accounting changes, tax treatment or major operating decisions

The practice manager can check document references and arithmetic. The owner can assess whether the explanation answers the business question. Escalate unresolved balances, accounting judgments, conflicting schedules and missing evidence to the CPA or responsible finance professional.

A revised answer should be checked again; correction does not automatically make it reliable. Keep the approved commentary with the source package and question log so the discussion remains tied to the version reviewed.

Turn Reviewed Answers Into Management Commentary

Draft commentary only after the evidence review. A useful paragraph contains:

  1. The verified change: what moved and over which period.
  2. The supported explanation: what the available records establish.
  3. The limitation: what remains unconfirmed.
  4. The next step: who will investigate or decide.

For example, location commentary can distinguish a rise in direct administrative payroll from a change in allocated central-office costs. That distinction matters before an owner considers staffing changes. A location can appear less profitable because an allocation changed, even when its direct spending did not.

Keep recommendations proportional to the evidence. “Confirm the shared-cost allocation before comparing locations” may be justified. “Reduce staff at the lower-margin location” requires substantially more operational and financial analysis.

FAQs

Can AI explain a medical practice’s financial statements without changing the books?

Yes, explanation can be a separate task. Use an approved statement package and keep the task limited to reading, comparing and drafting questions. Any proposed accounting correction should go through the finance team’s normal review rather than being applied from an AI response.

Can AI determine why payer receipts or patient balances changed?

It can compare authorized aggregate reports and identify questions to investigate. Statements alone may not establish the cause. Timing, adjustments and other factors require supporting evidence and review by the relevant billing or finance lead. Do not upload patient-identifiable details to general-purpose AI to obtain an explanation.

When should an owner involve the CPA?

Involve the CPA when source documents conflict, balances do not reconcile, accounting treatment is uncertain or an explanation depends on evidence that is missing. Also seek professional review before relying on generated commentary for tax conclusions, accounting changes or significant financial decisions.
