Top 5 AI Tools for Funding Scenario Simulations

October 25, 2026 · 10 min read

I’d choose a funding simulation tool by testing two things first: when cash runs out and how financing changes ownership. Compare $2 million now with $3 million six months later, then check the cash gap and dilution - not just the funding total.

Here’s how I’d sort these five tools for evaluation:

  • Quantari: Short-term cash planning, with documented 30-, 60-, and 90-day projections.
  • Runway: Hiring and spending scenarios that affect runway and funding timing.
  • Causal: Linked formulas for testing revenue, costs, and cash assumptions.
  • Carta: Financing scenarios focused on ownership and dilution.
  • Model Reef: Side-by-side funding and cash-flow scenarios, with AI functions still to verify.

What Funding Simulations Should Show

Cash Needs, Ownership, and Exit Proceeds

An operating forecast shows when cash runs out and how much funding the business needs. An equity model shows how that funding dilutes ownership. Base the forecast on collections and payments - not just revenue and expenses.

The closing date connects the forecast to the cap table: cash enters the forecast when the financing appears in the cap table. Investor rights also affect exit proceeds. Before relying on any tool below, check that its funding simulation provides these minimum outputs.

Example: Funding Amount, Timing, and Valuation

Start with a simple scenario: change one assumption at a time. Then combine downside cases to see how cash shortfalls and ownership change together.

Compare how each tool shows the effect on both runway and dilution. Track the cash gap and the date funding is next needed, not just ending cash. Once you’ve built the scenario, check every output against its underlying assumptions.

Outputs to Check

Check monthly burn, cash balance, cash-out date, funding gap, fully diluted ownership, option-pool dilution, SAFE conversions, and liquidation-preference payouts.

Trace each output back to its inputs, including collection timing, payroll timing, closing date, share counts, and financing terms. Use the cash-out date to plan funding before cash reaches zero.

Separate built-in calculations from custom work. A tool that forecasts cash may not handle SAFE conversions or other custom financing logic. Those calculations may need custom formulas, a spreadsheet, or a separate workflow.

Have a finance professional review the model. Treat AI outputs as inputs - not decisions.

1. Quantari

Quantari is an AI CFO tool built for cash planning. Its strength is operating cash forecasts - not dilution or investor-rights modeling. It can help founders plan the cash side of fundraising, but ownership math needs a separate tool.

Financing Terms

Quantari does not document direct cap-table or investor-preference modeling. Calculate equity, SAFE, and convertible-note terms separately, keeping cash timing apart from cap-table math.

To compare immediate funding with delayed funding, track how each assumption changes cash balance, runway, and burn.

Operating Forecasts

Quantari documents 30-, 60-, and 90-day cash-flow projections. Once funding timing is clear, use those forecasts to test how hiring, pricing, and spending changes affect cash and runway.

AI and Integrations

Quantari’s conversational interface lets users ask questions in plain English and receive guidance. Public listings show connections to QuickBooks Online and bank data. Before using that data for forecasts, check that it’s complete and correctly categorized.

Area What to check
Onboarding Connect supported accounting and financial accounts, then check categorization and historical data completeness before forecasting.
Reporting Check 30-, 60-, and 90-day projections, cash balance, runway, and accounts receivable/accounts payable (AR/AP) visibility.

Pricing and Stage Fit

Pricing differs across sources, so verify current rates before subscribing. Quantari suits founders who need short-term cash visibility; venture financing terms still need separate modeling.

2. Runway

Runway is a financial planning platform that tracks burn, hiring, and expenses. It helps you test how operating changes affect funding timing, rather than calculate cap-table math.

Setup

Compare hiring and expense assumptions across base and delayed-closing scenarios. This helps you see whether you need to secure funding earlier or seek a larger round.

Cost

The supplied sources don’t establish Runway’s pricing or access to scenarios. Confirm both during your evaluation.

Reports

Check how hiring changes, spending changes, and funding delays affect the zero-cash date. Use those outputs to determine whether the issue is operating spend, funding timing, or round size.

Integration Depth

If the outputs look right, check the data connectors behind them. Confirm which integrations are available and how often the data updates during a demo.

3. Causal

Use Causal when your funding model relies on linked formulas and you need to test what-if changes fast.

Setup

Causal turns plain-English assumptions into linked formulas for scenario models. In a live demo, check that revenue, hiring, expenses, and cash flow stay connected as assumptions change.

Cost

Pricing is unverified in the source material. Request a current quote and confirm which features come with each tier.

Reports

During the demo, test whether changing one assumption updates the full funding model.

Area What to verify
Model flexibility Revenue, hiring, expenses, and cash flow stay linked across funding scenarios.
Usability Founders can trace and edit AI-generated formulas that affect cash needs.
Collaboration Teams can share models and control edits.
Investor outputs Outputs include assumptions, funding needs, cash balances, dilution, and exit payouts - and update when inputs change.

4. Carta

Use Carta for equity-specific funding questions: how a round changes ownership, dilution, and investor holdings.

Setup

Carta models a financing round’s effect on share price, dilution, and ownership. Check which equity records the model requires and whether document processing can pull data from your legal documents.

Cost

Reports

Carta lets you compare ownership, valuation, share price, dilution, and investor holdings before and after a financing round.

Prepare Your Financial Inputs

Before running a funding scenario, start with reconciled books and clean, standardized data. Use consistent dates, account categories, and reporting periods so you can compare cash burn, runway, and dilution across scenarios.

Document your assumptions for revenue growth, CAC, churn, headcount costs, and runway. Test base, upside, and downside cases using the same baseline data. Link revenue and spending: a higher-growth case should include the hiring and customer acquisition costs needed to support that growth.

Include financing terms, option-pool treatment, and investor rights in your model inputs. Have finance and legal advisers review those inputs - and how they affect the results - before using them in negotiations or securities issuance materials.

FAQs

How far ahead should I model my funding needs?

Model your funding needs over 18 to 36 months. This gives you a long-term plan while keeping enough detail to track short-term cash flow.

Keep three scenarios: a base case for your standard growth plan, a downside case to identify survival triggers, and an upside case for aggressive scaling. Planning for all three helps you prepare for fundraising delays. Many startups misjudge their liquidity and face longer funding timelines than expected.

How can I validate AI-generated funding forecasts?

Start with clean, centralized, standardized data. Before modeling, reconcile accounting, payroll, and banking records to remove duplicates and resolve inconsistencies. Keep formats consistent: MM/DD/YYYY for dates and $1,000.00 for currency.

Review flagged transactions regularly and update business rules to maintain accuracy. Have experts compare AI forecasts with actual financial performance so projections stay grounded in accurate, verifiable data.

How much cash buffer should I plan for funding delays?

Use AI-driven scenario modeling to stress-test your cash position under a downside (P10) scenario. Pinpoint when cash would run out and how much extra capital - say, $500,000 - you’d need if milestones shift.

Investors often expect startups to start their next funding round after spending just 50% of their current capital. Use the downside model to set your fundraising timeline, not just plan for a cash shortfall.
