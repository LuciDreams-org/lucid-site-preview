AI-Assisted Payroll Tax Reviews for Medical Practices

November 4, 2026 · 10 min read

Payroll tax review is not finished when a medical practice approves payroll or sees a withdrawal from its bank account. The owner still needs a reliable connection between approved pay, the payroll register, tax liabilities, cash movements and filing confirmations.

AI-assisted exception review can support that process by comparing structured records and drafting a list of differences for investigation. It should not replace the payroll provider’s calculations, the CPA’s tax judgment or the practice’s approval controls. A generated explanation is a lead to investigate—not evidence that a tax obligation has been satisfied.

For a staffed private practice, the practical objective is straightforward: identify unexplained differences, assign each one to a responsible person and document the resolution. This article describes that review workflow. It is not a filing manual, a records-retention schedule or a promise of automated compliance.

Define the review before introducing AI

Start with the records and responsibilities, not the software. A practice administrator may manage time approvals and employee changes, while the payroll provider processes payroll and handles agreed tax services. The bookkeeper records payroll activity, the CPA evaluates tax questions, and the owner approves funding and unresolved material decisions.

Confirm these responsibilities against your actual service agreements. Do not assume that a provider handles every registration, payment or filing merely because it processes payroll.

Set up each review around the correct employing entity, payroll period and pay date. A practice with several locations under one employer needs location-level cost reporting without accidentally treating each location as a separate employer. A multi-entity practice needs separate employer-level reconciliations, even when administrators or providers work across locations.

Review layer Main question Primary evidence
Approved inputs Did payroll use authorized compensation and employee changes? Approved time, compensation changes and payroll change log
Payroll register Do pay, deductions and taxable wages have support? Draft and final registers, tax detail and deduction reports
Tax liabilities Do amounts owed reconcile to payroll activity? Liability reports and general ledger detail
Funding and payments Did cash movements match their intended purpose? Funding notices, bank activity and payment confirmations
Filings Is there evidence of the required submission and its status? Return copies, submission receipts and acceptance or rejection notices

An AI tool may help organize these comparisons if it can reliably read the selected records. Test that ability rather than assuming it exists. Straightforward arithmetic and duplicate checks may be better handled with spreadsheet formulas; AI can help summarize the resulting exceptions.

Step 1: Review approved inputs before payroll runs

Create a change log for the upcoming payroll. Relevant changes might include a new medical assistant, an administrative employee’s revised schedule, an approved provider bonus or a staff member working in a new state.

For each change, identify the effective date, approving person and source document. Compare that log with the draft payroll rather than relying only on whether total payroll looks similar to the previous period.

Useful exception checks include:

  • A compensation change appears in the register but not in the approved change log.
  • An approved bonus is absent or appears twice.
  • A new employee has incomplete payroll setup information.
  • A work-location change has not been reviewed for payroll tax implications.
  • A deduction or benefit changed without supporting authorization.

The administrator should resolve missing approvals. The payroll provider and CPA should address tax treatment, jurisdiction questions or worker-classification concerns. An AI-generated classification or tax setting should never become the basis for a change without qualified review.

Use current authoritative requirements and qualified advice when a change affects tax treatment. If necessary support is missing, ask the provider what must be resolved before processing and who is authorized to make that decision.

Step 2: Compare the register and taxable wages

Review the draft register at both the summary and employee-exception level. Total gross pay alone is not enough: different tax wage bases can differ from gross pay and from one another.

Ask the payroll provider to explain unexpected differences using the relevant earnings, deduction, benefit and adjustment detail. Do not instruct AI to make taxable wages equal gross wages or to force different tax wage bases to match.

For a private practice, separate compensation groups can make comparisons more useful. Provider compensation may move because of an approved bonus, while administrative payroll changes because of staffing or hours. Comparing each group with its approved inputs helps distinguish an expected business change from a processing error.

Give the review tool a narrow task: compare the supplied reports, identify differences and name the source rows supporting each finding. Require it to mark missing evidence rather than infer a tax explanation. Check report totals and extracted values against the originals before relying on its summary.

Step 3: Reconcile tax liabilities to the ledger

After payroll is finalized, connect the provider’s liability detail to the accounting records. For each relevant liability account, use this reconciliation:

Opening liability + payroll-generated liabilities − payments applied ± supported adjustments = closing liability.

Keep employee withholding and employer payroll tax expense distinct. Employee withholding is not an additional employer expense. Also avoid treating all payroll-related bank withdrawals as tax payments: a withdrawal may cover net wages, taxes, fees or a combination described in the provider’s funding report.

Illustrative example: a payroll-to-ledger discrepancy

The following amounts are hypothetical and do not represent tax rates or filing requirements.

A single-entity practice has a $1,000 opening payroll tax liability. The final payroll reports show $4,800 in new liabilities, comprising $3,200 in employee withholding and $1,600 in employer taxes. Supported tax payments applied during the review period total $4,500.

Reconciliation item Supported amount Ledger amount
Opening liability $1,000 $1,000
New payroll tax liabilities $4,800 $4,200
Payments applied ($4,500) ($4,500)
Closing liability $1,300 $700

The $600 difference points to an incomplete payroll entry—not necessarily an unpaid tax. Further review finds that the posting omitted $600 of employer payroll tax expense and the corresponding liability.

The bookkeeper prepares a correcting entry supported by the final payroll reports, and the designated reviewer approves it. The team separately checks payment evidence. It does not send another $600 merely to make the liability balance look right.

This is where exception review is useful: it identifies the break between systems while leaving the accounting correction and payment decision to the responsible people.

Step 4: Match funding to bank activity and payment evidence

Before releasing payroll, compare the funding requirement with available cash in the correct entity’s bank account. Payer receipts may be delayed while provider and administrative payroll remain scheduled obligations. The owner needs visibility into that cash requirement before approval, not after a failed withdrawal.

After processing, match the provider’s funding notice to bank activity. Separate net pay, tax funding and service fees according to the supporting report.

A provider debit confirms that money left the practice’s account. By itself, it does not establish that every required tax payment reached the correct agency or was applied to the correct employer and period.

Request the available payment confirmation and investigate discrepancies with the payroll provider. Check identifying details within the authorized review environment, including employer, jurisdiction, period and amount. Avoid copying sensitive identifiers into a general-purpose AI tool.

For shared staff costs, distinguish cost allocation from tax payment. Allocating administrative payroll across location P&Ls does not, by itself, change the employing entity or establish that another entity paid the tax.

Step 5: Close the loop on filings and confirmations

Maintain a filing-status checklist based on requirements verified by the payroll provider and CPA using current authoritative guidance. Record the responsible filer, applicable entity and period, required action date, submission status and supporting evidence.

Keep these statuses separate:

  • Prepared but not submitted.
  • Submitted, with confirmation pending.
  • Accepted or otherwise confirmed through the applicable process.
  • Rejected or requiring follow-up.

A draft return is not proof of submission. A submission receipt may still require follow-up if a separate acceptance or rejection notice is expected. Likewise, filing evidence does not replace payment evidence.

When a confirmation is missing, the administrator should request it from the provider and assign a follow-up date. Escalate rejections, conflicting amounts, agency notices or uncertainty about an approaching obligation to the CPA promptly. Do not wait for the next monthly close to investigate a potentially time-sensitive issue.

A payroll tax exception review worksheet

Use one worksheet per employing entity and review period. Add the report version, preparer, reviewer, status and resolution date to each item. The table below provides the core review fields.

Exception Review question Resolution owner Evidence required to close
Unapproved input change Does the register agree with authorized pay and effective dates? Practice administrator; owner for compensation approval Approved change record and corrected or explained register
Mismatched taxable wages Which earnings, deductions or adjustments explain the difference? Payroll provider; CPA for tax interpretation Tax detail, relevant setup support and documented explanation or correction
Unexpected liability movement Does opening balance plus activity reconcile to closing balance? Bookkeeper; CPA for unresolved accounting or tax questions Liability rollforward, final payroll report and approved entry support
Funding mismatch Does the withdrawal match net pay, taxes and fees as reported? Administrator and payroll provider Funding notice, bank transaction and reconciliation
Missing payment confirmation Was the payment made and applied to the intended obligation? Payroll provider; CPA if unresolved Payment confirmation and relevant employer, period and amount detail
Missing or rejected filing confirmation What is the actual filing status and next required action? Payroll provider and CPA Return copy, status receipt and documented resolution
Wrong entity or location mapping Is the employer correct, and are costs allocated consistently? Bookkeeper and administrator; CPA for entity questions Employer mapping, allocation support and corrected reports if needed

An exception is not closed because AI supplied a plausible explanation. Close it only when a person has checked the evidence and documented the conclusion. Track unresolved items with an owner and next action; do not let them disappear inside a narrative summary.

Use AI within a controlled review process

Begin with a completed payroll cycle and compare the tool’s findings with a human-reviewed reconciliation. Include known exceptions and ordinary, explainable changes. Check for both missed differences and false alarms before using the process on a live cycle.

Limit the tool to approved data and tasks. Do not allow a generated recommendation to change tax settings, authorize payments or submit returns without the established human approval process.

Payroll review generally should not require patient-level information. Use summarized compensation and financial records where possible. HHS explains the minimum necessary standard and its exceptions; determine appropriate handling with your advisers rather than assuming that a tool is suitable for sensitive information. Do not put patient-identifiable data into ordinary Slack or a general-purpose AI tool.

Maintain the underlying records outside the AI summary. The IRS employment-tax recordkeeping guidance identifies supporting records such as wage details, deposit information and filed returns. Have your CPA establish applicable retention requirements; this review workflow is not a destruction schedule. The IRS also discusses business records and bank reconciliation in Publication 583.

Make the owner’s review decision clear

At each payroll cycle, the owner or delegated approver should receive a short decision summary: funding required, significant changes, unresolved exceptions and actions needing approval. At month-end, review the liability reconciliation and outstanding confirmations by entity.

FAQs

Can AI determine whether our medical practice’s payroll taxes are correct?

Not on its own. It can help compare records and flag inconsistencies, but it may misread reports or suggest unsupported explanations. The payroll provider and CPA should verify tax treatment and current requirements. A clean exception report is not proof of compliance.

What should we review first if payroll and the ledger do not match?

Confirm that both reports cover the same employer, pay date and payroll version. Then compare gross pay, employee withholding, employer taxes, payments and adjustments separately. Determine whether the issue is a posting error, timing difference or payroll-processing problem before changing records or making another payment.

Does a payroll-provider withdrawal prove taxes were paid and returns filed?

No. Match the withdrawal to its funding report, then obtain the relevant payment and filing evidence. Funding, payment and filing are separate review steps, even when one provider handles all three.

How should a multi-location practice organize this review?

Reconcile payroll tax activity by employing entity first. Then use supported allocations for location-level staff costs. Keep entity obligations separate from management reporting, and ask the CPA to review uncertain employer or shared-cost arrangements.
