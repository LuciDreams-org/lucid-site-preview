Cross-Border Data Review for Medical Practice Finance Teams

November 4, 2026 · 9 min read

Hiring an overseas accountant or giving a vendor access to practice records creates a decision that goes beyond the monthly close: what information will that person see, from where, and under whose authority?

For a staffed private medical practice, financial records can include employee identifiers, provider compensation, bank details and patient information embedded in payment reports. A bookkeeper who needs to reconcile payer deposits may not need patient-level remittance detail. A contractor preparing location-level profit and loss statements may not need individual payroll records.

Before granting access, prepare a factual review packet for your legal, privacy, finance and IT advisers. The goal is not to decide which international transfer rule applies yourself. It is to give advisers enough detail to assess the proposed arrangement and identify conditions that must be met before work begins.

This guide covers that practice-side scoping workflow. It is not a cloud-vendor comparison, a technical data-localization guide or a CCPA-specific checklist, and it does not establish that any proposed access is lawful.

Why US hosting does not settle the question

Storage location and access location are different facts. Records may remain in a US-hosted accounting system while a contractor in another country views them, downloads a report or receives a screenshot through support.

That access deserves review even if no one intends to move the underlying database. The recipient may also use local downloads, email attachments, another contractor or a separate document-processing service. Those possibilities can change the scope of the arrangement.

For the review inventory, include overseas viewing, processing, sharing and storage. This is a practical scoping convention—not a legal conclusion that every activity has the same status under every law.

Ask separately:

  • Where are the primary records stored?
  • From which countries will people access them, including during travel?
  • Can users download, print, copy or forward them?
  • Who else can access the records through support, subcontracting or administration?
  • Where will working files and retained copies reside?

If an answer is unknown, record it as Pending vendor confirmation rather than assuming that US hosting resolves it.

Step 1: Define the finance task before requesting access

Start with the work product. “Bookkeeping access” is too broad to evaluate meaningfully.

A better request identifies the entity, task, required records and actions. For example: prepare the monthly bank reconciliation for the operating entity using bank statements and ledger transactions; submit proposed adjustments for the practice manager’s approval; do not initiate payments.

For a multi-location practice, distinguish location reporting from entity access. Two offices may belong to one legal entity, while a separate management entity pays shared administrative costs. A report organized by location does not necessarily justify access to every entity’s bank accounts or payroll files.

Finance task Starting information to evaluate Questions before expanding access
Reconcile payer receipts Bank deposits, ledger entries and summarized receipt reports Is patient-level detail needed to investigate a particular difference?
Prepare per-location P&Ls Revenue totals, expense coding and documented allocation methods Can location totals support the work without employee or patient identifiers?
Reconcile provider and administrative payroll Payroll summaries and payroll-related ledger balances Which exceptions require individual wage or deduction records?
Allocate shared costs Vendor invoices, entity ownership and allocation schedules Which entities’ records does the recipient actually need?
Assemble tax workpapers CPA-defined supporting schedules Which underlying documents are necessary, and who approves their release?

These are starting points for review, not universal access rules. If a summarized report cannot resolve an exception, define a separate approval path for the additional records.

Step 2: Classify the records, not just the application

One accounting folder can contain several types of sensitive information. Review the actual fields and attachments rather than labeling everything “financial data.”

Patient-related financial records

Payer remittances, patient balance reports, refund requests and deposit support may contain identifying information. The fact that a document supports accounting does not make its patient information irrelevant to privacy review.

HHS explains that accounting services can involve business-associate responsibilities when protected health information is involved. Have the appropriate adviser assess the relationship and required agreements; do not assume that an accounting engagement is exempt.

As a workflow preference, first consider whether summaries or reduced-field reports can support the task. HHS guidance on the minimum necessary standard describes requirements and exceptions. Your privacy adviser should determine how that standard applies to the proposed work.

Employee, provider and banking records

Payroll files may include tax identifiers, home addresses, compensation, deductions and deposit details. Provider compensation schedules may also contain confidential business terms.

Bank statements and tax workpapers can expose account identifiers, owner information or payment instructions. Separate permission to review transactions from authority to change bank details, add payees or release funds. An accountant’s need to reconcile an account does not by itself establish a need to move money.

Working files and communications

Include exports, screenshots, email attachments and shared spreadsheets. Ask whether the recipient proposes to use AI tools or other processing services and identify those services for review before use.

Do not put patient-identifiable information into ordinary Slack conversations or general-purpose AI tools. Use non-identifying task descriptions and an approved document-handling process for sensitive records.

Step 4: Send advisers a question packet they can answer

A useful packet contains the proposed engagement scope, inventory, representative field lists without sensitive values, relevant contracts and the vendor’s written answers. Avoid sending a live payroll file or patient ledger merely to explain the workflow.

Questions for legal and privacy advisers

Ask advisers to address the specific arrangement rather than requesting a general statement that a vendor is “compliant.”

Review area Questions to resolve
Parties and entities Which practice entity is engaging the recipient? Which entities’ records are included? Are all recipients and subcontractors identified?
Applicable obligations Which privacy, contractual or other requirements apply to these records, people, entities and access locations?
Agreements What confidentiality, data-handling or business-associate terms are needed? Do existing agreements cover the proposed work?
Access scope Which fields, systems and actions are necessary? Can a restricted report replace broader system access?
Onward sharing May the recipient use subcontractors, support personnel or additional processing tools? What notice or approval process is appropriate?
Retention and return Which records must be retained, by whom and for what purpose? What happens to working copies and backups when the engagement ends?
Incidents Who receives a suspected-incident report, who investigates, and who determines any notification obligations?
Changes What requires renewed review, such as another access country, new subcontractor or expanded dataset?

Country-specific conclusions and any required transfer arrangements belong with qualified advisers. Do not select a legal mechanism simply because it appears in a vendor template.

Questions for the CPA and finance lead

Confirm the reporting objective, source documents and exception process. Which records support the books? Who approves adjustments? Can sensitive detail remain with a designated US-based reviewer while the overseas recipient works from summaries?

IRS Publication 583 supports complete business records, separate business accounts and reconciliation. Reducing unnecessary access should not mean losing the evidence needed to support the accounts.

Ask the CPA to identify retention requirements by record category. A contractor’s working-copy policy is not the practice’s master retention schedule. Do not use engagement termination as an automatic destruction date for payroll, tax or other required records.

Questions for IT and the recipient

Ask IT to verify whether the proposed access limits are feasible. Identify available permissions, download restrictions, individual accounts and account-removal procedures without assuming the system supports every requested control.

Ask the recipient to confirm actual access countries, local storage practices, support access and subcontractors in writing. A sales contact’s location may not reflect where the work will occur.

Step 5: Record the decision and manage changes

Before provisioning access, keep a decision record that names the approving owner, adviser input, approved records, permitted tasks, countries, recipients and unresolved conditions. If material facts remain unknown, keep the affected access on hold rather than treating an unanswered questionnaire as approval.

Document operational responsibilities as well:

  • Practice manager: maintains the inventory and coordinates reviews.
  • Finance lead: verifies the business need and reviews accounting outputs.
  • Legal or privacy adviser: evaluates applicable obligations and contract questions.
  • IT administrator: implements and verifies approved access settings.
  • Recipient: follows the agreed scope and reports changes or suspected incidents through the agreed channel.

Set a review date and change triggers. A replacement contractor, travel to another country, new processing tool or request for patient-level detail can alter the approved facts. At offboarding, remove access and confirm the agreed treatment of working files while preserving records the practice must retain.

Keep the review tied to the finance decision

The practical outcome is a documented answer to a narrow question: may this recipient perform this finance task using these records, from these locations, under these conditions?

FAQs

Does overseas access matter if our accounting system is hosted in the United States?

Yes, it deserves review. Hosting identifies where a system stores records, not every location from which people can view or process them. Document remote access, downloads, support access and onward sharing. Advisers should determine the legal significance of those facts.

Can we send only totals to an overseas accountant?

That may be sufficient for some tasks, such as preparing a location-level P&L. Reconciliation exceptions may require additional support. Define the initial dataset and an approval process for exceptions rather than granting unrestricted access in advance. Review the contents of summary reports too; totals do not automatically eliminate sensitivity.

Does an accounting contract settle the privacy review?

No. Review whether it covers the actual records, entities, recipients, access locations, subcontractors, retention and incident responsibilities. When protected health information is involved, ask the privacy adviser to assess business-associate responsibilities and required agreements. A general confidentiality clause should not be treated as the complete answer.

Is this the same as choosing a cloud region or completing a CCPA checklist?

No. This workflow establishes the practice’s facts and proposed business use. Cloud configuration is a separate technical task, and CCPA applicability is a separate legal question. Neither replaces a review of who will access practice financial records, from where, and for what purpose.
