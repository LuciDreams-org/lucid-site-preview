5 Ways AI Can Support Medical Practice Financial Risk Reviews

November 4, 2026 · 10 min read

A medical practice financial risk review should answer a practical question: Which financial exceptions need the owner's attention, and what evidence should be checked before acting?

AI may help organize records, compare patterns, and draft exception summaries. It does not establish that an expense is improper, a payer is underpaying, or a loan covenant has been breached. Those conclusions require reconciled records and accountable human review.

For owners and managers of staffed U.S. private practices, five bounded uses are particularly useful: unusual expenses, deteriorating collections, vendor concentration, debt obligations, and payroll-cost exceptions. Each should end with a decision—not merely an alert.

This is a periodic identification and triage workflow. It is not a predictive risk model, a cash-flow forecast, or a detailed payment-fraud investigation.

Establish the Review Process Before Using AI

Start with a consistent reporting period, such as a monthly review after the books are reconciled. Review debt obligations against their actual contractual deadlines rather than waiting for the next meeting.

Assemble a small evidence packet:

  • Reconciled financial statements and general-ledger detail.
  • Payer receipts, patient-payment totals, and aggregate receivables aging.
  • Vendor spending and relevant agreements.
  • Executed debt documents, amendments, and lender correspondence.
  • Payroll registers, approved compensation changes, and staffing records.

Keep entity and location labels consistent. A shared administrative expense charged entirely to one office can look like a location-level problem even when total practice spending is unchanged. Conversely, consolidated results can hide deterioration at one office.

IRS Publication 583 explains the importance of complete business records, separate business accounts, and bank reconciliation. AI analysis should follow that foundation, not substitute for it.

For every exception, require the source record, comparison period, calculation, proposed explanation, reviewer, and follow-up date. Ask the AI tool to distinguish observed facts from possible explanations and to identify missing evidence. Recalculate important totals outside the generated narrative.

Limit Sensitive Information

Use aggregate collection reports wherever possible. Patient names, account identifiers, clinical notes, and detailed explanations of benefits generally are not needed for this review. Payroll records also contain sensitive employee information; use role-level or summarized data when it is sufficient.

Do not send patient-identifiable information through ordinary Slack channels or a general-purpose AI tool. If protected health information is involved, review the workflow and vendor arrangements with appropriate advisers. HHS guidance on business associates and its minimum necessary guidance provide relevant context; they do not certify any particular tool or service.

1. Flag Unusual Expense Patterns

Owner's decision: Determine whether to investigate a charge, correct its accounting treatment, review purchasing practices, or approve a legitimate change in spending.

AI can assist by grouping ledger transactions and identifying departures from a practice's own history. Useful comparisons include spending by vendor, expense category, entity, and location. Where the data supports it, compare supply spending with aggregate activity rather than looking only at dollar changes.

Required Records

Use general-ledger detail, invoices, credit-card statements, purchase approvals, and allocation schedules for shared costs. Include known changes such as a new office, equipment maintenance, or a bulk supply purchase.

A useful request is to identify the transactions driving each variance and separate price changes, purchase timing, volume changes, and possible coding differences. The tool should not invent a reason when invoices are unavailable.

Validation Step

The bookkeeper or controller should trace the flagged total to source documents and confirm the posting period, account coding, location, and entity. The practice manager then confirms the operational explanation.

Why the Alert Might Be Misleading

An annual renewal can resemble a sudden cost increase. A bulk order may cover several future periods. A corrected allocation may move expenses between locations without changing total practice costs. Missing invoices or inconsistent vendor names can also distort comparisons.

Close the exception only after recording whether it was an expected event, a bookkeeping correction, or an issue requiring purchasing follow-up. An unusual amount alone is not evidence of fraud.

2. Identify Aggregate Collection Deterioration

Owner's decision: Decide whether a collection change warrants a billing-team review, payer follow-up, or closer monitoring—not whether to change an individual patient's treatment or balance.

AI may help compare payer receipts, patient-payment totals, and aging categories across periods. It can summarize which payer groups or locations contributed most to a change and list the additional evidence needed to explain it.

Required Records

Use aggregate collections by payer and location, receivables aging snapshots, adjustment totals, refund totals, and aggregate activity measures. Identify the reporting basis: receipts by deposit date are different from collections associated with a particular service period.

Keep payer receipts separate from patient payments. A stable total can conceal weaker payer collections offset by stronger patient receipts, or the reverse.

Validation Step

Finance should reconcile receipt totals to bank deposits, accounting entries, and known timing differences. The billing manager should validate aging definitions, reporting cutoffs, and relevant payer or posting issues.

Compare like periods and comparable activity. A collection-to-charge comparison can mislead when charges and receipts come from different service periods or the charge structure changes.

Why the Alert Might Be Misleading

Deposit timing, holidays, refunds, posting backlogs, provider leave, and changes in payer mix can all affect reported collections. Growth can increase receivables without demonstrating worsening collection performance.

An aggregate alert cannot establish why a payer paid less. If the pattern remains unexplained, assign a defined billing review and a report-back date rather than accepting an AI-generated causal explanation.

3. Detect Vendor Concentration

Owner's decision: Determine whether dependence on a supplier warrants contract review, a backup option, or a documented continuity plan.

AI can help standardize vendor names and calculate each vendor's share of a relevant spending category. The analysis should distinguish financial concentration from operational dependence. A supplier may represent a modest share of total expenses but still be essential to daily operations.

Required Records

Use accounts-payable detail, card purchases, vendor lists, contracts, renewal information, and a manager's assessment of available alternatives. For multi-entity practices, identify whether purchases are centralized or contracted separately.

Choose the denominator carefully. A vendor's share of supply spending answers a different question from its share of all practice expenses. Do not include payroll or intercompany transfers in a purchasing denominator without a clear reason.

Validation Step

Finance should confirm vendor identity, remove duplicate records, and reconcile spending totals. The practice manager should verify contract terms, switching constraints, and whether alternative suppliers are genuinely usable.

Why the Alert Might Be Misleading

A one-time equipment purchase can dominate spending without indicating recurring dependence. Different vendor names may belong to the same supplier group. Centralized purchasing may intentionally concentrate spending to simplify operations.

There is no universal concentration percentage that makes a vendor unsafe. The decision depends on the service's importance, substitutability, contract terms, and the consequences of interruption.

4. Extract Debt-Covenant and Reporting Obligations

Owner's decision: Assign responsibility for lender reporting, verify covenant calculations, and seek advice when an obligation is unclear or potentially unmet.

AI can assist with extracting candidate obligations from loan documents. Useful fields include the borrower, covered entities, reporting requirements, testing periods, calculation definitions, notice provisions, and relevant document passages.

Required Records

Use executed agreements, amendments, waivers, guaranty documents where relevant, and lender correspondence. Add the financial statements and debt schedules needed for the actual contractual calculation.

Require a page or section reference and the exact supporting passage for each extracted obligation. If language is ambiguous or a schedule is missing, label the item Pending adviser verification rather than generating an answer.

Validation Step

The CPA or CFO should verify the financial calculation against the agreement's definitions. Counsel or the lender should clarify legal interpretation and ambiguous obligations as appropriate. The owner should confirm who prepares, reviews, and submits each required item.

Why the Alert Might Be Misleading

An amendment may replace an earlier requirement. A waiver may apply only to a particular period. A covenant may cover one borrowing entity rather than the consolidated practice. Contract-defined earnings or debt service may differ from ordinary financial-statement measures.

AI extraction is a draft checklist, not a legal interpretation or proof of compliance. Keep contract deadlines visible even when the financial review occurs on a different schedule.

5. Surface Payroll-Cost Exceptions

Owner's decision: Determine whether a payroll variance reflects an approved staffing change, a processing issue, or a cost pattern requiring management review.

AI may help compare provider and administrative payroll by location, role, and period. It can highlight changes in overtime, bonuses, employer taxes, benefits, or shared-staff allocations when those components are available separately.

Required Records

Use payroll registers, approved pay changes, time records where applicable, benefit invoices, staffing rosters, and allocation rules. Include known events such as new hires, departures, leave, extra coverage, and bonus payments.

Separate gross wages from employer taxes, benefits, and other labor costs. Keep contractor spending visible when it is relevant to the staffing comparison, rather than silently treating it as employee payroll.

Validation Step

The payroll administrator should reconcile payroll registers to the ledger and payment records, accounting for timing differences and corrections. The practice manager should confirm hours, staffing changes, and allocations. The CPA can review accounting treatment and unresolved differences.

Why the Alert Might Be Misleading

An extra payroll date in a month, a bonus cycle, retroactive pay, or temporary coverage may explain a spike. Payroll-to-collections ratios can rise because collections fell—not because staffing costs changed.

Do not use an AI exception alone to change employee pay or conclude that hours were improper. Resolve the underlying records and approvals first.

A Five-Part Risk-Review Worksheet

Use one row per risk area, then attach supporting detail for each open exception. The following synthetic example is entirely illustrative; all figures and circumstances are invented, not benchmarks or a client case study.

Risk area and illustrative signal Evidence to attach Accountable reviewer Follow-up action
Expenses: one location's supply expense rises from $12,000 to $18,000 Ledger transactions, invoices, purchase approvals, allocation schedule Bookkeeper and practice manager Verify whether a bulk order or miscoding explains the increase; review purchasing only after validation
Collections: monthly receipts decline from $210,000 to $185,000 Aggregate payer and patient receipts, aging snapshots, bank reconciliation, activity totals Billing manager and finance lead Separate deposit timing from persistent collection deterioration and report findings to the owner
Vendors: one supplier accounts for $60,000 of $100,000 in a defined annual supply category Cleaned vendor ledger, category definition, contract, alternatives assessment Practice manager Confirm the concentration and evaluate a practical backup source
Debt: extraction identifies a reporting obligation, but the referenced amendment is absent Executed agreement, obligation passage, document inventory, lender correspondence CPA/CFO, with counsel as needed Obtain the amendment and verify the obligation before treating the checklist as complete
Payroll: administrative labor cost rises from $70,000 to $79,000 Payroll registers, pay calendar, approved changes, time records, allocation schedule Payroll administrator and practice manager Reconcile the increase and distinguish calendar effects from recurring cost changes

Add a status, responsible person, and target date to every follow-up. Practical statuses are: unverified, explained, correction required, and escalated. If an explanation is accepted, retain the evidence and reviewer approval with the worksheet.

For example, the illustrative expense alert might be explained by an approved bulk order. That does not automatically settle the accounting treatment; finance still needs to confirm the appropriate period and classification. Likewise, the illustrative collection decline remains unresolved until the receipt reconciliation and billing review are complete.

Turn the Review Into Owner Decisions

Prioritize confirmed or time-sensitive issues rather than ranking alerts solely by dollar size. A contractual deadline may need attention before a larger but fully explained expense variance.

At the review meeting, resolve four questions for each open item:

  1. What is established by the records?
  2. What remains uncertain?
  3. Who has authority to decide or investigate?
  4. What evidence will close the item, and by when?

Do not let AI approve payments, change payroll, contact lenders, or resolve accounting judgments through this workflow. Its role is to assist with organization and exception summaries; authorized people remain responsible for decisions.

FAQs

Does a medical practice need AI to conduct a financial risk review?

No. Reconciled records, spreadsheets, and accountable reviewers can support the same process. AI may assist with grouping transactions, extracting document passages, and drafting summaries. Use it only where the output can be checked against source records.

How should owners choose alert thresholds?

Use the practice's own history, known operational changes, and financial significance. Consider both dollar amounts and percentage changes, and review entity and location effects. Test proposed thresholds against prior periods before relying on them; there is no universal threshold suitable for every practice.

Can an AI summary confirm a covenant breach or payroll error?

No. It can identify a possible exception. Covenant conclusions require the governing documents and verified calculations; payroll conclusions require reconciled records and appropriate approvals. Escalate unresolved legal or employment questions to qualified advisers.

What should happen when the records are incomplete?

Mark the affected conclusion as unverified, identify the missing evidence, and assign someone to obtain it. Missing data is not evidence that no risk exists. If an obligation is time-sensitive, escalate the uncertainty rather than waiting for a complete AI summary.
