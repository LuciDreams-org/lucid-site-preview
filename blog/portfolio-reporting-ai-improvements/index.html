Checking AI Portfolio Reports for Medical Practice Reserves

November 4, 2026 · 9 min read

A portfolio report can show a rising balance while a medical practice still faces a payroll funding gap. Investment gains, transfers between accounts and cash generated by patient care are different things. An AI-written summary can blur those differences unless someone checks the underlying statements and accounting records.

For owners and managers of staffed private medical practices, reviewing a completed portfolio report should answer two questions: Does the report agree with the source records, and what funds can the practice actually use when obligations come due?

This review applies to practice-held reserve accounts and investment holdings—not an owner's personal portfolio. It is a financial reporting workflow, not a recommendation to buy, sell or hold securities.

Define What the Portfolio Report Covers

Before reviewing performance, confirm the report's scope. A polished summary is not useful if it combines accounts belonging to different entities or leaves out a reserve account.

The report should identify:

  • The legal entity that owns each account.
  • The reporting period and balance valuation date.
  • The bank or custodian, with a masked account identifier.
  • Whether operating bank accounts are included or shown separately.
  • The accounting and valuation basis used for investment balances.
  • Any restrictions, pending transactions or missing statements.

For a multi-entity practice, show ownership before presenting a consolidated total. A balance held by one entity should not automatically appear as available cash for another entity's payroll. Any proposed intercompany movement needs separate accounting and appropriate review.

Also define the reporting boundary. A transfer from an operating checking account into a reserve account increases the reserve portfolio but does not create new cash for the practice as a whole. That distinction is essential when reviewing AI-generated commentary about “growth.”

What AI Can Assist With—and What Still Needs Review

Depending on the tool and documents supplied, AI may assist with extracting statement balances, grouping transactions, comparing account names and drafting explanations of changes. Those outputs are working material, not verification.

Common extraction errors include reading parentheses as positive amounts, confusing cost with market value, duplicating a statement page or treating a reinvested distribution as both new cash and a separate purchase without the corresponding offset.

AI-assisted task What the reviewer must validate
Extract statement figures Account, period, currency, signs, units and page references agree with the original statement
Classify account activity Transfers, income, sales proceeds, purchases and fees are not confused with one another
Summarize investment changes Realized and unrealized changes are separated and use consistent definitions
Draft liquidity commentary Availability is supported by account terms and transaction status, not inferred from an asset name
Flag unusual balances Exceptions are investigated rather than accepted as errors or dismissed automatically

Keep patient-identifiable information out of general-purpose AI tools and ordinary Slack messages. A reserve review generally needs bank and custody information, accounting totals and aggregate cash commitments—not patient-level billing records. Use approved processes for sensitive financial documents as well.

Reconcile the Completed Report Before Reading Its Conclusions

A useful review starts with source statements, not the executive summary. The IRS's Publication 583 guidance on business records and bank reconciliation supports maintaining complete business records and reconciling bank records. A portfolio review should apply the same discipline while also addressing investment-specific activity.

1. Match every account to its statement

Check the account owner, masked account number, statement period and closing balance. Confirm that all expected accounts appear once. A combined custodian summary and its individual account statements can otherwise lead to double counting.

If an account is missing, label the report incomplete. Do not let an AI estimate silently replace an unavailable statement.

2. Reconcile statement balances to the ledger

For bank accounts, identify outstanding payments, deposits in transit and unrecorded charges or interest. For investment accounts, compare holdings, cash balances and transaction activity with the accounting records. Have the CPA confirm appropriate accounting treatment where needed.

A difference is not automatically an error. Timing and valuation methods can explain legitimate differences, but the explanation must be documented. Avoid an unexplained adjustment simply to force agreement.

3. Rebuild the balance movement

For a report using market values, the opening balance plus external contributions, less withdrawals, plus income and investment changes, less separately reported fees should explain the closing balance. Check whether fees are already included in another reported figure before subtracting them again.

Transfers within the report's boundary should cancel. Purchases and sales change the composition of holdings; gross sale proceeds are not the same as investment profit.

4. Check investment labels and calculations

A realized gain or loss generally relates to a disposed holding and its applicable cost basis. An unrealized change reflects a valuation change in holdings still owned. Neither should be relabeled as patient-service revenue.

Confirm how the report treats accrued income, reinvested distributions, unsettled transactions and valuation adjustments. If it presents a return percentage, request the methodology and treatment of contributions, withdrawals and fees. Balance growth alone does not establish investment performance.

Sample Treasury Report: Balances, Changes and Availability

The following is an illustrative example. All amounts, holdings, timing and commitments are invented to demonstrate the review process. They are not investment recommendations or actual account terms.

Assume a single practice entity has three reserve holdings. Its operating checking account is outside this report's boundary. The reserve report uses statement market values at month-end.

Reserve balances and liquidity review

Account or holding Closing statement balance Availability shown for planning Reviewer check
Reserve bank account $187,000 $175,000 after $12,000 of outstanding disbursements Match statement to the reconciled ledger and confirm no additional restrictions
Custodial sweep balance $120,000 Transfer timing not yet confirmed; excluded from immediate funds Verify vehicle, withdrawal terms and expected bank receipt date
Security holding $200,000 Stated maturity is 45 days after period-end; earlier cash availability not established Verify holding details and payment timing; do not assume it can fund an earlier obligation
Total statement value $507,000 $175,000 currently verified for immediate planning Do not describe total market value as immediately spendable cash

The outstanding disbursements explain the difference between the reserve bank statement balance and its reconciled ledger balance. They must not be deducted again if a later cash forecast already starts with the reconciled amount.

The report should show a specific maturity, settlement or expected bank receipt date wherever evidence supports one. “Pending verification” is more useful than a guessed date. Refresh availability before authorizing a payment because a month-end report is not a current bank balance.

Movement during the illustrative month Amount Interpretation

The portfolio increased by $7,000, but $5,000 of that increase came from net transfers into the reporting boundary. The remaining $2,000 reflects the listed income, gains, valuation change and fees. It is not a measure of cash generated by practice operations or a calculated return percentage.

Concentration and exposure review

A custodian name alone does not identify every underlying exposure. The reviewer should check issuers, vehicles and restrictions rather than treating all assets held at one institution as identical.

Exposure in the illustrative report Amount identified Follow-up
Reserve bank relationship $187,000 Identify other practice balances at the same institution and verify applicable account protections separately
Sweep vehicle $120,000 Identify the underlying vehicle and exposures before completing the concentration summary
Single security issuer $200,000 Compare with the practice's documented treasury policy and refer investment questions to its adviser

The report can identify an exposure without deciding whether it is suitable. It should not imply that every account has the same protection, liquidity or risk.

Use Verified Availability to Check Practice Commitments

A portfolio report supports cash planning only when it connects verified availability with payment dates. Pair it with an operating cash forecast showing expected payer receipts, patient collections and practice expenses. Keep investment activity separate from those operating receipts and payments.

Continuing the illustrative example, suppose the practice has these commitments before any additional reserve funds have been verified as accessible:

Commitment Expected payment timing Amount
Provider and administrative payroll Next payroll cycle $95,000
Tax payments confirmed with the CPA Within the planning window $40,000
Equipment deposit under an approved contract Before the verified maturity date $60,000
Total commitments $195,000

Against $175,000 of verified available reserves, these commitments leave a $20,000 gap before considering available operating cash and expected operating receipts. The $507,000 portfolio headline does not resolve that timing gap.

The owner and finance team should confirm operating bank availability, assess collection timing and verify whether any additional reserve funds can arrive before payment dates. Uncertain payer receipts should remain clearly identified assumptions, not guaranteed cash. If a commitment cannot be funded on time, escalate it before authorizing payment.

This is a funding-calendar decision, not a reason for the report to recommend selling a security. Investment decisions belong with the practice's authorized decision-makers and investment adviser.

Review the Commentary and Record Approval

Once the numbers reconcile, read the narrative for unsupported conclusions. Replace language such as “strong operations increased reserves” when the movement actually came from transfers or market changes.

A better summary states what is known, what remains uncertain and what action is required. In the illustrative example:

Reserve statement value closed at $507,000. Net transfers from operating checking accounted for $5,000 of the $7,000 increase. Verified immediate reserve availability is $175,000 after outstanding disbursements. Sweep transfer timing remains unconfirmed. The finance team must compare operating cash and expected receipts with upcoming commitments before payment approval.

Record the report version, preparer, reviewer, source periods and unresolved exceptions. An owner can accept a report for planning with a clearly defined limitation; an unexplained reconciliation difference should not be hidden beneath an approval label.

FAQs

Does a higher portfolio balance mean the medical practice generated more operating cash?

No. Transfers into reserves, investment income and unrealized valuation changes can increase a portfolio balance. Review the operating cash forecast and reconciled accounting records separately to understand cash generated and used by practice operations.

Can an AI report prove that every balance is correct?

No. AI may assist with extraction and comparison, but accuracy requires reconciliation to source statements and accounting records. Missing accounts, duplicated transactions, incorrect classifications and unsupported liquidity assumptions still need human review.

Should all investments count toward the next payroll?

No. Include only funds whose availability before payroll has been verified, after accounting for outstanding payments and restrictions. A market value, maturity date or sale estimate does not by itself establish when money will reach the payroll funding account.

What should happen when the report does not reconcile?

Identify the affected account, amount, source and likely cause. Assign a reviewer and resolve the difference or state the limitation explicitly. Do not use an unexplained balancing adjustment or rely on the disputed amount to authorize commitments.

The Owner's Final Check

A useful portfolio report separates three things: what the practice owns, how its value changed and when funds can be used. Validate each against source records before relying on an AI-written explanation. Then connect verified availability—not the headline portfolio value—to payroll, taxes and capital commitments.
