How should a med spa record packages, memberships and gift cards?

December 7, 2026 · 9 min read

By Lucid · Last reviewed December 7, 2026

You count a prepaid package, membership credit or gift card as revenue when you give the treatment, not when the client pays. Until then, record it as deferred revenue, a balance you owe the client. A $2,400 package of six sessions becomes $400 of revenue at each visit, and you always know what you still owe.

Key points

  • In Example 1, you collect $2,400 in January but earn only $400 of it that month; you still owe the client $2,000 in sessions.
  • If you record the whole package as income the day the client pays, your best selling month looks like your most profitable one, and the months when clients use their sessions look worse than they are.
  • With a monthly rollforward, you know on any day how many dollars of sessions, credits and gift cards you owe your clients.

What is deferred revenue?

Deferred revenue is money a client has prepaid for treatments you still owe them. Accountants also call it unearned revenue, and ASC 606 calls it a contract liability. You list it with what you owe, not with what you've earned, until the client comes in.

  • Breakage is the part of a prepaid balance you expect clients will never use.
  • A banked credit is a membership credit a member hasn't used yet and can use in a later month.
  • A rollforward is a monthly schedule of what you owed at the start, what you sold, what clients used and what you owe at the end.

ASC 606 sets five steps: find the contract, list what you promised, set the price, split the price across what you promised, and count each part as revenue when you deliver it. At a med spa, clients pay first and come in later, so you spend most of your effort on the last step.

When does each prepaid item become revenue?

Each prepaid item becomes revenue when you deliver what the client paid for: a session, a treatment bought with a credit, or a treatment paid for with a gift card. Until then, you carry it as a balance you owe the client.

Prepaid item How you book it when sold When it becomes revenue What can go wrong
Package of sessions Cash in, deferred revenue up by the full price Each session, at its share of the price You count all of it the day of sale
Membership with banked credits Each monthly fee into deferred revenue When the member uses a credit Unused credits pile up and nobody tracks them
Gift card Cash in, gift card balance owed When someone redeems it, or as breakage Old balances sit on the books for years
Prepaid treatment or deposit Cash in, deferred revenue On the day of the treatment A refund or no‑show never gets recorded

If a package mixes treatments, such as three laser sessions and a syringe of filler, split the price across them by what each would sell for on its own. Then count each part when you give it.

How do I record a prepaid package, step by step?

You make one entry when the client pays and one at each visit. When the client pays, you add the cash and the same amount to deferred revenue. At each visit, you move that session's share from deferred revenue to service revenue.

  1. When you sell the package, debit cash and credit deferred revenue for the full price.
  2. Divide the price by the number of sessions to get each session's share.
  3. At each visit, debit deferred revenue and credit service revenue for that share.
  4. At month end, match your deferred revenue balance to the unused sessions in your point of sale system.
  5. When a package expires or you refund it, clear what's left in deferred revenue that day.

Example 1: a $2,400 package of six sessions, sold in January

Example numbers, not market prices. A client buys six laser sessions for $2,400 on January 10 and has her first session that day. She comes in once a month, skips April, and finishes in July.

Month Sessions given Revenue you record Deferred balance at month end
January 1 $400 $2,000
February 1 $400 $1,600
March 1 $400 $1,200
April 0 $0 $1,200
May 1 $400 $800
June 1 $400 $400
July 1 $400 $0
Total 6 $2,400

If you'd booked the $2,400 as January revenue, you'd have overstated January by $2,000, then shown $0 for her from February to July while you kept treating her.

Example 2: a $199 membership with a banked credit

Example numbers, not market prices. Each month the member pays $199 and gets one $199 credit, which she can bank.

Month Credits used Revenue you record Credits you owe at month end
Month 1 0 $0 $199
Month 2 1 $199 $199
Month 3 2 $398 $0

If the membership also buys perks with no credit attached, such as member pricing, ask your bookkeeper how to split the monthly fee.

How should a med spa record gift cards?

Record a gift card as a balance you owe when you sell it, and as revenue when someone uses it for a treatment. If your records show that clients never use some gift card value, you can count that expected breakage as revenue in step with redemptions under ASC 606. Without that history, you wait until the chance that anyone uses the balance is remote.

Some states treat a gift card balance that nobody uses as unclaimed property and require you to turn all or part of it over to the state after a set number of years. If your state does, you keep that part as a balance you owe the state, never as income. The rules differ by state and can depend on where the buyer lives, so check with your CPA before you count any old balance as income.

Do your books and your tax return count a package the same way?

Not always. Your monthly books follow ASC 606, so you can see what you earned each month. Your tax return follows IRS rules, which can count the same package in a different month or year. Keep the two apart, and let your CPA decide the tax timing.

For tax, the general IRS rule is that an advance payment counts as income in the year you collect it. On the accrual method, you can elect to report the part you haven't earned in the next tax year instead, and no later. If you file on the cash method, you report the money when you receive it. Some gift card sales also qualify as advance payments. In Example 1, the client finishes in the same year she pays, so you'll face this question mostly for packages you sell in November and December.

For when each method fits a practice, see the guide to cash vs accrual accounting for medical practices.

How do I know what I still owe my clients?

You keep a monthly rollforward: start with last month's balance, add what you sold, and subtract what clients used, refunds and expirations. That gives you what you owe at month end. Then you check it against the unused sessions, credits and gift cards in your point of sale system.

Line January, Example 1
Owed at the start of the month $0
Packages, credits and gift cards sold $2,400
Sessions and credits used $400
Refunds and expirations $0
Owed at the end of the month $2,000

If your rollforward and your point of sale report don't match, find the gap before you close the month. Look first for a refund, a no‑show or a package you sold outside the system.

Questions med spa owners ask about packages and memberships

This guide is general information, not tax advice for your practice. Tax rules change and depend on your state and your numbers. Talk to a CPA before you act on it.

Is a prepaid package income when the client pays?

Not in your monthly books. Until you give the sessions, the payment is a balance you owe the client, so you count each session as revenue on the day you give it. Your tax return may count it on a different schedule, and your CPA decides that.

How do I record a monthly membership with banked credits?

Put each monthly fee into deferred revenue, then move a credit's value to revenue when the member uses it. Keep a count of unused credits by member. If your membership terms say credits expire after a set time, clear the expired credits in the month they expire.

What happens to unused sessions when a package expires?

If your package terms let sessions expire, you clear what's left of the balance and record it as revenue that month. Before you do, check that your written terms say the sessions expire and that the client saw them at purchase. Ask your CPA whether you can keep the money.

Do unused gift cards ever become income?

Yes, in your books, once you have enough history to expect that part of your gift card value will never be used, or once the chance that anyone uses it is remote. That's breakage. Before you count any of it, ask your CPA whether you have to turn old balances over to your state.

How do refunds on a package work in the books?

You refund out of the balance you still owe, not out of revenue you've already earned. If a client used two sessions of the $2,400 package and you refund the other four, you take $1,600 out of deferred revenue and pay it back. The $800 you counted for the two sessions she had stays as revenue.

Does it matter if I file taxes on the cash method?

Yes, but only for your tax return. You can file on the cash method and still keep deferred revenue in your monthly books, because the books are where you see whether you earned money this month. Ask your CPA which tax method you use and how it treats packages you sell late in the year.
