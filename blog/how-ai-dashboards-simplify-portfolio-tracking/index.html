AI Dashboards for Medical Practice Treasury Portfolios

November 3, 2026 · 10 min read

A practice can report a substantial treasury portfolio and still have too little cash in the right account for payroll. Total portfolio value may include operating deposits, brokerage cash, securities and term deposits held by different legal entities. Those amounts are not equally available for tomorrow’s payments.

For owners and managers of staffed private medical practices, a treasury dashboard should answer a specific question: What does each entity own, when can it access the funds, and which planned payments already depend on them?

AI can help draft explanations and flag exceptions in a prepared dataset. It should not decide what is available, approve transfers or recommend securities. The foundation remains reconciled account records, clear ownership and human review.

Define the treasury portfolio before designing the dashboard

Start with an account inventory covering practice-owned cash and treasury holdings. Include operating bank accounts, reserve accounts, brokerage cash and any securities or term deposits held for the practice. Exclude owners’ personal investments from the practice total.

For each account, record the legal owner, institution, account identifier, purpose and responsible reviewer. Mask account numbers in owner-facing reports. When several locations operate within one entity, location labels can help explain planned uses, but they do not replace legal ownership.

Keep three distinctions visible:

  • Ownership: Which entity owns the account or holding?
  • Access: Is the amount withdrawable now, awaiting settlement or subject to maturity terms?
  • Commitment: Is accessible cash already needed for provider and administrative payroll, rent, vendors or another approved use?

Do not treat a combined report as permission to move funds between entities. Any proposed inter-entity transfer needs appropriate approval and accounting treatment.

The IRS guidance on business recordkeeping supports maintaining complete records, keeping business accounts separate from personal accounts and reconciling bank records. A dashboard should summarize that evidence, not substitute for it.

Show portfolio value and operating availability separately

The headline should not be a single large balance. Use separate measures with explicit definitions.

Measure What it answers Important limitation
Total reported portfolio value What are all included holdings worth at their stated valuation dates? Not necessarily cash available to spend
Accessible cash What cash is available for withdrawal or payment, after verified access conditions? May already be committed to operating needs
Near-term commitments Which approved payments fall within the review horizon? Must reflect payment timing, not just monthly expense totals
Cash remaining after commitments What accessible cash remains after those planned payments? A consolidated surplus can conceal an entity-level shortfall
Expected maturity proceeds Which holdings are scheduled to mature, and when? Terms and payment timing must be verified; current market value is not necessarily the maturity payment

Choose a planning horizon that matches the practice’s payment cycle, such as the period through the next payroll and major recurring payments. Show expected payer receipts separately rather than treating forecast collections as money already in the bank.

Patient balances are also not treasury cash. An accounts-receivable report may inform a collections forecast, but unpaid balances should not increase accessible cash on the treasury dashboard.

The $600,000 total is useful for oversight, but only $300,000 is classified as accessible cash. The security and term deposit are excluded from that subtotal. Concentration and maturity views

In this example, Bank Alpha holds $250,000, Custodian Beta holds $250,000 and Bank Gamma holds $100,000. Those represent approximately 41.7%, 41.7% and 16.7% of the reported portfolio.

These are institution-location concentrations, not a complete assessment of underlying exposure. A security held at a custodian may have a different issuer. Track issuer exposure separately, and do not infer deposit-insurance coverage from the dashboard total.

A maturity view should list each contractual maturity, the entity receiving proceeds, verified expected proceeds and intended use. Keep unknown amounts marked Pending statement or adviser verification. Do not copy current market value into the expected-proceeds field without checking the instrument’s terms.

Distinguish balances, market movements and cash flows

A balance is a snapshot. A cash flow is money received or paid during a period. A market-value movement changes the reported value of a holding without necessarily moving cash.

Those differences matter when an owner asks why the portfolio increased or decreased. A larger balance could reflect payer collections, an entity contribution or an unrealized valuation gain. It does not automatically represent investment income or improved operating results.

The following bridge continues the illustrative synthetic example:

Monthly portfolio bridge Amount Interpretation
Opening reported portfolio value $590,000 Prior month-end reconciled total
Payer receipts +$90,000 External cash inflow
Patient payments received +$20,000 External cash inflow, not unpaid patient balances
Interest received in cash +$2,000 Cash receipt supported by account records
Provider and administrative payroll −$80,000 External cash outflow
Rent −$15,000 External cash outflow
Other operating payments −$6,000 External cash outflow
Unrealized market-value change −$1,000 Valuation movement, not a cash payment
Closing reported portfolio value $600,000 Agrees to the account register

The month’s net external cash inflow is $11,000. The $1,000 unrealized decline reduces the total increase in portfolio value to $10,000. This bridge explains the change; it is not an investment-return calculation.

An illustrative $25,000 transfer from Entity A’s operating account to its brokerage cash balance would have no effect on the combined portfolio total. Record both sides and eliminate the internal movement from consolidated inflows and outflows.

If a security is sold or matures, distinguish proceeds from any realized gain or loss. Moving principal into cash does not make the entire receipt income. The CPA should review the accounting treatment and any difference between book and statement values.

Limit AI to draft summaries and reviewable exceptions

AI is most useful after the account register and reconciliation are prepared. Give it a defined task using approved, appropriately handled financial data, rather than asking it to infer missing ownership or liquidity terms.

Useful draft flags include:

  • A valuation date older than the reporting date.
  • A holding with no verified maturity or access terms.
  • An entity whose commitments exceed accessible cash.
  • An unexplained difference between an account statement and the ledger.
  • A transfer recorded on only one side.
  • A concentration above an owner-approved monitoring limit.

In the sample dashboard, a draft summary might state: “Entity B has a $10,000 cash gap against the listed two-week commitments. Its term deposit is excluded from accessible cash pending review of access terms.”

A reviewer must verify the figures, assumptions and wording before that summary reaches the owner. AI should not initiate transactions, decide legal ownership, certify reconciliations or recommend an asset allocation. Missing data should produce an exception, not a confident estimate.

Use aggregate financial information wherever possible. Patient names, claim details and other patient-identifiable information are unnecessary for this treasury view and should not be placed in ordinary Slack messages or general-purpose AI tools.

Monthly reconciliation-and-review checklist

Assign a preparer and reviewer, and record completion dates. Refresh the liquidity view sooner when payroll timing, a large payment or an account restriction makes month-end information insufficient.

Keep the reporting date, data-refresh date and review status visible. A recently generated report may still contain an older valuation. For urgent payment decisions, confirm current account availability directly rather than relying solely on the monthly dashboard.

FAQs

Is the total treasury portfolio available for payroll?

No. It may include securities, term deposits, unsettled proceeds or funds owned by another entity. Even accessible cash may already be committed. Review availability and planned uses by entity before relying on the consolidated total.

Can AI explain why the portfolio changed?

AI can draft an explanation from a reconciled balance bridge. A reviewer should confirm that it separates external cash flows, internal transfers, realized results and unrealized valuation movements. AI output is not evidence that the underlying records are correct.

Does a treasury dashboard need live integrations?

Not necessarily. A controlled report built from dated statements and verified exports can support monthly oversight. Its limitations must be visible. Decisions involving immediate payments require current availability checks, regardless of how the dashboard receives its data.

Should every location have its own treasury total?

Use legal entities as the ownership foundation. Location labels can explain planned spending within an entity, including shared-cost allocations, but should not imply that each location legally owns a separate share of an account. Keep allocation assumptions distinct from account ownership.
