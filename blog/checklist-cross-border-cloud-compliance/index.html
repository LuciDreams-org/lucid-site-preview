Cross-Border Cloud Review Checklist for Medical Practices

November 4, 2026 · 10 min read

Before adopting a cloud accounting platform, payroll document service or financial reporting tool, a medical practice owner needs more than a promise that data is “secure” or “hosted in the United States.” The practical questions are: What records will the vendor receive, where can those records go, who can access them, and what evidence supports the answers?

A service may store its main database in one country while using another location for backups, support or document processing. These are separate facts to collect—not reasons to assume a service is either acceptable or unacceptable.

This checklist helps owners and managers of staffed U.S. private medical practices assemble a pre-adoption review packet. It supports a decision about financial workflows; it does not establish compliance or prescribe how to meet foreign data-transfer laws. Have qualified legal, privacy and IT advisers evaluate the facts that fall within their responsibilities.

1. Define the Financial Workflow Before Reviewing the Vendor

Start with the business need, not the vendor’s feature list. Identify the records required to complete the work and the people who need access.

For example, a practice seeking per-location profitability reports may need general-ledger transactions, location codes, payroll allocations and shared-cost schedules. It may not need patient-level billing exports. A payroll service review, by contrast, may involve employee identifiers, wage details and bank information.

Write a short scope statement covering:

  • The financial task, such as monthly bookkeeping, payroll document storage or entity-level reporting.
  • The practice entities and locations included.
  • The source systems and proposed imports, exports or connections.
  • The users, outside advisers and vendor personnel who may need access.
  • The information excluded from the workflow.

Distinguish a one-time upload from continuing access. A recurring connection can expose future records as well as the initial dataset. Ask what permissions it requires, whether those permissions can be narrowed and how the connection can be revoked.

For a multi-entity practice, identify which entity signs the agreement and whose records the service will hold. Do not assume one account’s terms or permissions cover every related entity appropriately.

2. Inventory the Data the Service Will Receive

“Financial data” is too broad to support a useful review. List the actual fields and documents, including information inside attachments and free-text notes.

Data category Practice-finance examples Review question
Accounting records General ledger, vendor bills, shared-cost allocations, location P&Ls Which fields and attachments are necessary?
Bank and payment information Statements, payer deposits, account details, payment instructions Does the service need full account details or only transaction information?
Employee and provider records Compensation schedules, payroll reports, tax forms Which sensitive identifiers are included, and who needs access?
Patient-related financial records Patient balances, remittance details, refund support Could the file contain patient-identifiable information or protected health information?
Ownership and entity records Entity documents, owner contributions, distribution schedules Are records separated by entity and appropriate user permissions?
Support and diagnostic material Screenshots, uploaded files, error logs, support tickets Can sensitive information enter a second system during troubleshooting?

Inspect a representative file through an approved review process rather than relying on its filename. A bank transaction description or invoice attachment may contain more detail than expected.

Where the task permits, use summarized financial information instead of patient-level detail. The HHS minimum necessary guidance explains that the standard has exceptions; it should not be treated as a universal rule for every disclosure. For this workflow, the practical starting point is to avoid sending information the financial task does not require.

Do not place patient-identifiable data in ordinary Slack messages, general-purpose AI tools or routine vendor demonstrations. Use synthetic demonstration records and have advisers review the proposed handling of actual sensitive records before upload.

3. Separate Hosting, Backups and Remote Access

Ask the vendor for a written location map specific to the product, service plan and configuration you intend to buy. A company headquarters address is not a hosting location, and a primary hosting location does not describe every possible access path.

Component Facts to request What remains unanswered by “U.S.-hosted”
Primary storage and processing Countries or regions used for databases, files and processing Whether every service component uses the stated location
Backups and disaster recovery Backup and replica locations; failover destinations Whether recovery copies or emergency operations use other countries
Logs and diagnostics What logs contain and where they are processed Whether identifiers or document content appear outside the main database
Remote support Countries from which staff or contractors may access records Whether support access occurs outside the hosting country
Subprocessors Provider names, functions, data received and locations Whether document, support or other services create additional data paths
Downloads and exports Who can download files and any stated restrictions Whether local copies can be created outside the hosted environment

Include temporary processing in the questions. Ask whether document extraction, troubleshooting or optional AI features send content to another provider, and whether those functions can be disabled. Do not assume such features exist; ask the vendor to identify which ones apply.

If the answer is “we use global infrastructure,” mark the affected locations as unresolved. Ask for clarification rather than converting a general statement into a confirmed country list.

4. Request the Subprocessor List and Support-Access Details

A subprocessor list should help explain which other organizations handle practice data on the vendor’s behalf. Request the current list and save a dated copy with the review packet.

For each relevant provider, ask:

  • What function does it perform?
  • What categories of practice data does it receive or access?
  • Where does storage, processing or support occur?
  • Is it used by default or only with an optional feature?
  • How will the practice learn about changes?
  • What contractual options exist if a proposed change is unacceptable?

Review support access separately. Ask whether access is enabled by default, requires practice approval, expires after troubleshooting, or permits downloads. Have the IT adviser assess the available controls and supporting evidence.

Pay particular attention to support tickets and screenshots. Even when the accounting database has a defined location, copying a payroll report into a ticket may create another stored copy under different terms.

5. Collect the Documents That Govern the Service

Marketing language is useful for identifying questions, but it is not a substitute for the applicable agreement and technical documentation.

Request the service agreement, order form, applicable data-processing terms, privacy terms, security documentation, subprocessor list and any relevant service-specific addenda. Where protected health information may be involved, request the proposed business associate agreement for adviser review.

HHS cloud-computing guidance explains that a separate cloud service maintaining electronic protected health information on behalf of a covered entity or business associate is a business associate even if it lacks the decryption key. Appropriate business associate agreements and applicable safeguards are required; encryption alone is not sufficient. HHS also states that OCR does not endorse or certify cloud products.

Accordingly, neither “encrypted” nor “U.S.-hosted” settles the review. Ask counsel to determine the relevant roles and contractual requirements for the actual workflow. A proposed agreement also does not prove that a technical control operates as described.

Use explicit evidence labels:

Evidence status Meaning Example
Marketing assurance Broad claim without workflow-specific support “Enterprise-grade protection”
Vendor-reported fact Written answer describing the proposed service Support may access records from specified countries
Documented commitment Applicable contract language addressing the issue An agreement identifies a selected storage region
Technically reviewed IT adviser has reviewed relevant evidence or configuration Adviser records the permissions tested and any limitations
Unresolved Missing, ambiguous or conflicting information Backup locations remain unspecified

These labels are not a compliance score. Keep contradictory answers visible and ask the vendor to reconcile them.

6. Check Export, Retention and Deletion Before Uploading Records

A cloud service must support the practice’s financial continuity as well as its initial workflow. Ask how the practice can retrieve usable records if it changes providers or loses access.

Confirm:

  • Available export formats and whether exports include attachments, account mappings, entity codes and location details.
  • When exports must occur and whether termination affects access.
  • Any disclosed export or termination charges.
  • How active records, support copies, logs and backups are handled after deletion requests.
  • Whether deletion is immediate, scheduled or subject to documented exceptions.
  • How the vendor confirms completion and handles retained copies.

Do not treat account closure as proof that every copy has been deleted. Conversely, do not request deletion before the practice has preserved records it needs.

IRS Publication 583 supports maintaining complete business records and reconciling bank records. Have the CPA identify the records needed for accounting and tax work, while counsel addresses other applicable obligations and holds. The vendor’s default retention setting is not the practice’s retention policy.

Before adoption, test an export with synthetic records and ask the finance team whether it would be usable for reconciliation and reporting.

7. Complete a Vendor Fact Sheet

Use one fact sheet per proposed service. Link each answer to a dated document, vendor response or adviser note. Leave unknown fields visibly unresolved.

Fact-sheet field Information to record
Service and accountable owner Product, plan, configuration, practice decision-maker and vendor contact
Workflow and scope Financial purpose, entities, locations, users and excluded information
Data inventory Fields, attachments, sensitive categories and recurring connection permissions
Storage and processing Primary locations, temporary processing and configuration limitations
Backups and recovery Locations, replication and failover arrangements
Support access Access countries, personnel, approval process and download permissions
Subprocessors Dated list, functions, locations, data categories and change process
Contract packet Applicable agreements, versions and adviser review status
Exit arrangements Export formats, access limits, deletion terms and retained-copy exceptions
Evidence status Vendor-reported, contractually documented, technically reviewed or unresolved
Open questions Missing facts, responsible person and condition for resolution
Decision Approved scope, restrictions, approver and review triggers

A useful open question is specific: “Does the selected region cover uploaded payroll attachments and recovery backups?” An unhelpful entry is simply “confirm security.”

8. Hand the Packet to Advisers and Make a Scoped Decision

Send advisers the fact sheet, data inventory, proposed workflow, vendor documents and unresolved-question list—not just a sales presentation.

Reviewer Requested review
Practice owner or manager Confirm business need, authorized users and accountable decision-maker
CPA or finance lead Confirm necessary records, entity/location reporting, reconciliation and export usability
IT or security adviser Review access paths, configuration options, support controls and technical evidence
Legal or privacy adviser Evaluate applicable obligations, contractual terms, PHI handling and cross-border questions

Ask each reviewer to state conclusions, limitations and conditions. Separate “acceptable for summarized P&L reporting” from “acceptable for payroll documents” or “acceptable for patient-related financial records.” Approval for one dataset should not silently expand to another.

Choose among three outcomes: proceed within a documented scope, pause until named conditions are resolved, or decline the service. Keep test activity limited to synthetic data while material questions remain open.

Record review triggers such as a new subprocessor, changed support location, added AI feature, new entity or broader data category. This keeps the decision tied to the service actually being used.

Put the Review to Work

The goal is an evidence-backed adoption decision: the practice knows what the service will receive, which locations and access paths are documented, what remains unknown and who must resolve it.

FAQs

Does U.S. hosting settle the cross-border review?

No. Request separate facts about primary storage, backups, processing, subprocessors and remote-support access. A hosting statement does not answer every access question or establish compliance.

Can we send patient-level billing exports to demonstrate the workflow?

Use synthetic demonstration records instead. Determine whether summarized financial data can meet the need. If actual patient-related records are necessary, have the proposed handling, agreements and safeguards reviewed before uploading them.

What should we do when a vendor cannot identify all locations?

Record the missing facts explicitly and request a written response. Ask advisers whether the uncertainty prevents the proposed use or whether a narrower workflow can be evaluated. Do not label an unknown location as confirmed or infer approval from a general assurance.

Is a vendor fact sheet proof of compliance?

No. It is a decision-support document that organizes evidence and open questions. Its value depends on accurate answers, applicable agreements, technical review and qualified adviser judgment about the practice’s actual use.
