5 AI-Assisted Pitch Deck Reviews for Medical Practices

November 3, 2026 · 9 min read

A medical practice expansion pitch needs more than polished slides. It must explain what the requested capital will fund, how the financial projections were built, and what happens if hiring, opening or collections take longer than planned.

AI can help identify unclear language, conflicting figures and unanswered questions. It cannot establish that your books are accurate, confirm financing eligibility or replace professional judgment. The useful approach is to give AI five separate review assignments, then verify its criticism against source records.

These reviews are for owners and managers of staffed US private medical practices preparing a capital-raising presentation—for example, to support an additional location, added provider capacity or an equipment investment. They are not investor discovery, ongoing investor reporting or a substitute for legal review of an offering.

Prepare a controlled review packet

Before requesting feedback, assemble a dated version of the deck and a small supporting financial packet. Use approved tools and share only the information needed for the review. Keep patient-identifiable information out of ordinary Slack channels and general-purpose AI tools.

The packet should distinguish historical results from forecasts and include:

  • Closed-period financial statements, with the accounting basis identified.
  • A monthly cash forecast covering preparation, launch and the operating ramp.
  • A sources-and-uses schedule showing the proposed financing and planned spending.
  • Supporting inputs such as vendor quotes, lease estimates and staffing budgets.
  • An assumption register identifying each input’s source, owner and last review date.
  • Location and entity schedules where expansion affects more than one business unit.

Keep full source documents in the practice’s approved document environment. Use a redacted or summarized review packet when appropriate; removing names alone may not remove all identifying information.

HHS explains that accounting services can involve business-associate responsibilities when protected health information is involved. Its minimum necessary guidance provides context for limiting information use, subject to applicable exceptions. Have the appropriate adviser assess the workflow rather than assuming a tool is suitable because it accepts uploads.

1. Funding-use clarity review: What will the money actually buy?

The first review should test whether a reader can connect the requested capital to specific expenditures, timing and operating milestones.

An expansion slide that says “fund growth and improve efficiency” leaves important questions unanswered. Does the request cover construction, equipment, pre-opening administrative payroll or the cash gap before payer receipts arrive? Which costs are supported by quotes, and which remain estimates?

Ask AI to identify missing explanations rather than invent them:

Review this funding request for clarity. List each use of funds, its timing, supporting source and operating milestone. Flag missing information as unknown. Do not add costs, forecast benefits or infer financing terms.

For a multi-location practice, specify which location incurs the cost and which entity pays it. If the existing practice contributes cash, show that separately from new external funding. Do not count the same contribution twice.

Before and after: An administrative expansion example

Illustrative example only—not a practice case study, cost benchmark or financing recommendation. A practice is considering a second location and a hypothetical $300,000 funding request.

Before: “We need $300,000 to open a second office, hire administrative staff and accelerate growth.”

After: “The proposed $300,000 request covers the uses below. Buildout and equipment estimates are linked to dated vendor documents. Pre-opening administrative payroll comes from a role-by-role staffing worksheet. The working-capital reserve is provisional and will be tested against the monthly cash forecast before the deck is shared.”

Illustrative use Amount Supporting input to document Decision or milestone
Buildout and setup $120,000 Contractor estimate and planned payment schedule Confirm scope and payment timing before commitment
Equipment $60,000 Itemized vendor quotes Confirm purchase, delivery and installation assumptions
Pre-opening administrative payroll $30,000 Planned start dates, compensation and employer-cost assumptions Align hiring with opening readiness
Working-capital reserve $90,000 Preliminary assumption of three months at a $30,000 monthly net cash deficit Replace the shortcut with a monthly cash model
Total $300,000 Sources-and-uses schedule Reconcile to the funding-request slide

The revised wording does not prove the request is sufficient. It makes the evidence and remaining work visible. In particular, an average monthly deficit can conceal a larger cumulative shortfall caused by upfront payments or delayed receipts.

2. Financial reconciliation review: Do the slides agree with the records?

Next, test every material historical figure against a controlled source. AI may spot differences, but it cannot know which version is authoritative without instructions and human verification.

Create a slide-to-source map:

Deck claim Primary source Check before sharing
Historical revenue Closed-period P&L and supporting revenue schedules Same period, entity and accounting basis
Available operating cash Reconciled bank records and cash schedule Separate available cash from restricted or committed amounts
Provider and administrative payroll Payroll reports and general ledger Consistent treatment of wages, employer costs and accruals
Existing location profitability Location P&L and allocation schedule Disclose shared-cost allocation method
Expansion funding requirement Monthly cash forecast and sources-and-uses schedule Explain opening cash, timing and minimum cash needs

Do not treat all bank deposits as revenue. Transfers, financing proceeds and owner contributions can increase cash without representing operating revenue. Likewise, charges, accounting revenue and cash collections are not interchangeable.

IRS Publication 583 supports complete business records, separate business accounts and reconciliation of bank records. Those practices provide a foundation for financial review; they do not validate a particular deck or projection.

A useful prompt is:

Compare the deck figures with this approved source schedule. Report discrepancies with the slide reference, source reference, period, amount and possible explanation. Do not choose a replacement figure without evidence.

Have the bookkeeper or CPA resolve discrepancies. A timing difference, accounting adjustment or entity boundary may explain a mismatch. Replacing the slide number without understanding the cause can introduce another error.

3. Assumption consistency review: Does the expansion model tell one story?

A presentation may contain individually plausible assumptions that conflict when combined. For example, the staffing slide may show a provider starting later than the revenue forecast assumes. A cash forecast may assume immediate receipts even though the operating plan includes a gradual ramp.

Ask AI to trace dependencies across the deck, narrative and forecast:

Identify assumptions that conflict across these materials. For each conflict, cite both references and explain the financial consequence. Separate documented inputs from management estimates. Do not substitute industry averages.

Focus on the relationships that drive the funding decision:

Assumption Cross-check Why it matters
Opening date Buildout, equipment delivery and staffing schedules Cash costs may begin before operations
Provider start dates Payroll and capacity forecast Compensation and productive capacity may start at different times
Collections timing Aggregate historical receipt patterns and forecast logic Activity does not necessarily produce cash in the same month
Administrative staffing Hiring plan and payroll budget Pre-opening and recurring costs must both be funded
Shared overhead Existing and proposed location P&Ls Allocations can shift reported profit without changing total practice cash
Entity funding Entity-level cash schedules Cash held by one entity should not silently fund another in the model

Record the approved assumption once, assign an owner and update all affected slides. Avoid allowing the deck and spreadsheet to become separate versions of the expansion plan.

4. Downside-question review: What could make the funding insufficient?

A useful downside review asks questions that change a decision. It does not simply request a more pessimistic growth rate.

Have AI identify plausible vulnerabilities in the documented plan, such as:

  • What happens if the opening is delayed after payroll and occupancy costs begin?
  • What if provider recruitment takes longer than management expects?
  • What if payer receipts arrive later than the forecast assumes?
  • What happens to existing-location cash if that location must support the expansion?
  • Which expenditures can be deferred, and which become committed before opening?

Then build the selected scenarios in the financial model. AI-generated prose is not a substitute for recalculating cash balances.

For each scenario, show the changed assumption, resulting cash requirement, management response and unresolved dependency. If the plan assumes borrowing, include repayment terms only when they are documented or clearly labeled as provisional.

The owner’s practical decision may be to reduce the initial scope, phase hiring, preserve more cash or delay a commitment. The deck should explain those choices without implying that additional financing will necessarily be available.

Avoid presenting a downside case as exhaustive. It is a decision aid, not a guarantee against other risks.

5. Audience-comprehension review: Can the reader explain the request back?

The final review tests understanding rather than enthusiasm. Ask AI to summarize the presentation using only information explicitly stated in it:

Explain the practice’s funding request in plain language: amount requested, planned uses, historical financial position, key forecast assumptions and major risks. List questions you cannot answer. Do not infer valuation, ownership eligibility or legal terms.

Compare that summary with what you intended to communicate. If the reviewer cannot distinguish the existing practice’s performance from the new location’s forecast, revise the labels and layout. If it interprets projected collections as established results, change the language immediately.

Tailor emphasis to the intended recipient. A lender may focus on cash available for repayment and existing obligations; a prospective equity capital provider may ask different questions about governance and distributions. Qualified advisers should determine which financing structures and recipients are appropriate.

Legal offering language, ownership eligibility and transaction documents are outside the AI review’s remit. Do not use an AI comprehension score as evidence that a presentation is legally ready to circulate.

Use a feedback rubric, not a funding-readiness score

Apply the same rubric after each revision. A high aggregate score can conceal one unresolved material error, so use issue-level statuses instead.

Review mode Ready for adviser review when… Hold the deck when…
Funding-use clarity Uses total to the request, with timing and support identified Material costs or funding sources remain unexplained
Financial reconciliation Historical figures trace to approved records Material amounts, periods or entity totals disagree
Assumption consistency Timing and operating assumptions align throughout Revenue, staffing or cash schedules contradict one another
Downside-question testing Selected scenarios are calculated and responses are stated Cash needs depend on untested assumptions or unspecified financing
Audience comprehension A reader can accurately restate the request and distinguish actuals from forecasts Essential definitions, risks or decision points remain unclear

Classify each comment as verified correction, question requiring evidence, editorial preference or unsupported suggestion. Only verified corrections should change factual content automatically through your normal approval process. An unsupported suggestion should not become a claim simply because it sounds persuasive.

Verify, obtain adviser review and control the final version

Maintain a review log with the slide reference, AI comment, supporting record, responsible person and resolution. Check the underlying documents—not just another AI response. Recalculate totals and test the spreadsheet formulas affected by any change.

Before external sharing:

  1. Have the practice manager confirm operational facts, hiring plans and implementation timing.
  2. Have the CPA or finance adviser review historical figures, forecast logic, cash requirements and location/entity treatment.
  3. Have legal counsel review relevant financing communications and transaction issues, including matters outside the AI review’s scope.
  4. Confirm recipient permissions, remove unnecessary sensitive information and lock the approved version with its supporting schedules.
