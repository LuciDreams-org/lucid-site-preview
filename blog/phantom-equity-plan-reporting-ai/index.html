AI-Assisted Phantom Equity Reporting for Medical Practices

November 4, 2026 · 10 min read

A medical practice with an adviser-approved phantom equity arrangement needs more than a list of awards. It needs a reporting process that connects signed agreements, participant changes, valuation inputs, accounting entries and potential cash payments.

For an owner, the periodic report should answer three questions: What changed, what belongs in the books, and what cash might the practice need? Those answers are related, but they are not interchangeable. Vested units are not necessarily payable now, a potential payout is not necessarily the recorded liability, and an accounting accrual does not establish a participant’s legal entitlement.

AI can help extract terms, identify inconsistent records and draft explanations. It should not decide legal rights, establish practice value or determine accounting and tax treatment. Those decisions remain with the appropriate advisers and authorized practice leadership.

This guide focuses on reporting an existing arrangement—not designing a plan or deciding whether to offer one.

1. Establish the reporting rules before updating the numbers

Phantom equity generally describes contractual compensation linked to a notional ownership interest or business value rather than an actual ownership award. The signed arrangement determines the relevant formula, conditions and payment events. Do not substitute a generic description for the practice’s documents.

Before the first reporting cycle, assemble a short reporting memo approved by the appropriate advisers. It should identify:

  • The legal entity responsible for the arrangement.
  • The controlling plan documents, award agreements and amendments.
  • Who confirms participant eligibility, vesting events and payment authorization.
  • The approved valuation method and required valuation inputs.
  • The accounting basis used for the practice’s financial statements.
  • The CPA’s conclusions about recognition, measurement, presentation and review frequency.
  • Who resolves payroll and tax questions before a payment is processed.

A practice using cash-basis management reports may need a separate obligation schedule even when those reports do not include the same accruals as another reporting basis. The CPA should explain how the schedule relates to the books actually maintained.

Separate entity obligations from location reporting

For a multi-location practice, a manager may support several offices while holding an award issued by one entity. Record the contractual obligation at the responsible entity. If management allocates compensation cost among locations, document that allocation separately.

A per-location P&L allocation must not make the same liability appear in several entities. Conversely, a shared-cost allocation should not hide the obligation from the entity responsible for settlement.

2. Build a controlled register supported by source documents

Maintain one controlled grant register, with a separate row for each award. A participant with awards under different terms needs separate award records.

Use participant identifiers in working schedules. Keep the identity key and sensitive employment documents in a restricted location accessible only to people who need them.

Register field Supporting record Reporting purpose
Participant ID and award ID Authorized participant record and signed agreement Prevent duplicate or misassigned awards
Responsible entity Executed documents and adviser confirmation Map the obligation to the correct books
Units or participation measure Award agreement and amendments Establish the award quantity or formula
Vesting terms and effective dates Agreement and approved event records Track service and other conditions
Separations, forfeitures and cancellations Authorized personnel record and documented interpretation Explain changes without assuming their legal effect
Payout formula and conditions Controlling documents Support the approved calculation
Valuation reference and date Adviser-approved valuation support Identify the input used for the reporting period
Accounting balance and GL account CPA-reviewed calculation Connect the plan schedule to the ledger
Settlement status Authorization, payroll records and payment support Trace approved payments through completion

Preserve prior-period versions rather than overwriting history. Each change should identify its effective date, supporting document, preparer and reviewer.

The IRS guidance on business recordkeeping supports maintaining complete business records and reconciling financial records. For this workflow, apply that discipline by connecting schedule changes to their supporting documents and recorded transactions.

3. Follow the same sequence each reporting period

Assign a preparer, a reviewer and a reporting cutoff. Refresh personnel and payment activity during each close; obtain valuation updates at the frequency and event triggers agreed with the advisers. A new valuation is not simply an administrative assumption to generate each month.

Confirm documents and participant events

Start with the prior approved register. Ask the authorized practice administrator to confirm new awards, amendments, service milestones, departures, approved settlements and other relevant events through the cutoff.

Do not automatically treat a departure as forfeiture. Flag the event and obtain confirmation of its effect under the applicable agreement. Similarly, distinguish an amendment’s approval date from its effective date.

Validate valuation inputs and calculations

Record the valuation source, measurement date, entity covered and approval status. Check that the input matches the award’s contractual formula. A value for one practice entity should not silently become the value for every affiliated entity.

If a valuation input incorporates practice financial results, reconcile those underlying figures to the approved books. Payer receipts, patient collections, provider compensation and shared administrative costs may affect the relevant financial measure, but they do not independently establish practice value.

Use controlled formulas for calculations. Have the CPA determine whether the available inputs are suitable for financial reporting and how missing or outdated information affects the close. Mark unresolved inputs as Pending adviser verification, rather than treating an AI-generated estimate as approved evidence.

Review accruals and potential payments separately

Prepare the accounting schedule using the CPA-approved method. Separately track potential payment dates and conditions for cash planning.

The owner needs to know whether an obligation could compete with provider payroll, administrative payroll, rent or other operating commitments. That cash forecast should use clearly stated scenarios—not imply that every accrued amount is immediately payable.

4. Prepare a participant rollforward

A rollforward explains how the current register follows from the prior approved register. It should reconcile both award activity and the related accounting amounts, without assuming they move in identical proportions.

Illustrative example: participant unit activity

The following figures are synthetic and illustrate reporting mechanics only. They do not establish a valuation, accounting method or participant entitlement.

Participant ID Opening units New units Forfeited units Settled units Ending units Ending vested units
P-101 100 0 0 20 80 60
P-102 80 20 10 0 90 30
P-103 40 0 40 0 0 0
Total 220 20 50 20 170 90

The control equation is:

Opening units + new units − forfeited units − settled units = ending units.

In this illustrative example, each forfeiture and settlement has already been confirmed by an authorized reviewer. Ending vested units come from a separate vesting calculation. They should not be inferred from ending units alone.

Illustrative example: accounting balance rollforward

For the same synthetic participants, assume the CPA has approved the following accounting amounts. These amounts are not calculated merely by multiplying vested units by a single price.

Participant ID Opening liability Net expense or reversal Settlements reducing liability Ending liability
P-101 $12,000 $6,000 $4,000 $14,000
P-102 $6,000 $3,500 $0 $9,500
P-103 $2,000 −$2,000 $0 $0
Total $20,000 $7,500 $4,000 $23,500

Here, opening liability plus net expense, less settlements, equals ending liability. Maintain supporting calculations that explain the expense movement, including the effects of service, approved valuation changes and confirmed forfeitures where applicable.

Keep any employer payroll-related costs in a separately identified schedule if required by the CPA’s treatment. Do not silently combine those costs with participant award balances.

5. Reconcile the plan schedule to the general ledger

A complete participant schedule is not enough if the books contain a different balance. Reconcile the schedule by responsible entity and relevant GL account before closing the period.

Illustrative example: schedule-to-ledger reconciliation

Continuing the synthetic example, assume the ledger contains the opening liability, the approved settlement and only part of the period’s expense adjustment.

Reconciliation item Amount
Opening GL liability $20,000
Expense adjustment already posted $6,000
Settlement reduction already posted −$4,000
Unadjusted ending GL liability $22,000
CPA-approved additional accrual $1,500
Adjusted ending GL liability $23,500
Ending liability per participant schedule $23,500
Unreconciled difference $0

Under the assumptions of this example, the additional entry would debit compensation expense and credit the phantom equity liability for $1,500. It is an illustrative entry, not a prescribed treatment for every arrangement.

Investigate differences before posting. Common reconciliation categories include an omitted award, an entry in the wrong entity, an unrecorded settlement or different valuation versions. Do not post an unexplained balancing adjustment merely to make the schedules agree.

For settlements, reconcile the authorized gross award amount to payroll processing, any applicable deductions or withholding, the participant payment and remaining payable balances. A net bank payment alone may not explain the full reduction in the award obligation. Obtain payroll and tax instructions before processing; do not infer timing or treatment from the settlement date alone.

6. Give AI a bounded supporting role

AI is useful when its output can be checked against a defined source. It is less suitable as an authority on ambiguous terms or unsupported assumptions.

Potential AI task Required control
Extract draft terms from approved document copies Reviewer verifies each field against the controlling text
Compare current and prior registers Reviewer resolves exceptions using authorized records
Suggest formula checks Controlled calculations independently confirm the result
Draft a reconciliation explanation Every stated cause ties to the approved schedules
Draft an owner summary Finance reviewer approves amounts, conditions and wording

Do not let AI decide whether a participant has forfeited an award, select a valuation, determine tax treatment or post entries without approval. If documents conflict, route the question to the responsible adviser rather than asking the model to choose the controlling version.

Restrict participant information

Use only approved tools and access arrangements. Participant IDs reduce unnecessary exposure, but they do not make a sensitive compensation schedule suitable for unrestricted sharing.

Phantom equity reporting generally does not need patient-identifiable information. Use aggregate financial inputs where appropriate, and keep patient details out of ordinary Slack messages and general-purpose AI tools. The HHS minimum necessary guidance explains the standard and its exceptions; the practice should apply its own approved privacy procedures rather than assuming a financial workflow is exempt from review.

7. Deliver a concise owner reporting package

The final package should contain the approved participant rollforward, schedule-to-GL reconciliation, valuation references, settlement forecast and unresolved items. State the reporting date, preparation status and reviewer.

For each potential payment, identify the responsible entity, relevant condition, assumed timing and funding source. Show contingent scenarios separately from approved scheduled payments. Have the CPA review liability presentation and any current-versus-long-term classification rather than deriving classification solely from a manager’s expected payment date.

A useful owner summary explains the movement without overstating certainty: expense changed because of specified approved inputs; the ledger reconciles; and particular payment conditions remain unresolved.

Conclusion

Reliable phantom equity reporting connects approved terms to participant activity, valuation support, accounting balances and potential payments. Each connection needs evidence and a named reviewer.

Use AI to assist with extraction, comparisons and draft explanations. Keep legal interpretation, valuation, accounting and tax decisions with the appropriate professionals. The result should be a reconciled reporting package that helps the practice owner plan cash without confusing a forecast, an accrual and a contractual right.

FAQs

How often should a medical practice update phantom equity reporting?

Include participant activity and ledger reconciliation in the regular close process. Agree with the CPA and other advisers on valuation refreshes and additional reviews after relevant events, such as amendments or authorized settlements. The necessary work depends on the arrangement and reporting basis.

Are vested units the same as an amount currently payable?

Not necessarily. Vesting, measurement and payment conditions are separate questions. Track vested units, the accounting balance and potential settlement timing separately, using the signed documents and adviser-approved interpretations.

What if the participant schedule does not match the ledger?

Identify the difference by entity, participant, transaction and reporting period. Check document versions, valuation inputs, settlement records and posted entries. Record only supported, approved adjustments, and disclose unresolved differences to the reviewer before finalizing the report.

Can AI determine the liability or approve a payout?

No. AI may assist with controlled calculations or draft supporting explanations, but its output is not an approval. The CPA reviews accounting treatment, appropriate advisers resolve legal and tax questions, and authorized practice leadership approves payments under the arrangement.
