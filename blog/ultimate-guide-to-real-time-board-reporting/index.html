Current Board Reporting for Medical Practices: A Practical Guide

November 3, 2026 · 8 min read

A medical practice’s board or owner-governance group may need to act before the next accounting close. Payer receipts can arrive later than expected, a location may need additional staffing, or an equipment commitment may require approval while current-month expenses are still being recorded.

The solution is not to present unfinished accounts as final. It is to establish a reporting cadence that separates closed financial results, provisional operating information and forecasts, then routes each decision to the right reviewer.

For a staffed private practice, current board reporting should answer four questions: What changed? How reliable is the information? Who has reviewed it? What action is needed, and by when?

This guide describes a practical operating framework—not a legal board requirement. Adapt it to your practice’s governing documents, delegated authority, financing arrangements and professional advice.

Define What “Current” Means Before Sharing Numbers

Real-time reporting cannot mean continuously finalized accounts. Bank activity may update frequently, but accounting still requires reconciliation, expense classification, payroll adjustments, revenue review and other close procedures.

A payer deposit received today does not necessarily represent revenue earned today. A provider payroll run may include amounts relating to a prior period. An unpaid invoice may belong in the current period even though cash has not left the bank.

Use explicit status labels so recipients know what they can rely on.

Reporting status What it means Appropriate use Important limitation
Closed financial results Finance has completed the practice’s defined close procedures and approved the reporting version Reviewing profitability, budget performance and completed-period results Closed does not mean audited; later corrections may still be necessary
Provisional actuals Recorded activity or source-system totals that have not completed all close checks Monitoring receipts, spending and emerging variances Missing entries, coding changes and timing differences may affect results
Forecast An estimate of future cash or financial performance using stated assumptions Assessing payroll coverage, proposed spending and financing needs It is not a recorded result or a guarantee

Keep these categories visually separate. Do not place a closed monthly profit figure beside an unreviewed current-month profit estimate without identifying their different status and coverage periods.

Establish a Cadence That Matches Decision Timing

Not every figure needs the same refresh schedule. The reporting frequency should reflect how quickly the underlying issue could require action—not how frequently a dashboard can reload.

The following is a suggested cadence, not a required schedule.

Reporting layer Suggested timing Accountable reviewer Governance purpose
Cash and near-term commitments Weekly; more frequently during cash pressure Finance lead, with practice manager confirmation of upcoming obligations Identify funding or payment decisions before they become urgent
Interim exception update Weekly or when a material event occurs Finance lead and managing owner Explain changes that cannot wait for the next packet
Closed-period board packet After each agreed monthly or quarterly close Finance lead or CPA, then designated governance sponsor Review completed-period performance and approve decisions
Forecast refresh Alongside the packet and after significant changes Finance lead, with operating assumptions confirmed by management Reassess cash needs and proposed commitments

Agree on a reporting cutoff and circulation date. If a source is delayed, publish its actual data date rather than implying that every component is equally current.

For multi-location or multi-entity practices, identify which bank accounts, entities and locations are included. A consolidated cash total can conceal a shortage in the entity responsible for payroll or a lease payment. Do not assume funds are available for transfer merely because another entity has cash.

Assign Responsibility From Preparation Through Release

A board-ready update needs an accountable reviewer, not just a preparer. In a smaller practice, one person may hold several roles, but those responsibilities should remain explicit.

  • Practice manager: Confirms staffing changes, planned purchases, vendor commitments and operational explanations.
  • Bookkeeper or accounting preparer: Assembles source reports, records transactions and identifies unreconciled items.
  • Finance lead or CPA: Reviews accounting treatment, reconciliations, significant variances and the limitations of provisional figures.
  • Managing owner or designated governance sponsor: Confirms the requested decision, authority route and distribution list.
  • Board or owner-governance group: Considers matters reserved to it and records decisions through the practice’s established process.

Before release, the reviewer should check that the figures tie to the stated sources, the forecast assumptions are explicit, and the requested action follows from the evidence. Any open issue should have a named owner and expected resolution date.

An AI-generated summary is a draft, not an approved financial statement. A human reviewer must verify its figures, explanations and recommendations against the underlying reports before circulation.

Set Escalation Rules Before a Material Event Occurs

Routine reporting should not become a stream of alerts. Define which changes deserve an interim notice and which require discussion before anyone commits the practice.

Set practice-specific thresholds using both financial size and operational significance. An item may warrant escalation even when its dollar value is modest—for example, if it threatens payroll timing or exceeds delegated authority.

Event Interim update should explain Possible governance response
Payer receipts fall below forecast Affected period, known cause, cash effect and uncertainty Review payment priorities or request an updated cash forecast
Provider or administrative payroll increases unexpectedly Recurring versus temporary cost, effective date and approval status Review staffing assumptions or spending authority
Location results change materially Direct costs, shared-cost treatment and whether the figures are closed Request analysis before changing location budgets
A financing or equipment commitment is proposed Total cash obligation, timing, alternatives and authority needed Schedule a decision meeting before commitment
A significant accounting correction is identified Affected reports, estimated impact and review status Issue a corrected version and reconsider affected decisions

Distinguish notification from approval. Receiving an email update does not itself authorize spending, a distribution or a transfer between entities.

Build the Closed-Period Board Packet Around Pending Decisions

The board packet provides a stable reference point between interim updates. It should show the last closed position, what has changed since then and which choices require governance attention.

A useful packet outline is:

  1. Cover and status sheet: Reporting period, issue date, version, preparer, reviewer and included entities or locations.
  2. Decision register: Requested approvals, recommendation, deadline, responsible owner and supporting pages.
  3. Closed financial statements: Profit and loss, balance sheet and cash-flow information, with the accounting basis stated.
  4. Budget and prior-period comparison: Explanations for significant variances, separating timing effects from recurring changes.
  5. Location and entity schedules: Direct results, shared-cost allocations and relevant intercompany balances, where applicable.
  6. Cash forecast: Opening cash, expected payer and patient receipts, provider and administrative payroll, other commitments and key assumptions.
  7. Interim changes since close: Material events, provisional indicators and unresolved reconciliation items.
  8. Prior action log: Decisions made, responsible people, due dates and completion status.

For shared costs, disclose the allocation method and any change from the prior packet. A location can appear more profitable simply because central administrative expenses were allocated differently. That is not the same as an operating improvement.

Maintain supporting business records and reconciliations behind the packet. IRS Publication 583 provides guidance on business recordkeeping, separate business accounts and reconciling bank records; it does not prescribe this board-reporting format.

Use a Short, Clearly Labeled Interim Update

An interim update should report exceptions, not reproduce the entire board packet. Include the data dates, limitations, reviewer and exact action requested.

Illustrative interim update—not an actual practice report

All dates and amounts below are invented solely to demonstrate the format.

Field Example content
Issued November 12; version 1
Last closed period October, reviewed by the finance lead
Current coverage Bank activity through November 11; payroll schedule through November 20; receivables report through November 10
Status November information is provisional; the cash forecast is an estimate
Scope Operating entity and its two locations; affiliated real-estate entity excluded
Reviewed by Finance lead for financial consistency; practice manager for upcoming obligations

What changed: Expected payer receipts of $80,000 have shifted from this week to next week. The timing estimate has not yet been confirmed. This changes projected cash timing; it does not establish a revenue loss.

Decision-relevant effect: The base-case forecast shows $145,000 of cash before a scheduled $110,000 payroll and related funding requirement. Those figures alone are not a spending limit: the forecast must also account for vendor payments, outstanding checks and other commitments.

Limitations: The latest bank activity has not been fully reconciled. A vendor invoice remains uncoded. The current forecast excludes any new owner distribution.

Requested action: Defer the proposed equipment deposit until finance confirms receipt timing and refreshes the commitment schedule. The designated approver should respond before the deposit deadline under the practice’s authority policy.

Escalation: If the delayed receipts remain unconfirmed at the next review, convene the authorized decision-makers to assess payment timing and available funding options. The update does not authorize borrowing or an intercompany transfer.

Next update: Finance lead to circulate the revised forecast after the next receipt review, with any unresolved items carried forward explicitly.

Decide When an Update Needs a Meeting

A written update may be enough when management is acting within existing authority and no material trade-off requires approval. A meeting becomes useful when decision-makers need to evaluate alternatives, challenge assumptions or resolve conflicting priorities.

Consider convening the appropriate group when:

  • A proposed commitment exceeds delegated authority.
  • Payroll or other essential obligations may not be funded on time.
  • A distribution, financing decision or entity transfer requires review.
  • A material correction could change an earlier decision.
  • A location expansion, closure or major staffing change depends on uncertain assumptions.

State what happens if no decision is reached. Management might pause an uncommitted purchase while completing the analysis rather than interpret silence as approval. Use the practice’s established governance process for the actual approval and decision record.

Keep Distribution and Corrections Controlled

Board-level financial analysis generally needs aggregate information, not patient-level detail. Report payer receipt totals, patient-balance trends and collection assumptions without attaching identifiable patient records to routine packets.

HHS guidance on the minimum necessary standard explains the standard and its exceptions. Determine appropriate handling for your circumstances; do not assume a routine communication channel is suitable for protected health information.

When a report changes, issue a new version stating what changed, why it changed and whether any requested or completed decision is affected. Avoid silently replacing a file recipients may already have used.

Put the Cadence Into Operation

Start by documenting the reporting statuses, calendar, reviewers, escalation triggers and approval routes. Then run the process through a reporting cycle and review whether decision-makers received enough reliable information before each deadline.
