AI-Assisted Financial Data Access Reviews for Medical Practices

November 3, 2026 · 10 min read

A practice administrator may need to prepare payroll without approving it. A bookkeeper may need to reconcile payer deposits without initiating bank transfers. A location manager may need a location-level P&L without access to every provider’s compensation.

A financial data access review checks whether actual permissions match those business needs. For a staffed private medical practice, the practical question is: Who can view, change, export or approve financial information—and who has verified that access is still appropriate?

AI may help compare approved access exports with a current staff roster and a role-permission matrix. It can help organize potential exceptions, but people must verify the underlying permissions, decide what access is necessary and confirm that changes took effect.

This is an internal financial-control review. It is not a security audit, compliance certification, product ranking or guarantee that every improper permission will be detected.

1. Define the Review Scope and Decision Owner

Start with the systems that hold financial records or allow money to move. Assign a practice owner or authorized manager to approve access decisions, and identify the administrator who can implement changes in each system. Where possible, reviewers should not approve their own access.

For a multi-location or multi-entity practice, document which locations, legal entities and accounts each permission covers. A manager’s responsibility for one office does not automatically justify access to every entity’s bank accounts or payroll.

System or record set Permissions to examine Practice-finance question
Banking and payment services View balances, export activity, manage payees, initiate transfers, approve payments, administer users Can someone both change payment instructions and release funds?
Accounting system View reports, post or edit entries, change vendors, export records, reopen periods, administer users Does access fit bookkeeping responsibilities and entity coverage?
Payroll system View compensation, edit employee details, prepare payroll, approve payroll, export reports Are preparation, approval and sensitive payroll visibility appropriately separated?
Payer receipt and patient-balance reporting View collection summaries, export reports, change adjustment or refund settings where applicable Does finance staff need summary receipts rather than patient-level detail?
Shared financial files and reporting workspaces View, edit, download, share and manage access Can a restricted system report become broadly accessible after export?

Include outside bookkeepers, CPAs, payroll providers, temporary staff and nonhuman accounts. Do not assume that reviewing employee logins covers the entire access population.

2. Collect Evidence Without Collecting Unnecessary Sensitive Data

An access review usually needs permission metadata, not transaction-level patient or payroll records.

Ask an authorized administrator to obtain current user and permission exports, or equivalent administrative reports. Record the extraction date, system, entity scope and any limitations. If a report omits payment approval limits or inherited group permissions, obtain that information separately.

Prepare these inputs:

  • Current personnel roster: Active staff, roles, location or entity responsibilities, and effective dates for departures or transfers.
  • External-user register: Outside organizations, named users, engagement owners and access purposes.
  • Role-permission matrix: The access the owner has approved for each role.
  • System access evidence: Active and inactive users, assigned roles, direct permissions and relevant account scope.
  • Prior review workpaper: Open exceptions and evidence of previously approved changes.

Reconcile exports to the administrative user list where possible. Check for omitted entities, archived users, invitation-pending accounts, group-based permissions and separate administrator accounts. A complete-looking spreadsheet can still be incomplete.

Use review IDs in an AI comparison file when practical, keeping the identity cross-reference in a restricted location. Do not include passwords, recovery codes, API keys, full bank account numbers, Social Security numbers or patient-identifiable data. Payroll amounts are generally unnecessary for deciding whether someone should have payroll access.

For healthcare information, the HHS minimum necessary guidance provides relevant context, with exceptions and application depending on the circumstances. The simpler workflow is to keep patient information out of this review whenever it is not needed.

Do not upload sensitive exports to a general-purpose AI tool merely because it accepts files. Have the practice’s responsible privacy, security and technology advisers evaluate the proposed environment and data handling first. If a cloud service would maintain electronic protected health information, consult the HHS cloud-computing guidance; encryption alone does not resolve business-associate obligations.

3. Define Expected Access Before Asking AI to Find Exceptions

A staff title is not enough to establish permission needs. “Practice administrator” may mean payroll preparation in one practice and payment approval in another.

Create an owner-approved role matrix that specifies the action, entity scope and approval authority. Avoid a single “finance access” category.

Role Possible business need Permission requiring separate justification
Location manager View the assigned location’s operating reports Export all-entity payroll or modify banking users
Internal bookkeeper Post approved entries and reconcile accounts Approve transfers or change payment instructions without review
Payroll preparer Maintain authorized payroll inputs and prepare a payroll run Approve their own compensation changes or release payroll alone
Outside CPA Access records required for an agreed accounting or tax engagement Ongoing bank payment authority unrelated to that engagement
Practice owner or designated approver Review payroll and approve authorized payments Broad administrator access retained solely for convenience

These are starting points, not universal permission rules. Actual duties, staffing and system capabilities determine the design.

When separation is not practical, document a compensating review. For example, if an administrator maintains vendor records and prepares payments, an owner could independently review payment details and supporting documentation before release. The review must address the actual conflicting duties; simply signing a general checklist is not enough.

4. Use AI to Compare Evidence, Not Authorize Access

Once the inputs are approved for processing, AI may assist with matching role names, comparing access against the matrix and drafting an exception list. Straightforward spreadsheet joins and explicit rules may be sufficient for many checks; AI is optional.

Useful comparisons include:

  • Users with active access who do not match the current personnel or external-user registers.
  • Permissions beyond the approved role or location/entity scope.
  • Users who can both change payee details and approve payments.
  • Payroll users who can modify compensation and approve the same payroll run.
  • Unassigned administrator accounts or service accounts without a documented owner.
  • Prior remediation items that still appear in current access evidence.

Require each proposed exception to identify the source record, applicable rule, reason for the flag and missing information. Mark ambiguous matches as unresolved rather than allowing the tool to guess.

Illustrative example: A location manager transfers to another office, but an accounting export shows access to both locations. AI flags the mismatch against the current roster. The reviewer then checks whether temporary transition duties justify the overlap. The result might be removal, a documented temporary exception or correction of an outdated roster—not automatic revocation.

Test the comparison against known examples before relying on it. Confirm that inherited permissions, alternate usernames and entity labels are interpreted correctly. A role called “viewer” may still allow downloads; a role called “administrator” may have different powers across systems.

AI can miss exceptions or generate false positives. A review of available exports also cannot establish that no unknown account or unreported permission exists. Preserve these limitations in the workpaper.

5. Maintain an Access-Review Workpaper

Use a controlled workpaper to connect each access decision to evidence. Record the review period, preparation date, source files, scope limitations and owner sign-off. Keep sensitive source files in an approved restricted repository rather than embedding them in a broadly shared checklist.

The following is an illustrative workpaper, not evidence from an actual practice. User labels and evidence references are fictional placeholders.

System / scope User Permission Business need Reviewer / decision Remediation evidence
Bank / operating entity Former administrator A View accounts and initiate payments None; employment ended Owner: revoke immediately Administrator confirmation plus fresh user-list evidence showing access disabled; record completion date
Accounting / all entities Location manager B View and export all-entity reports Assigned-location reporting only Owner: restrict scope Change record and post-change permission report; verify remaining export access
Payroll / operating entity Payroll preparer C Edit compensation and approve payroll Preparation needed; approval assigned to owner Owner: remove approval right Updated role report and confirmation that owner approval remains configured
Accounting / shared service Service account D Import financial data and administer users Import function documented; user administration not justified System owner: validate dependencies, then narrow access Dependency check, revised permission evidence and confirmation that required import still works
Bank / operating entity Outside bookkeeper E View and export activity Reconciliation under current engagement Owner: retain Current access evidence and documented business-need approval

Add an action owner, due date, status and verification date for each exception. If the reviewer approves temporary access, record its purpose, expiration or recheck date, and the person responsible for follow-up.

“Request sent” is not closure. Completion requires evidence that the actual permission changed and that necessary work can still be performed.

6. Resolve Departed Users, Service Accounts and Conflicting Duties

Departed or reassigned staff

Do not wait for the next scheduled review to address departures. Confirm access removal across banking, accounting, payroll and shared reporting locations. Include secondary logins and delegated access. Transfer ownership of needed files or workflows without preserving unnecessary interactive access.

Revoking access and deleting business records are separate decisions. Preserve records according to the practice’s applicable requirements and adviser-approved policies.

Service and shared accounts

A service account may support a financial data import or scheduled process. Record its purpose, business owner, technical owner, permitted systems and required permissions. Do not put credentials or secret values in the workpaper.

Before disabling it, verify dependencies with the responsible administrator so a needed process is not inadvertently interrupted. Where multiple people use a shared interactive login, assess whether named accounts are available and document any unresolved attribution limitation.

Conflicting duties

Review combinations across systems, not just within one application. A person might change a vendor’s bank details in accounting and release the payment through a bank portal. Separate exports may look reasonable until the permissions are considered together.

Prioritize unexplained payment authority, payroll approval conflicts, unowned administrator access and departed-user access. If evidence suggests improper activity, involve the owner and appropriate advisers; do not treat the permission review itself as an investigation.

7. Close the Review and Make It Repeatable

The final owner summary should state:

  • Which systems, entities and user populations were reviewed.
  • Which source limitations remain.
  • Which exceptions were confirmed, rejected or left unresolved.
  • Which changes were implemented and independently verified.
  • Which temporary exceptions remain, with accountable owners and follow-up dates.

Choose a documented recurring review schedule based on the practice’s systems and risk, with additional checks after departures, role changes, new locations or changes in outside providers. Track unresolved high-risk exceptions and overdue actions rather than relying on a single “review complete” label.

Access controls support reliable financial work, but they do not replace reconciliations, payroll review or accurate bookkeeping. IRS Publication 583 provides separate guidance on complete business records, separate business accounts and reconciling bank records.

FAQs

Is a financial data access review the same as a security audit?

No. It evaluates whether financial-system permissions match approved responsibilities. A security audit can have a much broader scope, methodology and evidence requirements. This review does not certify compliance or establish that systems are secure.

Does AI need access to the practice’s live banking or payroll systems?

Not necessarily. An authorized administrator can provide approved, minimized permission exports for comparison in an evaluated environment. AI does not need payment authority, payroll approval rights or credentials to help identify mismatches. Export limitations still need human review.

What if an access export does not show whether a user can approve payments?

Record the missing information as a scope limitation. Ask the system administrator for the relevant role configuration or other permission evidence. Do not interpret a missing field as proof that approval authority is absent.

Who should approve the final access decisions?

The practice owner or a formally designated decision-maker should approve business need and exceptions. Authorized system administrators implement changes, and a reviewer verifies the resulting access. Avoid self-approval where practical, and document compensating review when staffing limits separation.
