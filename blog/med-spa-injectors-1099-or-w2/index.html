Can my med spa pay nurse injectors as 1099 contractors?

December 8, 2026 · 9 min read

By Lucid · Last reviewed December 8, 2026

Only if the injector really runs their own business. The IRS looks at who controls the work, who controls the money side, and how the two of you treat the relationship. If you set the schedule, supply the product and room, and set the prices, the IRS will likely see an employee, and you could owe back payroll taxes.

What's the difference between a W-2 injector and a 1099 injector?

A W-2 injector is your employee: you withhold their taxes, pay your share of Social Security and Medicare, and report their pay on Form W-2. A 1099 injector is a contractor: you pay the full amount with nothing withheld, they pay their own taxes, and you report the payments on a Form 1099.

A 1099 contractor is a self-employed worker you pay without withholding, reported on Form 1099-NEC once you pay them $2,000 or more in 2026. Self-employment tax is the 15.3% Social Security and Medicare tax a contractor pays on their own net earnings.

How does the IRS decide if an injector is an employee?

The IRS uses the common-law test. It sorts the facts into three groups, behavioral, financial and type of relationship, and weighs them all to judge your control over the work. No single fact decides it, and the IRS doesn't set a number of facts that does.

The common-law test is the IRS rule that a worker is your employee if you have the right to control the details of how they do the work, even if you don't use that right.

IRS group Points to employee at a med spa Points to contractor at a med spa
Behavioral You set shifts, book their clients and require your treatment methods They set their own hours and book their own clients
Financial You buy the product, supply the room and set the price They buy product, set prices and can make or lose money
Type of relationship Ongoing work with benefits, and injections are your main service A written contract for set work, and they serve other spas

Some control at a med spa comes from state rules, such as your medical director's protocols. Ask your CPA and a healthcare attorney how that weighs.

If you're still not sure, you or the injector can file Form SS-8. Form SS-8 is the form you file to ask the IRS to decide a worker's status, and a decision can take at least six months.

Do state rules change the answer?

They can. Your state may use a stricter test for unemployment tax, workers' comp or wage laws. Your state's nursing and medical boards decide who may inject, who must supervise and who may own the spa. An injector can pass the IRS test and still be an employee under state law.

An ABC test is a stricter test some states use, under which a worker is an employee unless the business proves all three of the test's conditions.

Federal wage law has its own test. The Department of Labor published a new rule for it on November 23, 2026. It takes effect January 22, 2027 and rescinds the 2024 rule.

What does one injector cost you on a W-2 vs a 1099?

On payroll, you'd pay about 9% on top of an injector's pay in taxes and insurance. In Example 1, a $90,000 injector costs you $98,097 a year as an employee and $90,000 as a contractor. As a contractor, the injector pays both halves of Social Security and Medicare.

What do you owe if the IRS reclassifies a 1099 injector?

You'll owe the employment tax you didn't pay. If the mistake wasn't intentional, you can usually use the reduced section 3509 rates, which are lower if you filed the 1099. You still owe your full share of Social Security and Medicare, and you can't collect the employee's share from the injector.

Section 3509 rates are reduced rates for the employee's share of Social Security and Medicare and for income tax withholding on a misclassified worker.

Example 2: the same $90,000 injector, reclassified for 2026. The mistake wasn't intentional. Interest, penalties, federal unemployment and state taxes aren't included.

Two IRS rules can lower this. Section 530 relief is a rule that can excuse you from the back tax if you had a reasonable basis for using a 1099, filed every 1099, and treated everyone in a similar job the same way. A reasonable basis can be a court case, a past IRS audit, your accountant's or lawyer's advice, or proof that a significant segment of your industry does the same.

The Voluntary Classification Settlement Program (VCSP) is an IRS program that lets you move contractors onto payroll from now on and pay 10% of one year's back tax at the reduced rates. In Example 2, that's $961.20, and the IRS won't audit those workers' classification for earlier years. You need their 1099s filed for the last three years and no employment tax audit open.

How do you move an injector from a 1099 to payroll?

You can switch an injector any time of year. Ask your CPA first whether the VCSP fits, because the IRS wants the application at least 120 days before the switch. Then set them up like any new hire. For that year, you'll file a 1099-NEC for the contractor months and a W-2 for the rest.

  1. Ask your CPA whether the VCSP or Section 530 applies.
  2. Have an attorney write the employment agreement.
  3. Collect Form W-4 and Form I-9, and report the new hire to your state's new hire registry.
  4. Add the injector to payroll, workers' comp and any benefits you offer.
  5. In January, file the 1099-NEC for the contractor months if they come to $2,000 or more, and the W-2 for the rest.

Questions med spa owners ask about 1099 and W-2 injectors

This guide is general information, not tax advice for your practice. Tax rules change and depend on your state and your numbers. Talk to a CPA before you act on it.

Can a nurse injector be a 1099 contractor?

Yes, if they really work for themselves. A nurse injector who sets their own hours, buys their own product, sets prices and works for several spas can pass the IRS test. Your state may still limit how they can work, so check with a healthcare attorney first.

What makes someone an employee instead of a contractor?

Your right to control the work. If you can tell an injector when to work, which products to use and how to do each treatment, the IRS will likely see an employee, even if you never use that right. Calling them a contractor in a contract doesn't change that.

What happens if I classify an injector wrong?

You'll owe the employment tax you should have paid, often at the reduced section 3509 rates, plus interest and penalties. Your state can bill you for unemployment tax and workers' comp too. If you think you've got it wrong, ask your CPA about the VCSP before the IRS contacts you.

Can my medical director be a 1099 contractor?

Often, yes, under the same IRS test. If you pay a medical director $2,000 or more for 2026, the payment may go on Form 1099-MISC, box 6, for medical and health care payments, even when they bill through their own professional corporation. Ask a healthcare attorney about your state's supervision rules.

Can I pay W-2 injectors on commission?

Yes. You can pay an employee a commission per treatment, or a base rate plus commission, through payroll. You withhold tax on commissions as supplemental wages. Federal and state wage laws can still require minimum wage and overtime in slow weeks, so ask an attorney to check your pay plan.

How do I move a contractor onto payroll?

Pick a switch date, collect a W-4 and an I-9, and add the injector to payroll and workers' comp. Ask your CPA about the VCSP first, since the IRS wants the application at least 120 days before the switch date. You'll give them a 1099-NEC for the months before the switch and a W-2 for the months after.
