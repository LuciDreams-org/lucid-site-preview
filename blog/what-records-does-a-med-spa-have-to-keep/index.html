What financial records does a med spa have to keep, and for how long?

December 8, 2026 · 9 min read

By Lucid · Last reviewed December 8, 2026

Keep most tax records for 3 years after you file the return they support, payroll records for at least 4 years, and equipment records until 3 years after the return for the year you sell the equipment. Keep your filed returns for good. Patient charts and drug logs follow state rules, so ask your attorney.

This guide is general information, not tax advice for your practice. Tax rules change and depend on your state and your numbers. Talk to a CPA before you act on it.

Key points

  • The IRS can usually review a return for 3 years after you file it, or 6 years if you left out more than 25% of your gross income.
  • You'll keep payroll records for at least 4 years after the tax is due or paid, whichever is later.
  • Example 1 is a month-by-month list of what to pull at year end and where to file each item.

What is a record retention schedule?

A record retention schedule is a written list of the records your med spa keeps, how long you keep each one, and where you file it.

  • The period of limitations is the time the IRS has to assess more tax on a return, and the time you have to amend it for a refund.
  • Basis is what you paid for an asset, such as a laser, plus improvements and minus the depreciation you've taken.
  • Depreciation is the deduction you take for the cost of equipment, either all at once or spread over several years.
  • A business associate agreement is a contract that lets a vendor handle protected health information for your med spa under HIPAA.

Which records does a med spa keep, and for how long?

A med spa keeps three kinds of records: tax records, with periods the IRS sets; payroll records, with a 4-year rule; and patient and drug records, with periods set by your state and federal health rules.

Record Keep for Who asks for it
Filed tax returns: income, payroll and sales tax For good IRS, your CPA, a lender, a buyer
Bank, card and payment processor statements 3 years after you file the return they support IRS, your CPA
Point-of-sale sales reports and daily close-outs 3 years after you file the return IRS, state revenue department
Package, membership and gift card sales 3 years after the return for the year you count the income IRS, your state for unused gift card balances
Invoices for injectables, skin care and supplies 3 years after you file the return IRS, your CPA
Retail sales tax records The period your state sets State revenue department
Payroll records: Forms W-4 and W-2, pay registers, tax deposits At least 4 years after the tax is due or paid, whichever is later IRS, state tax and labor agencies
Forms 1099-NEC you filed, with each Form W-9 At least 3 years from the due date; 4 if you withheld backup tax IRS
Equipment purchases: lasers, devices, furniture Until 3 years after the return for the year you sell or dispose of it IRS, a buyer, a lender
Formation papers, EIN letter, any S corp election As long as the business exists Your bank, IRS, a buyer
Records behind a bad debt deduction 7 years IRS
HIPAA policies, risk assessments and business associate agreements 6 years from creation or the date last in effect, whichever is later HHS Office for Civil Rights
Patient charts, consents and photos The period your state sets Your state medical board, a patient
Controlled-substance records, if your med spa holds any Ask your attorney DEA, your state board of pharmacy

Before you shred anything, the IRS says to check whether your insurer or lenders need it kept longer (IRS, How long should I keep records?). For payroll records in detail, read how long to keep W-2s and payroll records.

How far back can the IRS audit a med spa?

Usually 3 years from the date you filed the return. It's 6 years if you left out more than 25% of the gross income you reported, and there's no limit if you never filed or filed a fraudulent return. A return filed early counts as filed on its due date.

For a med spa, the 6-year rule matters when sales never reached the books, such as cash nobody entered. Matching point-of-sale sales to deposits each month is how you'd catch that.

Can I throw out paper receipts once I've scanned them?

Yes, if your scans are complete, legible and easy to find, and you keep them for the full period. The IRS applies the same rules to electronic records as to paper and can test your system. The IRS sets out the rules for electronic storage in Revenue Procedure 97-22.

Before you shred the boxes:

  1. Scan every receipt, invoice and statement.
  2. Name each file by year, type and month, such as "2026 injectables March".
  3. Open a sample from each year to make sure you can read it, and keep a backup.

Example 1: year-end records checklist for a 3-year-old med spa

Assumptions: a med spa opened in January 2024, so December 2026 ends its third year. It has two injectors on payroll and one aesthetician on a 1099, sells retail skin care, and bought a laser in June 2024. The owner keeps one cloud folder per tax year, plus "Assets" and "Tax returns" folders.

Month What you pull and file Where it goes
December 2026 Count product on the shelf December 31; list prepaid balances; get a Form W-9 from the aesthetician 2026 folder: Inventory, Prepaid, Contractors
January 2027 Download 12 months of bank, card and processor statements; export sales by month; save payroll registers and Forms W-2 and 1099-NEC 2026 folder: Bank, Sales, Payroll
February 2027 Save each 2026 sales tax return and payment confirmation, and the year's product invoices 2026 folder: Sales tax, Product
March 2027 Send your CPA the closed books; save the filed 2026 return and the depreciation schedule Tax returns folder; Assets folder: Laser 2024
April 2027 Make the shred list: nothing yet None

This med spa can't shred any tax record yet: it filed its first return, for 2024, in 2025, so the IRS can review that return into 2028. For the package and gift card records, see how a med spa records packages, memberships and gift cards.

Example 2: how long a med spa keeps the records for a laser

Assumptions: the same med spa buys a $90,000 laser in June 2024, sells it for $20,000 in 2031, and files the 2031 return in 2032.

Year What happens Records you keep
2024 You buy the laser and start using it Invoice, financing agreement, proof of payment, start date
2024 to 2031 Your CPA takes depreciation on your returns Each year's depreciation schedule
2031 You sell the laser for $20,000 Bill of sale and proof of payment
2032 You file the 2031 return, reporting the sale The return
2035 You reach 3 years after filing the 2031 return You can shred the laser file

You keep the file until 2035 even if your CPA deducted the whole $90,000 in 2024, because your CPA figures the gain from the basis left.

What should I export before I switch software?

Before you cancel the old system, export everything you'd need to prove a past year, since you may lose access once you stop paying. Save each export as a file you can open without that software.

  • Point-of-sale system: daily sales by payment type, refunds, and prepaid balances by client.
  • Accounting software: the general ledger, reconciliations and attached receipts.
  • Payroll company: pay registers, payroll tax returns and Forms W-2.

A med spa's client list can count as patient information, so store the export where your HIPAA policies apply. Keep each export for the same period as the paper records it replaces.

Are patient charts and drug logs covered by the same rules?

No. The IRS periods cover tax records only. Your state sets how long you keep patient charts, consents and photos, and federal and state drug rules cover any controlled-substance records. Ask your attorney or your state medical board.

HIPAA adds one rule: you keep your HIPAA policies and risk assessments for 6 years from creation or the date last in effect, whichever is later (45 CFR 164.316(b)(2)). Keep tax and patient records apart, so whoever shreds old receipts never touches a chart.

Questions med spa owners ask about record retention

How long does a med spa have to keep tax records?

At least 3 years after you file each return, for most records. Keep payroll records at least 4 years, equipment records until 3 years after the return for the year you sell the equipment, and your filed returns for good. If your state has its own sales tax period, follow the longer of the two.

What records should be kept for 7 years?

The IRS sets a 7-year period for records behind a bad debt deduction or a loss from worthless securities. Some owners keep everything for 7 years, because that also covers the 6-year rule for income left off a return. It's a safe default if you're unsure.

Can I keep receipts as photos instead of paper?

Yes. The IRS accepts electronic records that are complete, legible and easy to retrieve, and you can shred the paper once you've checked your copies. Take each photo so the date, vendor and amount are readable, and back the files up somewhere other than one phone.

How long do I keep records for equipment like lasers?

Keep them until 3 years after you file the return for the year you sell or get rid of the equipment. For a laser you own for seven years, that means keeping the invoice for about 11 years. If you financed it, keep the loan papers in the same file.

Are patient charts covered by the same rules?

No. Tax rules cover your financial records only. Your state sets how long you keep patient charts, and it may set a longer period for minors. Ask your attorney or your state medical board, then add the answer to your retention schedule.

What happens to my records if I change software?

You still owe the IRS the records after the software company closes your account. Export before you cancel and check that each file opens. Ask the old vendor in writing how long it keeps your data after you leave, and whether it will send a full export later.
