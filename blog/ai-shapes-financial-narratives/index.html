How AI Shapes Financial Narratives for Medical Practices

November 3, 2026 · 9 min read

A medical practice’s bank balance can rise while its profitability stays flat—or remains negative. If an AI draft describes that increase as evidence of stronger operations, partners may approve spending or distributions based on the wrong explanation.

The useful role for AI is narrower: help turn verified financial results into a clear explanation of what changed, what the evidence supports, what remains uncertain, and what action management proposes. The practice’s finance team must still establish the facts and approve the conclusions.

For owners and managers of staffed private medical practices, this is especially important when payer receipts cross month-end, payroll obligations accumulate, or shared costs move between locations. A plausible sentence is not necessarily an accurate financial explanation.

Start With the Decision Partners Need to Make

Before asking AI to draft anything, identify the decision the narrative should support. For example:

  • Does the cash increase support an additional owner distribution?
  • Is a location’s improved margin operational, or did the shared-cost allocation change?
  • Does higher payroll reflect additional staffing, a payment-calendar difference, or both?
  • Is a collections shortfall temporary, or does it require further investigation?

Choose one central question. Then separate the observed result from the proposed explanation.

“Cash increased” is an observation. “Cash increased because profitability improved” is a causal claim. The second statement needs evidence beyond a bank balance.

A useful partner narrative follows this structure:

Component Question it answers What to include
Verified change What happened? Amount, period, entity or location, and comparison basis
Supported explanation Why did it happen? A reconciled schedule or documented operational fact
Uncertainty What is not established? Missing evidence, unresolved differences, or alternative explanations
Proposed action What should we do? A decision, responsible person, and review point

AI can help organize these elements. It should not fill evidence gaps with confident language.

Build an Approved Financial Evidence Packet

Use a small, controlled set of financial inputs rather than uploading everything available. Each input should have a defined period, accounting basis, entity scope, preparation date, and reviewer.

For a cash-versus-profit explanation, the packet might contain:

Approved input What it can establish What it cannot establish alone
Reconciled bank activity Opening cash, receipts, payments, and closing cash Whether the practice earned a profit
Comparative P&L Revenue, expenses, and profit on the stated accounting basis Why cash changed
Aggregate receivables rollforward How opening receivables, revenue, receipts, and adjustments connect Whether collection timing will repeat
Aggregate receipt-timing schedule Which receipts relate to earlier-period balances Whether current-period services are more profitable
Payables and payroll-liability schedules Expenses incurred but not yet paid Whether all bank cash is available for distributions
Location allocation schedule How shared costs were assigned Whether an apparent location improvement is operational

Reconcile the schedules before using them to support a narrative. The IRS guidance on business recordkeeping supports maintaining complete records, keeping business accounts separate, and reconciling bank records. Those practices provide a sound foundation for financial explanations; they do not validate an AI-generated conclusion.

Keep patient identifiers out of the drafting workflow

Use only approved financial inputs without patient identifiers. Aggregate receivables, payer-category totals, payroll totals, and entity-level balances are generally more relevant to this task than individual patient records.

Do not paste patient names, account numbers, dates of birth, claim-level patient details, or identifiable billing notes into an ordinary Slack conversation or a general-purpose AI tool. Removing names alone may not remove identifying information.

The HHS minimum necessary guidance provides context for limiting protected health information, subject to its scope and exceptions. This workflow avoids patient-identifiable inputs rather than treating a financial drafting tool as approved to receive them. Any workflow involving protected health information needs separate review of the applicable obligations and arrangements.

Illustrative Example: Cash Rose, but Profit Did Not

The following figures are an illustrative example, not a client case study or benchmark.

A single-location practice prepares an accrual-basis monthly P&L. Its owners want to know whether a higher bank balance supports an additional distribution.

The finance team assembles these source schedules:

Source schedule Verified finding in this illustrative example
S1: Reconciled bank activity Cash increased from $120,000 to $160,000: $260,000 of operating receipts less $220,000 of operating payments
S2: Comparative accrual P&L Revenue was $220,000 and expenses were $230,000, producing a $10,000 loss; the prior month also showed a $10,000 loss
S3: Receivables rollforward Opening receivables of $180,000 plus $220,000 of revenue less $260,000 of receipts equaled closing receivables of $140,000, with no other adjustments
S4: Operating-liability rollforward Operating liabilities increased from $50,000 to $60,000 because $230,000 of expenses exceeded $220,000 of payments; the additional $10,000 remained payable at month-end
S5: Aggregate receipt-timing reconciliation A $40,000 delayed payer payment related to earlier-period receivables and was deposited during the current month

For simplicity, this example assumes no financing transactions, owner contributions or distributions, capital purchases, noncash expenses, or other reconciling items. A real practice must include any such items in its cash bridge.

The flawed AI draft

“The practice’s cash balance increased by $40,000 because profitability improved. Stronger collections demonstrate sustainable operating growth and support an additional owner distribution.”

The draft contains one verified observation and several unsupported conclusions:

Draft language Assessment Why it needs correction
“Cash balance increased by $40,000” Supported Agrees with S1
“Because profitability improved” Incorrect S2 shows an unchanged $10,000 monthly loss
“Stronger collections” Incomplete S5 identifies a delayed payment on earlier-period receivables, not proof of a lasting collection improvement
“Sustainable operating growth” Unsupported These schedules do not establish a recurring growth trend
“Support an additional owner distribution” Premature The draft has not evaluated upcoming obligations or the cash outlook

The central mistake is not arithmetic. It is causality: the draft turns a timing-related cash movement into an operating-performance claim.

Reconcile the explanation before rewriting it

The illustrative cash bridge is:

Component Effect on cash
Accrual-basis loss -$10,000
Reduction in receivables +$40,000
Increase in unpaid operating liabilities +$10,000
Net increase in cash +$40,000

The delayed payer payment helps explain the reduction in receivables. The increase in unpaid liabilities also supported cash at month-end, but those obligations still require payment.

This is why neither the bank balance nor the P&L should be interpreted alone.

The corrected partner narrative

“Cash increased by $40,000 during the month, from $120,000 to $160,000 [S1]. This was not an improvement in accrual profitability: the practice recorded a $10,000 loss, unchanged from the prior month [S2]. The cash bridge consists of that loss, a $40,000 reduction in receivables, and a $10,000 increase in unpaid operating liabilities [S3–S4]. A delayed $40,000 payer payment on earlier-period receivables contributed to the month’s receipts [S5]. These results do not establish a recurring improvement in collections or profitability. Management proposes deferring an additional distribution until the finance lead reviews upcoming payroll, payable due dates, and the near-term cash forecast with the owners.”

The bracketed references point to the illustrative internal schedules above, not external research. In an actual narrative, use the practice’s document names, periods, and versions so reviewers can locate the evidence.

Use Causal Language That Matches the Evidence

AI drafts often connect nearby facts with words such as “because,” “driven by,” and “reflecting.” Review those connections as carefully as the amounts.

Use three levels of language:

  • Established: “The payroll register shows that the increase included an additional payroll payment during the reporting period.”
  • Supported but incomplete: “Receipt timing contributed to the cash increase; the remaining difference is still being reconciled.”
  • Unconfirmed: “Management is investigating whether payer processing delays contributed to lower receipts.”

Do not turn an unconfirmed explanation into an established cause merely because it sounds reasonable.

For multi-location practices, check whether the comparison uses the same allocation method. A location’s reported margin may improve because more administrative expense was assigned elsewhere, even if its underlying operations did not change.

For multi-entity owners, distinguish external receipts from transfers between owned entities. An internal transfer can raise one entity’s cash balance without increasing combined cash. A narrative should state whether it describes a single entity, a location, or the combined practice.

Give AI a Bounded Drafting Request

Once the evidence packet is approved, a practical instruction is:

“Using only the approved financial schedules provided, draft an explanation for practice partners. State the verified change, identify the source for each factual claim, distinguish cash movement from accrual profit, and separate established causes from hypotheses. Do not infer recurring improvement from one period. List missing evidence instead of inventing explanations. End with the proposed action supplied by management.”

Supply the proposed action separately from the financial facts. That prevents an owner’s preferred decision—such as approving a distribution—from becoming supposed evidence that cash is available.

If a schedule is missing or does not reconcile, pause the causal explanation. The draft can describe the verified result and the unresolved question without claiming to know why it happened.

Apply a Claim-to-Evidence Checklist

Before partners receive the narrative, review every material claim:

Check Approval question
Amount Does the number agree with the approved source?
Comparison Are periods, accounting basis, and definitions consistent?
Scope Is this the correct location, entity, or combined group?
Cause Does evidence establish the explanation, or only suggest it?
Completeness Have transfers, unpaid obligations, and other reconciling items been considered?
Persistence Is “recurring,” “sustainable,” or “improving” supported by enough evidence?
Uncertainty Are unresolved differences and limitations explicit?
Action Does the proposal reflect upcoming obligations and remaining uncertainty?
Data handling Were only approved inputs without patient identifiers used?

A number can be correct while the sentence around it is misleading. Approval must cover both.

Require Human Approval Before Sharing

Assign clear responsibilities, even when one person performs more than one role:

  1. The preparer verifies the inputs. Reconcile balances, confirm the reporting basis, and record the source versions.
  2. The finance reviewer tests the explanation. Recalculate the bridge, challenge causal statements, and identify omitted factors.
  3. The practice manager confirms operational context. Check whether staffing changes, payment timing, or allocation changes are documented rather than assumed.
  4. The owner or designated partner approves the action. Separate agreement with the facts from authorization to spend, distribute, or change staffing.

Keep the approved narrative with its supporting schedules and approval record. If source figures change, revise the explanation and repeat the relevant checks. A polished draft is not a substitute for this review.

Conclusion

AI is useful for financial narratives when it helps express verified reasoning—not when it supplies a plausible cause for every variance. Start with the partner decision, reconcile the evidence, distinguish observations from explanations, and state uncertainty plainly. Then have people approve both the narrative and the proposed action.

FAQs

Can AI explain why a medical practice’s cash increased?

It can help draft an explanation from approved financial schedules. The explanation still needs human validation against bank activity, the P&L, receivables, liabilities, and any other cash movements. A higher bank balance alone does not establish improved profitability.

What if the practice uses cash-basis financial statements?

Label that basis explicitly. Collections of older receivables can affect cash-basis income, so do not reuse an accrual-based explanation without adjustment. Ask the finance reviewer to distinguish the reported result from underlying operating trends and explain any supporting receivables or unpaid-obligation schedules consistently.

What should partners receive when the cause is still uncertain?

Provide the verified change, the explanation currently supported, the unresolved question, and the person responsible for investigating it. Avoid definitive causal language until the evidence is sufficient. Any proposed action should acknowledge that uncertainty.
