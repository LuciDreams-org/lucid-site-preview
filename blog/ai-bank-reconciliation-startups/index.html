AI Bank Reconciliation Controls for Medical Practices

November 3, 2026 · 10 min read

An AI-generated bank match is a suggestion, not proof that a transaction is correct. For a staffed medical practice, the important question is not simply how many transactions software can match. It is which suggestions staff may accept, what evidence they must check and who approves exceptions before the books close.

A payer deposit can equal several possible receipt batches. A patient-payment settlement can arrive net of fees. A transfer between practice accounts can look like revenue in one account and an expense in another. Accepting a plausible match without checking its source can leave cash reconciled while revenue, expenses or entity balances remain wrong.

This article provides a control framework for AI-assisted matching: delegated authority, an exception-review matrix and a sample month-end approval packet. The goal is accountable human review—not replacing the accountant’s judgment with a confidence score.

Define what accepting a match actually does

Before delegating matching to staff, determine how your accounting workflow handles each action:

  • Match: Links a bank transaction to an existing accounting entry without creating another entry.
  • Create or categorize: Adds a transaction to the ledger, potentially affecting revenue, expenses, assets or liabilities.
  • Adjust: Changes an existing entry or posts a journal entry.
  • Exclude: Removes an imported item from the processing queue without necessarily changing the ledger.

These actions do not carry the same risk. An office manager authorized to match a documented supply payment should not automatically have authority to create a new expense, exclude a bank item or change payroll liabilities.

Test what each button does in the system your practice uses. Do not assume that a label such as “accept” means it only links records.

The IRS guidance on business recordkeeping supports keeping complete business records, separating business accounts and reconciling bank records. AI assistance does not remove those underlying responsibilities.

Why a high-confidence suggestion can still be wrong

Depending on the tool, candidate matching may compare amounts, dates, bank descriptions, reference numbers and prior selections. Those clues help narrow the search, but they do not establish the transaction’s purpose or authorization.

A confidence score may reflect similarity under a vendor’s model rather than a verified probability of correctness. Scores are not necessarily comparable across tools. Repeated prior selections may also reinforce an incorrect pattern.

For a medical practice, common ambiguities include:

  • Several payer receipts with the same amount but different settlement references.
  • Provider and administrative payroll debits occurring near one another.
  • A processor deposit covering multiple days of patient payments, less fees and refunds.
  • Similar vendor names used across locations or legal entities.
  • A downloaded bank transaction appearing again in a manual statement import.

No confidence score should override missing evidence, an account mismatch or an exception that requires accountant approval. A matching score also does not establish that a payment was authorized or that fraud is absent.

Establish review authority before enabling bulk acceptance

Use a written authority schedule approved by the owner and accountant. Define eligible transaction types, evidence requirements, amount limits where appropriate and escalation triggers. Limits should reflect the practice’s risks; there is no universal safe-dollar cutoff.

Role Permitted responsibility Boundary
Preparer or trained practice administrator Gather documents, assess suggestions and accept delegated routine matches No unsupported new entries, exclusions or accounting changes
Accountant or designated accounting reviewer Resolve exceptions, approve accounting treatment and review proposed adjustments Escalate unexplained or potentially unauthorized activity
Owner or authorized manager Approve policy, review unusual activity and authorize close under the agreed process Do not replace missing documentation with verbal reassurance

Whenever feasible, separate preparation from approval. If a small practice cannot separate every task, use compensating review: the owner receives bank statements independently and examines selected accepted matches, all adjustments and unresolved exceptions.

Record approval outside the matching screen if necessary. A controlled checklist or review file can capture the transaction reference, evidence, decision, preparer, reviewer and approval date. Do not assume the software retains all of this automatically.

Exception-review matrix: what staff may accept

The following is a starting policy to tailor with your accountant. “Staff may accept” means the employee has documented delegated authority and has completed the specified source checks.

Suggestion or exception Required source-document checks Review authority and action
Routine one-to-one vendor payment Bank detail, approved invoice or bill, payment reference, amount and correct entity Staff may match an existing entry if every check agrees; otherwise escalate
Payer deposit matching a recorded receipt batch Bank deposit reference, payer remittance or settlement summary, recorded batch total and receiving account Staff may match a fully documented batch; conflicting or multiple candidates require accountant review
Provider or administrative payroll withdrawal Approved payroll register, funding report, bank debit and relevant ledger entries Staff may link under an approved payroll procedure; differences and new entries require accountant review
Suspected duplicate import Statement transaction, import dates and identifiers, existing bank-feed item and ledger posting Hold; accountant or designated reviewer approves exclusion or correction
Transfer between accounts of the same entity Statements for both accounts, transfer reference and both ledger sides Staff may match only under a documented transfer rule; missing sides or proposed income/expense coding go to the accountant
Movement between separate practice entities Sending and receiving records, purpose and existing intercompany entries Accountant determines entity-level treatment; owner approves unusual movements
Net processor settlement Settlement report showing gross receipts, refunds, fees and net deposit; corresponding ledger entries Staff may match an already-reconciled clearing batch under policy; unexplained differences require accountant review
New payee, altered payment instructions or unfamiliar withdrawal Payment authorization, invoice, bank detail and independent verification through a known contact channel Hold and escalate to the owner and accountant; do not rely on the AI suggestion
Old outstanding item, proposed write-off or balancing adjustment Original entry, subsequent bank activity, supporting correspondence and proposed treatment Accountant review and designated approval before posting

A missing source document is an exception even when the amount and description appear correct. Keep the item open until evidence supports the decision.

Design controls for the hardest matching situations

Duplicate imports: distinguish a duplicated record from a second payment

An item appearing twice in an import queue is not necessarily two bank withdrawals. Conversely, two identical amounts may represent two real payments.

Compare bank transaction identifiers where available, statement activity, import coverage and ledger entries. Determine whether the duplication exists only in the feed, in the ledger, or in the actual bank activity. Excluding a duplicate import does not fix a duplicated expense already posted to the books.

Retain the reason for any exclusion. Never delete or exclude a record simply because doing so makes the reconciliation balance.

Transfers: require both sides and the right entity

For a same-entity transfer, trace the withdrawal and deposit across the two accounts. Confirm that neither side has been recorded as operating revenue or expense.

For separate legal entities, do not treat a matching amount as sufficient evidence of an ordinary internal transfer. The accountant must determine the appropriate intercompany, contribution, distribution or other treatment based on the facts. Location labels alone do not establish which entity owns the cash.

Settlement batches: reconcile the components before the deposit

A bank deposit may represent many payer receipts or patient payments rather than one ledger transaction. A processor may also net fees, refunds or other adjustments before sending cash.

Use this evidence chain:

Underlying receipt summary → settlement or remittance report → clearing-account entries → bank deposit.

Match through a documented batch or clearing account rather than forcing the deposit against an unrelated invoice with the same amount. Bank reconciliation establishes agreement of cash records; it does not by itself prove that individual patient balances or payer receivables were posted correctly.

Payroll: check funding components, not just the net amount

Payroll funding can include separate withdrawals for net pay, taxes, benefits or processing fees. A matching bank amount does not prove the provider/admin allocation or liability treatment is correct.

Require the approved funding report and corresponding accounting entries. Route unexplained differences and allocation changes to the accountant rather than allowing staff to create a generic payroll expense to clear the feed.

Sample month-end approval packet

Illustrative example: all amounts, references and transactions below are synthetic. This packet covers one operating account for one practice entity. It is a model for review, not a completed approval or a recommended transaction limit.

1. Account and reconciliation summary

The cover sheet identifies the entity, bank account ending, statement period, ledger account, preparer and reviewer. It confirms that the bank statement covers the complete period and that the ledger opening balance agrees to the prior approved reconciliation.

Reconciliation component Illustrative amount
Statement ending balance $108,500
Less: documented outstanding checks ($8,500)
Adjusted bank balance $100,000
Ledger cash balance before proposed adjustment $100,250
Less: unrecorded bank service charge ($250)
Adjusted ledger balance after approved posting $100,000
Remaining difference $0

The outstanding checks need an itemized schedule with payees, dates, references and subsequent-clearing checks. The service charge needs statement support and approval before posting. A zero difference alone does not establish that the packet is complete.

2. Match and exception decision log

These selected transactions support the review; they are not the complete account activity.

Reference and synthetic transaction Evidence in packet Proposed decision and required signoff
P-101: payer deposit, $18,400 Bank detail and remittance summary agreeing to recorded receipt batch Staff may accept existing batch match after checks; include in reviewer sample
S-102: processor deposit, $12,000 Settlement summary: $12,300 gross receipts less $300 fees; clearing-account detail Accountant approves fee entry if not already recorded; staff then links the supported settlement
W-103: payroll debit, $24,600 Approved funding report and matching ledger entry Staff may match under payroll procedure; reviewer checks supporting totals
T-104: transfer out, $10,000 Both same-entity statements and paired transfer entries Staff may match both sides under transfer policy; no revenue or expense entry
D-105: second imported copy of P-101 Statement shows one deposit; import comparison and ledger review confirm one posting Designated reviewer approves excluding the extra imported record only
F-106: bank service charge, $250 Statement charge with no ledger entry Accountant approves expense entry; preparer posts and reruns reconciliation

If the settlement report were missing or the receiving side of the transfer could not be found, those items would remain open. The preparer should not manufacture entries to reproduce the zero difference shown above.

3. Approval and release record

The packet should contain:

  • The complete bank statement and final reconciliation report.
  • The accepted-match log, linked source references and reviewer’s sample selection.
  • All exception decisions, including exclusions and proposed adjustments.
  • Evidence that approved entries were posted and the reconciliation rerun.
  • An outstanding-item schedule with responsible owners and follow-up dates.
  • Named preparer and reviewer approvals, plus owner approval where policy requires it.

Use explicit statuses such as prepared, pending evidence, accountant reviewed and approved for close. Record who approved each decision and when. If unresolved items remain, the accountant should document their effect and whether close can proceed; do not quietly label them resolved.

Monitor accepted matches, not just exceptions

A queue with few exceptions may indicate effective matching—or overly permissive rules. Review a sample of accepted matches and all higher-risk changes, including new payees, exclusions, adjustments and cross-entity movements.

Track missing documentation, overturned matches, recurring exceptions and items carried across close periods. Repeated errors should lead to narrower delegation or revised matching rules. Apply review before expanding bulk acceptance to another account or location.

Protect the information used in this process. Financial evidence can contain patient-identifiable information. Prefer settlement-level summaries and transaction references where sufficient, and use an appropriate approved channel when sensitive detail is necessary. HHS minimum-necessary guidance describes the standard and its exceptions; the practice should determine its applicable obligations. Do not put patient-identifiable records into ordinary Slack messages or general-purpose AI prompts.

FAQs

Can staff accept a high-confidence AI match without opening the supporting documents?

Not under the control framework described here. Staff should verify the required source evidence and confirm that the suggestion falls within their delegated authority. A confidence score does not prove authorization, correct entity treatment or an accurate receipt batch.

Does every suggested match need accountant approval?

No. A written policy can delegate routine matches to trained staff when the source records agree and no accounting change is needed. Exceptions, unsupported exclusions, new entries and ambiguous entity treatment should follow the accountant-review process. The reviewer should also sample accepted routine matches.

What if a settlement deposit does not equal any individual ledger entry?

Check whether the deposit represents a batch net of fees, refunds or adjustments. Obtain the settlement report and reconcile its components to the ledger or clearing account. Do not force a match to an unrelated entry or post an unexplained difference merely to clear the bank feed.

Is a zero reconciliation difference enough to approve month-end?

No. The reviewer also needs support for matches, outstanding items, exclusions and adjustments. Incorrect classifications or offsetting errors can exist even when cash balances agree. Approval should address both the reconciliation arithmetic and the evidence behind it.
