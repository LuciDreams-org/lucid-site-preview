5 Uses of AI in Medical Practice Cash Flow Sensitivity Analysis

November 3, 2026 · 9 min read

Before approving equipment, office improvements, or another discretionary purchase, a private medical practice owner needs to know which uncertain cash assumption could change the decision. Is it the arrival of payer receipts, the size of the next payroll, or a vendor payment falling earlier than expected?

Cash flow sensitivity analysis changes one input at a time to show how much a cash outcome moves. Its purpose is not to predict the future more confidently. It is to identify which assumption deserves attention before money is committed.

AI can assist with five parts of that work: proposing test ranges, recalculating cash outcomes, comparing driver influence, visualizing payment-date exposure, and documenting reviewer questions. A checked spreadsheet or other calculation model should remain the numerical source of truth. AI-generated arithmetic, formulas, and explanations require review.

Set Up the Spending Decision First

Start with a specific question: “Can this entity pay for the proposed purchase while preserving the cash needed for existing obligations?” Define the purchase amount, payment date, analysis period, and owner-approved cash floor before testing inputs.

The floor is a management decision, not a universal benchmark. It should reflect the practice’s obligations and access to usable cash. A positive ending balance alone does not establish that spending is affordable.

Build the baseline from:

  • Reconciled beginning cash, excluding funds unavailable for the decision.
  • Expected payer receipts and patient balance collections, grouped without patient-identifying detail.
  • Provider and administrative payroll cash requirements, including related payments when due.
  • Vendor invoices, rent, debt payments, and other scheduled outflows.
  • The proposed discretionary payment, shown separately from existing commitments.

Use cash receipts rather than charges or accrual revenue. A per-location P&L helps explain operating performance, but it does not establish when money reaches an entity’s bank account. For multi-entity owners, test cash where the payment obligation sits; do not assume another entity’s balance is available.

The IRS guidance on business recordkeeping supports maintaining complete records, separate business accounts, and bank reconciliation. Those records provide a sound starting point; they do not verify future receipt dates.

One-variable sensitivity versus a multi-variable scenario

A one-variable sensitivity test changes one assumption while holding the others constant. For example, move a receipt to a later week without changing its amount, payroll, or vendor payments.

A multi-variable scenario changes several assumptions together, such as delayed receipts plus higher payroll. It answers a different question: what happens under a combined set of conditions?

Start with one-variable tests to isolate influence. Otherwise, a lower cash balance may be difficult to attribute to a particular input. A combined scenario can be a subsequent check, but it should not replace the initial ranking of assumptions.

1. Propose Test Ranges for Uncertain Inputs

AI can help turn a broad concern into a defined sensitivity test. Give it an approved baseline and ask it to identify uncertain inputs, suggest candidate ranges, and state what evidence would justify each range.

Do not ask it to invent “typical medical practice” collection delays or payroll increases. A useful range should come from the practice’s own records, known commitments, or a clearly labeled management assumption.

Input Evidence to examine Potential test structure
Collection timing Recent deposit timing and the basis for expected payer receipts Move a defined receipt batch to a later date, keeping its amount unchanged
Payroll cash cost Payroll register, staffing schedule, approved compensation changes, and employer-related payments Increase the defined payroll cash amount while preserving payment dates
Vendor payment date Invoice terms, scheduled payments, and vendor correspondence Move an existing payment earlier without changing its amount

Have a manager or finance reviewer approve each range. If an expected deposit has no reliable timing support, document that gap rather than allowing AI to assign a probability.

Keep timing and amount tests separate. Receiving the same money later is different from never collecting part of it. Combining those changes under “slower collections” obscures what is driving the result.

2. Recalculate Cash Outcomes Under Each Assumption

AI can draft formulas or calculation code, but the reviewer must verify the resulting model. Every period should follow the same basic relationship:

Ending cash = beginning cash + cash receipts − cash payments.

The next period begins with the previous period’s ending balance. A timing change must move cash between periods—not accidentally duplicate or delete it.

Illustrative example: a discretionary purchase

All amounts, dates, and limits below are invented assumptions for illustration, not practice benchmarks. Figures are in thousands of dollars.

Assume one practice entity has $100,000 of beginning available cash and is considering a $15,000 purchase paid in Week 1. For this simplified screening example, the owner sets a $30,000 minimum week-ending balance. An actual approval review would also examine daily payment dates.

Illustrative baseline, before proposed purchase Week 1 Week 2 Week 3 Week 4
Beginning cash $100 $60 $80 $50
Collections $20 $60 $30 $70
Payroll cash payments $50 $0 $50 $0
Vendor payments $0 $30 $0 $20
Other cash payments $10 $10 $10 $10
Ending cash $60 $80 $50 $90

The lowest baseline week-ending balance is $50,000. Paying for the purchase in Week 1 lowers every subsequent balance by $15,000, leaving a minimum of $35,000.

Now change one assumption at a time. Each row below is a separate test against the same baseline.

Illustrative test Changed assumption only Lowest week-ending cash before purchase Lowest week-ending cash after purchase Week 4 cash after purchase
Baseline No change $50,000 $35,000 $75,000
Collection timing Move $20,000 of Week 2 collections to Week 4 $30,000 $15,000 $75,000
Payroll cost Increase each $50,000 payroll payment by 10%, to $55,000 $40,000 $25,000 $65,000
Vendor payment date Move the $20,000 Week 4 vendor payment to Week 3 $30,000 $15,000 $75,000

The baseline passes the illustrative week-ending floor, but each tested change causes the proposed purchase to breach it. The timing tests leave Week 4 cash unchanged, demonstrating why the final balance alone is insufficient.

3. Compare Which Driver Most Affects the Decision

AI can help organize checked results into a ranked comparison. Ask it to report the change from baseline, identify threshold crossings, and explain the distinction between temporary timing pressure and an increase in total cash cost.

In the illustrative example:

  • The collection-timing test reduces minimum week-ending cash by $20,000.
  • The payroll-cost test reduces it by $10,000.
  • The vendor-date test reduces it by $20,000.

Collection timing and the vendor date therefore have the largest effect within these selected tests. That does not establish that either is more likely to occur. Nor does it prove that payroll is inherently less important: the ranking depends on the size of each tested change.

The next action is to investigate assumptions that could change approval. Confirm the expected receipt timing and vendor due date first, while also resolving the payroll estimate because its tested increase breaches the floor.

Avoid ranking unlike inputs by percentage alone. A percentage change in payroll and a delay measured in days are not directly comparable. Compare their effects on the same decision measures: minimum cash, its date, and headroom above the approved floor.

4. Visualize Exposure Around Payment Dates

AI can help format a cash calendar or chart from reviewed calculations. The most useful view is often not a polished monthly graph, but a dated sequence showing receipts, payroll, vendor payments, and the proposed purchase.

Weekly summaries can hide an intraperiod shortage. A Friday deposit does not fund a payroll withdrawal that occurred earlier in the week. For dates near the cash floor, use daily balances and confirm the ordering of material transactions.

A practical review view should include:

Display element Decision it supports
Baseline and one sensitivity line at a time Isolates the effect of the changed input
Owner-approved cash floor Shows when available cash falls below the limit
Payroll and major vendor payment dates Identifies obligations creating the low point
Proposed purchase date Shows whether its timing overlaps the cash trough
Lowest balance and date Directs attention to the specific exposure window

Do not smooth away low points or rely only on month-end totals. If timing drives the result, the owner can evaluate deferring discretionary spending until the receipt arrives. Any vendor-date change should reflect actual terms or an agreed arrangement, not an assumption that required payments can simply be delayed.

5. Document Questions for the Human Reviewer

AI can draft a short review memo from the approved assumptions and checked results. Ask it to separate established facts, management assumptions, and unresolved questions. It should not fill evidence gaps with plausible explanations.

Reviewer question Supporting information Responsible reviewer
Why is this receipt expected on this date? Aggregate receipt schedule and supporting collection information Practice manager or revenue-cycle lead
What is included in payroll cash cost? Payroll register, staffing assumptions, and related payment schedule Payroll lead and finance reviewer
Can this vendor payment occur earlier than modeled? Invoice terms and payment authorization Accounts payable owner
Is beginning cash available to this entity? Reconciled accounts and identified commitments Bookkeeper or CPA
What must be resolved before purchase approval? Sensitivity results and owner-approved cash floor Practice owner

Use aggregate financial information wherever possible. Patient names, diagnoses, account identifiers, and clinical details are generally unnecessary for this analysis. Do not place patient-identifiable information in ordinary Slack messages or general-purpose AI prompts. HHS provides guidance on the minimum necessary standard and its exceptions; practices should confirm their particular obligations and approved workflows.

Check the Analysis Before Approving Spending

A finance reviewer should complete these checks before the owner relies on the result:

  1. Reconcile starting cash. Confirm account balances and identify cash unavailable for spending.
  2. Recalculate independently. Check every period’s arithmetic and roll-forward in the controlled model.
  3. Verify the changed input. Ensure each sensitivity changes only its named assumption.
  4. Check timing conservation. A receipt or payment moved within the period should leave total receipts or payments unchanged.
  5. Check payroll scope. Distinguish wages from the full cash requirement and avoid counting related payments twice.
  6. Inspect daily lows. Where balances approach the floor, verify actual payment and deposit sequencing.
  7. Record the decision condition. State whether approval depends on a receipt arriving, an estimate being confirmed, or the purchase being deferred.

For multiple locations, connect operating assumptions to the entity that receives and pays cash. An allocated shared cost on a location P&L is not necessarily a separate bank payment; avoid counting both the allocation and the underlying payment.

FAQs

Does AI make medical practice cash flow sensitivity analysis more accurate?

Not automatically. AI can help propose tests, draft calculations, and summarize results, but it can also introduce arithmetic errors or unsupported assumptions. Reliability depends on reconciled inputs, suitable ranges, and independent review. Sensitivity analysis shows what changes under an assumption; it does not establish how likely that assumption is.

Which cash assumption should a practice owner investigate first?

Prioritize an uncertain input whose tested change materially reduces minimum cash or crosses the approved floor. Consider both its influence and the available evidence. In the illustrative example, collection timing and the vendor payment date have the largest tested effects, but payroll also needs attention because its test would change the spending decision.

Is one-variable sensitivity analysis enough to approve a purchase?

Not necessarily. It helps identify influential inputs, but several unfavorable changes can occur together. After reviewing individual drivers, consider relevant combined conditions and daily cash sequencing. Approval should also account for existing obligations and the owner’s cash policy, rather than relying on a single favorable baseline.
