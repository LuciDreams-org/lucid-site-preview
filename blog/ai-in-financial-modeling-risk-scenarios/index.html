AI Financial Risk Models for Medical Practices: Testing Dependencies

November 3, 2026 · 10 min read

A financial model can calculate each assumption correctly and still give a practice owner the wrong picture of risk. The problem often sits between the assumptions: delayed payer receipts increase borrowing, payroll continues on schedule, and interest reduces the cash available for the next payment cycle.

If an AI-assisted model changes collection timing, payroll and borrowing costs independently, it can miss that chain. A polished summary does not prove that the underlying relationships work.

For owners and managers of staffed private medical practices, the review question is practical: Does the model show how one financial problem creates another—and where the practice runs out of room to respond?

This article walks through a hypothetical practice’s dependency map, assumption register and model-review worksheet. Every numeric input and output below is an illustrative example, not an industry benchmark, lending quote or prediction.

Start With the Financial Decision

Suppose a practice owner wants to know whether existing liquidity can support committed provider and administrative payroll if payer receipts arrive late while borrowing becomes more expensive.

The model should answer three questions:

  • How much cash remains before new financing?
  • How much additional borrowing is needed to preserve the owner’s cash floor?
  • Is that borrowing actually available to the entity making payroll?

These are linked questions. A model that reports only monthly profit, or only a final cash balance after an unrestricted financing plug, does not answer them.

Keep the distinction between earnings and cash timing explicit. A receipt that moves into a later month is not necessarily lost revenue. The immediate issue may be liquidity even when the modeled services and recognized revenue are unchanged. Your CPA should confirm the accounting treatment separately from the cash-flow scenario.

Build a Dependency Map Before Reviewing Outputs

A dependency map identifies the direction of each relationship. It also shows which costs cannot reasonably adjust during the modeled period.

Starting condition Direct effect Next linked effect What the reviewer should inspect
Payer receipts arrive later Current-period cash collections fall Borrowing need increases Does the delayed amount appear in a later period rather than disappear?
Provider and administrative payroll is committed Payroll cash outflow stays on schedule Less cash is available to absorb delayed receipts Does the model incorrectly reduce payroll when collections fall?
Borrowing increases Interest expense and cash payments increase Additional funding may be needed Is interest calculated on the relevant balance and timing?
The borrowing rate rises Each borrowed dollar costs more The cash shortfall grows when a draw is needed Does the higher rate flow through the borrowing calculation?
Credit capacity is reached Further financing is unavailable A cash-floor breach remains unresolved Does the model flag the gap instead of assuming unlimited credit?
Delayed receipts eventually arrive Cash increases in the later period Debt may be repaid, subject to other obligations Does the model avoid counting the same receipt twice?

The most important distinction is between a driver and a result. A collection delay is a driver. A line-of-credit draw is usually a result of that delay, other cash flows and the owner’s liquidity policy. Entering both as unrelated assumptions can break the model’s logic.

For a multi-location or multi-entity practice, draw this map at the level where cash and obligations actually sit. A location-level P&L does not establish that one entity’s bank balance or credit facility is available to another. Any assumed transfer should be identified and reviewed rather than treated as automatic funding.

Record the Assumptions and Their Limits

An assumption register explains what changes, what stays fixed and what evidence would support using the assumption in a real decision. It is especially useful when AI has helped draft formulas or scenario descriptions.

The register makes omissions visible. For example, an owner distribution cannot remain outside the model if it is expected to occur during the period under review.

The inputs should start with reliable records. IRS Publication 583 discusses complete business records, separate business accounts and bank reconciliation. Those records support the opening balances; they do not validate a scenario’s assumptions about future collections.

Calculate the Linked Cash and Borrowing Effects

In this illustrative example, base cash before new financing is:

$80,000 opening cash + $300,000 receipts − $210,000 payroll − $70,000 other payments = $100,000.

When $90,000 of receipts shifts into the following month, cash before financing falls to $10,000. Payroll does not fall just because collections are late. The practice needs financing to preserve its $40,000 cash floor.

But borrowing exactly $30,000 is insufficient if interest on that draw is paid during the same month. Interest creates another cash outflow.

For this simplified example:

  • Ending cash = cash before financing + new draw − interest.
  • Interest = new draw × monthly interest rate.
  • Required draw = cash-floor shortfall ÷ (1 − monthly interest rate).

That last formula resolves the dependency between the draw and its interest cost. It applies only to this example’s timing assumptions, zero opening debt and absence of fees. Actual borrowing may require daily balances, different interest-payment dates or other calculations.

The higher rate alone has no effect here because the practice has no opening debt and needs no base-case draw. Once receipts are delayed, the rate matters. The effect of a risk depends on the financial conditions around it.

The combined result also reveals how financing can conceal pressure: ending cash meets the floor, but little unused credit remains. A report showing only ending cash would miss that deterioration.

Finally, a monthly ending balance does not establish that every payroll date is funded. The reviewer must inspect receipt and payment dates to identify any earlier cash shortfall.

Use a Model-Review Worksheet to Challenge the Links

Ask the model preparer to show formulas or calculation steps, not just an AI-generated explanation. The narrative and the underlying calculation must agree.

A useful boundary test is to reduce available credit. Under the combined-risk assumptions, if only $25,000 were available, ending cash would be $34,625 after $375 of interest. The model should report a $5,375 shortfall against the cash floor, not manufacture another source of funds. These amounts are also illustrative.

Understand Correlation Without Assuming Causation

Correlation means two variables tend to move together. It does not establish why they move together or prove that one causes the other.

A practice may observe that slower collections and higher administrative overtime occur in the same periods. Both might reflect a shared operational problem. Alternatively, the pattern could be temporary or based on too little history.

Treat three relationships differently:

  • Mechanical dependencies: A larger credit draw produces more interest under the same terms. Encode the formula directly.
  • Observed associations: Two measures move together in practice records. Investigate timing, explanations and exceptions before relying on the relationship.
  • Deliberate combined stresses: Two risks are tested together because the owner wants to understand the exposure, even without evidence that they commonly coincide.

The example above uses the third approach for collection delays and higher rates. It does not claim that collection delays cause interest rates to rise. Once combined, however, they interact through borrowing.

Without evidence supporting scenario probabilities, label outcomes as conditional tests—not as predictions or percentile estimates.

Prevent Double-Counting and False Confidence

Double-counting can make a model look more conservative while actually making it less interpretable. Check for these errors:

  • Timing loss counted as permanent loss: Delayed receipts are removed from the current month and never restored, despite an assumption that they will be collected later.
  • Payroll counted twice: Payroll-related cash costs appear in both a payroll total and a separate expense line.
  • Borrowing entered twice: A manual emergency draw is added on top of a formula-generated financing requirement.
  • Shared costs duplicated: A central administrative cost is allocated to locations and then added again to the consolidated total.
  • Interest duplicated: Interest is included in operating payments and calculated again in the financing section.

Uncertainty also needs a visible place. The delayed amount, arrival date and credit availability may all be uncertain. A model can calculate a precise draw from uncertain inputs without making those inputs more reliable.

Ask which unresolved assumption would change the owner’s decision. If a small additional delay would exhaust credit capacity, confirming collection timing and lender availability matters more than refining immaterial expense estimates.

Define AI’s Role and the CPA Review

AI assistance can be used to draft a dependency map, suggest missing links or explain formulas. Those drafts still need validation against practice records and actual financing terms. Do not assume the tool understands contractual payroll commitments, entity restrictions or the difference between a delayed receipt and an uncollectible balance.

Before using the output for a decision, have the CPA or finance reviewer confirm:

  • Opening cash, debt and payroll totals reconcile to supporting records.
  • Cash timing and accounting revenue are not confused.
  • Financing formulas reflect actual terms, availability and payment dates.
  • Shared costs and entity-level balances are handled consistently.
  • Scenario assumptions, exclusions and unresolved questions are documented.

Use aggregate financial information where practical. Do not place patient-identifiable information into ordinary Slack messages or general-purpose AI tools to explain a collection scenario.

FAQs

What is a dependency in a medical practice financial risk model?

A dependency is a relationship in which one input or result affects another. Delayed payer receipts can increase borrowing needs; the resulting borrowing can increase interest payments. A useful model carries those effects through rather than changing each assumption in isolation.

Does combining risks mean they are likely to happen together?

No. A combined-risk test can examine exposure without assigning a probability. State whether the combination reflects an observed relationship, a mechanical dependency or a deliberate stress assumption. Do not treat a hypothetical combination as evidence of likelihood.

Why can ending cash look acceptable while liquidity risk increases?

New borrowing can restore ending cash while using most of the available credit. Month-end totals can also hide a shortfall before a payroll date. Review cash before financing, unused credit, interest and dated cash movements alongside ending cash.

What should an owner request before relying on an AI-assisted model?

Request the dependency map, assumption register, reproducible calculations and combined-risk outputs. Have the CPA or finance reviewer verify the underlying records, financing terms, entity-level funding assumptions and unresolved limitations before the model supports a borrowing or spending decision.
