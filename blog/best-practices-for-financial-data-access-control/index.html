Designing Financial Access Controls for Medical Practices

November 3, 2026 · 11 min read

When an office manager joins your practice, should they be able to enter invoices, change vendor banking details and release payments? When an outside accountant needs financial statements, do they also need access to payroll records or bank transfers?

These are financial-design decisions, not just software setup questions. Giving everyone broad access may keep work moving, but it can also let one person change payment instructions, send money and conceal the transaction. Overly narrow permissions create a different problem: payroll, vendor payments and month-end reporting can stall when the owner is unavailable.

Start by defining what each role needs to do, then have qualified IT advisers validate how your systems enforce those boundaries. The goal is a usable permission structure for a staffed private medical practice—not a claim that any particular configuration ensures compliance or prevents every loss.

Start With Financial Workflows, Not Job Titles

Least privilege means giving a person only the access needed for their assigned work, within the relevant systems, entities and locations. A job title alone does not establish that scope.

An office manager may need to submit approved administrative payroll changes but not view every provider’s compensation agreement. A location manager may need their site’s profit and loss statement without access to other locations’ bank accounts. An outside bookkeeper may need bank statements and transaction-entry rights without authority to release payments.

Before creating accounts, list the systems and tasks involved:

Financial area Work that needs an owner Permission boundary to define
Banking and payment services View receipts, prepare payments, release funds Separate viewing and preparation from payment release
Accounting Enter transactions, reconcile accounts, prepare statements Distinguish routine entry from adjustments, deletion and administration
Vendor records Add vendors or change payment instructions Separate proposed changes from verification and approval
Payroll Maintain employee records, prepare payroll, authorize funding Separate preparation from approval, especially for the preparer’s own pay
Reporting Review location P&Ls, entity statements and shared costs Define both viewing scope and export rights
Patient-balance and payer reporting Supply financial information for reconciliation Use summarized information where sufficient; avoid unnecessary patient detail

Include connected applications, shared folders and adviser portals in this inventory. Access to a downloaded payroll file can be just as consequential as access to the payroll system itself.

The IRS guide to starting a business and keeping records supports complete business records, separate business accounts and bank reconciliation. Your permission design should allow those tasks to happen without giving every participant authority to move money.

Build a Role-to-Permission Matrix

Use a written matrix as the approved design before anyone configures access. Record the actual person assigned to each role separately so staff turnover does not require redesigning the entire policy.

The following is an illustrative role design, not a universal staffing model or a description of any vendor’s available settings.

Role Viewing Transaction entry Vendor changes Payment release Payroll administration Reporting exports
Practice owner or designated financial approver Practice-wide financial information as needed Normally delegated Approves sensitive changes after independent verification Authorized release within documented authority Approves payroll and compensation changes Approved practice-wide reports
Office manager or AP preparer Assigned operating accounts and invoices Enters bills and prepares payment batches Proposes changes; does not independently approve them No, unless a documented exception applies Submits approved staff changes if assigned Limited operating reports
Internal or outside bookkeeper Assigned entity records and statements Posts routine entries and reconciles accounts; material adjustments follow approval policy Only if specifically assigned, with separate verification Normally none Summary payroll entries unless detailed administration is assigned Accounting reports within engagement scope

A person may hold more than one role, but each combined permission needs an explicit decision. Do not let software defaults silently turn a bookkeeper into a payment approver or a location manager into a practice-wide administrator.

Add Scope and Restrictions to Every Role

The matrix should be accompanied by a short access record containing:

  • System and account: Name the bank, accounting file, payroll environment or reporting folder.
  • Entity and location: Specify which legal entities and locations are included.
  • Action: Distinguish viewing, entry, editing, deletion, approval, release and export.
  • Sensitive information: Identify whether compensation details or patient-related information are necessary.
  • Approver and implementer: State who authorizes access and who configures it.
  • Duration: Give temporary assignments an end date.
  • Backup coverage: Identify an authorized substitute rather than sharing credentials.

Treat user administration separately from financial processing. Someone who can create users or expand their own permissions may be able to bypass the intended division of duties. Administrative access therefore needs its own approval and oversight.

Design Boundaries Around Money Movement and Exports

Separate Vendor Changes From Payment Release

A payment approval is weaker if the approver never sees that the vendor’s destination account changed. Establish a process for verifying new or changed payment instructions through a trusted contact method already on file—not solely through contact details supplied in the change request.

The payment approver should receive enough information to review the payee, purpose, amount and relevant destination changes. Set additional approval requirements according to the practice’s risks and operating needs rather than adopting an arbitrary universal dollar threshold.

Separate Payroll Preparation From Approval

Payroll administration can expose compensation, tax and banking information. Assign access according to the work performed, not general managerial seniority.

The payroll preparer can assemble the proposed run and explain changes. A separate authorized reviewer should examine new employees, compensation changes, unusual adjustments and funding before submission where the workflow permits. A preparer should not be the sole reviewer of changes to their own compensation or payment instructions.

Treat Exports as a Separate Permission

View-only access does not necessarily mean a person cannot download information. Confirm actual behavior with your IT adviser and system provider.

For a multi-location practice, a manager may need a location P&L but not a detailed file containing practice-wide wages. Shared-cost allocations can often be reviewed through allocation schedules rather than unrestricted access to every source account. For multi-entity owners, distinguish consolidated reporting from access to each entity’s underlying records and bank accounts.

Use aggregate payer receipts and patient-balance summaries when they answer the financial question. Where patient information is involved, obtain appropriate privacy guidance. HHS explains the minimum necessary standard and its exceptions; it should not be reduced to a blanket rule that every financial workflow has identical requirements.

Use an Approval Workflow for Joining, Changing Roles and Leaving

Initial permission architecture defines the available roles. A change-approval process determines who may receive those roles and when. Neither should depend on an informal message saying, “Give them the same access as the last manager.”

Illustrative Onboarding Workflow

Consider a new office manager responsible for vendor invoices and operating reports, but not payment release or payroll administration.

Step Responsible party Required decision or evidence
Define the assignment Hiring manager List tasks, locations, entities and required systems
Select permissions Finance lead Match tasks to the approved matrix and identify any exceptions
Approve access Owner or authorized approver Approve the scope, start date and any temporary access
Configure accounts Authorized system administrator Create named access and apply the approved restrictions
Validate the result IT adviser with finance lead Test permitted tasks and important prohibited actions
Confirm readiness Manager and new user Confirm instructions, escalation contacts and backup coverage
Close the request Access coordinator Record what was granted, by whom and when

Testing should include negative checks: can the new manager unexpectedly release a payment, see another location’s payroll or grant someone else access? If a system cannot enforce the intended restriction, document that limitation before accepting the setup.

Handle Role Changes as Replacements, Not Additions

When an employee moves from payroll support to location management, reassess their full permission set. Coordinate removal of obsolete rights with the new assignment instead of simply adding access.

Apply the same approval process to outside advisers whose scope changes. Tax preparation, bookkeeping and CFO analysis do not automatically require identical permissions. Any temporary overlap should have a stated purpose, reviewer and end date.

Illustrative Offboarding Workflow

Before the departure takes effect, identify outstanding payroll runs, scheduled payments, reconciliations and reports. Assign replacement owners so removing access does not interrupt essential work.

At the approved separation time:

  • Disable the person’s access across financial systems and related reporting locations.
  • Remove payment authority and administrator privileges separately where required.
  • Have qualified IT personnel address active sessions, connected applications and recovery methods as applicable.
  • Transfer ownership of recurring tasks, scheduled reports and approval responsibilities.
  • Address practice information held in authorized work locations or devices through the practice’s established process.
  • Preserve business records and available activity history; disabling access should not mean deleting necessary accounting evidence.
  • Confirm completion with the responsible system owners, including banks and outside advisers where relevant.

Do not assume disabling one login removes access everywhere. Record confirmation for each system and any unresolved item requiring follow-up.

Create a Small-Team Exception Process

A small practice may not have enough staff to separate every task. An exception should be a documented risk decision, not an undocumented permanent shortcut.

For example, an office manager might both enter invoices and maintain vendor records because no other administrator is available. The owner could retain payment release, independently verify changed payment instructions and review supporting documents before releasing funds. An outside bookkeeper could reconcile the account without payment authority.

If complete separation remains impossible, document:

  1. The operational reason and the conflicting permissions.
  2. The specific accounts, entities and tasks affected.
  3. The owner approving the exception and its end or reassessment date.
  4. The compensating review, including reviewer, evidence and timing.
  5. What happens if that review cannot be completed.

A compensating review must examine underlying evidence, not merely ask whether everything looks correct. Depending on the exception, that could mean reviewing bank activity directly, comparing released payments with invoices, inspecting vendor changes or reviewing payroll changes against approved instructions.

A later reconciliation can help detect problems, but it does not replace pre-release approval or guarantee recovery of funds. Keep preventive approval wherever practical.

Know What Permission Design Does—and Does Not—Cover

Financial access controls depend on several related processes, but they are not interchangeable.

Process Question it answers
Initial permission architecture What should each role be allowed to do?
Access-change approval Should this person receive these permissions now?
Periodic access recertification Does existing access still match current responsibilities?
Identity authentication Is the person signing in who they claim to be?
Activity monitoring or AI-assisted access review Does actual access or activity warrant investigation?

Choose a recurring access-review schedule based on risk, and review access when responsibilities change. Recertification does not replace prompt onboarding, role-change or departure actions.

Authentication controls, including multifactor authentication where appropriate, also do not determine whether a verified user should be allowed to release payments. Have qualified IT advisers validate authentication, technical enforcement and system limitations without assuming that one platform’s permissions carry over to another.

If evaluating an AI access-audit tool, ask what systems it actually covers, what evidence it uses and who approves its recommendations. Do not treat a generated finding as authorization to expand access or as proof of compliance. Do not upload patient-identifiable information to a general-purpose AI tool or ordinary Slack conversation.

Turn the Design Into an Operating Standard

The owner’s deliverable is a manageable set of records: a system inventory, approved role matrix, named-user assignments, change requests and documented exceptions. Finance defines the business boundaries; authorized administrators implement them; qualified IT advisers validate technical enforcement.

Lucid Financials provides CPA-led bookkeeping, tax and CFO services for US private medical practices, combining AI-assisted financial work with a human finance team. Its role can include helping clarify accounting responsibilities and the reporting needs of location and entity owners. That financial work should remain distinct from technical access administration. Slack support should not be treated as permission to share patient-identifiable information.

A practical design lets staff complete necessary work while keeping sensitive changes and money movement subject to deliberate approval. Start with the next hire or role change, then apply the same structure to existing access.

FAQs

Should an outside bookkeeper have bank-payment authority?

Not automatically. Bookkeeping often requires statements, transaction details and reconciliation access rather than authority to send money. Define the engagement first. If payment preparation is included, distinguish it from release authority and document who reviews and approves payments.

What if our financial system cannot separate the permissions we want?

Ask the provider and a qualified IT adviser to confirm the limitation. Consider a narrower role, a different workflow or a report instead of direct access. If broader access is unavoidable, require an owner-approved exception with a specific compensating review and reassessment date. Do not assume a written policy creates a technical restriction.

Does a location manager need access to every entity’s books?

Usually the starting point should be the information needed to manage that location. A location P&L and shared-cost schedule may be sufficient. Broader access should follow a documented responsibility, not simply the availability of a consolidated dashboard.

Is an access review enough when someone leaves?

No. Departure requires a coordinated removal process at the approved time, including relevant banking, payroll, accounting, reporting and administrative access. A later periodic review is a backstop, not a substitute for offboarding.
