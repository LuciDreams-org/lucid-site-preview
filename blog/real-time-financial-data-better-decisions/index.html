When Current Financial Data Is Decision-Ready for Medical Practices

November 3, 2026 · 9 min read

A current bank balance can help a medical practice owner answer “What is in the account?” It cannot, by itself, answer “What can we safely spend?” Payroll may be scheduled but not withdrawn. Vendor invoices may be waiting for entry. A payer deposit may still need investigation before the practice treats it as available operating cash.

Real-time financial information is useful when its limitations are clear. The goal is not to accelerate every decision. It is to match each decision to the evidence it needs.

A routine, verified bill may be payable before the monthly books close. An equipment purchase requires a forward cash view. An owner distribution usually needs stronger support, including reliable entity-level balances and review of unresolved accounting issues.

Four Types of Financial Information—and What Each Can Tell You

“Current” describes timing, not completeness. Before approving a payment, identify which kind of information you are using.

Information type What it shows What it does not establish
Bank activity Posted deposits, withdrawals and the bank’s reported available balance All outstanding payments, unpaid invoices, accrued obligations or correct accounting classifications
Posted accounting entries Transactions entered in the general ledger through a stated date That every transaction is included, correctly classified or reconciled
Estimated accruals Estimates of obligations or activity not yet fully recorded, such as earned provider compensation or an unbilled service cost Final amounts; estimates need documented assumptions and later adjustment
Closed-period results Financial statements after defined reconciliation, adjustment and review procedures Today’s liquidity, future collections or assurance that no later correction will be needed

These layers complement one another. A closed monthly profit and loss statement helps evaluate operating performance. Today’s verified cash position and upcoming obligations help determine whether a payment can be released.

The accounting basis matters, too. A cash-basis P&L may not include unpaid bills. Even when financial statements use the cash basis, the owner still needs an obligations schedule for spending decisions. Do not assume that an expense missing from the P&L represents money available to spend.

The IRS discusses complete business records, separate business accounts and bank reconciliation in Publication 583. Those recordkeeping practices provide a foundation; they do not make a current balance sufficient evidence for every decision.

A Decision-Readiness Matrix for Medical Practices

Use the following as a working approval framework, not a mandatory reporting schedule. Freshness requirements should reflect payment timing, the practice’s cash volatility and the consequences of being wrong.

Decision Required checks Appropriate data freshness Unresolved items that should limit approval
Pay a routine vendor invoice Verify invoice, service or goods received, due date, payment details and whether it was already paid; check cash after scheduled obligations Current invoice and payment records; bank position checked before release Duplicate invoice, changed payment instructions, disputed charge or unexplained cash difference
Release provider and administrative payroll funding Confirm approved payroll totals, related employer costs, funding dates and other withdrawals due before the next dependable receipts Final payroll funding information for that run and a current cash check Missing compensation adjustments, uncertain funding amount or insufficient verified cash
Approve or defer equipment Confirm full cash commitment, deposit and installment dates, financing terms if applicable, and impact on operating reserves Current quote, latest closed results and an updated forward cash forecast Unrecorded obligations, uncertain collections or unclear costs beyond the purchase price
Approve an owner distribution Review reconciled entity-level cash, liabilities, tax estimates, reserves, governing documents and applicable restrictions with advisers Latest reliable closed period, updated through the proposed payment date for material activity Unreconciled cash, uncertain liabilities, unresolved owner-account entries or questions requiring CPA or legal review
Commit to ongoing staffing expense Evaluate recurring compensation costs, recent normalized performance and downside cash scenarios Latest closed results plus current staffing costs and collection assumptions Incomplete provider compensation, temporary receipt spikes or unclear shared-cost allocations
Transfer money between practice entities Verify which entity owns the cash, the purpose, accounting treatment and effect on each entity’s obligations Current balances and reconciled intercompany records Unmatched intercompany amounts, uncertain treatment or restrictions requiring adviser review

A decision can be ready even while unrelated work remains open. An unresolved equipment classification might not prevent payment of a verified utility invoice. It could, however, affect a profitability analysis or owner-distribution review.

The question is whether the open item could change the decision—not whether every accounting task is finished.

Why a Current Cash Balance Can Overstate Spendable Funds

Illustrative example: A staffed private medical practice has a bank-reported available balance of $180,000. The owner is considering a $45,000 equipment purchase.

The manager prepares an obligations bridge. Each item below is separate, is not already reflected as a bank withdrawal and has been checked for overlap.

Cash bridge item Amount
Bank-reported available balance $180,000
Less: approved provider and administrative payroll funding ($62,000)
Less: related payroll tax and benefit funding not included above ($12,000)
Less: unpaid vendor invoices due in the planning window ($24,000)
Less: released payments not yet reflected by the bank ($8,000)
Less: identified refund obligations ($9,000)
Less: management’s operating cash buffer ($30,000)
Provisional capacity for discretionary spending $35,000

The $45,000 purchase exceeds that provisional capacity. The balance is current, but it does not support the purchase without another funding plan or a revised commitment.

The operating buffer is a management choice in this example, not a universal reserve requirement. The appropriate amount depends on payroll timing, collection variability and other obligations.

Expected payer receipts and patient-balance collections belong in the forecast, not in today’s verified cash. If the purchase works only when uncertain receipts arrive on time, consider delaying it, negotiating payment timing or evaluating financing before committing.

This bridge is also provisional. If an unknown invoice or unexplained withdrawal could materially change it, resolve that issue before treating the remaining amount as available.

Build a Decision Packet Before Requesting Approval

A useful approval packet can be brief. It should let the owner see what is known, what is estimated and what remains open without reconstructing the accounting work.

1. Define the decision and the entity

State the amount, purpose, intended payment date and legal entity making the payment. Identify whether the commitment is reversible, recurring or difficult to unwind.

For a multi-location practice, distinguish the location receiving the benefit from the entity paying the bill. A consolidated cash total can hide a shortfall in the entity that must fund payroll or rent.

2. Label the evidence and its cutoff

Record separate dates for:

  • Bank balances checked.
  • Accounting transactions posted through.
  • Bank and credit-card accounts reconciled through.
  • Payables and payroll schedules reviewed through.
  • Latest closed financial statements.

Avoid a single “updated today” label. A dashboard may refresh today while its payroll accrual or vendor obligations remain incomplete.

3. Bridge from bank cash to available capacity

Identify outstanding payments, approved payroll, unpaid bills, debt payments and other known obligations. Then show the operating buffer separately.

Check for double counting. For example, a bill marked paid in the accounting system may already be included among outstanding payments. Subtracting both would understate available cash.

For material receipts that remain unidentified, investigate their source and treatment before relying on them for discretionary spending. A bank deposit is not automatically evidence of earned revenue.

4. Show the consequence of uncertainty

List each unresolved item, its potential cash effect, the person responsible and the next review point. Where the amount is uncertain, use a supported range rather than false precision.

For a purchase, ask whether the practice could still meet obligations if collections arrive later than expected. For a staffing commitment, test the recurring cost rather than only the first payroll. For a distribution, ask whether a plausible adjustment would eliminate the proposed payment capacity.

5. Record the approval condition

Use a clear outcome: approve, approve subject to a named check, defer or decline. Record who authorized it and what evidence supported the decision.

A condition such as “release after the payroll funding amount is confirmed” is more useful than “looks fine.” Recheck the packet if the payment date changes or a material new obligation appears.

When to Wait for Reconciliation or CPA Review

Reconciliation compares accounting records with supporting account activity and explains differences. It does not replace invoice approval, forecasting or review of missing obligations, but it is essential when the decision depends on a trustworthy cash balance.

Wait for reconciliation when:

  • The bank and ledger differ by an unexplained amount that could affect the decision.
  • Old outstanding checks, transfers or duplicate entries distort available cash.
  • A material deposit or withdrawal has not been identified.
  • An owner payment depends on balances that have not been reliably reconciled.

Seek CPA review when:

  • A proposed distribution depends on uncertain liabilities, owner balances or tax estimates.
  • The treatment of a material receipt, financing transaction or intercompany transfer is unclear.
  • Catch-up entries or accounting-basis differences materially affect reported profit.
  • Provider compensation accruals or other estimates could change the owner’s conclusion.

Questions about ownership documents, lending restrictions or legal permission to distribute funds may also require legal counsel. Accounting profit and cash availability alone do not establish that a distribution is permitted.

Not every routine payment must wait for month-end close. If a verified bill is due, cash is supported and the open accounting issue cannot materially affect payment capacity, an authorized reviewer may have sufficient evidence to proceed. Document that judgment rather than treating all provisional data as either unusable or fully reliable.

Use Closed Results and Current Activity Together

Closed results are particularly useful for decisions involving profitability: adding recurring staff expense, evaluating a location or assessing sustainable owner compensation. Current activity then updates that baseline for changes since close.

For a per-location P&L, check that shared administrative costs and provider compensation are treated consistently. A location can appear unusually profitable because its share of costs has not yet been posted. Likewise, a strong collection week may reflect delayed payer receipts rather than a lasting improvement in operating performance.

Compare like periods, explain material adjustments and keep estimates visible. A provisional report is useful when readers know which conclusions might change.

Conclusion

Decision-ready financial information is not simply the newest number. It is evidence that is complete enough for the specific commitment being considered.

Use current bank activity to establish cash, accounting records to explain it, obligation schedules to identify claims against it and closed results to evaluate performance. When an unresolved item could change the answer, wait for the relevant reconciliation or professional review. When it cannot, document the basis for proceeding.

FAQs

Can a medical practice pay bills before the monthly books close?

Yes, a routine payment may be supportable before close when the invoice is verified, duplicate-payment checks are complete and current cash covers scheduled obligations. An unexplained cash difference or material missing liability may require additional review before release.

Does a profitable P&L mean an owner distribution is affordable?

No. Profit and available cash measure different things. Review reconciled entity-level cash, unpaid obligations, tax estimates, operating needs and applicable restrictions. Ask the CPA to review unresolved accounting issues, and involve legal counsel when permission under governing documents or other restrictions is uncertain.

How fresh does financial data need to be for an equipment purchase?

Use the current purchase terms, current cash and obligations, and the latest reliable closed results. Update the cash forecast through the payment commitments. If approval depends on uncertain collections or unresolved balances, defer the commitment or resolve those issues first.
