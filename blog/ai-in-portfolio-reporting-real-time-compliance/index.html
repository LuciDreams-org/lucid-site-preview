AI Portfolio Reporting for Medical Practice Treasury Policy Reviews

November 3, 2026 · 10 min read

A medical practice’s reserve portfolio report should help answer an operational question: Do the documented reserves support the practice’s cash needs within its approved treasury policy? A total account value or recent performance figure cannot answer that alone.

Provider and administrative payroll, rent, shared overhead, and other scheduled obligations have payment dates. Payer receipts and patient collections may arrive differently from the cash forecast. The owner needs evidence about when reserve funds are accessible, which entity owns them, and whether the holdings appear consistent with constraints established with the practice’s investment adviser.

AI may help organize dated custodian records, compare reported fields with documented policy criteria, and draft an exception list. It cannot establish that money is available today merely because a dashboard refreshed today. Nor can it determine legal compliance from a portfolio summary.

This article outlines a review workflow for US private practices with staff. The purpose is to document questions and route them to the right human reviewer—not to recommend securities or automate investment decisions.

Define the Policy Before Testing the Portfolio

A review needs an approved reference point. Without one, an AI-generated warning may reflect an invented rule rather than the owner’s actual policy.

Ask the owner, finance lead, and investment adviser to confirm the current treasury policy and its scope. It should identify:

  • The practice entities and accounts covered by the review.
  • The operating obligations reserves are intended to support.
  • Required access timing for each liquidity category.
  • Adviser-established investment constraints, including any concentration limits and valuation requirements.
  • The people authorized to investigate, approve exceptions, and direct account activity.
  • The review cadence and circumstances requiring an earlier review.

Record the policy version, approval date, and effective date. Keep an adviser recommendation distinct from an owner-approved policy change. If a criterion is ambiguous, document that ambiguity rather than asking AI to choose an interpretation.

Start With the Practice’s Cash Calendar

Use a dated cash forecast showing provider and administrative payroll, rent, debt payments, planned equipment purchases, and other material obligations. Identify uncertain inflows separately from confirmed receipts.

For multi-location practices, location-level performance can explain cash needs, but it does not establish ownership of reserves. A location P&L and a legal entity’s account balance answer different questions. Do not assume funds held by one entity are available to another; route proposed transfers through the appropriate financial and legal review.

The portfolio review should connect documented liquidity needs to account evidence, not replace the cash forecast.

Build a Dated Evidence Packet

Before using AI, assemble the records a human reviewer would need. These may include custodian statements, holdings reports, transaction histories, withdrawal terms, adviser correspondence, reconciled accounting balances, and the approved treasury policy.

Maintain a source register with the account owner, reporting period, document date, retrieval date, and any known gaps. Distinguish originals from working spreadsheets and AI summaries.

The IRS guidance on business recordkeeping supports complete business records, separate business accounts, and bank reconciliation. Those fundamentals help establish reliable accounting inputs; they do not establish whether a portfolio satisfies an investment policy.

Why Current-Looking Data Can Be Incomplete

A report generated today may contain holdings valued at an earlier date. A displayed balance may not explain pending transactions, withdrawal restrictions, or the time needed for funds to reach the operating account.

Check these dates separately:

Date or status What it tells the reviewer What it does not establish
Report generation date When the report was produced That all underlying values are current
Holdings or valuation date When positions or values were measured Current proceeds available for withdrawal
Transaction status and date Whether activity is pending or completed Whether every relevant transaction is included
Retrieval date When staff obtained the record That the source updated at that time
Access terms and confirmation date Documented conditions for obtaining funds That an older confirmation still applies

An apparently complete report can also omit an account. Reconcile the account list to accounting records and prior review packets before treating the portfolio total as complete.

Use a Treasury Policy-to-Report Matrix

The matrix below is a review template, not a recommended investment policy. Populate it using the practice’s approved terms and adviser-confirmed definitions. Leave missing facts explicitly unresolved.

Review area Policy criterion to document Report fields to examine Evidence sources Apparent exception or gap
Access timing Required availability before specified operating payments Available balance, pending activity, transfer timing, withdrawal conditions Custodian records, account terms, dated adviser or custodian confirmation, cash forecast Report shows value but does not establish access before payroll

For concentration testing, confirm the denominator and grouping rules before calculating a result. Different account scopes or classifications can change the answer. AI should not silently substitute its own definitions.

Give AI a Bounded Documentation Task

Where the practice has an appropriately reviewed tool and workflow, AI may assist with extraction and comparison. Its role should be narrow enough that staff can verify each result.

A useful task specification is:

Compare the supplied records only against the supplied approved policy. For each criterion, identify the supporting document and page or row, relevant dates, extracted facts, missing evidence, and any apparent exception. Do not infer missing access terms, classify uncertain holdings without confirmation, recommend transactions, or conclude legal compliance.

Potential uses include organizing documents by account and period, identifying missing statements, drafting a policy-to-report comparison, and preparing questions for the adviser. These are proposed workflow uses, not claims about any particular provider’s software.

Verify the Extraction and Calculations

A finance team member should check account identities, dates, signs, units, position values, and totals against the originals. Recalculate policy tests independently rather than accepting generated arithmetic.

Preserve a reference to the actual source. If a statement and an adviser email conflict, retain both and ask for clarification. Do not let an AI summary overwrite the conflicting evidence.

Use explicit review statuses:

  • Supported by supplied evidence: The reviewer found support for the particular criterion tested.
  • Apparent policy exception: The supplied evidence appears inconsistent with a documented criterion.
  • Unable to assess: Required information is missing, stale, ambiguous, or conflicting.

“Unable to assess” is not a pass. Conversely, an apparent exception is not proof of wrongdoing.

Document Exceptions for Adviser Review

The exception register should turn a flag into a defined investigation. Record what appears inconsistent, why it matters to the practice, and who must resolve it.

Illustrative example: A practice has a $180,000 payroll payment due in eight business days. Its approved policy requires the designated payroll reserve to be accessible before that payment. A newly generated portfolio report shows $250,000, but the supplied records do not establish access timing for $100,000 of that total.

The correct finding is not “payroll is covered” or “the practice is noncompliant.” It is that the available evidence does not establish whether the designated reserve meets the access requirement. The owner needs a dated answer from the adviser or custodian and an updated cash assessment from the finance team.

Exception-register field Illustrative entry
Policy reference Approved payroll-reserve access requirement
Observed evidence Current report total includes an amount with unconfirmed access timing
Evidence gap No current documentation establishing when those funds can reach the operating account
Operational significance Payroll availability cannot be confirmed from this packet alone
Responsible reviewers Finance lead and investment adviser; custodian confirmation requested
Required response Confirm access terms and timing, then update the cash assessment
Closure evidence Dated confirmation and documented reviewer conclusion

Set an investigation deadline based on the obligation date, not just the next routine meeting. If the issue remains open, retain it as unresolved and escalate to the authorized decision-maker. Do not close an exception solely because a later dashboard shows a higher balance.

Separate Internal Policy Exceptions From Legal Conclusions

A treasury policy is an internal decision framework. An apparent departure may reflect a genuine policy exception, a reporting error, a changed circumstance, or an outdated policy. It is not automatically a legal violation.

Likewise, a report showing no apparent policy exceptions does not prove compliance with all applicable laws, account agreements, or other obligations. Those conclusions require the relevant facts and qualified professional review.

Keep responsibilities clear:

Reviewer Primary responsibility in this workflow
Practice owner or authorized manager Confirm operating priorities and approve decisions within their authority
Finance or CPA team Validate accounting inputs, reconciliations, cash needs, and documentation
Investment adviser Interpret adviser-established constraints and investigate portfolio questions
Custodian Provide account records and clarify account-specific transaction or access information
Legal counsel, when needed Assess legal questions rather than relying on an internal policy label

A documented temporary exception should identify its approver, rationale, scope, review date, and follow-up requirements. Approval records the decision; it does not eliminate the underlying liquidity uncertainty or resolve a separate legal question.

Make the Review Packet Useful at the Owner Meeting

Present a short decision summary backed by the detailed evidence:

  1. Scope and cutoff: Entities, accounts, policy version, and dates covered.
  2. Liquidity assessment: Upcoming obligations compared with documented access timing.
  3. Open exceptions: Apparent departures and evidence gaps, each with an owner and deadline.
  4. Adviser responses: Confirmed facts, remaining questions, and proposed decisions for authorized review.
  5. Prior-item status: What closed, what remains unresolved, and the supporting records.

Review on a schedule suited to the practice and reconsider timing when cash needs, accounts, or policy terms change. A new location, a material payer-receipt delay, or a planned owner distribution may warrant an earlier review.

Conclusion

A useful treasury review connects operating needs, approved policy terms, and dated account evidence. AI can help organize the comparison, but people must verify the facts, investigate exceptions, and authorize decisions.

The strongest report is not the one that looks most current. It is the one that makes its limits visible: what is supported, what appears inconsistent, what remains unknown, and who will resolve it before the practice needs the cash.

FAQs

Can AI certify that a medical practice’s reserve portfolio complies with its treasury policy?

No. AI can assist with a comparison against supplied criteria, but people must validate the sources, calculations, and interpretations. Any conclusion should be limited to the accounts, dates, and criteria reviewed. Legal compliance is a separate question for qualified professionals.

What if the report is dated today but some holdings have older valuations?

Record both dates and ask the adviser whether the underlying values are adequate for the policy test. Do not treat the generation date as the valuation date. Where current evidence is required but unavailable, mark the test unable to assess.

Should the practice change investments when AI flags an exception?

Not on the flag alone. Verify the evidence, assess the cash need, and ask the investment adviser to investigate. Any action should follow the practice’s authorization process. A flag is a question requiring review, not a securities recommendation.

Can a multi-entity practice review all reserves as one total?

A consolidated view may be useful, but retain entity-level detail. Confirm ownership, policy scope, and access restrictions for each account. A combined total does not establish that every entity can use every reported dollar.
