AI-Assisted Equity Dilution Forecasts for Medical Practices

November 4, 2026 · 9 min read

Before a medical practice accepts a proposed capital contribution or grants an ownership interest, its owners need to understand how the transaction could change their percentages. A capitalization worksheet makes that change visible before anyone treats a proposal as an agreed outcome.

AI can help organize inputs, draft formulas and compare scenarios. It cannot establish who is eligible to own an interest, interpret uncertain agreement terms authoritatively or turn a forecast into an executed transaction. Executed agreements, instrument terms and adviser-confirmed eligibility remain authoritative.

For a staffed private practice, the useful question is specific: “If this entity issues these interests under these assumptions, what will each holder own afterward?” That is different from deciding how to value the practice, choosing between debt and equity or designing an employee-retention program.

Define What the Dilution Forecast Measures

Ownership dilution occurs when an issuance increases the total interests used in the ownership calculation without a proportional increase in an existing holder’s interests. An owner can retain the same number of units while holding a smaller percentage of the total.

For a simple, single-class worksheet:

  • Pre-transaction ownership: holder’s existing units ÷ total existing units.
  • Post-transaction ownership: holder’s units after the transaction ÷ total units after the transaction.
  • Percentage-point change: post-transaction percentage minus pre-transaction percentage.

These formulas are useful only when the units belong in the same calculation. Different classes, contingent awards or special economic rights may require separate schedules rather than one blended percentage.

Keep four questions separate

Question What the worksheet can show What needs separate review
What percentage does each holder own? Units divided by the defined total Which interests belong in that total
Who can approve decisions? A separate voting schedule, if confirmed Voting rights, approval thresholds and reserved decisions
Who receives distributions? A separate allocation schedule based on confirmed terms Distribution provisions and any priorities
What is an interest worth? Not established by the ownership percentage alone Economic rights, obligations and valuation analysis

A smaller ownership percentage does not, by itself, prove a particular loss of voting control or dollar value. Likewise, a stated ownership percentage does not establish an entitlement to the same percentage of every distribution.

Assemble an Entity-Specific Starting Point

Start with the legal entity involved—not the practice’s brand name or a combined financial report.

A multi-location practice might operate several locations inside one entity, or use multiple entities with different owners. A location-level profit and loss statement does not establish a separate ownership interest. An issuance in one entity should not automatically change the capitalization worksheet for another.

Prepare the following inputs before asking AI to calculate anything:

Input What to record Why it matters
Entity and effective date Exact entity and ownership snapshot date Prevents mixing entities or periods
Existing interests Holder identifier, class and units or documented percentage Establishes the starting capitalization
Supporting documents Executed agreements, amendments and issuance records Supports the opening balances and rights
Proposed issuance Recipient identifier, class, units and status Defines the proposed change
Outstanding instruments or awards Relevant terms, conditions and inclusion basis Prevents omitted or double-counted interests
Transaction sequence What occurs first and what depends on completion Clarifies the denominator at each step
Open questions Unconfirmed terms, eligibility and required approvals Keeps assumptions from appearing settled

Use neutral holder labels in a working model when names are unnecessary. Patient balances, claim details and clinical records are not needed for an ownership calculation. Do not put patient-identifiable information into ordinary Slack conversations or a general-purpose AI tool.

If opening records conflict, resolve the difference with the appropriate advisers before presenting a forecast as reliable. AI should not select whichever ownership figure makes the spreadsheet balance.

Illustrative Capitalization Worksheet

The following is an invented, illustrative example—not a recommended transaction structure or a statement that any recipient is eligible to own a medical practice.

Assume one practice entity has a single class of equal units. Owner A holds 600 units and Owner B holds 400 units. The proposal would issue 200 new units to a proposed contributor and 50 new units to an award recipient.

For this scenario only, assume all proposed units become issued and outstanding at the modeled closing. There are no other interests included in the calculation. Advisers would need to confirm whether these assumptions match the actual documents and permitted ownership arrangements.

Holder Existing units Pre-transaction ownership Proposed new units Post-transaction units Post-transaction ownership
Owner A 600 60.00% 0 600 48.00%
Owner B 400 40.00% 0 400 32.00%
Proposed contributor 0 0.00% 200 200 16.00%
Proposed award recipient 0 0.00% 50 50 4.00%
Total 1,000 100.00% 250 1,250 100.00%

The post-transaction denominator is 1,250 units: 1,000 existing units plus 250 newly issued units.

Owner A’s calculation is 600 ÷ 1,250 = 48%. That is a decline of 12 percentage points, from 60% to 48%. Relative to the original percentage, the reduction is 20%: 12 ÷ 60. These are different measurements and should be labeled clearly.

The table does not determine the amount of the proposed contribution, the value of the award, voting rights or distribution entitlements. Those require their own confirmed inputs.

Compare alternative assumptions

The next table uses the same invented starting capitalization. Each column is an independent alternative, not a sequence of completed transactions.

Output Contribution only: 200 new units Base proposal: 200 contribution units + 50 award units Larger issuance: 300 contribution units + 50 award units
Total post-transaction units 1,200 1,250 1,350
Owner A 50.00% 48.00% 44.44%
Owner B 33.33% 32.00% 29.63%
Proposed contributor 16.67% 16.00% 22.22%
Proposed award recipient 0.00% 4.00% 3.70%

Percentages are rounded for display; calculations should retain full precision.

Notice that the same 50-unit award produces different percentages when the total issuance changes. A proposal for “50 units” is not equivalent to a promise of “4% after all transactions.” If a proposal uses a target percentage, the worksheet must solve for the required units using the agreed denominator and sequence. It should not silently treat that percentage as a fixed unit count.

Use AI to Assist the Model, Not Approve the Transaction

AI is most useful when the task is bounded and the assumptions are explicit. It can help draft a worksheet, explain formulas, compare alternatives and identify missing inputs. Its output still needs independent arithmetic and document review.

A useful request based on the illustrative example would be:

Build a single-class capitalization worksheet with 600 units for Owner A and 400 for Owner B. Model an issuance of 200 units to Contributor C and 50 units to Recipient D. Show existing units, new units, post-transaction units and pre- and post-transaction percentages. State the denominator and formulas. Do not infer voting rights, distributions, eligibility, tax treatment or valuation. List unresolved assumptions separately.

Then ask for checks, not just a polished summary:

  • Do the holder-level unit counts reconcile to the total?
  • Do unrounded ownership percentages sum to 100%?
  • Has any interest been counted twice?
  • Are the alternative scenarios independent or sequential?
  • Is the model showing currently issued interests or a defined potential future basis?
  • Which conclusions depend on unconfirmed terms?

Keep the calculations in a spreadsheet or another reviewable calculation file. Recalculate key outputs independently rather than accepting AI-generated arithmetic because the explanation sounds confident.

Make uncertain terms visible

If an award has conditions, create clearly labeled alternatives based on adviser-confirmed terms. Do not automatically count it as currently issued ownership, and do not assume it has no future dilution effect.

Similarly, a cash payment should not be converted into ownership units unless the proposed issuance terms supply that connection. A capital contribution is a financial input; the governing documents determine whether and how it changes ownership.

Check the Transaction Type and Sequence

A forecast can be mathematically correct and still model the wrong event.

New issuance versus transfer: If the entity issues new units, the total outstanding units increase under the simple model above. If one owner transfers existing units to another eligible holder, the holder balances change but the total unit count may remain unchanged. Those are different worksheets.

Immediate versus conditional award: Keep current ownership separate from modeled future ownership. Label any future inclusion basis explicitly rather than presenting a potential interest as already outstanding.

One step versus several steps: When a proposal defines an interest by reference to a percentage, confirm whether that percentage is measured before or after other proposed issuances. Include a separate row or schedule for each step when needed.

Entity cash versus owner proceeds: Track whether money is proposed to enter the practice entity or go to a selling owner. Do not present an owner-to-owner payment as cash available for provider payroll, administrative payroll or other practice expenses.

These distinctions help the owner ask better questions without treating the forecast as transaction advice.

Prepare a Review Packet for Your Advisers

Before a decision meeting, assemble a short packet containing:

  1. The opening capitalization: entity, date, holders, classes and supporting documents.
  2. The proposed change: fixed units or target percentage, recipient, conditions and transaction sequence.
  3. The scenario worksheet: baseline, alternatives, formulas and rounding policy.
  4. A rights checklist: voting, distributions and other economic terms requiring separate confirmation.
  5. An unresolved-items list: eligibility, approvals, document interpretation and accounting or tax questions.

Ask counsel to confirm ownership eligibility, governing-document interpretation and required approvals. Ask the CPA to review the applicable accounting and tax questions. The owner should approve a clearly identified scenario only after those questions have been addressed—not an unlabeled spreadsheet version circulating in email.

After execution, update the ownership records from the final documents. Reconcile any related entity cash movements and accounting entries separately. Do not simply relabel the forecast as the completed capitalization table.

Conclusion

A useful dilution forecast makes the denominator, proposed issuance and unresolved assumptions easy to inspect. It shows what changes under each scenario without suggesting that ownership percentage alone determines control, distributions or value.

Use AI to help prepare and challenge the calculations. Use executed documents and qualified advisers to establish what the practice can actually implement.

FAQs

Does every capital contribution dilute existing medical practice owners?

No. A payment alone does not establish a change in ownership. The result depends on the governing documents and transaction terms. For forecasting, model the actual proposed issuance or adjustment rather than assuming that cash received automatically creates new units.

Does an equity award belong in the current ownership total?

That depends on the award’s terms and status. Separate currently issued interests from potential future interests, and label each calculation basis. Ask advisers to confirm how conditions, vesting or other instrument provisions affect the relevant schedule.

Can an AI tool confirm who may own a private medical practice?

Do not rely on it for that determination. Eligibility requires adviser review of the relevant entity, jurisdiction and proposed holder. A mathematically valid scenario is not evidence that the transaction is permitted.

Does dilution reduce an owner’s distributions by the same percentage?

Not necessarily. Ownership percentages and distribution rights are separate questions. Review the governing provisions and model distributions separately using confirmed terms rather than assuming they follow the capitalization percentages.

What should I do if AI and my spreadsheet produce different results?

Compare the inputs, denominator, transaction sequence and inclusion of conditional interests. Check the formulas independently and retain full precision until display. Resolve the discrepancy before sharing the result as a decision-ready forecast.
