AI-Assisted Accounts Receivable Review for Medical Practices

November 3, 2026 · 9 min read

A medical practice’s accounts receivable report shows outstanding balances—not necessarily the cash available for provider payroll, administrative wages or next month’s rent. Billed charges, contractual adjustments, unapplied receipts and posting delays can all affect what that report means.

AI-assisted analysis can help organize authorized financial summaries, compare periods and draft an exception list. It should not decide which balances are collectible, authorize write-offs or replace reconciliation by the billing and accounting teams.

For owners and managers, the goal is a repeatable financial review: understand what changed, determine which balances need attention, reconcile the reports and assign each unresolved item to someone who can verify it. This article focuses on that oversight workflow, not claim submission, clinical coding, patient collection tactics or dispute resolution.

Start With a Consistent Receivables Review Packet

Before using AI, agree on the source reports, reporting date and definitions. Comparing an aging report exported today with last month’s frozen accounting records can create apparent discrepancies that are simply timing differences.

Prepare the following packet for each reporting period:

Summary What it should show Financial purpose
AR aging Balances by age bucket, payer or patient responsibility, entity and location where available Identify concentration and changes in older balances
AR activity Beginning balance, charges, applied payments, adjustments, transfers and ending balance Explain the movement in outstanding AR
Receipts Payer and patient receipts, refunds and deposit timing Connect collection activity to cash records
Unapplied receipts and credits Amounts, age and reason categories Identify cash not yet assigned and credits needing review
Adjustments Categories, amounts and available approval references Distinguish expected reductions from unusual activity
Accounting balances Relevant receivable, cash, clearing and allowance accounts, if used Reconcile billing summaries to the books

Keep separate legal entities distinct. For a multi-location practice, also confirm how locations are assigned. A receipt deposited centrally may relate to care delivered elsewhere; deposit location alone is not a reliable basis for location-level collection comparisons.

Save the report date, filters and version with the packet so reviewers can reproduce the analysis.

Define Aging Before Interpreting It

An aging bucket is meaningful only if everyone understands its starting point. Reports may measure age from the service date, original billing date or another system-defined date. Those measures answer different questions.

A practice might use buckets such as 0–30, 31–60, 61–90, 91–120 and over 120 days. These are reporting conventions, not universal deadlines or proof that a balance is uncollectible.

Document these definitions:

  • Aging basis: Which date starts the clock?
  • Balance basis: Does the report show billed charges, balances after posted adjustments or an estimated collectible amount?
  • Responsibility: Are payer and patient balances separate, and how are transfers treated?
  • Credits: Are credit balances displayed separately or netted against positive receivables?
  • Scope: Which entities, locations and reporting dates are included?

A responsibility transfer can also affect reported age, depending on system configuration. Ask the billing team whether older balances can appear newer after a transfer or rebilling event. Otherwise, an apparent improvement in aging may reflect presentation rather than payment.

Billed amounts are not expected collectible balances

Gross billed charges can differ substantially from the amount the practice expects to receive. Posted contractual adjustments reduce recorded balances; additional expected reductions may still require evaluation. Some remaining balances may also be uncertain.

Illustrative example: A practice has $300,000 in outstanding billed balances before $90,000 of identified, unposted contractual adjustments. That leaves $210,000 before considering other collectibility concerns. If the CPA determines that a further $15,000 valuation allowance is appropriate for accrual reporting, the resulting net receivable would be $195,000.

Neither $300,000 nor $195,000 is automatically a forecast of next month’s deposits. Collection timing is a separate estimate. Have the billing team validate adjustment support and the CPA determine the appropriate accounting treatment; AI should not infer either from age alone.

Use AI to Organize Exceptions, Not Approve Transactions

With suitable tools and reviewed inputs, AI-assisted analysis can support tasks such as:

  • Comparing aging buckets across reporting periods.
  • Highlighting payer groups with rising balances and declining receipts.
  • Identifying missing locations, inconsistent labels or duplicate summary rows.
  • Grouping unusual adjustments for human review.
  • Drafting explanations of changes and an owner meeting agenda.

Use spreadsheet formulas or accounting reports to verify totals and ratios independently. A fluent explanation does not establish that an AI-generated calculation is correct.

A bounded request is more useful than asking AI to “fix AR.” For example:

Using only this approved aggregate summary, compare the current and prior periods. Identify changes in older balances, unapplied receipts and adjustment categories. Show the calculations, separate observations from possible explanations, and list missing information. Do not recommend write-offs or changes to source records.

The output should be a review queue. Humans verify the evidence, make decisions and enter approved corrections in the appropriate system.

Minimize information before analysis

Use authorized summaries containing only the detail needed for financial review. Owner-level analysis generally does not require patient names, dates of birth, diagnoses, medical record numbers or narrative notes. Even combinations of fields in a summary can create privacy risks, so removing names alone is not sufficient assurance.

HHS guidance on the minimum necessary standard explains its application and exceptions. Have the responsible privacy or compliance lead approve the data scope and tool workflow.

If a cloud service will maintain electronic protected health information on the practice’s behalf, evaluate the applicable business-associate arrangements and safeguards before use. HHS cloud-computing guidance explains why encryption alone does not resolve those obligations. Do not put patient-identifiable information into ordinary Slack or an unapproved general-purpose AI tool.

Reconcile Receivables Before Drawing Conclusions

An aging report should connect to underlying activity and, where applicable, the accounting ledger. An unexplained difference is a financial exception—not something to hide with a balancing entry.

1. Rebuild the billing-system movement

For a consistently defined outstanding-balance report, use this roll-forward:

Beginning AR + posted charges − applied payments − balance-reducing adjustments ± other supported changes = ending AR.

Separate reversals, refunds and transfers according to how the source system records them. Transfers within the same reporting population should not create new total AR, although they may change payer, patient or location subtotals.

Illustrative example: Beginning AR of $420,000, plus $180,000 in charges, less $125,000 in applied payments and $55,000 in adjustments, produces ending AR of $420,000, assuming no other changes. The unchanged total does not establish that collections are healthy: balances may have shifted into older buckets.

2. Bridge billing AR to accounting records

For accrual books, reconcile the billing-system balance to the relevant general-ledger control accounts. Show supported differences separately, including cutoff timing, mapping differences and valuation entries. Keep gross AR and any related allowance distinct so reviewers can understand the bridge to net AR.

For cash-basis books, the billing aging may be a management schedule rather than a balance-sheet receivable. The bookkeeper and CPA should document that relationship instead of forcing a match to an account that is not maintained on the same basis.

3. Reconcile receipts separately

Cash received is not always the same as payments applied to receivables during the period. Review bank deposits, payment summaries, clearing accounts, refunds and unapplied receipts together. Explain differences such as deposits in transit or processing deductions rather than assuming they represent missing collections.

The IRS guidance on business records supports complete records, separate business accounts and bank reconciliation. For this workflow, those records provide the evidence needed to distinguish a cash issue from a posting issue.

Review Collection Trends Without Misleading Ratios

Use consistent definitions over time rather than importing a generic benchmark. Payer mix, reporting basis and posting timing can make comparisons unreliable.

Measure Review method Important limitation
Older-balance share Divide positive AR above the chosen age threshold by total positive AR on the same basis Do not silently net credits against the denominator
Receipts trend Compare payer and patient receipts across comparable periods Deposits may relate to earlier billing periods
Unapplied receipts trend Compare amount and age by reason Cash may already be in the bank without reducing individual balances
Adjustment trend Compare amounts and categories with prior periods Lower AR may reflect adjustments rather than collections
Days in AR Use a documented AR balance and a consistently defined daily revenue denominator Gross charges, net revenue and receipts are not interchangeable

A same-month receipts-to-charges ratio is not necessarily a collection rate for that month’s services. Where reliable cohort reporting exists, review how balances from the same service or billing period resolve over time.

Use trends to ask focused questions: Did receipts slow, did posting fall behind, or did adjustments increase? Do not treat an AI explanation as the answer until the responsible team supplies evidence.

Aging-Review Worksheet: Assign and Close Exceptions

Use one worksheet for financial handoffs rather than maintaining separate, inconsistent lists. The entries below are illustrative examples, not practice benchmarks or actual cases.

Exception Financial exposure Assigned owner Evidence or next action Status and target Closure requirement

Unapplied receipts remain unresolved $8,000 Billing team, with bookkeeper Identify receipt batches; compare bank receipt and billing application In review; next weekly review Application or supported classification reconciles

In the working version, add the entity, location, reporting date, preparer, evidence link, reviewer and closure date. Keep patient-level investigation inside authorized billing workflows; use reference IDs or aggregate categories in the finance worksheet.

“Assigned” does not mean “resolved.” Close an item only when evidence supports the conclusion and any approved correction has been checked. If a balance enters a dispute process, track only the financial handoff and status here; manage the dispute in its separate workflow.

Turn the Review Into an Owner Decision

At the monthly review, the owner or manager should leave with four answers:

  1. Are the reports reconciled? Identify unresolved differences and any effect on confidence in the financial statements.
  2. What explains the change in AR? Separate charges, collections, adjustments, transfers and valuation changes.
  3. What can reasonably support near-term cash planning? Use expected receipt timing, not the aging total alone, when assessing payroll and other commitments.
  4. Who owns each exception? Record a responsible person, next action and review date.

FAQs

Can AI determine which medical practice receivables should be written off?

AI can flag balances for review, but age or pattern recognition alone does not justify a write-off. The billing team must verify supporting information, authorized personnel must approve balance changes, and the CPA should determine any financial-reporting treatment. Keep those decisions separate from AI-generated suggestions.

Why can receipts increase while the aging report stays high?

New charges may replace collected balances, receipts may remain unapplied, or cash receipt and payment-posting dates may fall in different periods. Review the AR roll-forward and receipt reconciliation before concluding that the aging report is wrong or collections have deteriorated.

Should billing AR always equal the balance sheet?

Not necessarily. Accrual books may include supported cutoff differences or valuation entries, while cash-basis books may not record billing receivables as a balance-sheet asset. The bookkeeper and CPA should document the reporting basis and reconcile the schedules that should agree.

What information should an owner provide for AI-assisted AR analysis?

Start with approved aggregate aging, receipts, adjustment and reconciliation summaries. Include consistent dates and definitions, but exclude unnecessary patient information. Have the responsible privacy or compliance lead approve the tool and data workflow before any sensitive information is used.
