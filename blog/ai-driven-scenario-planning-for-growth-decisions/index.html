AI-Assisted Growth Decision Scenarios for Medical Practices

November 3, 2026 · 10 min read

Opening another location creates a sequence of commitments—not a single growth decision. A lease may become binding before construction is complete. Provider and administrative hiring may need to begin before opening. Payroll and rent can start well before payer receipts and patient collections cover the new location’s costs.

For a staffed private medical practice, the useful question is not simply, “Will the location be profitable?” It is: Which commitments can we make now without putting the existing practice’s cash needs at risk?

AI-assisted scenario comparisons can help organize that decision. A finance team can use AI to draft assumption tables, prepare alternative schedules and summarize differences from an approved financial model. But source documents must support the assumptions, a human must verify the calculations, and the owner must approve the decision criteria.

The goal is a short decision memo that supports one of three actions: proceed, phase the commitment or defer it until specific evidence is available.

Define the commitment before comparing outcomes

“Open another office” is too broad for an approval request. Separate the project into decisions that have different costs and reversibility:

  • Authorize site investigation and professional review.
  • Sign a lease and accept its payment obligations.
  • Approve construction and equipment orders.
  • Commit to provider and administrative start dates.
  • Release additional staffing or space after the initial opening.

For each decision, identify the cash required now, the obligations created for later and the last practical point at which the practice can stop or change course.

A refundable deposit, a noncancelable equipment order and a multiyear lease do not represent the same exposure. Have the appropriate advisers review contractual obligations; do not ask an AI tool to determine whether a commitment is legally reversible.

Set owner-approved criteria first

Before reviewing an attractive expansion case, agree on:

  • Protected cash: the minimum available balance needed for existing-practice payroll, occupancy, debt payments, planned tax payments and other obligations.
  • Funding limit: how much unrestricted cash the owner will make available to the project, by entity.
  • Downside tolerance: the cash shortfall the practice is willing and able to absorb under a specified stress case.
  • Evidence requirements: what must be confirmed before a lease, purchase order or employment commitment is approved.
  • Stop conditions: which unresolved assumptions require deferral rather than another optimistic revision.

These criteria are owner decisions, not outputs an AI model should invent.

Build an assumption register tied to evidence

Use a single register for the alternatives so that favorable assumptions do not quietly change between columns. Give each input a source, date, responsible person and status: verified, provisional or unresolved.

Decision input Evidence to obtain What it changes
Lease commencement and rent obligations Current lease draft and adviser-reviewed terms Cash paid before opening and exposure if opening slips
Build-out and equipment payments Written quotes, payment schedules and delivery estimates Opening cash requirements and timing
Provider and administrative hiring Recruiting estimates, proposed compensation and start-date commitments Pre-opening payroll and operating capacity
Payer participation readiness Status confirmation from the responsible credentialing or contracting team When the planned receipt assumptions are supportable
Collection timing Aggregate historical payer receipts, patient collections and receivables reports Delay between services and cash availability
New-location activity Owner-reviewed operational evidence and documented limitations Whether the proposed revenue ramp has a defensible basis
Existing-practice cash needs Reconciled books, payroll schedules, payables and planned payments Cash that expansion cannot safely consume
Shared costs and entity funding Allocation policy, account balances and adviser-reviewed funding plan Location profitability and usable cash by entity

Existing-location collection history is a starting point, not proof that a new site will perform identically. Separate evidence of potential activity from evidence of collectible revenue. Do not treat a list of interested patients, referral expectations or an AI-generated market estimate as confirmed demand.

Complete records also matter. IRS Publication 583 supports maintaining business records, separate business accounts and bank reconciliation. Those practices help establish a reliable starting cash position; they do not validate an expansion forecast by themselves.

Keep the AI task narrow

A useful request to an AI assistant is:

Using only the approved assumption register, draft proceed, phase and defer schedules. Preserve the same starting cash and existing-practice obligations. Mark missing inputs as unresolved. Do not invent patient volume, payer terms, financing or probability estimates. Identify which commitments occur before supporting evidence is available.

Have the finance team verify the resulting schedules in the underlying model. Keep patient-identifiable information out of ordinary Slack messages and general-purpose AI tools. Aggregate financial information is generally more appropriate for this analysis; workflows involving protected health information require separate privacy review.

Compare alternatives on the same cash basis

Compare actions the owner can actually take—not just optimistic and pessimistic versions of one expansion plan.

The following is an illustrative example only. All amounts, timing assumptions and criteria are invented for explanation, not benchmarks or recommendations.

A practice is considering an additional location. It has $900,000 of unrestricted starting cash and sets a $450,000 protected cash floor. Its existing location is assumed to generate no net cash surplus after all planned payments in the baseline. The owner therefore has $450,000 of headroom before reaching the floor.

The alternatives share a six-month decision window:

Alternative Illustrative commitment plan Baseline opening and collections assumptions
Proceed with full opening Commit to the full premises, build-out and staffing plan Open in month three; model initial receipts after a collection lag
Phase the opening Use a smaller initial footprint and staffing plan, subject to confirmed availability and contract terms Open in month four; defer additional capacity until a later approval
Defer the location Authorize investigation only; no lease, build-out or hiring commitment No new-location receipts or operating costs during the window

Phasing is only a real alternative if the landlord, vendors and staffing plan allow it. Otherwise, it is a hypothetical option that should not appear as available in the decision memo.

In this illustration, the full opening fails the owner’s cash criterion even before stress testing. A favorable future P&L does not solve the cash shortfall unless an acceptable, documented funding source is available when needed.

For each location, keep profitability separate from cash funding. A shared administrative cost allocation affects location P&L, but it is not automatically an additional cash payment. Conversely, a deposit or equipment purchase can consume cash without appearing as an immediate operating expense. Avoid counting either item twice.

Test linked delays before committing

A useful downside case connects events that could occur together. A construction delay may extend rent paid before opening. A provider’s committed start date may create payroll before the location is ready. Later service dates then push collections further out.

For this illustrative downside case, assume opening slips by one month, initial collections arrive later than planned, opening payments increase and the existing practice uses $60,000 of cash over the six-month window. These are stress assumptions, not predicted outcomes.

Six-month downside measure Proceed Phase Defer
Opening or investigation cash $420,000 $210,000 $20,000
New-location operating gap through cash trough $270,000 $165,000 $0
Existing-practice cash use $60,000 $60,000 $60,000
Peak combined cash draw $750,000 $435,000 $80,000
Lowest available cash balance $150,000 $465,000 $820,000
Headroom above protected floor −$300,000 $15,000 $370,000

The phased option remains above the floor, but only narrowly. That does not make it approved. It means unresolved costs or additional delays could change the decision.

These figures measure modeled cash draw within the stated window, not maximum contractual loss. Extend the schedule if cash is still declining at month six. Separately quantify remaining lease obligations, cancellation costs, committed compensation and other payments that survive a pause. Do not approve a project based on a forecast that stops before those obligations become visible.

If the locations sit in separate entities, also test cash availability by entity. A consolidated balance does not establish that every dollar is available to fund the new site. Document proposed transfers or financing with the appropriate advisers before relying on them.

Turn the comparison into a stage-gated decision memo

The memo should identify what the owner is approving today—not authorize the entire project through an ambiguous “go” decision.

Illustrative decision memo: additional location

Decision requested: Authorize up to $20,000 for investigation, included within—not added to—the modeled project costs. Do not authorize the lease, build-out or hiring commitments yet.

Alternatives reviewed: Full opening, phased opening and deferral, using the same starting cash, protected floor and existing-practice assumptions.

Financial finding: Full opening breaches the illustrative protected cash floor in both cases. Phasing preserves $15,000 of downside headroom before unresolved contractual exposure. Deferral preserves the most cash but delays the potential expansion benefit.

Proposed path: Investigate whether the phased alternative is genuinely available. Return for approval only after the outstanding evidence and longer-horizon obligations are quantified. The owner—not the AI assistant—decides whether the remaining risk is acceptable.

Gate Commitment under review Evidence required before approval Hold or defer when
Site investigation Limited diligence spending Written scope, spending cap and accountable project lead Investigation cannot resolve the major cost or timing uncertainties
Lease Deposits and future occupancy obligations Reviewed terms, feasible phased premises, build-out schedule and extended downside cash model Obligations exceed available funding or phase terms remain unconfirmed
Build-out and equipment Purchase orders and construction payments Current quotes, payment milestones, delivery dates and cancellation exposure Unpriced work or payment timing breaks the approved cash limit
Hiring Provider/admin offers and start dates Documented staffing need, lead times, compensation and opening dependencies Payroll would begin before readiness without funded coverage
Additional capacity Remaining space, equipment or staffing Actual aggregate receipts, cost performance and updated commitment schedule Expansion depends on unsupported receipts or threatens existing-practice cash

Record the approver, evidence reviewed, spending limit and conditions for each gate. If a condition fails, bring that commitment back for a decision before releasing funds. This is different from endlessly refreshing a forecast: each review exists to authorize, limit or stop a specific obligation.

FAQs

Should a medical practice sign a lease if the new location is forecast to be profitable?

Not on profitability alone. Review when rent, build-out, payroll and equipment payments occur relative to collections. Test the lowest available cash balance, the existing practice’s protected needs and obligations that remain if opening is delayed or canceled. A profitable forecast can still require more cash than the owner is willing to commit.

How can AI help without inventing demand for the new location?

Restrict it to approved inputs and defined tasks, such as drafting alternative schedules, flagging missing assumptions and summarizing differences. Require every activity and collection assumption to trace to owner-reviewed evidence. Unsupported inputs should remain unresolved—not be filled with a plausible-looking estimate or assigned an invented probability.

When is phasing better than deferring expansion?

Phasing deserves consideration when it genuinely reduces binding commitments while preserving a workable opening plan. It still needs source-supported costs, feasible staffing and sufficient downside cash headroom. Defer when the phased option is unavailable, critical assumptions remain unresolved or commitments would put the existing practice’s cash needs at risk.
