Early-Stage Funding Pitch Checklist for Medical Practices

November 3, 2026 · 10 min read

Opening a staffed medical practice requires more than a convincing description of the services you plan to offer. A lender or prospective eligible equity partner needs to understand what the venture will cost, when collections could support operations, and what happens if the opening or revenue ramp takes longer than expected.

Your funding pitch should connect the request to a documented operating plan: facility commitments, provider and administrative staffing, payer receipts, patient collections and the cash needed between spending and receiving payment.

This checklist is for initial external funding for a new staffed practice or a separately planned new venture. It is not a recurring investor-reporting template or a guide to selling an established owner's existing ownership interest. Those transactions have different financial purposes and supporting requirements.

Before You Build the Presentation

Write a short funding summary that answers:

  • What practice or venture is being funded, and which entity will receive the money?
  • How much is needed, and how much will the owner contribute?
  • What will the funding pay for?
  • Which commitments are already signed, and which depend on financing?
  • When could operating receipts cover operating payments and debt service?
  • What protects the business if receipts arrive later than planned?

Keep uncertain items visible. A pending payer agreement, unsigned lease or tentative provider start date should not appear as a completed milestone.

1. Define the Venture and the Funding Boundary

Describe the practice's services, planned location, staffing model and ownership structure in plain language. Explain who will manage the opening and who is responsible for the financial plan.

For an owner who already operates another practice, distinguish the new venture from existing operations. Identify whether the proposal concerns a new location within an existing entity or a separate entity, subject to professional review of the structure.

Show which costs belong exclusively to the venture and which will be shared. An existing practice might provide administrative support, for example, but that support still consumes resources. Explain the proposed allocation method rather than treating shared labor as free.

If the existing practice will provide cash, disclose the amount and timing. Do not count the same money as both available operating cash for the existing practice and committed startup capital for the new venture.

2. Reconcile the Funding Request to Uses and Owner Contributions

Build a sources-and-uses schedule before choosing the requested amount. Each material use should have a quote, agreement, estimate or clearly stated assumption behind it.

Illustrative example: a new practice funding request

The following figures are invented for illustration, not cost benchmarks or a recommended financing mix.

Uses of funds Illustrative amount Supporting detail
Facility buildout and deposits $180,000 Contractor estimates, lease terms and payment schedule
Equipment and furnishings $70,000 Vendor quotes and purchase timing
Pre-opening setup and staffing $30,000 Recruiting, training and setup budget
Post-opening operating cash deficit $190,000 Monthly cash forecast through the projected low point
Additional cash contingency $30,000 Separate buffer for identified uncertainties
Total uses $500,000
Sources of funds Illustrative amount Evidence needed
Owner cash contribution $125,000 Available funds and proposed contribution timing
Requested external financing $375,000 Proposed financing structure and funding schedule
Total sources $500,000

Separate pre-opening costs from post-opening operating deficits so payroll or rent is not counted twice. Likewise, do not add a contingency that is already embedded in every cost estimate without explaining the overlap.

For the owner's contribution, state whether funds are available now, dependent on another transaction or already spent. Ask the funding provider how it treats documented prior expenditures; do not assume they count toward its contribution requirements.

3. Document Staffing and Facility Commitments

Payroll and occupancy costs can begin before meaningful collections. The pitch should show when commitments become binding and which can still be adjusted.

Commitment What to document Decision the funding plan must address
Provider staffing Start dates, compensation assumptions and planned capacity Does hiring match the realistic opening and volume ramp?
Administrative and support staff Roles, hiring sequence, wages, benefits and employer payroll costs Which positions are needed before opening?
Facility Deposits, rent commencement, buildout obligations and estimated completion What happens financially if opening is delayed?
Equipment Deposits, delivery, installation and financing terms Can purchases be staged without disrupting opening?
Shared services Allocation method and expected payments to related operations Is the venture bearing a reasonable, visible share of costs?

Include owner compensation rather than assuming the owner can work indefinitely without pay. Distinguish compensation for work from ownership distributions.

List major dependencies alongside the budget. If facility readiness or payer participation affects the first collection date, that dependency belongs in the cash forecast—not just in a narrative footnote.

4. Build Revenue Assumptions From Practice Capacity and Collections

A practice funding pitch needs a bottom-up explanation of revenue, not a claim about capturing a small share of a large healthcare market.

Start with provider availability and realistic appointment capacity. Then estimate completed visits, service mix and expected collectible amounts. Explain the basis for each assumption and identify where evidence is still limited.

Illustrative example: Two providers completing 12 visits each per day over 20 working days would produce 480 completed visits. At an assumed average collectible amount of $140 per visit, that represents $67,200 associated with those services. These invented figures are not specialty benchmarks, and they do not establish when cash will arrive.

The forecast must separately address collection timing. Payer receipts and patient balances may be collected after the service month. Avoid treating scheduled appointments, billed charges, recognized revenue and cash receipts as interchangeable.

Useful supporting evidence may include:

  • Documented payer participation status and relevant payment assumptions.
  • Proposed provider schedules and hiring availability.
  • Aggregated historical collections from genuinely comparable operations, with differences explained.
  • Local demand research and documented referral relationships, without assuming referrals guarantee volume.
  • A stated allowance for cancellations, unfilled capacity and amounts that may not be collected.

A pre-revenue practice should label projections as projections. Another location's results can inform assumptions, but they are not the new venture's operating history.

5. Calculate Working Capital Through the Cash Low Point

A profitable-looking annual forecast can still conceal an early cash shortage. Prepare a monthly cash forecast from pre-opening spending through the point when the venture is expected to support itself. Use more detailed timing where major payments or payroll create short-term pressure.

For each period, show:

Beginning cash + financing and owner contributions + operating receipts − operating payments − capital purchases − debt service = ending cash.

Identify the lowest projected cash balance and the additional buffer management wants above that point. Explain why the buffer is appropriate for the uncertainties in this particular plan rather than applying a generic runway target.

Keep the funding calculation consistent with the sources-and-uses schedule. If equipment is paid through separate financing, show the financing proceeds and related payments correctly rather than counting the purchase as both an immediate cash use and an unexplained financed asset.

The forecast should also distinguish available funding from hoped-for funding. An unapproved credit line or possible future partner contribution is not committed liquidity.

6. Test Downside Repayment Capacity

For a loan request, show how cash could support principal and interest under the proposed terms. Accounting profit alone does not establish repayment capacity: equipment purchases, collection delays and other cash obligations also matter.

Prepare a base case and a downside case. Make the downside operationally specific.

Downside event Financial effect to model Possible management response
Opening is delayed Additional pre-opening payroll and occupancy payments Revise hiring dates or spending where commitments permit
Collections arrive later A deeper or longer cash deficit Reassess required working capital and available financing
Completed visits ramp more slowly Lower receipts while fixed costs continue Stage discretionary spending and reassess staffing capacity
Buildout exceeds estimates Greater upfront funding need Reprice scope and identify a documented funding source

Quantify when cash falls below the planned minimum and which response must occur before that date. If the downside case cannot cover debt service, say so. The appropriate decision may be a smaller project, more owner capital, different terms or postponement—not a more optimistic presentation.

For equity discussions, show the same downside cash needs, but do not describe projected distributions as loan-like guaranteed repayments.

7. Use an Annotated Funding-Request Outline

A concise presentation can follow this outline, with detailed schedules in an appendix. Each section should help the recipient evaluate a decision rather than repeat the practice's mission.

Section What to include Question for a lender Question for a prospective eligible equity partner
Venture and management Services, location, responsible leaders and execution experience Who will operate the business and manage repayment? Who will manage the venture and make key decisions?
Funding request Amount, recipient entity and proposed structure What borrowing terms and funding conditions apply? What ownership and economic terms are proposed, subject to review?
Sources and uses Owner contribution, external funding and documented spending Are costs and required contributions supported? How much capital is committed, and might more be needed?
Operating model Staffing, facility and collection assumptions What supports reliable cash generation? What drives sustainable earnings and potential distributions?
Forecast and downside Cash low point, debt service and response plan Can obligations be met under slower performance? How would losses or additional capital needs be handled?
Supporting records Agreements, quotes, financial schedules and open issues What documentation remains before a credit decision? What diligence and professional review remain before commitment?

Ask prospective lenders about documentation, collateral, guarantees, reporting requirements and restrictions that could affect operations. Do not assume these are identical across offers.

Before pursuing equity, have qualified counsel assess ownership eligibility, proposed structure, governance and applicable requirements. Financial projections do not establish that a prospective partner may legally own an interest. Have the financial and legal advisers review proposed compensation and distribution arrangements before presenting them as settled terms.

8. Assemble Supporting Records and Reconcile the Package

Create an organized document index that ties each major forecast assumption to its support. Include:

  • Formation and ownership documents, as applicable.
  • Owner contribution evidence and a schedule of prior startup spending.
  • Facility documents, equipment quotes and material commitments.
  • Staffing plans and supporting compensation estimates.
  • Payer participation status and the basis for collection assumptions.
  • The sources-and-uses schedule, monthly forecast and downside model.
  • Available financial statements, bank reconciliations and debt schedules.
  • Related-entity support commitments and shared-cost allocation methods.

A new practice may have little operating history. Provide actual startup activity and opening balances rather than manufacturing historical results. Label existing-practice financials separately when they are included as context or support.

The IRS business recordkeeping guidance supports complete records, separate business accounts and bank reconciliation. These practices also help explain how owner funding and startup expenditures move through the books.

Use aggregated financial information for the funding package wherever possible. Patient-identifiable records generally are not needed to explain visit volumes or collection assumptions. If a proposed disclosure could involve protected health information, obtain appropriate review before sharing it. HHS explains that accounting services can involve business-associate responsibilities when PHI is involved.

Final Review Before Outreach

Reconcile the requested amount across the summary, presentation, forecast and supporting schedules. Date the package, assign responsibility for unresolved assumptions and distinguish signed commitments from estimates.

FAQs

What should I say at the start of a medical practice funding pitch?

State what you are opening, the amount requested, the owner contribution and the main uses of funds. Briefly explain how the practice expects to generate collections and which opening dependencies remain unresolved. Keep the figures consistent with the detailed forecast.

What evidence can a practice provide before it has revenue?

Provide documented startup costs, staffing plans, facility commitments, payer participation status and a capacity-based collection forecast. Use relevant comparable operating data cautiously and explain differences. Pre-opening evidence supports assumptions; it is not proof that projected collections will occur.

How much working capital should a new practice request?

Base the amount on the forecast cash low point, collection timing, debt service and a separately explained contingency. Avoid a fixed rule based only on months of expenses. Test delayed opening and slower collections before finalizing the request.

How is an initial funding pitch different from investor reporting or an owner sale?

An initial funding pitch explains how new capital will establish and sustain the venture. Recurring investor reports compare actual results with the plan after funding. An established-owner secondary sale transfers an existing ownership interest and does not necessarily put new cash into the practice. Keep these purposes and cash flows separate.
