Cross-Border Cloud Vendor Reviews for Medical Practices

November 3, 2026 · 9 min read

A finance or payroll vendor may serve a U.S. medical practice while using infrastructure, subcontractors or support teams in other countries. Selecting a U.S. storage region does not, by itself, answer who can access the data, where backups reside or what happens when a support ticket includes an attachment.

For a practice owner, the immediate task is not to interpret every international privacy law. It is to assemble a reliable, vendor-specific record so IT, privacy counsel and the CPA team can evaluate the proposed arrangement before sensitive records move.

This review should support a concrete decision: whether to use the vendor, limit its scope, negotiate terms or pause implementation. It should also protect financial continuity—payroll processing, access to accounting records, entity-level reporting and the ability to export records if the relationship ends.

Start with the service your practice will actually buy

Review the application and contracting entity, not just the cloud infrastructure brand behind it. A payroll application hosted on a major cloud platform has its own support arrangements, subprocessors, contract terms and data-handling practices.

Document the proposed service before requesting assurances:

  • Which practice entities and locations will use it?
  • Will it handle payroll, bookkeeping, accounts payable, expense management or financial reporting?
  • Which records will staff upload, and which systems will send data to it?
  • Will the CPA team, outside bookkeepers or other advisers receive access?
  • Is this a new implementation, renewal or expansion of an existing service?

A vendor review for aggregate location P&Ls is different from one for payroll records containing employee identifiers and bank details. Patient-level payment files create a different set of questions again.

Applicability depends on the actual data, parties, activities and jurisdictions. Overseas involvement is a reason to investigate, not a standalone conclusion that a service is permitted or prohibited.

Build a data-flow inventory before reviewing assurances

Use one inventory for the proposed configuration. Record what is confirmed, the evidence supporting it and what remains unknown. Do not convert an unanswered question into an assumption that data stays in the United States.

The following is an illustrative inventory structure, not a description of any vendor’s capabilities.

Data category Practice source and purpose Locations and access to establish Evidence or unresolved question
General ledger and bank activity Entity-level accounts, reconciliations and monthly close Production storage, backups, adviser access and support access Can transaction descriptions or attachments contain patient information?
Payroll and employee records Provider/admin payroll, deductions and payroll funding Processing countries, support locations and payroll subprocessors Which employee identifiers and bank details are exposed to each party?
Payer receipts and patient balances Deposit reconciliation and receivables analysis Import staging, report storage, exports and troubleshooting access Can the financial purpose be met with aggregate or reduced-detail files?
Vendor invoices and expense receipts Accounts payable and shared-cost allocation Attachment storage, document-processing services and support tools Do invoices or receipts contain employee or patient details?
Location/entity reporting Per-location P&L and consolidated management reporting Report storage, emailed copies and external adviser access Are shared-cost schedules appropriately limited to authorized users?
Support tickets and diagnostic files Resolving errors and failed imports Ticketing systems, support staff countries and log storage Do screenshots, logs or attachments reproduce sensitive records?

For each flow, identify the sender, recipient, purpose, data fields, storage or processing country, access method and onward recipient. Distinguish live records from backups, temporary import files, diagnostic logs and exported reports.

Keep the inventory itself free of unnecessary sensitive information. Field names and sanitized samples usually serve the discovery process better than live payroll files or patient account screenshots.

Separate storage location from access location

Ask the vendor to distinguish:

  • Primary application storage and processing.
  • Backup and disaster-recovery locations.
  • Subprocessors that receive or can access data.
  • Routine support access and exceptional engineering access.
  • Ticketing, monitoring and diagnostic systems.
  • Copies created through exports, implementation work or account closure.

“U.S.-hosted” is incomplete if it addresses only primary storage. Ask whether personnel outside the United States can view, retrieve or download records, and under what circumstances. Record exact countries where available; leave unresolved locations visible for review.

Request evidence tied to the proposed service

A marketing page or general security statement is a starting point, not a complete review packet. Request documents that apply to the actual product, service tier, contracting entity and configuration under consideration.

Document or response What to establish Lead reviewer
Order form and service agreement Contracting parties, purchased scope and incorporated terms Owner and counsel
Data-processing terms Described processing, responsibilities and relevant location commitments Privacy counsel
Subprocessor list Provider identity, function, location information and change process IT and privacy counsel
Written residency and support response Storage, backups, remote access and exceptions for this service IT
Available independent assurance reports Scope, reporting period, exclusions and customer responsibilities IT; CPA team where relevant

Export, retention and deletion terms Record retrieval, termination handling and backup treatment IT, CPA team and counsel

Keep dated copies or version references. If a sales response differs from the contract, flag the discrepancy rather than treating the more favorable statement as controlling.

Independent reports can inform a review, but they do not establish that every service, configuration or data flow is covered. Nor does the infrastructure provider’s documentation replace the application vendor’s answers.

Address patient information explicitly

Do not assume that all accounting data is protected health information, or that finance work can never involve it. The HHS overview of the HIPAA Privacy Rule explains covered entities and business associates; accounting services can involve business-associate responsibilities when PHI is involved.

HHS cloud guidance explains that a cloud service maintaining ePHI on behalf of a covered entity or business associate is a business associate even if it cannot decrypt the information. Encryption alone does not settle the required contractual and safeguard questions. HHS also does not endorse or certify particular cloud products.

Have privacy counsel evaluate the actual arrangement. Where financial analysis can use less patient information, design the workflow accordingly, taking account of the HHS minimum necessary guidance and its exceptions.

Assemble an example vendor-review packet

Consider an illustrative practice evaluating a cloud payroll service for several locations under separate entities. The vendor says production data is stored in the United States, but its initial response does not identify overseas support locations.

A useful packet would contain the following items—not simply a folder of policy documents.

1. Decision cover sheet

State the business purpose, affected entities, proposed launch, internal sponsor and current status. Identify the decision needed: for example, whether to authorize implementation after specified questions are resolved.

Include the operational dependency. If payroll is moving, describe how the practice will continue paying staff if implementation is delayed. Do not let a planned launch date substitute for an approval decision.

2. Data-flow inventory and sanitized sample

List proposed payroll fields, source systems, recipients and known locations. Include a blank template or sanitized sample to show the intended upload without distributing live employee records.

Identify optional fields and attachments separately. A feature being available does not mean the practice needs to populate it.

3. Evidence index

Index the contract, data-processing terms, subprocessor list, available reports and written vendor responses. For each item, record its date, applicable product and any scope limitation.

4. Open-question register

Assign each question an internal owner, vendor contact and required resolution point. In this example, questions could include:

  • Which countries can support personnel access payroll records from?
  • Does troubleshooting permit downloads or copies into a separate ticketing system?
  • Which subprocessors handle the proposed payroll service?
  • What location commitments appear in the contract?
  • What records and supporting documents can the practice export at termination?

5. Recommendation and decision log

Separate technical findings, legal questions and financial-operating requirements. The packet should show who reviewed each issue and why the final decision follows from the evidence.

Use a decision log that makes uncertainty visible

The following illustrative entries show how to record decisions without declaring a vendor broadly “compliant.”

Issue Current evidence or gap Reviewer Decision or next action
Overseas support access U.S. production storage confirmed; support countries unanswered IT and privacy counsel Keep approval pending for sensitive-data use until reviewed
Patient information in reconciliation files Proposed export includes patient-level detail CPA team and privacy counsel Evaluate a reduced-detail reconciliation workflow before upload
Separate payroll entities Implementation plan does not explain entity-specific exports CPA team and IT Request sample exports and confirm entity-level reconciliation
Contract mismatch Sales email describes restrictions absent from draft terms Counsel and owner Resolve the discrepancy before signing
Exit access Reports are described, but supporting-document export is unclear CPA team and IT Confirm retrieval scope and test an export before migration

Record the decision date, approver, approved scope, conditions, supporting evidence and review trigger. Possible outcomes include approval for a defined use, approval subject to completed conditions, a narrower pilot using sanitized data, deferral or rejection.

A material unknown should remain an unknown. IT can assess technical controls and access paths; privacy counsel can assess applicable obligations and contractual questions; the CPA team can assess record completeness, reporting and reconciliation needs. The owner coordinates the business decision rather than asking one adviser to cover every discipline.

Protect financial continuity during contracting and implementation

Cross-border discovery should not obscure the practice’s accounting requirements. Before migration, confirm that the proposed workflow preserves:

  • Separate entity records and appropriate location reporting.
  • Payroll funding and reconciliation procedures.
  • Supporting documents needed to explain transactions and adjustments.
  • Usable exports of ledgers, payroll reports and relevant attachments.
  • A transition plan if the vendor relationship ends.

IRS Publication 583 supports complete business records, separate business accounts and bank reconciliation. A vendor’s export feature is useful only if it returns the records the practice actually needs.

Ask the CPA team and counsel to establish applicable retention requirements before accepting deletion settings. Vendor defaults are not a practice retention schedule, and termination should not trigger disposal of records still needed for tax, employment or other obligations.

After approval, compare the implemented configuration with the reviewed scope. Revisit the packet when the vendor changes subprocessors or location terms, the practice adds an entity, a new integration introduces different data, or support requests broader access.

Where the finance team fits

A CPA-led finance team can help identify required records, reduce unnecessary detail in financial reporting and test whether exports support reconciliation and entity-level reporting. IT and privacy counsel address their respective technical and legal questions.

FAQs

Does U.S. data storage rule out cross-border access?

No. Primary storage, backups, processing, support access and diagnostic systems may have different locations. Request service-specific answers and identify any exceptions to the vendor’s location commitments.

Does overseas support automatically make a vendor unacceptable?

No blanket conclusion follows from overseas support alone. The assessment depends on the actual data, parties, jurisdictions, access arrangements and contracts. Document those facts for IT and privacy counsel rather than inferring permission or prohibition from a country name.

Is a certification or assurance report enough to approve the vendor?

No. Review its scope and limitations alongside the proposed data flows, contract terms, subprocessors and customer responsibilities. A report about infrastructure does not answer every question about the application vendor or the practice’s configuration.

What should happen when the vendor cannot answer a material question?

Record the gap, assign an owner and decide whether it blocks the proposed use. The practice might pause sensitive-data uploads, narrow the scope or evaluate another vendor. Do not mark the issue resolved merely because implementation is scheduled.
