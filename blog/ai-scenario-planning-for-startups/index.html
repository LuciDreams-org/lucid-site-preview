AI Scenario Planning for Medical Practice Decision Dependencies

November 3, 2026 · 10 min read

A medical practice expansion forecast can look affordable while describing an impossible sequence: patient visits begin before the space is ready, new providers generate receipts before their start dates, or financing arrives before its funding conditions are satisfied.

The problem is not necessarily the arithmetic. It is that linked assumptions have been allowed to change independently.

Dependency-based scenario planning connects operational milestones to their financial consequences. If build-out slips, the model should reconsider opening dates, payroll commitments, financing availability and collection timing together—not simply move revenue to another month.

AI can help propose these connections and identify inconsistencies for review. But AI-generated causal relationships are hypotheses, not evidence. Practice leadership, operational owners and the finance team must verify each relationship before it drives a decision.

This workflow helps owners and managers of staffed US private medical practices answer a specific question: Which commitments can we safely make now, and which should wait until a prerequisite is confirmed?

Start With the Decision and Its Prerequisites

Define the decision narrowly. “Expand the practice” is too broad. “Authorize the next build-out payment and confirm staff start dates for a second location” identifies commitments that a dependency model can evaluate.

Then separate three kinds of relationships:

  • Hard prerequisites: An activity cannot start until another condition is met. For example, management may require the premises and essential equipment to be ready before opening.
  • Timing relationships: One event starts a clock for another. Completed services may lead to payer receipts later, according to the practice's actual collection experience.
  • Associated assumptions: Two outcomes may move together without one necessarily causing the other. A slower provider recruitment process and higher recruiting expense may belong in the same stress scenario, but neither should automatically determine the other.

This distinction prevents false precision. A signed financing agreement may establish a funding condition. A pattern in historical data may suggest an association. An AI suggestion alone establishes neither.

Avoid assigning scenario probabilities unless there is a defensible basis. Owners can compare a delayed opening and a financing interruption without claiming to know their likelihood.

Build a Dependency Map Before Changing Forecast Cells

Use a map that connects the operational event, its prerequisite and the affected cash flows. Keep it readable enough for the practice manager, owner and finance reviewer to challenge together.

The following is a planning template, not a statement that every practice has these requirements. Each relationship needs local verification.

Decision or milestone Proposed dependency to verify Financial consequence if delayed Person responsible for confirmation
Complete build-out Contractor milestones, access and required completion conditions Additional project costs; continued occupancy costs without new-location receipts Project lead
Receive financing proceeds Executed funding conditions and lender release timing Cash may leave before financing becomes available Owner and finance lead
Start provider and administrative staff Employment commitments, readiness and training schedule Payroll may begin before the location produces receipts Practice manager
Open the location Premises, equipment, staffing and other applicable readiness requirements Visits and related collections begin later Operations lead
Receive payer payments Services performed and applicable payer and billing readiness Receipts lag opening; timing may differ by payer Revenue-cycle lead
Receive patient balances Services performed and actual collection patterns Some cash arrives after the initial service period Revenue-cycle lead
Cover shared administrative costs Existing central-office capacity and expansion workload Added support costs may arise before opening Practice manager and finance lead

A compact sequence might be:

Verified funding conditions → available financing → remaining project payments → location readiness → visits → payer and patient receipts.

Payroll can run alongside that sequence rather than waiting for it. Rent may follow a contractual date instead of the opening date. Those parallel commitments often explain why a delayed opening consumes cash even when some operating expenses are deferred.

Do Not Force Every Cost to Move With Opening

Classify material commitments by what actually controls them:

  • Calendar-based: Payments due on specified dates, regardless of opening.
  • Milestone-based: Payments triggered by completion, delivery or another verified event.
  • Activity-based: Expenses that change with visits, staffing hours or another operating driver.
  • Management-controlled: Spending that can still be deferred, subject to existing commitments.

Do not assume staff start dates or vendor payments can be postponed without consequence. Review the underlying agreements before treating a cost as avoidable.

Use AI to Challenge the Map, Not Approve It

Give AI a de-identified milestone list, summarized financial assumptions and the proposed dependency map. Ask it to identify missing links, contradictions and questions—not to certify readiness.

A useful request is:

Review this proposed location-opening sequence. Identify assumptions that cannot reasonably vary independently, distinguish hard prerequisites from possible associations, and list evidence management should obtain. Do not invent contract terms, payer requirements or financing conditions.

AI may flag a forecast that starts receipts before visits, or one that moves the opening date without moving a conditional loan draw. These are review prompts. A plausible explanation is not proof that the relationship applies to the practice.

For financial analysis, prefer location-level totals, staffing roles and summarized receipts over patient-level records. Do not place patient-identifiable information in ordinary Slack conversations or a general-purpose AI tool. The HHS minimum necessary guidance provides relevant context for limiting information use where that standard applies; the practice should confirm its specific workflow requirements.

Record Approved Relationships in an Assumption Register

The dependency map shows connections. The assumption register records why those connections are accepted and who must update them.

The entries below are illustrative planning entries, not verified facts about any practice.

Assumption Evidence to obtain or review Approved modeling treatment Owner and update trigger

Financing is released only after a readiness milestone Executed financing documents and lender confirmation Move the draw with the verified release condition Finance lead; update when conditions or timing change
Certain staff costs begin before opening Employment commitments and training plan Preserve committed payroll even when opening slips Practice manager; update after staffing changes

Add a status—proposed, verified or unresolved—and a last-reviewed date. Material unresolved assumptions should have an alternative scenario or remain explicit approval conditions, rather than silently becoming base-case facts.

Illustrative Scenario: A Two-Month Opening Delay

All dates, dollar amounts, collection patterns and financing terms in this example are hypothetical. They demonstrate dependency logic, not expected results for a medical practice.

An owner is deciding whether available cash can support a new location if its opening moves from May to July.

Hypothetical Inputs

  • Cash available for the project on April 30 is $300,000, after reserving funds needed by the existing practice.
  • The base opening date is May 1; the delayed opening date is July 1.
  • A $100,000 financing draw becomes available in the opening month, conditional on a verified readiness milestone. No debt repayments fall within this illustrative period.
  • Remaining build-out payments are $40,000 in May. The delay adds $20,000 in June.
  • Pre-opening payroll and other cash operating costs are $30,000 monthly. Once open, total monthly operating cash costs are $60,000.
  • Collections are zero in the opening month, then $50,000, $80,000 and $100,000 in successive months. This simplified pattern shifts with opening.
  • The owner's illustrative minimum project cash reserve is $200,000.

The example excludes other cash movements. A real decision requires a longer forecast with actual financing terms, capital commitments and ongoing cash needs.

The calculation is:

Ending cash = beginning cash + receipts + financing proceeds − build-out outflows − operating outflows.

The delayed case falls to $180,000 in June, below the hypothetical reserve, even though financing arrives in July. August cash is $140,000 below the base case: receipts are $180,000 lower, operating outflows are $60,000 lower and build-out costs are $20,000 higher.

This is not simply “two months of lost revenue.” Receipts are shifted beyond the comparison period, some costs are deferred, other costs continue, and financing arrives later. The forecast should extend far enough to show the continuing collection ramp and later obligations.

What Should the Owner Decide?

The immediate issue is the June cash shortfall relative to the owner's reserve—not just the August balance.

Before authorizing additional commitments, management could evaluate whether to defer genuinely discretionary spending, revise staffing timing where feasible, or secure confirmed funding available before the low point. None of those actions should enter the forecast as a solution until its feasibility and terms are verified.

If the existing location would fund the gap, show the transfer separately and test the effect on its payroll and operating cash. For multiple entities, do not treat every bank balance as freely available; have the finance team review the proposed funding arrangement and document the relevant transactions.

Validate Formulas and Timing Before Acting

A human finance reviewer must validate formulas, including any formulas proposed by AI. Correct-looking totals can hide incorrect sequencing.

Use these checks:

  1. Trace each changed milestone. A delayed opening should update affected visits, receipts, costs and conditional funding—not unrelated commitments.
  2. Test prerequisite logic. Opening must not precede any required readiness date. Funding must not arrive before its verified release condition.
  3. Check cash versus profit. Loan proceeds increase cash, not operating revenue. Capital payments and debt principal do not belong in operating profit merely because they use cash.
  4. Check cost duplication. A shared administrative allocation may affect a location P&L without creating an additional practice-wide cash payment.
  5. Reconcile opening balances. Start with reconciled cash and identify outstanding commitments. IRS Publication 583 supports complete business records and bank reconciliation.
  6. Inspect the lowest cash point. Monthly ending balances can conceal a payroll or vendor-payment gap within the month. Review shorter intervals around major commitments.

Record the reviewer, version and unresolved items. When an actual milestone changes, update its dependent assumptions and retain a brief explanation of the revision.

Turn the Scenario Into an Approval Rule

The final output should be a decision record, not just a forecast. State which commitment is approved, which prerequisites remain open, who verifies them and what change triggers another review.

For example: “Do not authorize the next discretionary expansion payment until financing availability and the revised pre-opening cash requirement are confirmed.” That is more actionable than labeling a scenario “pessimistic.”

FAQs

What is a decision dependency in medical practice scenario planning?

It is a relationship in which one event constrains another event's timing or financial effect. A location cannot generate receipts from services it has not yet provided, for example. Other dependencies—such as financing release conditions—must be verified against the practice's actual arrangements.

Can AI determine which assumptions are causally linked?

AI can suggest relationships and flag contradictions, but its suggestions are hypotheses, not evidence. Management should verify them using contracts, operational plans, collection history and responsible-person confirmation before including them in an approved scenario.

Should every assumption worsen in a delayed-opening scenario?

No. Some costs may fall or move later, while committed payroll, rent or project payments continue. Financing and collections may also shift. Change each assumption according to its verified dependency rather than applying a blanket adjustment.

What should owners review when a dependency changes?

Review the affected milestones, the earliest cash shortfall, funding availability and commitments that are still avoidable. Then update the assumption register and obtain human validation of revised formulas before making the next commitment.
