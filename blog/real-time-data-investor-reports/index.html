Using Current Data in Medical Practice Investor Reports

November 3, 2026 · 9 min read

An investor asks whether your practice can fund an additional location. Your latest closed financial statements cover last month, but you have today’s bank balance and a recent collections report. Which numbers can you responsibly share?

The answer depends on what each number establishes. A bank balance shows cash recorded by the bank at a particular time. A collections report shows receipts recorded under that report’s definitions and cutoff. Neither, by itself, establishes current revenue, profitability, or cash available for expansion.

Current information can supplement closed-period reporting without pretending to be closed-period reporting. For a staffed private medical practice, a useful interim investor update identifies the source, effective date, unresolved items, and person responsible for review. It also explains how the update will connect to the next closed-period report.

Start With the Investor’s Decision

Before sending new figures, identify the question they need to answer. Is the investor asking about near-term liquidity, a change in collections, or the earnings of a particular location? These questions require different evidence.

Investor question Useful current information What it cannot establish alone
Can the practice meet upcoming payroll and vendor payments? Bank balances, known outgoing payments, and expected receipts shown separately Distributable cash or sustainable profitability
Have collections changed since the last report? Comparable payer and patient receipt totals with matching cutoffs Revenue earned during the period or the cause of the change
Is a location performing better? Preliminary location results with allocation status disclosed Final location profitability before shared costs and adjustments are reviewed
Can an entity support a planned investment? Entity-specific cash, obligations, and current forecast assumptions Cash available from other entities or certainty about future collections

Choose the smallest set of measures that answers the question. More frequently refreshed numbers are not necessarily better evidence.

For a distribution or expansion decision, for example, a current cash observation can flag a concern. It should not become an approval to spend without considering obligations, restrictions, and the practice’s review process.

Distinguish Four Types of Financial Evidence

1. Bank balances: a current cash observation

Record whether the figure is a posted balance or an available balance, which accounts it includes, and when it was obtained. Pending transactions, outstanding checks, and scheduled payments may change the interpretation.

A payroll withdrawal that has not reached the bank can make cash appear more available than it is. An incoming deposit may also represent borrowing or an owner contribution rather than patient-care receipts.

Share a bank balance as a dated observation—not as profit, revenue, or unrestricted spending capacity. Keep known commitments alongside it, without presenting projected cash as an actual bank balance.

2. Preliminary collections: recorded receipts, not necessarily current-period revenue

A billing-system collections report and a bank deposit report may cover different events. A payer payment can be recorded before its deposit settles. A bank deposit may combine several receipts. Patient refunds, reversals, and posting corrections may appear later.

State whether collections means gross receipts, receipts net of refunds, or another consistently defined measure. Identify whether the cutoff uses payment date, posting date, or deposit date.

Collections may relate to services from earlier periods. They can help explain cash movement, but should not be relabeled as revenue earned this month.

3. Preliminary accrued results: a current estimate of performance

For a practice reporting on an accrual basis, interim results may include revenue estimates, unpaid expenses, payroll accruals, and other adjustments. These can offer a more relevant view of performance than cash alone, but only when the preparation method and missing work are clear.

Provider and administrative payroll may not yet include all compensation earned through the reporting date. Vendor invoices may be missing. Revenue estimates may still require review of contractual adjustments or collectibility. Shared administrative costs may not yet be allocated across locations.

Do not describe a partially updated ledger as a completed accrual P&L. Identify which adjustments have been posted and which remain estimates or omissions. If the practice reports on another accounting basis, name that basis rather than implying accrual reporting.

4. Reconciled, closed-period statements: the reporting baseline

Closed-period statements should remain the reference point for comparisons, with their accounting basis and scope stated. Closing work includes reconciliation and review of relevant balances and adjustments; it does not merely mean downloading the latest transactions.

Reconciliation also has a scope. A reconciled bank account does not establish that payroll accruals, revenue adjustments, or location allocations are complete. An internally closed report is not automatically audited or independently assured.

The IRS guidance on business records supports keeping complete records, separating business accounts, and reconciling bank records. Those practices help substantiate reporting, but do not turn an interim extract into final financial statements.

Explain Why a Fresh Number Can Still Be Incomplete

Use three timestamps when they differ:

  • Activity cutoff: The latest transaction or service period included.
  • Source timestamp: When the underlying report or balance was generated.
  • Review timestamp: When the finance reviewer checked it for release.

A report generated today might contain activity posted only through an earlier date. A ledger refreshed this morning may still exclude an invoice received this afternoon. A collections total may include late postings for prior service periods.

For each measure, describe the most consequential delay. Avoid a vague “data may change” disclaimer when the actual gap is known. “Administrative payroll accrual pending” is more useful than “preliminary” alone.

If an important source is unavailable, retain its actual cutoff or omit the measure. Do not carry an old figure into a new update under a fresh date without identifying it as unchanged.

The accompanying narrative should explain the decision implications. In this example, the bank observation and outgoing commitments help frame liquidity, but they do not establish a cash surplus. Collections remain provisional, and current earnings are deliberately withheld because significant adjustments are unfinished.

Next reporting milestone: The illustrative October closed report is planned for November 13. The finance lead will reconcile the interim measures to the comparable closed-period figures, explain differences, and identify any outstanding exceptions. If the target changes, the owner will communicate the revised timing rather than silently leaving the interim update in place.

Set Review and Release Responsibilities

Assign responsibility before the next investor request. In a smaller practice, one person may hold several roles, but preparation and approval should still be explicit.

Responsibility Suggested owner Required action
Source preparation Practice manager or designated finance preparer Preserve the source extract, cutoff, definitions, and entity scope
Financial review CPA, controller, or designated finance lead Check classifications, reconciliation status, missing adjustments, and consistency with prior reports
Release approval Practice owner or authorized executive Confirm the update answers the investor’s question and approve its limitations
Correction and close bridge Named finance lead Track changes, notify the approver, and connect provisional figures to the next close

Use a short release check: Is the source identifiable? Is the measure defined? Are known gaps disclosed? Would a reader mistake it for final results?

If those questions cannot be answered, share a narrower statement or explain when the information will be available. A supported bank observation is preferable to an unsupported profit estimate.

Keep Entity and Location Comparisons Consistent

A multi-location practice should distinguish location performance from entity cash. One entity may hold the bank account while several locations share payroll and administrative costs. A strong collections week at one site does not establish that site’s fully allocated profit.

For interim comparisons, preserve the same location definitions and shared-cost treatment used in the closed baseline. If allocations are unfinished, label results “before shared-cost allocation” rather than presenting them as final location earnings.

Similarly, do not combine entity bank balances without explaining the scope. Intercompany transfers are not new patient receipts. If eliminating transfers or reconciling entity balances remains unfinished, disclose that limitation or withhold the combined total.

Correct Updates and Bridge Them to the Close

An interim update needs a visible correction process, not just a disclaimer.

  1. Preserve the issued version. Keep its date, sources, assumptions, and approval record.
  2. Record the change. Identify the original figure, revised figure, reason, affected period, and reviewer.
  3. Assess the decision impact. Escalate changes that could affect payroll planning, investment, distributions, or the reported direction of performance.
  4. Notify recipients when warranted. Do not wait for the next routine report if the earlier update could mislead an active decision.
  5. Complete the bridge. In the next closed report, explain how the interim figures changed through additional activity, timing differences, reclassifications, refunds, accruals, or corrections.

Compare like with like. Bridge interim collections to the comparable closed receipt measure, then separately explain their relationship to reported revenue. Do not present the difference between collections and accrual revenue as though it were necessarily an error.

Share Financial Evidence Without Patient Detail

Investor updates generally need aggregate financial information, not patient-level billing records. Summarize payer receipts and patient balances without names, account identifiers, or clinical details. Review supporting files before sharing them.

HHS guidance on the minimum necessary standard explains the standard and its exceptions. Confirm the applicable requirements for the practice and recipients rather than treating an investor request as permission to disclose patient information. Do not place patient-identifiable data in ordinary Slack messages or general-purpose AI tools.

Use Current Data as a Supplement, Not a Substitute

A responsible investor update says what is known now, what remains unfinished, and when the figures will be reconciled. It does not require an always-on dashboard or a promise of real-time integration.

FAQs

Can we share a bank balance before the monthly close?

Yes, as a dated bank observation with its account scope and balance type identified. Disclose known outgoing commitments and incomplete reconciliation. Do not describe the balance as profit or cash available for distribution.

Should preliminary collections appear beside closed-period revenue?

They can, but label them as different measures. Explain the collection cutoff, treatment of refunds, and posting delays. Avoid calculating a growth rate between collections and accrual revenue; compare consistently defined measures over comparable periods.

What if current earnings are too incomplete to share?

Withhold the earnings figure and name the missing work, such as provider compensation accruals or revenue adjustments. Share narrower supported observations if useful, and provide the next review milestone. A partially completed P&L should not be presented as final performance.

Does every change require a separate investor correction?

Not necessarily. Establish escalation criteria based on decision impact and reporting significance. Distinguish expected period progression from errors in an issued update. If a change could mislead an active decision, communicate it promptly and explain the correction again in the next closed-period bridge.
