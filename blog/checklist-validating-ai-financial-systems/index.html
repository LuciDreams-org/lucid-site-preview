Checklist for Validating AI Financial Systems in Medical Practices

November 3, 2026 · 10 min read

Before an AI-assisted financial system helps prepare your practice’s books or management reports, establish what it must do—and demonstrate that it does it. A plausible expense classification or polished location P&L is not enough. The output must agree with source records, follow approved accounting treatments and reach the right reviewer when something goes wrong.

For an owner or practice manager, validation is an acceptance decision: Can this specific workflow be used for its stated purpose, with defined limits and human oversight? Run that process before launch and repeat the affected tests after material changes.

This checklist focuses on accounting workflows for staffed US private medical practices. It does not validate clinical decisions, certify a vendor’s security or establish legal compliance. Passing a test does not prove universal accuracy or justify removing human review.

1. Define exactly what you are accepting

Validate a bounded workflow rather than an entire product described as “AI accounting.” Write a short acceptance statement identifying:

  • Purpose: For example, propose expense classifications for staff review or prepare a draft per-location P&L.
  • Inputs: Approved bank exports, payroll summaries, ledger records and allocation schedules.
  • Outputs: Draft entries, exception lists, reconciliations or management reports.
  • Boundaries: Which entities, accounts, locations and reporting periods are included?
  • Restrictions: Can the system suggest entries only, or can an authorized person release them for posting?
  • Reviewers: Who checks results, who approves accounting treatments and who authorizes launch?

A useful scope statement might be: “Prepare draft location expense reports from approved ledger data using the CPA-reviewed allocation schedule. Do not change source entries, move cash or release final reports without review.”

Exclude unsupported workflows explicitly. Acceptance for expense classification does not establish suitability for payroll processing, payer-contract analysis or tax calculations.

Keep three different reviews separate

Review Main question Typical evidence
System validation Does the configured workflow behave as intended under defined conditions? Test inputs, expected results, actual outputs and documented acceptance
Routine financial review Are this month’s books and reports complete and accurate? Bank reconciliations, payroll tie-outs, exception resolution and report review
Vendor security and legal review Is the arrangement appropriate for the data, access and obligations involved? Relevant vendor documentation, agreements and specialist review

None replaces the others. Successful validation does not make monthly reconciliations optional.

2. Assign acceptance and rollback responsibilities

Name people, not just departments. In a smaller practice, one person may hold several responsibilities, but material financial treatments should still receive CPA review.

Responsibility Assigned role Required decision or action
Business acceptance Owner or designated practice leader Approve scope, restrictions and launch decision
Test coordination Practice manager or finance lead Maintain test files, evidence and unresolved-issue register
Financial treatment approval CPA Review classification, timing, allocation and entity-accounting assumptions
Configuration and access Authorized administrator or vendor contact Implement approved settings and resolve technical failures
Exception review Named finance reviewer, with a backup Investigate rejected, ambiguous or inconsistent items
Rollback execution Named administrator, coordinated with finance lead Stop affected processing and restore the approved fallback workflow

The owner should know who can stop the workflow immediately. Do not make the vendor’s availability the only way to prevent further incorrect output.

3. Prepare representative cases with known expected results

Use synthetic accounting records without patient-identifiable data. Fictional payer labels, aggregate receipt amounts, employee role labels and dummy account identifiers can test many accounting behaviors without exposing real patient information.

Avoid assuming that removing patient names makes a real file safe to use. For these acceptance tests, create fictional records from the outset. Do not put patient-identifiable information into ordinary Slack messages or a general-purpose AI tool.

HHS explains that business-associate responsibilities can arise when services involve protected health information. Any proposed use of actual sensitive data requires its own appropriate review; synthetic testing is not a substitute for that review.

Establish the answer before running the test

Have the finance reviewer prepare the expected result independently. Obtain CPA approval where the answer depends on an accounting treatment, such as payroll accruals, shared-cost allocations or intercompany balances.

Record the accounting basis, reporting period, chart of accounts and allocation policy. Otherwise, a difference may reflect an unstated assumption rather than a system defect.

The amounts below are illustrative examples only, not practice benchmarks or recommended accounting policies.

Add cases that reflect your practice’s complexity: refunds, reversals, negative amounts, closed periods and new locations. A clean single-location example is not sufficient evidence for a multi-entity rollout.

4. Reconcile sources to outputs

Test the full path from source file through draft output—not just whether a final total looks reasonable.

For each case:

  1. Record source totals, row counts, date range, entity and location identifiers.
  2. Identify excluded, rejected or transformed records and explain why.
  3. Compare accepted records with resulting classifications or entries.
  4. Reconcile output totals to the source, including documented reconciling items.
  5. Inspect account and location detail, not only the grand total.
  6. Confirm that generated summaries use the reconciled figures.

A location report can total correctly while assigning payroll to the wrong office. Likewise, an entity-level report can appear reasonable while treating an intercompany transfer as income.

The IRS guide to starting a business and keeping records supports complete business records, separate business accounts and bank reconciliation. Those basic controls remain relevant when AI assists with financial work.

For fixed accounting amounts in a controlled test, require exact agreement unless an explicitly documented rounding rule applies. Do not use an average accuracy score to excuse a wrong entity assignment, missing transaction or duplicate posting.

5. Test permissions, failures and repeatability

Verify permissions by attempting restricted actions

Test separate user roles with synthetic data. Confirm that a report viewer cannot change account mappings, a preparer cannot approve their own restricted changes, and a location-limited user cannot access another location’s restricted information where that restriction is required.

Also test who can export data, change allocation rules, post entries and reopen periods. Record the actual result of each attempted action. A description of intended permissions is not evidence that they work.

Make the workflow fail safely

Introduce a malformed file, missing column, unknown account, interrupted import and unavailable source. Confirm that the workflow:

  • Identifies what failed and what remains incomplete.
  • Does not silently fill missing financial facts with guesses.
  • Prevents incomplete output from appearing final.
  • Routes the issue to a named reviewer and backup.
  • Can resume without duplicating previously accepted activity.

Test exception resolution through closure. An alert is not enough if nobody owns the next step.

Repeat the same test

Rerun unchanged inputs using the same recorded configuration. Financial totals, entity assignments and approved classifications should remain consistent. Narrative wording may vary, but amounts and factual statements must remain tied to the underlying records.

If results change, investigate before acceptance. Record the model or service version when available, prompt or instruction version, mappings, configuration and run date. If a vendor does not expose version information, document that limitation and the retesting approach.

6. Maintain a test register and issue log

Keep evidence outside the generated answer itself. Save the synthetic input, independently prepared expected result, actual output, reconciliation and reviewer conclusion in an approved repository.

Use a register like this one. These are template entries, not completed test results.

Test ID Acceptance criterion Evidence to retain Status Issue and sign-off
REC-01 Receipt detail and output both equal $20,000 Source file, output and reconciliation Not run Finance reviewer pending
DUP-01 Repeated import does not add another payment Both run outputs and duplicate notice Not run Administrator review pending
ALLOC-01 Approved split produces $6,000/$4,000 without changing total expense Allocation policy and location reports Not run CPA treatment sign-off pending
ACCESS-01 Restricted user cannot change mappings Role setup and attempted-action result Not run Access reviewer pending
ERROR-01 Missing location is held for review Exception record, notification and resolution evidence Not run Routing owner pending
ROLL-01 Processing stops and fallback resumes without duplication Stop/resume record and reconciliation Not run Finance lead and administrator pending

Use clear statuses: Pass, Fail, Blocked or Not run. A missing expected result or unavailable test environment is not a pass.

For each unresolved issue, record its impact, affected tests, owner, workaround, target resolution and retest evidence. Distinguish a cosmetic report problem from a defect affecting amounts, access or posting.

CPA sign-off should identify the financial treatments reviewed, the applicable configuration and any limitations. It is not a blanket endorsement of the vendor, every future output or legal compliance.

7. Make an explicit launch decision

Launch only within the tested scope. Document the accepted configuration, approved uses, required human reviews and remaining restrictions.

Unresolved defects involving wrong totals, entity mixing, unauthorized access, duplicate postings or ineffective rollback should block the affected workflow. A noncritical presentation issue may permit restricted acceptance if the owner documents a workable control and a resolution owner.

Before launch, rehearse rollback:

  • Who pauses imports, posting or report distribution?
  • What last-known-good configuration or manual process will be used?
  • How will affected transactions be identified and reconciled?
  • Who approves corrections to entries already posted?
  • How will duplicate processing be prevented when work resumes?
  • Who notifies staff that earlier reports should not be used?

Do not assume restoring settings reverses financial effects. Posted entries and distributed reports may need separate correction and review.

8. Revalidate after material changes

Material changes include a new data source, chart-of-accounts revision, payroll mapping change, new entity or location, revised allocation method, altered permissions, or a significant vendor or model update.

Assess which workflows are affected, rerun their tests and include related checks for unintended effects. A new location allocation rule, for example, should trigger tests of both location reports and the practice-wide total.

Routine monthly review still covers current-period bank reconciliations, payroll tie-outs, unusual transactions and unresolved exceptions. If those reviews reveal behavior outside the accepted design, pause the affected workflow and reopen validation rather than repeatedly correcting the same error manually.

FAQs

Is validation the same as checking the books each month?

No. Validation tests whether a configured workflow behaves as intended using known inputs and expected results. Monthly review checks actual period activity for completeness and accuracy. A validated workflow can still receive incomplete data or encounter a new exception, so both are necessary.

Does passing these tests establish HIPAA compliance or vendor security?

No. Accounting acceptance tests do not establish regulatory compliance, certify security or replace review of data handling and relevant agreements. Use synthetic records for testing and separately evaluate any proposed use of protected health information with appropriate advisers.

Who should sign off before launch?

The practice owner or designated leader should authorize business use. The finance reviewer should confirm reconciliations and test evidence, the CPA should approve relevant financial treatments, and the administrator should confirm access and recovery procedures. Record unresolved limitations rather than treating any one signature as universal approval.

Can human review stop after the system passes?

No. Successful tests support only the defined use and tested conditions. Keep the agreed review controls, reconcile current financial records and investigate exceptions. Material changes or unexpected behavior require renewed testing before expanding reliance.
